The Ana tree (*Faidherbia albida*) is a large, thorny legume native to Africa and the Middle East and introduced to India and Pakistan. It can reach 30 m tall and has roots extending to about 40 m deep. Unusually, it sheds its leaves during the rainy season, allowing crops to grow beneath it without heavy shade. It is valued for nitrogen fixation, erosion control, livestock fodder, timber, food, beverages, and traditional medicine. Its leaves reportedly contain dimethyltryptamine (DMT). [Sources: s1:p3–p10]
