The Chinese elm (*Ulmus parvifolia*), also called lacebark elm, is a small-to-medium deciduous or semideciduous tree native to eastern Asia. It typically grows 10–18 m tall and has small, glossy leaves, autumn-produced flowers and samaras, and distinctive flaking, mottled bark [s1:p1–p2].

Widely cultivated as a hardy ornamental and bonsai, it tolerates pruning and harsh urban conditions and shows strong resistance to Dutch elm disease and several pests [s1:p7, s1:p10–p12]. Its hard, tough wood is used for furniture, flooring, tool handles, veneer, and longbows [s1:p4–p5]. The species has naturalized in several countries and is considered invasive in parts of the United States [s1:p23].
