Creeping gloxinia (*Lophospermum erubescens*) is a climbing or sprawling perennial native to Mexico’s Sierra Madre Oriental. It uses twining leaf stalks to climb and bears tubular pinkish-red flowers, typically from April through January in its native habitat. Widely grown as an ornamental since 1830, it prefers frost-free conditions but can survive colder winters if its roots and base are protected. It has naturalized across many tropical and subtropical regions. Despite its common name, it is not closely related to true gloxinias. [Sources: s1:p1, s1:p3–p5, s1:p11–p14]
