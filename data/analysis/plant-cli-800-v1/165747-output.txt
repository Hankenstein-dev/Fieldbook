Rustwort (*Nowellia curvifolia*) is a tiny, delicate liverwort found on rotting, bark-free logs across northern temperate and subarctic forests. It forms flat mats and has two rows of distinctive bilobed, sail-like leaves; each lower lobe folds inward to create a water-holding sac. Its colour varies seasonally from green and brown to rose or reddish purple. It reproduces through spores and single-celled gemmae and can serve as an indicator of forest ecosystem conditions. Asian populations may represent distinct geographic races or cryptic species. [Sources: s1:p2, s1:p8–p11, s1:p14–p15]
