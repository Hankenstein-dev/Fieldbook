Alligatorweed (*Alternanthera philoxeroides*) is a South American perennial herb now invasive in more than 30 countries. It grows on land and in shallow water, where its hollow, sprawling stems form dense floating mats; it has opposite leaves and small, white flowers on stalked spikes (s1:p1–p2).

These mats displace native vegetation, obstruct waterways and irrigation, degrade habitat, and increase flooding and mosquito-breeding risks (s1:p3–p6). Outside its native range, alligatorweed spreads chiefly through stem and leaf fragments, so incomplete manual removal can worsen dispersal (s1:p7–p9). Management includes early detection, physical containment, biological-control insects, and repeated herbicide applications (s1:p11–p12). Import and trade are prohibited throughout the European Union (s1:p3).
