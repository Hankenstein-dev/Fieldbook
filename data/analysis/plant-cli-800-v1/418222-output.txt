Small mallow (*Malva pusilla*) is an annual or biennial herb in the mallow family. It grows 10–51 cm tall, with rounded, serrated leaves and small white-to-pale-pink flowers. Native to parts of Europe and western Asia, it commonly inhabits grasslands, pastures, roadsides, wastelands, gardens, and farmland, where its deep taproot and long-lived seeds can make it a persistent weed. Its leaves and seeds are edible and mildly flavored, although the leaves may accumulate high nitrate levels. Reported traditional uses include herbal remedies, natural dyes, and using the root as a toothbrush. [s1:p2–p11]
