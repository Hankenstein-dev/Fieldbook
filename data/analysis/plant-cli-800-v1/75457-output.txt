Anredera cordifolia (Madeira or mignonette vine) is a fast-growing, evergreen succulent climber native to South America. Its heart-shaped fleshy leaves, fragrant cream flowers, rhizomes, and easily detached aerial tubers help identify it. Introduced widely, it is highly invasive and can smother vegetation and collapse mature trees.

The leaves and stems may be eaten raw or cooked, and the rhizomes are edible; the aerial bulbils are inedible. Leaves contain beta-carotene, vitamin E, calcium, and mucilage. Because fragments readily form new plants, discarded material should never be composted or placed in green waste. (Sources: s1:p1, p3–p8, p14–p16, p18, p24–p25)
