Molasses grass (*Melinis minutiflora*) is a perennial African grass with fragrant foliage and colorful flower clusters. It grows about 30–60 inches tall, forming dense mats from long, slender stems; its wind-dispersed seeds aid its spread. Introduced elsewhere for livestock feed, it has become invasive in regions including Hawaii, South America, and parts of the Pacific. Its dry, brittle growth burns readily, promoting a recurring grass–fire cycle that suppresses native vegetation and reduces biodiversity. [s1:p1–p4]
