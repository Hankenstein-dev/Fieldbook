Tōtara (*Podocarpus totara*) is a long-lived podocarp tree endemic to New Zealand. It usually grows 20–25 m tall, occasionally exceeding 35 m, and has flaky purplish-to-golden-brown bark, stiff needle-like leaves, and fleshy red mature cones bearing one or two seeds (s1:p2, s1:p5).

It occurs across the North and South Islands, mainly in fertile, well-drained lowlands but also in montane and lower-subalpine habitats up to 600 m (s1:p15). Its durable, rot-resistant wood was especially important for Māori carving and waka construction and was later widely used for piles, railway sleepers, and fence posts (s1:p21–p22). The red flesh surrounding its seeds is edible (s1:p24), and tōtara symbolizes strength and mana in Māori culture (s1:p26).
