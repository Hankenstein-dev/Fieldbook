Cuphea carthagenensis (Colombian waxweed, also called sete-sangrias) is a South American herb in the family Lythraceae. It grows 20–60 cm tall, with reddish glandular hairs, opposite leaves, small pink-purple flowers, and capsule fruits. It prefers moist, sandy soils and can become invasive in fields, vacant lots, and pastures.

The whole plant is used in South American folk medicine, but its therapeutic effects remain poorly studied. Excessive consumption may cause diarrhea, prolonged use may sharply lower blood pressure, and use in children is not recommended.
