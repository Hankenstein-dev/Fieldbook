L119@P2: Studies of bryo-communities of continental
L120@P2: Europe in general show a tendency to
L121@P2: overemphasize the presence of "pure", strictly
L122@P2: delimited communities. A few studies of
L123@P2: Macaronesian bryo-vegetation (v. H~~BscHMANN
L124@P2: 1971, 1974) are unfortunately examples of this
L125@P2: tendency, leading to descriptions of numerous
L126@P2: more or less local miniassociations, mostly
L127@P2: characterized by dominant species. The aims to
L128@P2: range Macaronesian endemic bryo-communities,
L129@P2: synsystematically under Central-European
L130@P2: alliances and orders has also led to unfortunate
L131@P2: results. This approach seems especially dubious,
L132@P2: considering the high frequency of
L133@P2: Azorean/Macaronesian endemic species in most
L134@P2: of the Azorean communities described. Also
L135@P2: bryo-communities of central and northern Europe
L136@P2: tend in a westward direction to become less easily
L137@P2: characterized by differential species with high
L138@P2: differential values. This feature of importance to
L139@P2: the work on bryo-ecology and bryo-sociology
L140@P2: becomes strongly accentuated in Macaronesia.
L141@P2: Man does not like chaos. Azorean bryovegetation may appear highly chaotic in several
L142@P2: aspects, with numerous transitions between
L143@P2: communities and numerous different primary
L144@P2: stages of succession on various substrates, linked
L145@P2: to accidental diaspore supply and "first on the
L146@P2: spot" effects. In general, it is unwise to try to
L147@P2: explain all combinations of species using
L148@P2: ecological correlations. This is naturally also the
L149@P2: case with epiphyllous bryo-communities, which
L150@P2: mostly consist of short-term presence of primarily
L151@P2: colonizing, weakly competitive and very small
L152@P2: hepatics. The constitution of these communities
L153@P2: and the species dominance is often accidentally
L154@P2: linked to diaspore supply (cf. OLARINMOYE
L155@P2: 1975b) and the frequency of colonizing species
L156@P2: rapidly becoming fertile.
L157@P2: Establishment of the epiphyllous hepatics
L158@P2: depends on the season of diaspore supply.
L159@P2: Survival and development at the beginning of the
L160@P2: rainy season is usually less successful than at the
L161@P2: end, when diaspores do not get so easily washed
L162@P2: away from glabrous leaf-surfaces (cf. WINKLER
L163@P2: 1967). There are also habitat conditions with
L164@P2: accidental properties (wind, temperature, moisture
L165@P2: supply etc.) of extreme nature, difficult to
L166@P2: measure, but of grcat importance to colonization
L167@P2: and survival of the epiphyilous bryophytes. They
L168@P2: invade and die hardly as a consequence of mean
L169@P2: values of environmental conditions. Mean values
L170@P2: of annual precipitation and air humidity are thus
L171@P2: only rough indications of habitat conditions,
L172@P2: favourable to the existence or not of oprimally or
L173@P2: suboptimally developed epiphyllous vegetation.
