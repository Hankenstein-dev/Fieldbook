L44: cite22†Ludwigia L45: # Ludwigia erecta (L.) H.Hara
L46: 
L47: First published in J. Jap. Bot. 28: 292 (1953)
L48: 
L49: This species is accepted
L50: 
L51: The native range of this species is Tropical & Subtropical America. It is an annual or subshrub and grows primarily in the seasonally dry tropical biome. It is has social uses, as animal food and a medicine and for food.

L64:   * cite28†Jussiaea erecta L. L65: ### According to Flora of Somalia
L66: 
L67: M. Thulin et al. Flora of Somalia, Vol. 1-4 [updated 2008] https://plants.jstor.org/collection/FLOS
L68: Morphology General Habit
L69:     Erect herb to 3 m tall, glabrous
L70: Morphology Leaves
L71:     Leaves lanceolate to elliptic, 2–13 x 0.2–4.5 cm, acute to acuminate, rarely obtuse at the apex; petiole 2–15 mm long
L72: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences
L73:     Flowers in upper axils, sometimes ± aggregated
L74: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Calyx
L75:     Sepals 4, 2–6 mm long
L76: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Corolla
L77:     Petals 3.5–5 mm long
L78: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Androecium Stamens
L79:     Stamens 8
L80: Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
L81:     Capsules 10–19 mm long, 4-ribbed, 4-angled
L82: Morphology Reproductive morphology Seeds
L83:     Seeds 0.3–0.4(–0.5) mm long.
L84: Distribution
L85:     S3 (near Faanole) widespread in tropical Africa, native in the New World.
L86: Ecology
L87:     Altitude c. 35 m.
L88: Vernacular
L89:     Fiideey-malabeey (Somali).
L90: Note
L91:     L. erecta has previously not been recorded from Somalia, and is still not known from Kenya. In Somalia the leaves are said to be cooked to make sauce for maize porridge.
L92: cite29†[FSOM] L93: ### According to Flora Zambesiaca
L94: 
L95: Onagraceae, P. H. Raven. Flora Zambesiaca 4. 1978
L96: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Calyx
L97:     Sepals 4, 2–6 × 1–1·5 mm., lanceolate-acuminate.
L98: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Corolla
L99:     Petals 3·5–5 × 2–2·5 mm., obovate.
L100: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Androecium Stamens
L101:     Stamens 8, subequal; filaments c. 1·5 mm. long; anthers c. 0·6 mm. long, shedding pollen directly on the stigma at anthesis.
L102: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Pollen
L103:     Pollen shed in tetrads.
L104: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Disc
L105:     Disk not elevated, with a sunken, white-hairy nectary around the base of each epipetalous stamen.
L106: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Gynoecium Style
L107:     Style 0·5–1 mm. long; stigma globose, 1–1·1 mm. thick, its upper 2/3 receptive.
L108: Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
L109:     Capsule 10–19 × 2–2·5 mm., glabrous, rarely puberulent, pale brown with 4 prominent dark brown ribs, sharply 4-angled with 4 nearly flat walls, irregularly and readily loculicidal, subsessile or on a pedicel up to 2 mm. long.
L110: Morphology Reproductive morphology Seeds
L111:     Seeds 0·3–0·4(0·5) × 0·2–0·3 mm., pluriseriate in each locule of the capsule, free, pale brown, minutely cellular-pitted, elongate-obovoid; raphe c. 1/5 the diameter of the body.
L112: Cytology
L113:     Chromosome number n=8.
L114: Morphology General Habit
L115:     Subglabrous erect herb from 3 cm. to more than 3 m., sometimes more or less woody at base, freely branched, the stems sharply angled from the decurrent leaf-bases.
L116: Morphology Leaves
L117:     Leaves 2–13 × 0·2–4·5 cm., lanceolate to elliptic, rarely ovate, narrowly cuneate at the base, the apex acuminate to acute, rarely obtuse; main veins 16–27 on each side of midrib; petiole 2–15 mm. long.
L118: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers
L119:     Flowers solitary in upper axils.
L120: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences Bracteoles
L121:     Bracteoles c. 0·5 mm. long.

L172: cite35†[AERP] L173: ### According to Flora of Tropical East Africa under the synonym Jussiaea erecta
L174: 
L175: Onagraceae, J. P. M. Brenan. Flora of Tropical East Africa. 1953
L176: Morphology General Habit
L177:     Annual herb to 3 m. high, in Africa absolutely glabrous, even under a x 20 lens; but a variant with ovaries, sepals, midribs and lateral nerves of leaves minutely puberulous occurs in America.
L178: Morphology Leaves
L179:     Leaves lanceolate, sometimes ovate or elliptic, mostly 2–13 cm. long and 0.5–4.3 cm. wide, acute or subacuminate, rarely obtuse at apex; petiole 3–15 mm. long.
L180: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Calyx
L181:     Sepals almost always 4 (5 seen only in one flower), 3–6 mm. long, 1–1.5 mm. wide.
L182: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Corolla
L183:     Petals yellow, elliptic or obovate, 4–5 mm. long, 2–2.5 mm. wide.
L184: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Androecium Stamens
L185:     Stamens twice as many as sepals.
L186: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Gynoecium Pistil
L187:     Apex of ovary, supporting the 0.5–1 mm. long style, flat or only slightly raised.
L188: Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
L189:     Capsules thin-walled, 1–1.9 cm. long, 2.5–5 mm. wide; pedicels 0–8 mm. long.
L190: Morphology Reproductive morphology Seeds
L191:     Seeds pluriseriate in each cell, free, oblong-ellipsoid, narrow-raphed, 0.3–0.4 (–0.5) mm. long, 0.2–0.3 mm. wide, pale brown.
L192: Figures
L193:     Fig. 2/2, (p. 7).
L194: Habitat
L195:     Imperfectly known, probably various wet habitats; Speke and Grant’s specimen collected on “river bank”; near sea-level to 1100 m.
L196: Distribution
L197:     P T3 T4 T6 T8 widespread in tropical Africa from Senegal, the French Sudan and the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan southwards to Angola, Southern Rhodesia and Portuguese East Africaalso on the Comoro Islands, Madagascar and the Seychellesin America from Florida southwards to Brazil, Paraguay and Boliviaapparently quite absent from Asia although commonly recorded, due to misapplying the name J. erecta L. to J. suffruticosa L.
