L31: Distribution Mostly Piedmont and Sandhills; also Wilson, Washington, and Currituck counties.
L32: Native of Europe; in N.A. NY to MO south to FL and TX; also OR to B.C.
L33: Abundance Uncommon to fairly common in Piedmont, rare elsewhere. Easily overlooked and may occur in many more counties.
L34: Habitat Dry to xeric sandy soil of roadsides, fields, disturbed areas, lawn weed, cropfields, campus lawns.
L35: Phenology Flowering and fruiting late March-May.
L36: Identification This is a low, semi-erect to erect plant with several stems no more than 4 inches tall. Leaves are alternate, obovate (fan-shaped), deeply or shallowly cut into lobes. The flowers are less than 1.5 mm across, 4-12 in a dense cyme opposite a leaf.
L37: Taxonomic Comments According to FNA (2014), the name Alchemilla microcarpa has been erroneously applied to Aphanes australis. This species was named as Alchemilla microcarpa in RAB (1968).
