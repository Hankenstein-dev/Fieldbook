L46@P0: ABSTRACT. Although kiwifruit (Actinidia) is popular worldwide, its complex morphology has resulted
L47@P0: in long-standing confusion regarding its nomenclature, classification, and identification. In an attempt to resolve this issue, we conducted a phylogenetic analysis on 72 wild Actinidia accessions in Taiwan. We used
L48@P0: 60 morphological characters as taxonomic traits to construct a seriated heat map that revealed A. callosa var.
L49@P0: ephippioidea and A. rufa (sensu Flora of Taiwan, 2nd Edition) as introgressive hybrids between A. chinensis
L50@P0: var. setosa and A. callosa (sensu Flora of Taiwan, 2nd Edition), as well as five significant groups of Actinida in
L51@P0: Taiwan. Based on these results, we coded the indicator response matrix for the logistic regression models as
L52@P0: dichotomizers and used a Bayesian discriminant model as a polychotomizer. After classifier modeling, the two
L53@P0: classifiers were combined to identify Actinidia specimens in domestic and international herbaria. As a result,
L54@P0: a taxonomic revision was made: A. callosa var. callosa and A. callosa var. ephippioidea were revised as A.
L55@P0: rufa; A. rubricaulis was revised as A. callosa var. discolor; A. chinensis var. setosa was elevated to A. setosa;
L56@P0: and A. tetramera was a misidentification of A. arguta. Of these, only A. setosa is endemic to Taiwan.

L93@P1: The examples above demonstrate the controversy in
L94@P1: the classification of Actinidia. Although recent molecular
L95@P1: studies have helped resolve phylogenetic and identification
L96@P1: problems concerning Actinidia (Li et al., 2002; Zhao et al.,
L97@P1: 2007), they have not helped much in resolving Actinidia’s
L98@P1: classification and nomenclature problems. In 1989, Tang
L99@P1: and Xiang first tried to use statistical methods to resolve
L100@P1: the classification of the Clematoclethra complex (Actinidiaceae) in China and pointed out that numerical taxonomy
L101@P1: and statistics were effective ways to resolve classification
L102@P1: issues of plant taxa, especially those with complex morphological characters. Xu et al. (1998) employed 11 leaf
L103@P1: characters of A. chinensis cv. Tong-Shan no. 5 in conducting discriminant and cluster analyses to identify male and
L104@P1: female plants. Discriminant analysis yielded a high rate
L105@P1: of sexual identification of cultivars and cluster analysis
L106@P1: implied that it was difficult to distinguish female from
L107@P1: male plants by leaf characters. He et al. (2000a, b) selected
L108@P1: micromorphological characters of foliar trichomes and
L109@P1: performed quantitative taxonomic analyses to study the
L110@P1: classification and phylogenetic relationships of 27 Actinidia species and two varieties in China. The results indicated
L111@P1: that genus Actinidia is a monophyletic group. Phylogenetic
L112@P1: analysis of 22 morphological characters revealed two
L113@P1: monophyletic groups within Actinidia in China (Li et al.,
L114@P1: 2000). Yang (2001) used 19 fruit traits from 12 taxa of
L115@P1: Actinidia in China in a Q and R-type cluster analyses. The
L116@P1: results showed that many traits of Actinidia are closely
L117@P1: related. In 2006, Guo and Zhang first tried to use dielectric
L118@P1: properties of kiwifruit and a back propagation network of
L119@P1: an artificial neural network to clear up the classification of
L120@P1: two A. deliciosa cultivars, ‘Hayward’ and ‘Qinmei’, which
L121@P1: are hardly distinguishable from each other. The study
L122@P1: only used 20 samples of each variety and got recognition
L123@P1: rates of 100% on training samples and up to 90% on test
L124@P1: samples. Furthermore, Cuong et al. (2007) employed a
L125@P1: principal component analysis and cluster analysis to resolve the nomenclature of the Actinidiaceae in Vietnam,
L126@P1: and produced a taxonomic revision. Subsequently, Chen
L127@P1: et al. (2008) selected 10 characters of the fruits and leaves
L128@P1: of 11 Actinidia species to carry out a cluster analysis. The
L129@P1: results implied that morphological traits of Actinidia are
L130@P1: important for phylogenetic studies.
L131@P1: Previous research shows that both statistical methods
L132@P1: and morphological characters are crucial to the taxonomic
L133@P1: study of Actinidia. Thus far, all numerical taxonomic work
L134@P1: on Actinidia has been on the native species of China and
L135@P1: Vietnam. The purpose of this study is to use numerical
