L139:   * Paleolaria cite68†Cass. L140: Palafoxia, or palafox, is a genus of North American cite58†flowering plants in the cite64†Bahia tribe within the cite62†Asteraceae (sunflower family).^{cite69†[2] }^{cite70†[3] }
L141: ## Description
L142: 
L143: [cite71†edit ]
L144: 
L145: The erect, slender cite72†stem grows 30–60 centimetres (12–24 in) tall, branching in the lower half and is sparsely leaved. It is glandular and hairy on the upper parts.^{cite73†[4] }
L146: 
L147: The glabrous, glandular cite74†leaves are lanceolate, 3–20 millimetres (1⁄8–3⁄4 in) wide and 4–7.5 cm (1+5⁄8–3 in) long, and are arranged alternately.^{cite73†[4] }
L148: A few cite75†flower heads appear at the end of the upper branches. The reddish to pink ray florets have three narrow lobes. They are subtended by involucral cite76†bracts .^{cite73†[4] }
L149: 
L150: The seed-like cite77†fruit is narrow with a cite78†pappus of several pointed scales (giving rise to the common name 'Spanish Needles' for cite53†P. arida ). These plants self-sow freely.^{cite73†[4] }
L151: ## Taxonomy
L152: 
L153: [cite79†edit ]
L154: 
L155: The genus is named after cite80†José de Palafox y Melzi, Duke of Saragossa (1776–1847), a Spanish captain-general in the cite81†Peninsular War against the invading armies of cite82†Napoleon .

L171: 
L172: [cite121†edit ]
L173: 
L174: These are cite122†drought-tolerant , annual cite123†herbs growing on sandy plains, dunes, deserts (cite124†Mojave desert , cite125†Sonoran desert ) and rangeland, native to the United States and Mexico. P. callosa is cite126†naturalised in Hawaii.
L175: ## Ecology
L176: 
L177: [cite127†edit ]
L178: 
L179: They are attractive to bees, butterflies and birds and are used as food plants by the cite128†larvae of some cite129†Lepidoptera species including cite130†Schinia niveicosta , which feeds exclusively on P. linearis.
