L130: Eucalyptus globulus subsp. maidenii, commonly known as Maiden's gum,^{cite56†[2] } is a subspecies of tree that is cite57†endemic to southeastern Australia. It has mostly smooth bark with some persistent slabs of old bark at the base, juvenile leaves with one glaucous side, lance-shaped adult leaves, flower buds arranged in groups of seven, white flowers and conical, cite58†pedicellate , sometimes cite59†glaucous†en.wiktionary.org fruit that is more or less square in cross-section
L131: Flower buds Fruit Trunk of tree in cite60†Kunming Botanical Garden , China
L132: ## Description
L133: 
L134: [cite61†edit ]
L135: Eucalyptus globulus subsp. maidenii is a tree that typically grows to a height of 45–50 m (148–164 ft) and forms a cite62†lignotuber . The bark is mostly smooth, shedding in long strips to leave a white or greyish surface. There is sometimes rough, partially shed bark at the base of the trunk and ribbons of shedding bark in the upper branches. Young plants and cite63†coppice regrowth have stems that are more or less square in cross-section, with a prominent wing on each corner.
L136: The juvenile leaves are cite64†sessile , arranged in opposite pairs, elliptic to egg-shaped, the lower surface covered by a white, waxy bloom, 40–110 mm (1.6–4.3 in) long and 17–60 mm (0.67–2.36 in) wide. Adult leaves are arranged alternately, the same shade of green on both sides, lance-shaped to curved, 120–355 mm (4.7–14.0 in) long and 12–40 mm (0.47–1.57 in) wide on a cite65†petiole 15–37 mm (0.59–1.46 in) long.^{cite56†[2] }^{cite66†[3] }^{cite67†[4] }
L137: The flower buds are arranged in leaf cite68†axils†en.wiktionary.org in groups of seven on a flattened cite69†peduncle 8–25 mm (0.31–0.98 in) long, the individual buds on a thickened cite58†pedicel 3–8 mm (0.12–0.31 in) long. Mature buds are club-shaped to pear-shaped, 8–11 mm (0.31–0.43 in) long and 5–7 mm (0.20–0.28 in) wide with a beaked or flattened cite70†operculum that has a central knob. Flowering has been recorded in March and the flowers are white.
L138: The fruit is a woody, conical, sometimes glaucous cite71†capsule , 5–8 mm (0.20–0.31 in) long and 6–10 mm (0.24–0.39 in) wide with the valves at about rim level.^{cite56†[2] }^{cite66†[3] }^{cite67†[4] }
L139: ## Taxonomy and naming
L140: 
L141: [cite72†edit ]
L142: Maiden's gum was first formally described in 1890 by cite73†Ferdinand von Mueller who gave it the name Eucalyptus maidenii and published the description in cite74†Proceedings of the Linnean Society of New South Wales from collections by cite75†William Bäuerlen .^{cite76†[5] }^{cite77†[6] } In 1974, cite52†James Barrie Kirkpatrick described four subspecies of E. globulus and changed the name E. maidenii to E. globulus subsp. maidenii.^{cite78†[7] }^{cite79†[8] } The epithet maidenii honours cite80†Joseph Maiden .^{cite56†[2] }
L143: ## Distribution and habitat
L144: 
L145: [cite81†edit ]
L146: 
L147: This subspecies of E. globulus grows in forest in mountain valleys, on slopes and ridges in near-coastal ranges of New South Wales south of the cite82†Shoalhaven River and in eastern Victoria near the upper reaches of the cite83†Genoa and cite84†Cann Rivers .^{cite66†[3] }^{cite67†[4] }^{cite85†[9] }
