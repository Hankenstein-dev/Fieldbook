L26: Etymology of Scrophularia
L27:     Scrophulária, -ae, f. - medieval Latin scrophularia, -ae f. = figwort (Scrophularia sp. pl., mainly S. nodosa L., Scrophulariaceae).
L28: According to Ambrosini (1666), called Scrophularia because of its knotty root and because of the effect (for it is believed to be useful for [combating] the nodules or swellings commonly known as scrofula); although others say that it is so called because pigs like them, or because they multiply and proliferate like the sows that are called Scrophae (those intended for breeding) -Lat. scrofulae, -arum f. pl. = scrofula or swellings; related to Lat. scrofa, -ae f. = breeding sow; Lat. -aria, -ariae f.
L29: = suffix indicating relation in a broad sense.
L30: Etymology of alpestris
L31:     bot. alpestris, -e = alpine, characteristic of high mountains, but rather below the alpine belt [Lat. Alpes, -ium f. plural = the Alps; Lat. -estris, -estre = suffix indicating place of habitation].
