L22@P0: Introduction
L23@P0: Centaurea comprises about 250 species (Susanna
L24@P0: & Garcia-Jacas 2007). It is mainly distributed in
L25@P0: Europe, the Mediterranean region, and SW Asia,
L26@P0: with some introductions in other more or less
L27@P0: distant regions. The paraphyletic nature of its
L28@P0: classical delimitation is well known (Susanna
L29@P0: et al. 1995, García-Jacas et al. 2000), so that its
L30@P0: classification is ongoing and subject to information derived from molecular characters in phylogenetic studies (Bremer 1994, Gabrielian 1995,
L31@P0: Susanna et al. 1995, Wagenitz & Hellwig 1996,
L32@P0: Garcia-Jacas et al. 2000, 2001). One result has
L33@P0: been the segregation of certain groups traditionally included in Centaurea (e.g., subgen. Centaurea, now in the genus Rhaponticoides), and
L34@P0: re-incorporation of others that had been segregated into separate genera (see López & Devesa
L35@P0: 2008a). It also became necessary to select a new
L36@P0: type for the genus: Centaurea paniculata L.
L37@P0: (Greuter et al. 2001).
L38@P0: Currently, included in the section Centaurea are most taxa of the traditional subgenera
L39@P0: Acrolophus (= Acosta; Holub 1972) and Phalolepis. The sections form a monophyletic group
L40@P0: (Garcia-Jacas et al. 2006, Suárez-Santiago et al.
L41@P0: 2007) in which there has been frequent hybridization between species (Ochsmann 2000) and they
L42@P0: also share the same derived pollen type (Jacea
L43@P0: type, following Wagenitz 1955). Although most
L44@P0-1: species of the section Centaurea are found in 2 López et al. • ANN.BOT.FENNICI Vol.48
L45@P1: the East Mediterranean and the Irano–Turanian
L46@P1: region, the Iberian Peninsula and NW Africa also
L47@P1: constitute a major centre of diversification.
L48@P1: In the Iberian Peninsula, the section Centaurea includes in its current delimitation more
L49@P1: than 60 taxa, with a high degree of endemism,
L50@P1: and notoriously difficult taxonomy. An essential
L51@P1: source for the group in the area is the synthesis
L52@P1: by Dostál (1976) for Flora Europaea, which
L53@P1: for the taxa of the Willkommia group is complemented by important subsequent contributions
L54@P1: (Blanca 1980a, 1980b, 1981a–f, 1984, Talavera
L55@P1: 1984b). For the taxa of the C. alba group, however, there clearly remain uresolved taxonomic
L56@P1: problems which led to new contributions (Talavera 1984a). This is also the case with the C.
L57@P1: paniculata group, for which there are classical
L58@P1: reviews by Arènes (1949, 1951). Both groups
L59@P1: have been the subject of recent studies (López
L60@P1: 2008), which gave novel karyological (López &
L61@P1: Devesa 2008c) and taxonomic (López & Devesa
L62@P1: 2008a, 2008b, 2008d) contributions of interest.
L63@P1: The goal of the recent studies cited above
L64@P1: was the account of the genus for the volume XVI
L65@P1: of Flora Iberica, and as a further contribution
L66@P1: we here study C. langei, a species of the C. paniculata group endemic to the N and NW Iberian
L67@P1: Peninsula. This comprises a very variable taxon,
L68@P1: with seven subspecies being recognized, some
L69@P1: hitherto regarded as independent species, and the
L70@P1: recognition of one new subspecies described in
L71@P1: the present work.
L72@P1: Material and methods
L73@P1: This study is based on morpho-geographical and
L74@P1: biometrical analyses carried out on material collected by the authors and on material deposited
L75@P1: in different herbaria (BC, BCN, COFC, COI,
L76@P1: COI-Willk., JACA, JAEN, LISE, LOU, MA,
L77@P1: MAF, MGC, SALA, SANT, SEV, UNEX; abbreviations according to Holmgren et al. 1990).
L78@P1: Some of these herbaria contain material of interest for lectotypifications, others were consulted
L79@P1: for that purpose only (C-Lange, LISI, LY-Rouy).
L80@P1: For C. langei subsp. dominguezii, chromosome counts of root meristem cells were
L81@P1: made. The antimitotic agent used was 0.002 M
L82@P1: 8-hydroxyquinoline (Tjio & Levan 1950), and as
L83@P1: fixative a mixture of absolute alcohol and ferric
L84@P1: acetate (3:1). The stain used was alcoholic acetocarmine (Snow 1963) applied for 24–48 hours.
L85@P1: The description of the chromosome morphology
L86@P1: follows the terminology of Levan et al. (1964).
L87@P1: Chromosome size is expressed by three values:
L88@P1: the length of the longest chromosome, the length
L89@P1: of the shortest chromosome and the mean length
L90@P1: (with the standard deviation in parentheses).
L91@P1: Results and discussion
L92@P1: Centaurea langei includes plants of the NW
L93@P1: Iberian Peninsula which many authors (among
L94@P1: others Willkomm 1865, Merino 1915, Arènes
L95@P1: 1949, Dostál 1976) have identified as C. aristata
L96@P1: Hoffmans. & Link or C. micrantha Hoffmans. &
L97@P1: Link. However, C. aristata is a different species
L98@P1: (cf. López & Devesa 2008b), and C. micrantha
L99@P1: is a homonym of a name (C. micranthos S.G.
L100@P1: Gmel.) previously used in a different sense,
L101@P1: which led to its substitution by new names (C.
L102@P1: hoffmanseggiana, Lázaro (1896: 889); C. hoffmanseggi, von Hayek (1901: 588). This is also
L103@P1: the case with C. divergens Lange (1862), non C.
L104@P1: divergens Vis. (1847), and C. langeana Willk.
L105@P1: (1865).
L106@P1: Within the group of C. paniculata, C. langei
L107@P1: presents the greatest affinity with C. limbata and
L108@P1: C. hanryi. It shares with these two species the
L109@P1: characters of oblong-obovoid achenes, brownish
L110@P1: at maturity, and involucral bracts with an appendage well differentiated from the base, with at
L111@P1: least the upper lateral fimbriae concolorous, and
L112@P1: it is differentiated by the cauline leaves generally
L113@P1: being entire (López & Devesa 2008a, 2008d). It
L114@P1: has marked variability, with seven groups of populations or subspecies being recognizable that are
L115@P1: geographically well defined, but with very similar characteristics and some degree of sympatry
L116@P1: (Fig. 1), and not infrequent transitional individuals in zones of contact. Thus it is not surprising
L117@P1: that from this geographic nucleus a multitude of
L118@P1: subspecies, varieties, and have been described
L119@P1: (Merino 1915, Arènes 1949).
