L82: ## Novas ocorrências de Zygnematophyceae (Streptophyta) em ambientes lóticos subtropicais, Estado do Paraná, Brasil
L83: 
L84: AuthorshipSCIMAGO INSTITUTIONS RANKINGS
L85: ## ABSTRACT
L86: The study aimed to identify and record Zygnematophyceae species, highlighting new occurrences in three river basins of western Paraná State (Paraná III, Piquiri, and Iguaçu). Phytoplankton samples were collected over two years using a 25µm mesh net. Microalgae were analyzed via microscopy, and taxonomic classification followed specialized literature. Samples were deposited in the UNIOESTE herbarium (UNOPA) and data made available on speciesLink (cite21†www.splink.cria.org.br†www.splink.cria.org.br ).
L87: Zygnematophyceae along the basins comprised 41 taxa, distributed in three families: Closteriaceae, Desmidiaceae and Mesotaeniaceae. The study registered ten new citations for the State of Paraná: Closterium parvulum Nägeli, Euastrum obesum var. extensum A.M.Scott & Prescott, Staurastrum pseudotetracerum (Nordstedt) West & G.S.West, S. volans var. fuquenense Coesel, S. teliferum Ralfs, S. leptocladum f. africanum G.S.West, S. levanderi Grönblad, S. levanderi var.
L88: hollandicum (Coesel & Joosten) Coesel & Meesters, S. subgemmulatum West & G.S.West and Closterium boergesenii (Schmidle) Coesel & Meersters, the last six of which are new citations for Brazil. This study significantly contributes to knowledge of microalgal biodiversity in subtropical lotic environments, expanding the record of Zygnematophyceae species and reinforcing the importance of floristic surveys for conservation and environmental monitoring.
L89: Keywords:
L90: biodiversity; desmids; microalgae; Taxonomy

L92: O estudo teve como objetivo identificar e registrar espécies de Zygnematophyceae, destacando novas ocorrências em três bacias hidrográficas do oeste do Estado do Paraná (Paraná III, Piquiri e Iguaçu). Amostras de fitoplâncton foram coletadas ao longo de dois anos usando uma rede de malha de 25 µm. As microalgas foram analisadas por microscopia, e a classificação taxonômica seguiu a literatura especializada.

As Zygnematophyceae ao longo das bacias compreenderam 41 táxons, distribuídos em três famílias: Closteriaceae, Desmidiaceae e Mesotaeniaceae. L98: ## Introduction
L99: Freshwater resources are indispensable for a diverse set of economic and social activities (cite22†Tang et al. 2022 ). However, disturbances caused by anthropogenic factors in lotic systems jeopardize the integrity of these ecosystems (cite23†Kim et al. 2019 ). Rivers utilized for water abstraction in urban and rural areas are particularly susceptible to these impacts, primarily due to the input of nutrients and pollutants that alter water quality (cite24†Xiao et al. 2019 , cite25†Zhang et al. 2019 ).
L100: In this sense, phytoplankton represents a significant component of aquatic ecosystems and has frequently been considered a valuable bioindicator for assessing the impact of human activities on aquatic ecosystems. This is due to its functional assessment characteristics, including a relatively short life cycle, high productivity, and a representative population size (cite26†Dong et al. 2022 , cite27†Rempel et al. 2022 ).
L101: Furthermore, phytoplankton serves as the primary producer in these ecosystems and exhibits rapid responsiveness to environmental alterations. These changes may include reductions in dissolved oxygen levels, increases in nutrient concentrations, and the introduction of toxic contaminants, which can influence the morphology, physiology, distribution, and abundance of phytoplankton populations (cite28†Casé et al. 2008 , cite29†Kruk et al. 2017 , cite23†Kim et al. 2019 ).
L102: Among the classes of phytoplankton, Zygnematophyceae Round ex Guiry is distinguished as an effective bioindicator in aquatic ecosystems. Its members exhibit considerable morphological diversity, are cosmopolitan, and are frequently encountered in mesotrophic environments in continental waters (cite30†Coesel 1975 , cite31†2001 , cite32†Gontcharov 2008 ).
L103: The scientific information on the class Zygnematophyceae in different environments, especially with regard to different trophic states, may prove useful as a basis for attempts to utilize these algae as a tool in environmental bioindication (Garraza et al. 2019).
L104: Nevertheless, the existing literature on this group in the State of Paraná, particularly in lotic environments, remains insufficient due to the lack of fundamental taxonomic research, given the considerable diversity of species that have been scientifically documented but not yet formally registered.
L105: In a concise bibliographic survey, we identified only ten inventories of the phytoplankton class Zygnematophyceae in rivers in the western region of Paraná, four of which refer to the Tibagi River (cite33†Bittencourt-Oliveira 1993a cite34†b , cite35†Bittencourt-Oliveira & Castro 1993 , cite36†Bittencourt-Oliveira & Mecenas 1994 ), four in the Iguaçu National Park (cite37†Bortolini et al. 2010 , cite38†Biolo et al. 2013 , cite39†Aquino et al.
L106: 2016 , cite40†2017 ) and two studies in reservoir tributaries (cite41†Silva & Cecy 2004 , cite42†Biolo et al. 2008 ).
L107: In light of the ongoing loss of habitat, the scientific recording of biodiversity, through species descriptions, is of great importance and urgency (cite43†Britz et al. 2020 ). Furthermore, taxonomic composition serves as a valuable tool in a range of ecological studies, highlighting the continued necessity for floristic surveys.
L108: With regard to the history of biodiversity in the State of Paraná, cite44†Aquino and collaborators (2018 ) recorded the last taxonomic study of Zygnematophyceae, which makes six years without new studies of this class.
L109: The aim of this study was to conduct a taxonomic survey of the Zygnematophyceae phytoplankton in lotic environments with varying physical characteristics in the western region of Paraná. The objective was to identify new occurrences, provide descriptions, illustrations, and morphometric and meristic data for the species found throughout the study area.
L110: ## Material and methods
L111: Study area and sampling sites - The western region of the State of Paraná, Brazil, comprises 54 municipalities and is situated within the drainage basins of the Paraná III, Piquiri, and Iguaçu rivers (cite45†AMOP 2018 ) (figure 1). Its economy is centered on agricultural production, with the majority of municipalities characterized by extensive monoculture and urbanized areas (cite46†UNDP 2018 ).
L112: Additionally, the Baixo Iguaçu Hydroelectric Power Plant (HPP) (25°30’18’’S and 53°40’24’’W) represents the final planned cascade dam along the Iguaçu River, a tributary of the Paraná River.
L113: cite47†Image: thumbnail of Figure 1†minio.scielo.br L114: 
L115: Figure 1
L116: Municipalities in the western region of Paraná State, Brazil, selected for a taxonomic study of microalgae.
L117: A total of 31 sampling points were selected in the Lower Iguaçu (LI), Paraná III (PIII), and Piquiri (PQ) river basins, with consideration given to the utilization of the rivers for the collection of water for public supply and power generation in the respective cities. Furthermore, the selection of river basins was undertaken to encompass the entire western region of the State of Paraná, with particular consideration given to the ecological and hydrological characteristics of the rivers.
L118: This choice enabled the study of a range of environmental conditions and levels of impact, thereby enriching the analysis of the distribution and diversity of Zygnematophyceae species. The selected sampling points included the municipalities of Capitão Leônidas Marques, Realeza, Lindoeste, Capanema, Planalto, Guaraniaçu, Catanduvas, Três Barras do Paraná, Boa Vista Aparecida, Foz do Iguaçu, Medianeira, Santa Tereza do Oeste, Cascavel, and Toledo.
L119: The sampling points included 15 rivers: Iguaçu, Andrada, Capanema, Monteiro, Gonçalves Dias, Silva Jardim, Santo Antônio, Baú, Alegria, Toledo, Cascavel, Arroio Passo Liso, Jacutinga, Tamanduá, and Itaguaçu.
L120: Sample collection and analysis methodology - The phytoplankton was collected using a 25 µm mesh plankton net and then preserved in Transeau solution (cite48†Bicudo & Menezes 2017 ). The qualitative study of the phytoplankton was conducted using an Olympus CX41 photomicroscope in conjunction with an Olympus SC30 camera, with images captured at 400 x and 1000 x magnification. Five to ten qualitative slides were analyzed per sample or until no new occurrences were observed.
L121: Dimensions are expressed in micrometers (μm), designated as C for length, L for width, I for isthmus, and P for process. The phytoplankton material was sampled in 2019 and 2020, allowing for an adequate degree of detail to be achieved regarding the phytoplankton community, including potential seasonal and temporal variations (cite49†INAG 2009 ).
L122: The systematic and taxonomic framework was based on specialized literature and recent articles (cite50†Oliveira et al. 2016 , cite51†Bicudo et al. 2018 , cite52†2019 , cite53†John et al. 2022 , cite54†Guiry 2013 , cite55†Guiry & Guiry 2025 ). The frequency of occurrence of the taxa was calculated according to cite56†Dajoz (2005 ), with the taxa being considered as follows: constant (C ³ 70%), common (30% ³ C £ 70%), sporadic (10% ³ C £ 30%), or rare (C £ 10%).
L123: To establish the occurrence of taxa in the State of Paraná, only taxonomic studies that included descriptions, measurements, and/or illustrations were considered, as this enabled comparisons to be made with the species found. New records for Paraná are indicated by an asterisk (*), and taxa not previously documented in Brazil are designated with a double asterisk (**).
L124: The material collected is deposited in the Herbarium of the State University of Western Paraná - UNOPA, Cascavel Campus, linked to the Brazilian Herbarium Network, and the computerized data is available on speciesLink (cite21†www.splink.cria.org.br†www.splink.cria.org.br ) (table 1).
