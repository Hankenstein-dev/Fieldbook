L166: Anacamptis is a cite92†genus of cite82†flowering plants in the orchid cite93†family (cite94†Orchidaceae ); it is often abbreviated as Ant in cite95†horticulture . This genus was established by cite89†Louis Claude Richard in 1817; the cite96†type species is the cite97†pyramidal orchid (A. pyramidalis) and it nowadays contains about one-third of the cite98†species placed in the "cite99†wastebin genus " cite100†Orchis before this was split up at the end of the 20th century,^{cite101†[1] } among them many that are of cite102†hybrid origin.
L167: The genus' cite103†scientific name is derived from the Greek word anakamptein, meaning "to bend backwards".
L168: These terrestrial orchids occur on cite104†grasslands , cite105†limestone or cite106†chalk deposits, or on cite107†dunes in cite108†Eurasia , from the cite109†Mediterranean region to cite110†Central Asia .
L169: ## Systematics
L170: 
L171: [cite111†edit ]
L172: 
L173: Except the cite97†pyramidal orchid (A. pyramidalis), all species of Anacamptis seem to form a cite112†clade around the cite113†green-veined orchid (A. morio). They have a cite114†diploid cite115†chromosome number of 32 or 36. A useful character for distinguishing Anacamptis from cite100†Orchis – where the green-veined orchid clade was formerly included – is the basal fusion of the three cite116†sepals in Anacamptis.^{cite117†[2] }
L174: The few bigeneric cite102†hybrids are typically between closely related genera, in particular cite118†Serapias . Hybrids between the pyramidal orchid and the cite119†early purple orchid (Orchis mascula), found in cite120†Cumbria (cite121†UK ) in 1997, proved short-lived because of the highly divergent cite122†genomic lineages of the parents.^{cite117†[2] }
