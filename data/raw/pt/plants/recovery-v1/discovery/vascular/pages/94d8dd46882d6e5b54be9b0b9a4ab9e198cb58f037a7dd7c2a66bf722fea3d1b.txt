L161: Sobralia is a cite82†genus of cite83†orchids native to cite84†Mexico , cite85†Central and cite86†South America .^{cite81†[1] }^{cite87†[2] } The plants are more commonly terrestrial, but are also found growing cite88†epiphytically , in wet forests from cite89†sea level to about 8,800 ft. The genus was named for Dr. cite90†Francisco Sobral , a Spanish cite91†botanist . The genus is abbreviated Sob in trade journals.
L162: Their reed-like cite92†stems range in height from about 1 ft (33 cm) (such as in Sobralia galeottiana) to 44 ft. (13.4 m) (in Sobralia altissima).^{cite93†[3] } They have typically heavily veined, bilobed, plicate, apical cite94†leaves all along the stem. The cite95†inflorescences on the apex of the stem carry one or two successive ephemeral cite96†flowers with large cite97†sepals and cite98†petals . The short duration of the flower is caused by a self-digesting cite99†enzyme .
L163: The cite100†lip is entire or lobed and clasps the cite101†column at its base. This columns carries eight soft cite102†pollinia . These flowers range in color from pure white to yellow, green, pink, purple, red, brown, and even a blue violet.^{cite103†[4] }
