L116@P2: USE AND MANAGEMENT
L117@P2: Nurseries could produce trees with a single leader
L118@P2: and market them as street trees. They stay fairly
L119@P2: small, grow fast when young but slow down later as
L120@P2: the crown broadens. Great for beneath powerlines.
L121@P2: Chinese Photinia needs well-drained soil and a full
L122@P2: sun location. Leaves often become infected with leaf
L123@P2: spot fungi when grown in shade or when the leaves
L124@P2: remain too moist. Plants grow at a moderate rate and
L125@P2: tolerate pruning very well, although the bottom of the
L126@P2: plant often thins when clipped into a hedge. There are
L127@P2: much better plants for hedges, although Photinia
L128@P2: makes a good tall screen plant. Well-adapted to sand
L129@P2: or clay (well-drained), acid or alkaline soil, Photinia
L130@P2: likes to be kept on the dry side and is very droughttolerant. It is popular in all of USDA hardiness zones
L131@P2: 7 and 8 as a highway median plant and could be used
L132@P2: more as a multi-stemmed specimen, street or patio
L133@P2: tree.
L134@P2: ‘Nova’ reaches 10 feet in height with a seven to
L135@P2: eight-foot spread. There is a hybrid between redleaved Photinia (Photinia glabra) and Chinese Photinia
L136@P2: (Photinia serrulata) which perhaps displays the best
L137@P2: characteristics of both parents, called Photinia x
L138@P2: fraseri. It is very popular in the nursery trade and is
L139@P2: often used as a hedge or foundation plant, a usage
L140@P2: which is quite inappropriate due to its large size and
L141@P2: rapid growth rate.
L142@P2: Propagation is by cuttings.

L144@P2: It is usually pest-free but caterpillars, mites, scales,

L147@P2: Chinese Photinia is usually disease-free, except for
