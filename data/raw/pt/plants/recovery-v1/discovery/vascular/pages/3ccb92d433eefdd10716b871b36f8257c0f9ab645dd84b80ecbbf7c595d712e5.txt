L6@P2: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L7@P2: The granite rock pile that forms Dauan, along with nearby Saibai and Boigu Islands, form the Northern
L8@P2: Island Group of Torres Strait, located approximately 150 km north of Thursday Island (see Figure 1).
L9@P2: Whilst Saibai and Boigu Island are extensions of the alluvial Fly Platform, geologically part of the
L10@P2: Papua New Guinea mainland, Dauan is formed on continental basement rock which extends
L11@P2: northward from Cape York Peninsula to Mabadauan Hill on the south-west coast of Papua New
L12@P2: Guinea.
L13@P2: A total of 14 vegetation communities, within ten broad vegetation groups and 14 regional ecosystems
L14@P2: are recognised on the island. The total known flora of comprises 402 species (14 ferns, 388
L15@P2: angiosperms), with 317 native and 85 naturalised species. Nine plant species are considered
L16@P2: threatened at the commonwealth and state levels and a further 25 species considered to have
L17@P2: significance at a regional level.
L18@P2: As for the majority of Torres Strait Islands there is a lack of systematic survey of fauna habitats on the
L19@P2: island. A desktop review identified 135 fauna species that are reported to occur on Dauan. This can
L20@P2: be compared with the 384 terrestrial fauna species that have been reported for the broader Torres
L21@P2: Strait Island group. The Dauan fauna comprises 20 reptiles, 100 birds, 3 frogs and 12 mammals. Of
L22@P2: these, one reptile, one bird and four mammal species are introduced. Three bird species, two bats
L23@P2: and two reptile species reported to occur on the island are listed as threatened under either state or
L24@P2: federal legislation. There are also an additional 32 migratory bird species considered to have
L25@P2: significance at federal level that are reported to occur on the island.
L26@P2: Although recent reports have added significant information to what is known of the fauna on Dauan
L27@P2: Island there undoubtedly remains much to learn. The rocky, inaccessible nature of the islands interior
L28@P2: means that opportunistic recording of fauna species has previously been limited to the more easily
L29@P2: accessible, and often disturbed, parts of the island. Well-planned and systematic survey work is
L30@P2: required to adequately assess the island’s fauna in its entirety. The proximity of the New Guinea
L31@P2: mainland means that bird and bat species are likely to be added to the island’s species list each time
L32@P2: any substantial survey effort is undertaken.
L33@P2: Within the ten broad vegetation groups (or management units) identified on the island, a number of
L34@P2: issues for future management are identified as necessary for the future biodiversity maintenance and
L35@P2: ecological health of the island. These are:
L36@P2: Maintenance of traditional burning regimes within native grassland habitats.
L37@P2: Monitoring of landscapes threatened by changing burning regimes.
L38@P2: Monitoring for the introduction and spread of a number of exotic species, both fauna and
L39@P2: flora, throughout the island landscape.
L40@P2: A requirement for further survey work to document the poorly known faunal assemblage
L41@P2-3: on the island.Habitat Management Profile – Dauan Island ii
L42@P3: Continued collection of floristic information, specifically those plants with cultural and
L43@P3: biodiversity significance.
L44@P3: Further survey and documentation of the complex and diverse cultural landscape on the
L45@P3: island.
L46@P3: It is important that any future surveys on Dauan Island are undertaken in collaboration with the Dauan
L47@P3: people and include study of Dauan traditional ecological knowledge and ethnotaxonomy.
L48@P3: Furthermore all mapping and assessment work must comply with Dauan research protocols (to be

L181@P8: 1.0 Introduction
L182@P8: Dauan Island represents one of six islands selected for a supplementary stage of funding for
L183@P8: development of a biodiversity management profile. The document aims to identify the biodiversity
L184@P8: features, landscape processes, and cultural values (from both a landscape and site specific
L185@P8: perspective) that are intrinsic to the island. It also aims to identify management issues and
L186@P8: recommends actions to ensure future conservation of the islands natural values.
L187@P8: 1.1 Cultural Setting
L188@P8: The population of Dauan Island consists of 163 people (2011 census). Land tenure is DOGIT (Deed
L189@P8: of Grant in Trust) and the Registered Native Title Body Corporate1(RNTBC or PBC in shortened form)
L190@P8: is the Dauanalgaw (Torres Strait Islanders) Corporation who hold the title of the land on behalf of the
L191@P8: traditional owners. The local dialect is Kalaw Kawaw Ya. Native title was granted on July 6, 2000.
L192@P8: 1.2 Geographic Setting
L193@P8: The granite rock pile that forms Dauan, along with nearby Saibai and Boigu Islands form the Northern
L194@P8: Island Group, located approximately 150 km north of Thursday Island (see Figure 1). Whilst Saibai
L195@P8: and Boigu Island are extensions of the alluvial Fly Platform, geologically part of the Papua New
L196@P8: Guinea mainland, Dauan is formed on continental basement rock which extends northward from Cape
L197@P8: York Peninsula to Mabadauan Hill on the south-west coast of Papua New Guinea. Dauan, with an
L198@P8: area of 364 ha, rises dramatically from the ocean to a height of 295m on Mt Cornwallis, the highest
L199@P8: point on the island and the second highest mountain peak in the Torres Strait Island group after
L200@P8: Banks Peak (Mua Island) at 399m.
L201@P8: The village is located on the northern side of the island, facing the Papua New Guinea mainland
L202@P8: which is clearly visible to the north. The mean annual rainfall of 1 082 mm (BOM 2008a) makes
L203@P8: Dauan the driest recording station in the Torres Strait with approximately half the rainfall of Badu
L204@P8: which at 1 983mm is the wettest recording station in the Torres Strait Islands (BOM 2008b). A
L205@P8: number of minor soaks and streams emerge from the base of the islands rocky interior. Being spring
L206@P8: fed, these watercourses generally remain moist throughout the year

L212@P9: 1.3 Geological Context
L213@P9: The continental basement rock that forms the Australian landmass extends northward from Cape York
L214@P9: Peninsula to Madabuan on the south coast of the Papua New Guinea mainland. Dauan Island
L215@P9: represents the second northern most outcrop of the Australian landmass after Madabuan. Whilst
L216@P9: Dauan rises from the water, Madabuan, 27km to the north-east rises from the Fly Platform, the largest
L217@P9: tract of low-lying country in Papua New Guinea (Loffler 1977).
L218@P9: Dauan is formed on relatively coarse grained pink granite, part of the Badu suite of intrusive rocks that
L219@P9: are of Permo-Carboniferous (290 million years) age (Von Gnielinski et al. 1998). The granite rocks
L220@P9: form a massive pile of rounded granite boulders which give the impression of being precariously
L221@P9: perched atop each-other. Extensive bare boulder talus and scree slopes provide an indication of the
L222@P9: relative instability of the boulder mass. The boulders also pile to form deep crevasses and ravines
L223@P9: which make traverse of the slope both difficult and dangerous.
L224@P9: The island is fringed by a number of coralline beach ridges with a relatively broad system of beach
L225@P9: ridges accreted on the islands north-west, the site of the island dump. A number of restricted alluvial
L226@P9: fans are also found on the islands footslopes, the most extensive of these forms the substrate to the
L227@P9-10: island settlement. Habitat Management Profile – Dauan Island 9

L263@P11: 4.0 Legislative and Policy Considerations
L264@P11: Biodiversity (plants, animals and their habitats) is regulated at state and national levels by a range of
L265@P11: legislative mechanisms which classify animal species, plant species and habitats according to their
L266@P11: rarity, population size, distribution and threats. The legislative classification is generally used as a
L267@P11: way to assign significance to a particular species or ecological value. If an animal, plant or vegetation
L268@P11: type is listed on any Australian or Queensland government legislation, it is subject to rules which
L269@P11: protect it from being destroyed or harmed. For example, if a certain orchid species is listed on the
L270@P11: legislation it would mean that the orchid could not be collected from the bush and sold at a nursery
L271@P11: without the necessary authorisation and permits. Similarly, if an animal such as a bat species or bat
L272@P11: colony, which was listed as threatened under legislation, lived in a rock shelter where a housing
L273@P11: development was proposed, then detailed studies would be required to determine how the bats would
L274@P11: be affected by the development. A description of relevant components of the major legislation
L275@P11: mechanisms requiring consideration is provided briefly below.
L276@P11: The Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (EPBC Act): The EPBC
L277@P11: Act, an initiative of the Australian Government, provides recognition of four classes of wildlife and
L278@P11: habitat being those which are:
L279@P11: Extinct in the wild.
L280@P11: Critically endangered.
L281@P11: Endangered.
L282@P11: Vulnerable.
L283@P11: Plant and animal species and habitats scheduled under these categories are referred to collectively
L284@P11: as ‘Threatened Wildlife’. The EPBC Act also provides for protection of those species which are
L285@P11: considered migratory under international conventions which include:
L286@P11: Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (Bonn
L287@P11-12: Convention).Habitat Management Profile – Dauan Island 11
L288@P12: China-Australia Migratory Bird Agreement (CAMBA).
L289@P12: Japan-Australia Migratory Bird Agreement (JAMBA).
L290@P12: Republic of Korea-Australia Migratory Bird Agreement (ROKAMBA).
L291@P12: Interference or destruction of plants, animals or areas of habitat for species listed as threatened under
L292@P12: the EPBC act requires specific authorisation from the regulator (Australian Government) who is likely
L293@P12: to provide conditions under which the interference can take place. Interference (such as removal of
L294@P12: protected orchid species) without authorisation is in breach of the EPBC Act.
L295@P12: Nature Conservation Act 1992: The Nature Conservation Act (NC Act) is a legislative mechanism of
L296@P12: the Queensland Government that is regulated by the Department of Environment and Heritage
L297@P12: Protection (EHP). The Nature Conservation (Wildlife) Regulation 2006 is subordinate to the NC Act
L298@P12: and defines five classes that are:
L299@P12: Extinct in the wild.
L300@P12: Endangered.
L301@P12: Vulnerable.
L302@P12: Near-Threatened.
L303@P12: Least concern.
L304@P12: These classes collectively relate to native species that are protected wildlife (plants and animals).
L305@P12: Vegetation Management Act: The Vegetation Management Act 1999 (VMA) is a state regulated
L306@P12: planning initiative that underpins the regional management of vegetation in Queensland. Under the
L307@P12: VMA, conservation significance to particular vegetation groups termed regional ecosystems (REs) is
L308@P12: assigned on a consistent state-wide basis. The classification of regional ecosystems is based on a
L309@P12: hierarchical system with a three-part code defining bioregion, followed by land zone, and then
L310@P12: vegetation. Thirteen bioregions are classified in Queensland with the Torres Strait Islands being a
L311@P12: sub-province of the broader Cape York Peninsula bioregion.
L312@P12: Land zones are geological and geomorphic categories that describe the major geologies and
L313@P12: landforms of Queensland. The system is based primarily on geology, with geologic age considered
L314@P12: an important determinant. The classification of land zone generally utilises available geological
L315@P12: information (Neldner et al. 2005) although field inspection is utilised as a supplementary measure
L316@P12: where geological mapping is inadequate.
L317@P12: The status of REs is based on their pre-clearing and remnant extent, and is gazetted under the VMA
L318@P12: and listed in the RE Description Database maintained by the EHP. The Vegetation Management
L319@P12: Status (VMS) of a regional ecosystem is described in line with the following:
L320@P12: Endangered regional ecosystem: a regional ecosystem that is prescribed under a regulation and
L321@P12-13: has either:Habitat Management Profile – Dauan Island 12
L322@P13: less than 10% of its pre-clearing extent remaining, or
L323@P13: 10% to 30% of its pre-clearing extent remaining and the remnant vegetation remaining is
L324@P13: less than 10 000 hectares (ha).
L325@P13: Of Concern regional ecosystem: means a regional ecosystem that is prescribed under a regulation
L326@P13: and has either:
L327@P13: 10% to 30% of its pre-clearing extent remaining, or
L328@P13: more than 30% of its pre-clearing extent remaining and the remnant vegetation
L329@P13: remaining is less than 10 000 ha.
L330@P13: Least Concern regional ecosystem: Means a regional ecosystem that is prescribed under a
L331@P13: regulation and has more than 30% of its pre-clearing extent remaining and the remnant vegetation
L332@P13: remaining is more than 10 000 ha.
L333@P13: Hence, the majority of vegetation scheduled under the VMA as ‘Of Concern’ on Dauan (e.g.
L334@P13: evergreen vine forest habitat RE3.12.35) is classified as such because on a regional level (Cape York
