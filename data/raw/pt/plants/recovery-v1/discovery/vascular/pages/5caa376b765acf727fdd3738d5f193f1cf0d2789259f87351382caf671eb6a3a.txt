L157: ## Description
L158: 
L159: [cite83†edit ]
L160: 
L161: Cupressus funebris is a medium-sized cite84†coniferous cite85†tree growing to 20–35 m tall, with a trunk up to 2 m diameter. The foliage grows in dense, usually moderately decumbent and pendulous sprays of bright green, very slender, slightly flattened shoots.
L162: 
L163: The cite86†leaves are scale-like, 1–2 mm long, up to 5 mm long on strong lead shoots; young trees up to about 5–10 years old have juvenile foliage with soft needle-like leaves 3–8 mm long.
L164: The seed cite87†cones are globose, 8–15 mm long, with 6–10 scales (usually 8), green, maturing dark brown about 24 months after pollination. The cones open at maturity to shed the seed. The pollen cones are 3–5 mm long, and release pollen in early spring.

L167: ## Distribution
L168: 
L169: [cite88†edit ]
L170: 
L171: The precise natural range of Cupressus funebris is uncertain due to a long history of cultivation. Trees are recorded from forest habitats in the provinces of cite89†Guizhou , cite90†Hunan , and cite91†Chongqing .
L172: More generally, it also occurs in cite92†Anhui , cite93†Fujian , southern cite94†Gansu , cite95†Guangdong , cite96†Guangxi , cite97†Henan , cite98†Hubei , cite99†Jiangxi , cite100†Shaanxi , cite101†Sichuan , cite102†Yunnan , and cite103†Zhejiang , typically planted around cite104†monasteries and cite105†temples or in gardens.
L173: 
L174: Whether Cupressus funebris naturally occurs also in northern Vietnam is uncertain; if so, it probably is the most threatened conifer of that country.^{cite82†[2] }
L175: ## Cultivation
L176: 
L177: [cite106†edit ]
L178: 
L179: Cupressus funebris is cultivated as an cite107†ornamental tree , due to its graciously weeping form and texture, and planted in cite108†gardens and public cite109†parks in other warm temperate regions, such as cite110†California . It is used as a cite111†houseplant and cite112†conservatory tree in colder climates.
