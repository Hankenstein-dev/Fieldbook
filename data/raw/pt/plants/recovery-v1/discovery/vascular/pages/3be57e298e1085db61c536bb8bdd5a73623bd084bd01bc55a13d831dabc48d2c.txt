L231:     Relationships. — Foliar morphology and crowded distal internodes give M. linearifolium an aspect reminiscent of M. uribei Wurdack, a Colombian species of restricted
L232:     distribution. In describing this species, Wurdack (1969) recognized two varieties based
L233:     on differences of foliar and cauline pubescence. The typical variety is glabrous except
L234:     for three stout, smooth trichomes at the apex of the hyaline margined leaves. The
L235:     variety arcabucense has sparsely strigillose cauline internodes and leaves that are glabrous above and sparsely beset with smooth, appressed trichomes below. Both varieties
L236:     share a distinctive, smooth, vernicose seed that is 1 mm long. Monochaetum linearifolium differs from both varieties in having distinctly barbellate trichomes, cylindric
L237:     hypanthia 3-3.5(-4) mm wide, shorter sepals (4.5-6 mm long), white petals, and
L238:     minutely foveolate seeds 0.5 mm long. The similarity between these two species may
L239:     reflect recent divergence from a common ancestral stock or morphological convergence. The distinct elevational ranges of these taxa and their restriction to disjunct
L240:     areas that have had dissimilar geologic histories lead me to favor the latter explanation.
L241:     Narrow leaved forms of M. calcaratum, Pringle 8359 (MEXU, NY, US), also superficially resemble M. linearifolium, but differ in having glabrous cauline internodes,
L242:     longer leaves (1.8-3.5 cm), oblong-urceolate hypanthia (8-10 x 4-5 mm) with smooth
L243:     trichomes, and much larger antepetalous anthers.
L244:     Despite these similarities, the relationship of M. linearifolium appears to be with M.
L245:     deppeanum, a variable species consisting of several isolated populations from Mexico
L246:     to Nicaragua. Both species have a barbellate trichome covering, cylindric hypanthia,
L247:     and single-flowered inflorescences, yet each has a different geographic distribution
L248:     and can be separated consistently by the diagnostic features outlined in the key to the
L249:     species.
L250:     Sympatry. —Monochaetum floribundum is the only species known to grow with M.
L251:     linearifolium. Both species occur near each other, but the population size of M. floribundum exceeds that of M. linearifolium. Natural populations of the latter in the Cordillera de Tilaran were observed at least twice yearly from 1971-73. As of my last visit
L252:     to the area, some colonies had been almost totally decimated owing to the local pattern of land use.
L253:     Monochaetum linearifolium apparently rarely hybridizes with M. floribundum.
L254:     One intermediate plant, Almeda & Wilbur 1050, found growing adjacent to its putative parents, has a combination of characters that casts little doubt on its hybrid origin
L255:     (table 9). No pollen or young floral buds were available, so it was not possible to determine its fertility or meiotic behavior. Provided the natural hybrids are as fertile as
L256:     those encountered in other interspecific combinations, the rarity of intermediates may
L257:     be due to factors which limit the extent of interspecific pollination. Monochaetum
L258:     linearifolium is never as abundant as M. floribundum; its inflorescences are few flowered and inconspicuous; its flowering period is rather brief.
