L135: Frullania polysticta is a species of cite62†liverwort in the family cite63†Frullaniaceae . It is found only in cite64†Madeira and the cite65†Canary Islands and is considered a cite46†vulnerable species .
L136: ## Distribution and habitat
L137: 
L138: [cite66†edit ]
L139: Frullania polysticta can be found on all islands of both the Madeira and Canary Islands cite67†archipelagos . It is widely distributed across Madeira and is similarly frequent on most of the Canary Islands, though it is rarer on cite68†Fuerteventura and cite69†Lanzarote . It occurs primarily in cite70†laurel forests at elevations of 500–1,200 m (1,600–3,900 ft) cite71†above sea level , occurring most frequently above 800 m (2,600 ft).
L140: It typically grows as an cite72†epiphyte on the bark of trees, but can also be found on shaded rocks and humid soil amongst cite73†leaf litter .^{cite48†[1] }
L141: It has been recorded from the cite74†Azores ,^{cite75†[3] } however, these identifications were later determined to be erroneous.^{cite76†[4] }
L142: ## Description
L143: 
L144: [cite77†edit ]
L145: 
L146: Frullania polysticta is a robust, irregularly branched, mat-forming liverwort that is light green to reddish brown in colour.^{cite78†[5] } The shoots measure 5–8 cm (2.0–3.1 in) long and 1,400–2,200 μm (1.4–2.2 mm) wide. The overlapping leaves are borne on stems measuring 120–220 μm (0.12–0.22 mm) in diameter. F. polysticta is cite79†dioecious , with male and female plants being of similar size.^{cite75†[3] }
L147: ## Ecology
L148: 
L149: [cite80†edit ]
L150: Frullania polysticta can be considered a laurel forest habitat cite81†specialist .^{cite82†[6] } Known cite83†host tree species include cite84†Erica arborea , cite85†Ilex canariensis , cite86†Laurus azorica , cite87†Laurus novocanariensis , cite88†Ocotea foetens , and cite89†Picconia excelsa .^{cite48†[1] }^{cite78†[5] }^{cite90†[7] } It has been recorded growing in association with other cite91†bryophytes , including cite92†Dicranum scottianum , cite93†Frullania dilatata , cite94†Frullania azorica , cite95†Frullania teneriffae , cite96†Kurzia trichoclados , cite97†Leucodon canariensis , cite98†Metzgeria consanguinea , cite99†Metzgeria furcata , cite100†Plagiochila bifaria , cite101†Radula lindenbergiana , cite102†Saccogyna viticulosa , and cite103†Sematophyllum substrumulosum , and in association with cite104†lichens such as cite105†Anaptychia ciliaris , cite106†Degelia plumbea , cite107†Lobaria pulmonaria , and cite108†Leptogium species.^{cite75†[3] }
L151: Frullania polysticta is a long-lived cite109†perennial species with an estimated cite110†generation length of 11 to 25 years.^{cite48†[1] } It is the only known host of cite111†Octosporella microtricha , a species of cite112†parasitic fungus .^{cite113†[8] }
L152: ## Conservation status
L153: 
L154: [cite114†edit ]
L155: Frullania polysticta is listed as cite46†vulnerable on the cite115†International Union for the Conservation of Nature 's cite116†Red List under criterion A3c, based on its decreasing population and observed cite117†habitat loss . It is threatened by cite118†urbanization , cite119†tourism , cite120†overgrazing , and cite121†climate change . No specific conservation measures are in place to protect this species, however, it is known to occur within cite122†protected areas such as cite123†Madeira Natural Park .^{cite48†[1] }
