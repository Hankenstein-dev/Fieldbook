L6@P1: Summary: Based on field data obtained between 1998 and 2001 and all available literature, we
L7@P1: present an updated list of the endemic taxa of vascular plants of the Azores archipelago including distribution, habitat and estimated population size. Of the 197 indigenous species of the
L8@P1: Azorean flora, 70 species, i. e. 35.5 %, are classified as endemic. Half of these taxa are widespread and common, 25 % are endangered and three may be extinct.
L9@P1: Key words: Azores; flora; endemics.
L10@P1: Zusammenfassung: Auf der Grundlage von im Zeitraum 1998-2001 gesammelten Verbreitungsdaten und unter Berücksichtigung sämtlicher zugänglicher Literatur wurde eine aktuelle
L11@P1: Liste der endemischen Farn- und Blütenpflanzen der Azoren mit Angaben zu Verbreitung, Habitat und geschätztem Gesamtbestand erarbeitet. Von den 197 indigenen Arten der Azorenflora
L12@P1: wurden 70, also 35.5 %, als Azorenendemiten eingestuft. Die Hälfte davon ist weit verbreitet
L13@P1: und häufig, ein Viertel gefährdet und drei sind verschollen.
L14@P1: 1. Introduction
L15@P1: The Azores archipelago is composed of nine volcanic islands, colonised in the
L16@P1: early 15th century. It is located in the Northern Atlantic Ocean close to the middleAtlantic ridge with a distance of more than 1 000 km to the American and the
L17@P1: European coast.
L18@P1: The native flora of the Azores is very poor in species in comparison to the indigenous flora of the closest neighbouring archipelagos: Madeira and the Canaries.
L19@P1: In a recent survey of the total flora (SCHÄFER 2003), only 197 species were classified as indigenous taxa (24 %). Whereas the Canarian flora is famous for radiations
L20@P1: resulting in large groups of specialised endemics, e. g. in genera like Aeonium, Argyranthemum, Pericallis or Sonchus, similar cases are unknown from the Azorean
L21@P1: flora and fauna. Instead, many Azorean endemics are characterised by a wide ecological and altitudinal range. Erica azorica, one of the most striking examples, can
L22@P1: be found from sea level up to 2 300 m a. s. l. on the peak of Pico mountain.
L23@P1: 2. Method and Material
L24@P1: Data on distribution and population size of all endemics was collected during a
L25@P1: mapping project of the Azorean flora between 1998 and 2001 on all islands of the
L26@P1: archipelago. The islands Flores, Faial and Santa Maria were studied in detail for a
L27@P1: period of six-seven months each, resulting in 1x1 km2
L28@P1:  UTM-grid distribution maps
L29@P1: for all vascular plant taxa on these islands. The other islands were visited for twothree weeks. The population size of all endemics was estimated from the number of
L30@P1: colonised islands and their frequency in the studied UTM squares.

L350@P6: endemic to the Azores: the monotypic Azorina (Campanulaceae), probably a very
L351@P6: old Gondwana relict (EDDIE 1997). The most important families among the endemics are Poaceae (nine endemic species), Asteraceae (seven endemic species) and
L352@P6: Apiaceae (six endemic species). About half of the endemics are common or very
L353@P6: common species, occurring on most of the islands in forests, hedges and cliffs, and
L354@P6: forming a total population of up to or more than 100 000 individual plants. To this
L355@P6: group belong the very important trees Erica azorica, Ilex perado ssp. azorica, and
L356@P6: Juniperus brevifolius. About 25 % of the endemics occur on several islands in
L357@P6: populations of 10 000 or more individuals and thus are also not endangered. The
L358@P6: remaining endemics are all endangered as they are restricted to few islands and
L359@P6: their population size is less than 10 000 plants. Three taxa have not been recorded

L414@P7: With more than one third of the indigenousspecies being endemic to the archipelago, the Azorean flora is another nice example for speciation on isolated islands.
L415@P7: Although the archipelago is probably less than five Mio. years old (SCHÄFER 2003)
L416@P7: this high percentage of endemics makes its flora unique and conservation a must.
L417@P7: The 30-40 common endemics with a wide ecological range do not need special
L418@P7: programs like seed exchange and cultivation in botanical gardens. However, the
L419@P7: habitats of all of them have been drastically reduced in the past. The stands of temperate juniper rainforest are still being destroyed to create pastures and construct
L420@P7: new roads in the central regions of the islands. The low altitude habitats on all of
L421@P7: the islands are rapidly invaded by exotic plant species like the Himalayan Hedychium gardneranum, the Australian Pittosporum undulatum, and the American
L422@P7: Lantana camara, an important threat to native flora and vegetation. All endemics
L423@P7: with populations smaller than 10 000 individuals need special conservation programs to survive the next few decades. They should be propagated in local and foreign botanical gardens and reintroduced to appropriate habitats under the control of
L424@P7: the local conservation department. Endemic hybrids will occur wherever their parents are common and thus do not need special protection. Further taxonomic work
