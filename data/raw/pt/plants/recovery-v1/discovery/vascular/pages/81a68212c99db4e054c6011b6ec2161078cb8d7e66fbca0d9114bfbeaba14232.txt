L3@P0-1: Fund & the Carbon Tax FundWhat is this guide for?
L4@P1: This guide is designed to help non-specialists identify the
L5@P1: 15 species of Sphagnum moss commonly found in lowland
L6@P1: raised bogs, cutover raised bogs, and bog woodland in
L7@P1: Ireland. It is not designed for use in blanket bogs or heaths,
L8@P1: where other species are frequently found. It includes a key
L9@P1: and short species descriptions that use simple characters,
L10@P1: only a few of which require the use of a hand lens. This guide
L11@P1: is by no means fool-proof, and other references should be
L12@P1: used to confirm identifications made using this guide if a
L13@P1: definite identification is needed (see Resources below).
L14@P1: Sphagnum characteristics
L15@P1: Hanging branches
L16@P1: (appressed to stem)
L17@P1: Terminal bud Capitulum
L18@P1: Spreading branches
L19@P1: Sphagnum mosses share a basic structure that no other
L20@P1: moss has. A Sphagnum plant consists of a stem and
L21@P1: numerous leafy branches, which arise from the stem usually
L22@P1: in clusters of 3-6. At the top of the stem, younger, shorter
L23@P1: branches are tightly packed together in a head, called the
L24@P1: capitulum. In some species, the capitulum is shaggy and
L25@P1: untidy looking, while in others it forms a neat, five-pointed
L26@P1: star when viewed from above. In the centre of the capitulum
L27@P1: is the terminal bud, the growing tip of the stem. In most
L28@P1: Sphagnum mosses it is hidden by the young branches, but
L29@P1: the terminal bud is larger and more visible in a few species.
L30@P1: Further down, the mature branches are differentiated in some
L31@P1: species into spreading branches and hanging branches.
L32@P1: Hanging branches can sometimes cling tightly to and hide
L33@P1: the stem, and the leaves of hanging branches are often paler
L34@P1: and shaped differently than those of the spreading branches.
L35@P1: Branch leaves are typically broad at the base and taper to a
L36@P1: rounded or more-or-less sharp point. In some species, the
L37@P1: tips of the branch leaves form a hood, like that on a cloak.
L38@P1: Hooded leaves are characteristic of a particular group of
L39@P1: large, chunky Sphagnum mosses. Branch leaves on some
L40@P1: species are arranged into neat rows so that if a branch is
L41@P1: cut in half, the cross-section looks like a five-pointed star.
L42@P1: In some Sphagnum mosses, the branch leaves lie flat on
L43@P1: the branch and overlap each other, which are described as
L44@P1: appressed leaves, giving the branch a smooth appearance
L45@P1: (see Sphagnum austinii). In other species, they spread
L46@P1: out at an angle of 45° or more (see Sphagnum medium).
L47@P1: Sphagnum mosses with tightly appressed leaves often have
L48@P1: branches that narrow to a long, fine point. The branches
L49@P1: of species with spreading branch leaves often look rather
L50@P1: stubby. All these differences provide ways to tell one
L51@P1: species apart from another.
L52@P1: Leaves also grow directly on Sphagnum stems, and these
L53@P1: are different shapes and sizes from those growing on the
L54@P1: branches. Stem leaves provide very useful characters
L55@P1: for more detailed examination of Sphagnum mosses, but
L56@P1: because they can be difficult to see in the field, especially
L57@P1: for beginners, this guide only uses stem leaves for a few
L58@P1: species.
L59@P1: Sphagnum colour
L60@P1: Many Sphagnum mosses are brightly and variably coloured,
L61@P1: which can be a great aid in identifying species. Unfortunately,
L62@P1: colours can also be misleading. All Sphagnum species vary
L63@P1: in colour according to environmental conditions and the time
L64@P1: of year. The typical range of colour found in each species is
L65@P1: indicated in the species descriptions below.
L66@P1: All Sphagnum growing in shade, such
L67@P1: as under heather on the open bog or
L68@P1: below a bog woodland canopy, tend
L69@P1: to be more green than normal. This
L70@P1: is especially the case for normally red
L71@P1: Sphagnum species, such as Sphagnum
L72@P1: rubellum and Sphagnum medium, which can turn almost
L73@P1: completely green in the shade. Usually, there is at least a
L74@P1: little of the normal colouration in the leaves of some plants to
L75@P1: show that this is a shade form of a typically red species.
L76@P1: Flooding by peaty water can make Sphagnum colours more
L77@P1: dingy or brown. This is often seen in the springtime in plants in
L78@P1: hollows or pool edges that have been inundated over the winter.
L79@P1: In contrast, most Sphagnum mosses are brighter and richer
L80@P1: in colour in the late summer and autumn. Reds and browns
L81@P1: darken, yellows change to orange, and even green mosses
L82@P1: become brighter. Since the autumn leaves of Irish trees are
L83@P1: rather disappointing, the bog is the place to go in Ireland to
L84@P1: really experience autumn colour.
L85@P1-2: Line drawing by Sharon PilkingtonSphagnum habitats
L86@P2: Open, uncut bog is the primary habitat for raised bog
L87@P2: Sphagnum mosses. This is the habitat they have created
L88@P2: themselves, building up deep deposits of Sphagnum peat
L89@P2: over thousands of years. Different Sphagnum species are
L90@P2: found in different microhabitats depending on height above
L91@P2: the water table. Some Sphagnum mosses can tolerate drier
L92@P2: conditions and form mounds or hummocks that are raised
L93@P2: above the bog surface. Some Sphagnum species, such as
L94@P2: Sphagnum austinii and S. beothuk, can form very large, tall
L95@P2: hummocks, while others, such as Sphagnum rubellum, tend
L96@P2: to form lower hummocks. Others prefer to be closer to the
L97@P2: water table and form patches or sometimes extensive lawns
L98@P2: on the flat bog surface. These include some of the larger
L99@P2: species, such as Sphagnum papillosum and S. medium.
L100@P2: The wettest parts of an intact bog are pools that contain water
L101@P2: nearly the year round. Aquatic Sphagnum species, such as
L102@P2: Sphagnum cuspidatum and S. auriculatum prefer this habitat.
L103@P2: Intermediate between the pools and the lawns are hollows
L104@P2: that are flooded in winter but dry out somewhat in the summer.
L105@P2: Discrete hollows and the margins of permanent pools can be
L106@P2: occupied by either lawn or pool species.
L107@P2: The same patterns generally occur
L108@P2: on cutover bog, but microhabitats
L109@P2: are not as well developed except
L110@P2: on the oldest and wettest parts.
L111@P2: The same species that are found on
L112@P2: intact bogs can also be found on the cutover (if conditions are
L113@P2: right), although some species, like Sphagnum austinii and S.
L114@P2: beothuk, tend to be rather rare.
L115@P2: Flushes are microhabitats where there is some movement of
L116@P2: surface water, which provide slightly more nutrients and oxygen
L117@P2: to the plants growing there. On intact bogs, flushes occur in
L118@P2: natural flow paths or areas of subsidence. Flushed conditions
L119@P2: are more common on cutover bog, where the ground surface
L120@P2: is often irregular or sloping and where there may be some
L121@P2: groundwater seepage through shallow peat. Some Sphagnum
L122@P2: mosses, such as Sphagnum fallax and S. palustre, are
L123@P2: restricted to flushes and are never found on unflushed open
L124@P2: bog, while others can be found in either habitat.
L125@P2: Bog woodlands can be found in flushes on
L126@P2: intact bog and can also occur on cutover
L127@P2: bog. Most bog woodlands on cutover are
L128@P2: dry, but some wetter ones can support
L129@P2: an abundance of Sphagnum species
L130@P2: that prefer flushed conditions, especially
L131@P2: Sphagnum palustre. Sphagnum fimbriatum
L132@P2: and S. squarrosum are found more often
L133@P2: in wet bog woodland than in open flushes.
L134@P2: Sphagnum rubellum is also frequent in bog woodlands
L135@P2: (usually green, unfortunately), and likes to grow in nearly any
L136@P2: raised bog habitat.
L137@P2: How to Use the Key
L138@P2: First, find a good, healthy patch of Sphagnum moss, as it
L139@P2: is easier to identify a group rather than a single plant. Make
L140@P2: sure that all the plants you are examining look more or less
L141@P2: the same, allowing for variation in colour within a patch. It is
L142@P2: common for a few plants of one species to occur mixed up
L143@P2: in a patch of another species, but these are usually obviously
L144@P2: different in shape and size.
L145@P2: Work through the key and answer the Yes/No questions.
L146@P2: Pick up and look closely at a separate plant or two when you
L147@P2: need to. (Since Sphagnum plants don’t have roots, you can
L148@P2: tuck them back in when you are done, and they will keep
L149@P2: growing.) Using a hand lens (10x up to 20x) is always a good
L150@P2: idea; where a hand lens symbol is shown in the key, then
L151@P2: a lens really is needed. When “leaves” are referred to, this
L152@P2: refers to the leaves of spreading branches unless stated
L153@P2: otherwise.
L154@P2: When you have reached a conclusion, compare your
L155@P2: plants with the species description and photo. The species
L156@P2: descriptions summarise the typical size & colour of the
L157@P2: species, its usual habitat, its key ID features, and how best
L158@P2: to separate it from lookalikes. Since Sphagnum species
L159@P2: names have changed a lot recently, other names for the
L160@P2: species that might appear in older books and reports are
L161@P2: also listed.
L162@P2: If you are happy you have made the right choice, well
L163@P2: done! If not, look first at the lookalikes section for your
L164@P2: choice, which highlights similar species and points out the
L165@P2: main differences. If this doesn’t help, then work backwards
L166@P2: through the key and see where you think you may have gone
L167@P2: wrong. If you are still having a hard time, some additional
L168@P2: sources of help are listed under the Resources section.

L267@P4: Sphagnum auriculatum
L268@P4: Size & colour: Medium. Dark green to mustard yellow
L269@P4: to coppery brown
L270@P4: Habitat: Bog pools and drains, especially in western
L271@P4: bogs
L272@P4: Key ID features: Outer capitulum branches are
L273@P4: swollen with more-or-less appressed leaves. Some
L274@P4: branches are curved, resembling a cow’s horn. Stem leaves are large and
L275@P4: concave.
L276@P4: Lookalikes: Sphagnum inundatum is a moss of flushes in uplands and
L277@P4: the west. This species is usually yellower with branch leaves that stick out
L278@P4: more, but it is sometimes indistinguishable in the field or even under the
L279@P4: microscope.
L280@P4: Other names: Sphagnum denticulatum
L281@P4: Sphagnum austinii
L282@P4: Size & colour: Medium. Pale brown often with
L283@P4: greenish capitulum centres and red tinges towards
L284@P4: the branch tips
L285@P4: Habitat: Hummocks on open, intact bog, rarely on
L286@P4: cutover
L287@P4: Key ID features: Closely appressed leaves and smooth, pointed branches.
L288@P4: Hummocks are very tight and firm and usually hard to the touch.
L289@P4: Lookalikes: Usually easy to identify, but dense patches of brightly coloured
L290@P4: Sphagnum papillosum can resemble it.
L291@P4: Other names: Sphagnum imbricatum
L292@P4: Sphagnum beothuk
L293@P4: Size & colour: Small. Ginger to chocolate brown
L294@P4: Habitat: Hummocks on open, intact bog, rarely on
L295@P4: cutover
L296@P4: Key ID features: Dense but not firm hummocks. All
L297@P4: brown. Stem leaves point upwards.
L298@P4: Lookalikes: Recently split from Sphagnum fuscum,
L299@P4: which seems to be rare in Ireland and may be more of an upland species. S.
L300@P4: fuscum tends to be paler, but can only be separated from S. beothuk under
L301@P4: the microscope.
L302@P4: Other names: Sphagnum fuscum
L303@P4: Sphagnum cuspidatum
L304@P4: Size & colour: Medium. Light yellow-green to
L305@P4: green, sometimes with orange tints in the capitulum.
L306@P4: Sometimes with pink at the base of the branches.
L307@P4: Habitat: Bog pools, drains and hollows
L308@P4: Key ID features: Long, very narrow, needle-like leaves. Feathery
L309@P4: appearance in pools, but resembles wet fur when taken out of the water.
L310@P4: Lookalikes: Sphagnum fallax usually has a star-shaped capitulum, often
L311@P4: has leaves in neat rows, and has young branches emerging from the
L312@P4: capitulum in pairs.
L313@P4: Other names: None
L314@P4: Sphagnum divinum
L315@P4: Size & colour: Large. Wine-red to pink, often
L316@P4: mottled green
L317@P4: Habitat: Lawns and hollows in slightly flushed bogs
L318@P4: Key ID features: Hooded leaves overlapping and appressed with pointed
L319@P4: branches.
L320@P4: Lookalikes: Recently split from Sphagnum medium, which has stubby
L321@P4: branches with more widely spreading leaves and tends to be darker.
L322@P4: Other names: Sphagnum magellanicum
L323@P4: Sphagnum fallax
L324@P4: Size & colour: Medium. Green to yellow-orange to
L325@P4: mustard brown. Sometimes with pink at the base of
L326@P4: the branches.
L327@P4: Habitat: Lawns in flushes and bog woodland
L328@P4: Key ID features: Capitulum looks like a 5-pointed star,
L329@P4: branch leaves usually in neat rows. Young branches
L330@P4: emerge from capitulum in pairs.
L331@P4: Lookalikes: Similar to two closely related species
L332@P4: Sphagnum angustifolium and S. flexuosum, which are
L333@P4: found in more base-rich habitats. Sphagnum pulchrum
L334@P4: has leaves in very sharp rows, but has dark stems. See also Sphagnum

L336@P4: Other names: Sphagnum recurvum var. mucronatum
L337@P4: Sphagnum fimbriatum
L338@P4: Size & colour: Medium. Always green.

L340@P4: Key ID features: Broad stem leaves with frilly
L341@P4: margins standing erect and visible when the capitulum
L342@P4: is removed. Prominent terminal bud in centre of
L343@P4: capitulum.
L344@P4: Lookalikes: Green forms of other Sphagnum mosses, especially
L345@P4: Sphagnum rubellum.
L346@P4: Other names: None

L348@P4: Size & colour: Large. Wine-red, sometimes mottled

L385@P5: Size & colour: Small. Red to candy pink but often
L386@P5: green in shade

L402@P5: from the branches, giving plants a spiky appearance.
L403@P5: Prominent terminal bud in centre of capitulum.
L404@P5: Lookalikes: Sphagnum palustre sometimes has
L405@P5: leaves bent away from the branches, but it is a larger moss with hooded
L406@P5: leaves.
L407@P5: Other names: None

L411@P5: in shade. Sometimes pale brown.

L413@P5: open bog. Less frequent on intact bog.

L416@P5: dried have a metallic sheen.

L418@P5: neater with leaves often in rows. The pink colour of Sphagnum rubellum is
L419@P5: richer than that of S. subnitens.
