L152: ## Description
L153: 
L154: [cite81†edit ]
L155: 
L156: These plants are small and cite82†thalloid , i.e. not differentiated into root, stem and leaf. Depending on species, the thallus may be strap-shaped and about 0.5 to 4 mm wide with dichotomous branches or may form cite83†rosettes or hemirosettes up to 3 cm in diameter, that may be gregarious and form intricate mats.^{cite80†[2] }^{cite84†[3] }
L157: The thallus is cite85†dorsiventrally differentiated. Its upper (dorsal) surface is green and cite86†chlorophyll -bearing, with a mid-dorsal longitudinal sulcus (furrow or groove). Air pores occasionally break through the dorsal surface, giving the thallus a dimpled appearance. In exceptional members such as Riccia caroliniana ^{cite87†[4] }^{cite88†[5] } of Northern Australia and Riccia sahyadrica ^{cite89†[6] } of Western Ghats, the photosynthetic region is confined to the lower half of the thallus.^{cite90†[7] }
L158: The lower (ventral) surface has a mid-ventral ridge bearing multicellular scales that originate as a single row but normally separate into two rows as the thallus widens. The scales are multicellular and cite91†hyaline (glassy) in appearance, or violet due to the pigment cite92†anthocyanin .^{cite80†[2] }
L159: cite93†Rhizoids are nearly lacking in aquatic forms, but there are usually numerous unicellular rhizoids of two types on the ventral surface.^{cite80†[2] } One type is called smooth and the other type is the pegged or tuberculated rhizoids; these help in anchorage and absorption. The inner surface of the smooth rhizoids is smooth while that of the tuberculate rhizoid will have internal cite94†cell wall projections.
L160: ## Distribution and habitat
L161: 
L162: [cite95†edit ]
L163: 
L164: One of the more than 100 species in this genus is the "slender riccia" (cite96†Riccia fluitans ), which grows on damp soil or, less commonly, floating in ponds,^{cite90†[7] } and is sometimes used in cite97†aquariums .
L165: ## Reproduction
L166: 
L167: [cite98†edit ]
L168: 
L169: Plants are usually cite99†monoicous , and sexual reproduction is by cite100†antheridia and cite101†archegonia . Asexual reproduction occurs by cite102†spores , by fragmentation of the rosettes, and by formation of apical cite103†tubers . Spores are large (45 to 200 cite104†μ ) and formed in tetrads.^{cite80†[2] }
L170: 
L171: The cite105†sporophyte of Riccia is the simplest amongst cite106†bryophytes . It consists of only a capsule, missing both foot and seta, and does not perform photosynthesis.^{cite107†[8] }
