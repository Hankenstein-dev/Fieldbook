L159: Verbena canadensis (syn. Glandularia canadensis), commonly known as rose mock vervain,^{cite61†[2] } rose verbena,^{cite62†[3] } clump verbena^{cite63†[4] } or rose vervain^{cite64†[5] } is a cite65†perennial cite66†herbaceous cite50†flowering plant in the verbena family (cite54†Verbenaceae ) with showy pink to purple flowers.
L160: It is native to the eastern and south-central areas of the United States.^{cite60†[1] } This species is widely cultivated as an ornamental, and naturalized populations have been established outside its native range, such as in the northeastern U.S.^{cite67†[6] }^{cite68†[7] }
L161: ## Description
L162: 
L163: [cite69†edit ]
L164: V. canadensis is a perennial herb^{cite70†[8] } that grows low to the ground, typically to about 1 foot (30 cm) high. Roots will form where stems touch the ground, and over time the plant will spread to form a low mound.^{cite71†[9] } Leaves, up to 8 centimetres (3 in)long, are dark green on the upper surface and lighter green below. Leaves are semi-evergreen, opposite and pinnately-lobed.^{cite63†[4] } Flowers are pink to purple, and consist of a tubular cite72†corolla that opens up into 4 or 5 spreading lobes.
L165: Flowers bloom in the spring and can persist until fall.^{cite68†[7] } The plant attracts butterflies, rabbits, and deer.^{cite70†[8] }
L166: ## Distribution and habitat
L167: 
L168: [cite73†edit ]
L169: 
L170: Its natural habitat is in sunny areas such as glades, forest openings, and on bluffs.^{cite68†[7] }^{cite74†[10] } It is tolerant of dry conditions.^{cite75†[11] } In some areas, the presence of Verbena canadensis is indicative of high-quality natural communities.^{cite74†[10] }
