L176: Fruit of Erodium ciconium Erodium lebelii Erodium glandulosum - cite98†MHNT L177: Erodium is a cite99†genus of cite90†flowering plants in the botanical family cite94†Geraniaceae .
L178: The genus includes about 120 species with a subcosmopolitan distribution, cite100†native to Europe, cite101†North Africa , cite102†Asia , cite103†Australia , and more locally in cite104†North and cite105†South America .^{cite106†[1] } They are cite107†perennials , cite108†annuals , or cite109†subshrubs , with five-petalled flowers in shades of white, pink, and purple, that strongly resemble the better-known cite110†Geranium (crane's-bills).^{cite111†[2] } In English-speaking areas of Europe, the species are known as stork's-bills.^{cite112†[3] } In North America they are known as filarees or heron's bill.
L179: ## Taxonomy
L180: 
L181: [cite113†edit ]
L182: cite114†Carl Linnaeus grouped in the same genus (Geranium), the three similar genera Erodium, Geranium, and cite115†Pelargonium . The distinction between them was made by cite116†Charles Louis L'Héritier de Brutelle based on the number of cite117†stamens or cite118†anthers ; five in Erodium,^{cite119†[4] } seven for Pelargonium, and ten for Geranium.^{cite119†[4] } However, the three genera have the same characteristics in regard to their fruit, which resemble long bird beaks.
L183: That characteristic is the basis for the names: Geranium evokes the crane (Greek geranos), Pelargonium the stork (pelargos), and Erodium the heron (erodios). Erodium species also differ in having cite120†pinnate leaves, whereas Geranium species have cite121†palmately lobed or divided leaves.^{cite122†[5] }
