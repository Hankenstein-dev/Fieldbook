L422: ## Begonia × albopicta
L423: 
L424: Narrow pointed green leaves carry fine pale spots on Begonia × albopicta. Their slender shape and light pattern keep the plant fine and delicate, even after the stems have gained height and begun to harden.
L425: ### Fine spotted foliage
L426: 
L427:   * Leaves: Narrow pointed green blades with fine pale spots
L428:   * Stems: Slim jointed upright stems that become woody near the base
L429:   * Growth habit: Upright and lightly branched when young, becoming an open shrub with most leaves near the younger branch tips
L430: 
L431: Origin & background
L432: ### A Brazilian hybrid lineage
L433: 
L434: Begonia × albopicta is the named hybrid of Begonia maculata and Begonia olbia, two species associated with southeastern Brazil.
L435: 
L436: Both parent species grow within the warm, humid Atlantic Forest, where rainfall is regular and direct sunlight is broken by the canopy.
L437: 
L438: Plant care
L439: 
L440: ### Care for cane-forming foliage plants
L441: #### Substrate and roots
L442: 
L443: Begonia × albopicta depends on fine, easily torn roots below the cane bases. Use only enough extra pot depth to stabilise albopicta; unused wet mix remains saturated for too long.
L444: 
L445: #### Light
L446: 
L447: Keep the plant beside a large east-facing window, or a short distance from a south or west window behind a sheer curtain. A little early-morning or late-afternoon sun is useful after acclimation.
L448: #### Watering
L449: 
L450: Check Begonia × albopicta below the surface and water when the upper layer of the substrate has dried while the centre of the root ball still feels faintly moist. On albopicta, check the centre of the root ball as well as the surface.
L451: 
L452: #### Temperature
L453: 
L454: Begonia × albopicta grows reliably in steady indoor warmth. Pull the plant away from cold windows at night, especially while the substrate is still moist.
L455: #### Humidity and airflow
L456: 
L457: Household humidity is normally sufficient away from heaters, although moderate to high humidity can improve leaf expansion during active growth. Clean, full-sized new leaves on albopicta indicate that humidity is sufficient.
L458: 
L459: #### Fertilising
L460: 
L461: Give Begonia × albopicta a dilute balanced houseplant fertiliser as new cane leaves and side shoots extend. Extra fertiliser will not correct weak canes caused by insufficient light.
L462: 
L463: Issues & solutions
L464: ### Begonia × albopicta: common symptoms
L465: 
L466: #### Yellowing between the dots on spotted leaves
L467: 
L468: Evenly distributed pale spots are normal. General paling and weak new growth can follow depleted or damaged roots. A yellow halo around a glassy lesion is typical of leaf spot. Check root condition and growth first; isolate and remove leaves only when lesions are actively enlarging.
L469: #### Cane growth becomes long and bare
L470: 
L471: A cane may keep extending after its oldest spotted leaves are shed. If the upper leaves remain full-sized, shorten the firm cane above a lower node and root the top to rebuild the plant. When the gaps between new leaves also widen, provide brighter filtered light before pruning again.
L472: #### Leaves droop and fall after watering
L473: 
L474: If the mix is still wet, empty any runoff held in the outer pot and allow air to return. Persistent drooping needs a root check. Remove black, mushy roots and any soft stem base, use a smaller pot if necessary, and root healthy cane tips if the base continues to decline.
L475: #### Brown patches appear on the light-facing leaves
L476: 
L477: Dry, bleached patches usually indicate direct-sun or heat damage, especially after a move. Restore filtered light and avoid contact with hot glass. Translucent spots, yellow borders or marks appearing throughout the plant need a disease check.
L478: #### New leaves and flowers show streaks or black specks
L479: 
L480: Inspect for thrips, particularly inside young folded leaves and flower clusters. Their irregular silver scars differ from the plant’s stable round spotting. Isolate, remove badly damaged flowers and treat all life stages at the label interval; sticky cards help show if adults remain active.

L482: Safety
L483: ### Begonia × albopicta is toxic to people and pets
L484: 
L485:   * Keep this plant away from children, cats and dogs.
L486:   * The roots and rhizomes contain the highest levels of soluble oxalates, and the sap can irritate skin.
L487:   * Chewing may cause mouth irritation, drooling, vomiting or stomach pain. Contact a veterinarian or medical professional if symptoms develop.

L490: ### When was Begonia × albopicta first published, and what does it mean?
L491: 
L492: Begonia honours Michel Bégon. The epithet albopicta combines Latin albus, white, with pictus, painted or marked.
L493: 
L494: Carl Linnaeus established the genus Begonia in 1753 in Species Plantarum, page 1056.
L495: 
L496: William Bull first described Begonia × albopicta in 1885 in Nursery Catalogue of William Bull, volume 210, page 13.
