L33: ## Conioselinum gmelinii (Cham. & Schlecht.) Steud. (C. chinense auct. non [L.] B.S.P., C. chinense [L.] B.S.P. var. pacificum [S. Wats.] Boivin, C. pacificum [S. Wats.] Coult. & Rose)
L34: 
L35: cite25†Image L36: ### overview
L37: Usually hairless, sometimes glaucous, perennial from a short, stout rhizomatous stem-base with a cluster of fleshy roots or a single tap-root; single stem, leafy, 20-120 cm tall. Leaves all on the stem, egg-shaped to deltoid in outline, two to three times pinnately dissected, with lobed or cleft leaflets, leaf stalks sheathing at the base. Flowers white, small, numerous in 8-30 small compact heads in one to several compound umbels; involucral bracts one to few, linear in shape, or lacking.
L38: Fruits oblong-oval, 5-8 mm long, hairless, ribbed with the lateral ribs having broad, thin wings (Pojar and MacKinnon, 1994).
L39: ### family
L40: 
L41: Apiaceae
L42: 
L43: ### ecological setting
L44: 
L45: Common on gravelly beaches, sandy shores, upper tidal marshes, grassy bluffs and headlands, mostly along or near the coastline (Pojar and MacKinnon, 1994).
L46: 
L47: ### soil texture
L48: 
L49: Light textured sandy and loamy soils (Fern, 2000).
L50: 
L51: ### soil reaction salinity
L52: 
L53: Fairly wide range of soil reactions from acidic to alkaline. Can tolerate salinity (Fern, 2000).
L54: 
L55: ### moisture regime
L56: 
L57: Moist to wet (Fern, 2000).
L58: ### shade tolerance
L59: 
L60: Shade intolerant to mildly tolerant. Requires full sun (Fern, 2000).
L61: 
L62: ### successional status
L63: 
L64: ### bec zone subzone status
L65: 
L66: Occurs on grassy bluffs and rocky headlands within Garry Oak ecosystems.
L67: 
L68: ### site rehabilitation
L69: 
L70: Potential reclamation species for erosion control on moister disturbed sites.
L71: 
L72: ### wildlife
L73: 
L74: Browsed by deer, elk, moose and bear. Flowers attract butterflies.
L75: 
L76: ### landscaping
L77: 
L78: Potential garden ornamental for the moist sunny garden.
L79: ### first nations
L80: 
L81: Known as wild carrot or Indian carrot. Cooked and eaten historically by coastal Aboriginal Peoples, though not apparently used now. Leaves used to make a tonic for the treatment of colds and sore throats and in steam baths to treat rheumatism (Pojar and MacKinnon, 1994; Turner, 1995).

L104: ### collection and abstraction
L105: 
L106: Collect seed as soon as ripe by hand stripping. Clean by drying and fanning.
L107: 
L108: ### seed storage
L109: 
L110: Store in airtight containers at 2º C. Can be stored for up to three years.
L111: ### fruit seed dormancy treatment
L112: 
L113: No dormancy treatments are necessary. Leach seeds in tap water for four hours before sowing. Sow stored seeds in fall or early spring. As soon as plants are large enough to handle, prick out into separate pots (Fern, 2000).
