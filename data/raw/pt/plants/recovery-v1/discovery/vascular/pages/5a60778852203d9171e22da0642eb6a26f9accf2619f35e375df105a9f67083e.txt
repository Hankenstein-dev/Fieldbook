L5@P0: Orobanche haenseleri var. deludens Beck (Orobanchaceae), a
L6@P0: problematic taxon described from Algeciras (Cádiz, S Spain) is

L9@P0: M.J.Y. Foley and better included, as a variety, under O. gracilis

L11@P0: and NW Africa).

L23@P0: Introduction
L24@P0: Beck (1922: 38) described Orobanche haenseleri var.
L25@P0: deludens on the basis of the material collected by Wolley-Dod near Algeciras. The following description was
L26@P0: provided: “Validissima. Scapus 11-16 cm longus, crassus, copiose squamatus, bracteis ovalibus triangularibus copiose glandulosis. Spica –36 cm longa, densi
L27@P0: in parte inferiore laxiflora. Calycis segmenta libera.
L28@P0: Laciniae labii inferioris rotundatae, eae labii superi
L29@P0: truncatae, dentatae et dente major apiculataae, omnes
L30@P0: copiose glanduloso pilosae. Filamenta 1-2 mm supra
L31@P0: basim corollae inserta, infra pilosa supra parce glanduloso pilosa. Stylus copiose glanduloso-pilosus. - Hispania ad aquas cadentes prope Algeciras m. Martio
L32@P0: 1912 (Wolley-Dod)”.
L33@P0: The taxon has been largely ignored by subsequent
L34@P0: authors. Only Foley (2001b: 54), after studying the
L35@P0: original material, concluded that it was impossible to
L36@P0: identify the specimen accurately, although he also stated that it did not fit O. haenseleri Reut. It is clear that
L37@P0: Orobanche haenseleri var. deludens Beck constitutes a
L38@P0: problematic taxon that remains as an unidentified entry in the current edition of Flora iberica. “Deludens”,
L39@P0: from the latin verb “deludo” meaning to mock, to
L40@P0: cheat, to simulate, to do trap, to be deceptive, is a very
L41@P0: adequate name for this taxon, since it has been a confusing case during many years.

L72@P1: first recognized by Haenseler (1837), who found it

L77@P1: F.W. Schultz 1843) (Willkomm, 1893: 186), or even
L78@P1: described as a new species, O. ulicis Haens. ex Willk.,
L79@P1: non Des Moul. (Willkomm, 1846: 313). In recent

L81@P1: spruneri (F.W. Schultz) Beck (Pujadas-Salvà & LoraGonzález 1996: 218; Pujadas-Salvà 2002: 431), or

L83@P1: Foley (Foley 2001a: 226).

L138@P3: Relationships with Orobanche gracilis
L139@P3: Orobanche gracilis var. deludens has often been confused with O. gracilis var. gracilis, to which it is very
L140@P3: close morphologically. We believe that the varietal
L141@P3: rank –the same established by Beck– is the more appropiate for this plant because there is no clear-cut
L142@P3: geographical separation between both varieties. In addition, the two are linked by forms intermediate in leaf
L143@P3: width, corolla color, and hairiness and width of the staminal filaments.
L144@P3: Biomolecular analyses further justify the use of the
L145@P3: varietal rank for this taxon and its subordination to
L146@P3: O. gracilis. Thus, RAPD analysis (Román & al., 2003)
L147@P3: revealed very short genetic distances among the four
L148@P3: O. gracilis populations studied. The first population of
L149@P3: O. gracilis to appear in the dendrogram sequence corresponded to var. deludens from Sierra de Cabra (Córdoba, Spain, COA 29293) and the other three to var.
L150@P3: gracilis (Román & al., 2003: 640). Likewise, results by
L151@P3: Schneeweiss & al. (2004), repeatedly included var. gracilis and var. deludens (as O. austrohispanica) within
L152@P3: the same small clade based on the short genetic distances found.
