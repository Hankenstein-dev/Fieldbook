L160: Brachychiton (kurrajong, bottletree) is a cite84†genus of 31 cite85†species of cite86†trees and large cite87†shrubs , native to cite88†Australia (the cite89†centre of diversity , with 30 species) and cite90†New Guinea (one species). Fossils from cite91†New South Wales and New Zealand are estimated to be 50 million years old, corresponding to the cite92†Paleogene .
L161: ## Description
L162: 
L163: [cite93†edit ]
L164: 
L165: They grow to 4 – 30m tall, and some are dry-season cite94†deciduous . Several species (though not all) are cite95†pachycaul plants with a very stout stem for their overall size, used to store water during periods of cite96†drought . The cite97†leaves show intraspecific variation and generally range from entire to deeply palmately lobed with long slender leaflet-like lobes joined only right at the base. Their sizes range from 4 – 20 cm long and wide.
L166: All species are cite98†monoecious with separate male and female flowers on the same plant. The flowers have a bell-shaped cite99†perianth consisting of a single series of fused lobes which is regarded as a cite100†calyx despite being brightly coloured in most species. The female flowers have five separate cite101†carpels that can each form a woody fruit containing several seeds. The flower colour is often variable within species.
L167: Eastern forest species drop their foliage before flowering but those of the drier regions carry the flowers while in leaf.
L168: ## Name
L169: 
L170: [cite102†edit ]
L171: 
L172: The name Brachychiton is derived from the cite103†Greek brachys, short, and cite104†chiton , tunic, in referring to its loose cite105†seed coats. The generic name is often misconstrued as being of neuter gender, with the specific epithets then incorrectly amended. Thus B. rupestre and B. populneum are sometimes seen in horticultural books and magazines.
L173: 
L174: Kurrajong comes from the cite106†Dharuk language garrajuŋ 'fishing line', as fishing lines were made from kurrajong bark.^{cite107†[2] }

L221: ## Cultivation
L222: 
L223: [cite150†edit ]
L224: 
L225: A few species of kurrajong are popular garden trees and have been introduced to hot dry regions including the cite151†Mediterranean , cite152†South Africa and the western cite153†United States . These species are also hybridised for horticultural purposes, B. populneo-acerifolius being one example. Kurrajongs are known to bloom erratically in cultivation.
