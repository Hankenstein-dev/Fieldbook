L154: Simethis is a genus of plants in the family cite54†Asphodelaceae , subfamily cite55†Hemerocallidoideae .^{cite59†[2] } It contains only one known species, Simethis mattiazzii, commonly called the Kerry lily.^{cite58†[1] }^{cite60†[3] }
L155: It grows in dry places especially near the coast in Western Europe (cite61†Ireland , cite62†France , cite63†Spain , cite64†Portugal , cite65†Italy ) and northern cite66†Africa (cite67†Algeria , cite68†Morocco , cite69†Tunisia ). Its common name is based on its occurrence at a few sites in cite70†County Kerry , cite61†Ireland .^{cite58†[1] }
L156: ## Description
L157: 
L158: [cite71†edit ]
L159: 
L160: Growth habit (wild specimen, cite72†Ceuta , Spanish territory in N. Africa)
L161: The Kerry lily is a perennial plant reaching a maximum height of 25 cm (10 in). It has a vertical cite73†rhizome and fleshy roots. The leaves grow from the base of the plant and are up to 30 cm (12 in) long; they are narrow, linear and grass-like, and sometimes curl. The cite74†inflorescence is a sparsely-branched, erect, wiry stem bearing a few small leaves and a loose spike of three to ten cite75†flowers with six cite76†tepals .
L162: The flowers are 2 cm (0.8 in) in diameter, purple-grey in bud and gleaming white when they open. The six cite77†stamens have fuzzy white cite78†filaments tipped by yellow cite78†anthers . The fruit is a three-lobed cite79†capsule . The Kerry lily flowers in May and June.^{cite80†[4] }^{cite81†[5] }
L163: ## Distribution and habitat
L164: 
L165: [cite82†edit ]
L166: The Kerry lily has a scattered, mainly maritime, distribution in Western Europe and North Africa.
L167: The only place in the cite83†British Isles where it is found is in cite70†County Kerry in southwestern Ireland, where it is restricted to a 20 km^{2} (7.7 mi^{2}) area around cite84†Derrynane .^{cite85†[6] } It occurs in western France, the Pyrenees, the Atlantic coastal strip of northern and western Spain and Portugal, the Mediterranean coastal strip of Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia, and isolated sites in western Italy, Sicily, Corsica and Sardinia.
L168: In different parts of its range it inhabits cite86†grassland and heathland habitats, maquis, shrubland and cork-oak woodland. It is common in the Atlantic belt of Europe but rare in many of the other scattered locations in which it grows.^{cite81†[5] }
L169: This plant has a cite87†relictual distribution, with southwestern Ireland being its most northerly occurrence. It was recorded from Dorset in southern England, but seems to now be extinct there. It is more common in Brittany and the Loire Valley, and the northern flanks of the cite88†Pyrenees but much rarer further inland. It is also common in the coastal strip of the Iberian Peninsula, and was discovered growing on the island of cite89†Marettimo , Sicily, for the first time in 2012.
L170: The population in North Africa is very fragmentary.^{cite81†[5] }
