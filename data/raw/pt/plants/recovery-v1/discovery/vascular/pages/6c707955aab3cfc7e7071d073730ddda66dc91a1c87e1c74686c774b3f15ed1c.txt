L221@P9: Discussion: Rechinger has formed for this species a new monotypic
L222@P9: section — Dictyocarpa Rech. f. This section has been formed because
L223@P9: the new species was characteristic in the following respects. It had unarmed shoots, a very original colour of the flowers, beaked keels, recurvate teeth of the calyx and the purple venation of the legumes. However on detailed examination of the herbarium specimens it was found
L224@P9: that in this new species spinescent shoots do in fact occur and of the
L225@P9: same type as in other species of the section Armata (particularily in
L226@P9: C. armata). As regards other morphological characters, which associate
L227@P9: C. porphyrogramma with the section Armata there are several, namely:
L228@P9: a) very short leaves arranged in fascicles, b) leaflets very small, 2 rarely
L229@P9: 3 pairs in number, c) the pubescent leaf axis hardening and remaining
L230@P9: on the shoot for a longer time while the leaflets have fallen off, d) flowers small, single, d) the keel is beaked.
L231@P9: C. porphyrogramma shows the greatest affinity to the Pakistan C. armata which it resembles also in the almost identical colour of the keel
L232@P9: and the wings, and in the venation of the fruits which however is much
L233@P9: more prominent. Among the differentiating characters one could mention the glabrous ovaries and fruit, in which respect C. porphyrogramma
L234@P9: resembles C. komarovii. It is differentiated from all species of the
L235@P9: Armata section in having recurvate calyx teeth, which however cannot
L236@P9: be a sufficient reason to maintain the independent section Dictyocarpa.
L237@P9: Thus on the basis of the characters discussed above it seems to me that
L238@P9: C. porphyrogramma should be included in section Armata and the term
L239@P9: Dictyocarpa considered as a synonym for that section.
L240@P9: It appears that C. porphyrogramma, similarily as other species of section Armata, is limited in its occurance to only a very small range of
L241@P9: distribution. Stands of these species are to be found along the belt of
L242@P9: mountains extending from southern Caucasus (C. komarovii) through
L243@P9: north Iran (C. uniflora — Prov. Gilan), and Afghanistan, till central
L244@P9: W. Pakistan (C. armata). The range of this new species considerably
L245@P9: reduces the distance which so far has been separating the ranges of
L246@P9: C. uniflora and C. armata (fig. 4).
