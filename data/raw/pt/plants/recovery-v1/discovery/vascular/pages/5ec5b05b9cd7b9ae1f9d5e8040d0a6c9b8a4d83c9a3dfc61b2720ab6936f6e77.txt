L6@P0: crustose red algal genus Peyssonnelia (Peyssonneliales, Rhodophyta) in the
L7@P0: Azores: from five to one species. Arquipelago. Life and Marine Sciences 32: 1-9.
L8@P0: The family Peyssonneliaceae comprises a worldwide group of non-calcified to calcified,
L9@P0: crust-forming red algae of great ecological significance. Of the genera currently recognized
L10@P0: in the family, Peyssonnelia has been widely considered to contain the largest number of
L11@P0: species, with five members reported for the Azores. Using rbcL as a molecular marker, we
L12@P0: here report on the taxonomic identity of recent collections of Peyssonneliaceae from the
L13@P0: Azorean islands of São Miguel, Graciosa and Pico, and compare those specimens in a
L14@P0: worldwide context. Only a single Peyssonnelia species, P. squamaria, is confirmed for the
L15@P0: Azorean archipelago, with three different haplotypes. Although the populations in the
L16@P0: Azores are genetically different from those occurring in the Mediterranean, this separation
L17@P0: appears to be relatively recent.

L22@P0: INTRODUCTION
L23@P0: The family Peyssonneliaceae (Denizot, 1968),
L24@P0: recently elevated to ordinal rank (Krayesky et al.
L25@P0: 2009), comprises a worldwide group of noncalcified or calcified, crust-forming red algae that
L26@P0: are of great ecological significance (Peña & Barbara 2013). Of the genera currently recognized in
L27@P0: the family, Peyssonnelia (Decaisne, 1841) has
L28@P0: been considered the richest in terms of species
L29@P0: number (Pueschel & Saunders 2009). A combination of vegetative and reproductive characters are
L30@P0: currently used to distinguish species of
L31@P0: Peyssonnelia, such as location and degree of calcification, variations in crust adherence, morphology, anatomy and differences in reproductive
L32@P0: development (Maggs & Irvine 1983). The identification of Peyssonnelia species is challenging,
L33@P0: resulting in a number of species usually underestimated or overestimated (Dixon & Saunders
L34@P0: 2013). Comparative morphology and DNA sequence analysis confirm that most species originally reported as belonging in Peyssonnelia in
L35@P0: fact belong to other genera (Fredericq et al. 2014)
L36@P0: within the Peyssonneliales (Krayesky et al. 2009).
L37@P0:  Peyssonnelia sensu stricto (following Krayesky
L38@P0: et al. 2009) represents species characterized by a
L39@P0: hypothallus that cuts off additional cells forming
L40@P0: multicellular rhizoids (Krayesky 2007). Recent
L41@P0: studies based on worldwide collections indicate
L42@P0: that species of Peyssonnelia sensu stricto have a
L43@P0: narrow distribution and do not occur in most
L44@P0: ocean basins, for example, the Gulf of Mexico
L45@P0: (Krayesky et al. 2009; Fredericq et al. 2014).
L46@P0:  Based on general flora studies, five species of
L47@P0: Peyssonnelia have been reported for the Azores
L48@P0: (Parente 2010): the generitype P. squamaria
L49@P0: ((S.G. Gmelin) Decaisne, 1842) described from
L50@P0-1: Italy; P. rubra ((Greville) J. Agardh, 1851) described from the Ionian Sea, Greece; P. polymor-Gabriel  et  al.
L51@P1: 2
L52@P1: pha ((Zanardini) F. Schmitz in Falkenberg, 1879)
L53@P1: described from the Adriatic Sea; P. coriacea
L54@P1: (Feldmann, 1941) described from Tangier, Morocco; and P. rosa-marina (Boudouresque &
L55@P1: Denizot, 1973) described from Port-Cros, Mediterranean France (see Guiry & Guiry 2015). Only
L56@P1: one species of Peyssonnelia, P. squamaria (Fig.
L57@P1: 1), was recognized for the Azores by Krayesky
L58@P1: (2007) and Krayesky et al. (2009) after examination of multiple collections.
L59@P1: Using rbcL as a molecular marker, we report on
L60@P1: the taxonomic identity of recent collections of
L61@P1: Peyssonneliaceae from the islands of São Miguel,
L62@P1: Graciosa and Pico in the Azores, and discuss the
L63@P1: connection between the various Azorean haplotypes. The identity of the Azorean specimens is
L64@P1: compared with those of Peyssonnelia sensu stricto in a worldwide context.

L66@P1: MATERIAL AND METHODS
L67@P1: Samples of Peyssonneliaceae were collected in
L68@P1: the Azores and the Mediterranean during low tide
L69@P1: or by snorkeling and SCUBA diving. Samples
L70@P1: were kept in coolers until processed, and then
L71@P1: dried in silica gel. Dried samples were ground
L72@P1: with mortar and pestle, and total DNA was extracted using DNeasy Plant mini Kits (Qiagen
L73@P1: Valencia, CA, USA). All the resulting DNA extracts were deposited in the Seaweed Lab at the
L74@P1: University of Louisiana at Lafayette (ULL).
L75@P1: Chloroplast-encoded rbcL gene sequences were
L76@P1: amplified using PCR primers and protocols described in Lin et al. (2001) and Gabriel et al.
L77@P1: (2010). Resulting PCR products were gel-purified
L78@P1: and sequenced in both directions using Bigdye
L79@P1: terminator v 3.1 (Life Technologies Grand Island
L80@P1: NY, USA) on the ABI 3130xl genetic analyzer at
L81@P1: ULL and assembled with Sequencher v. 5.2
L82@P1: (Gene Codes Corporation). Newly acquired sequences, in addition to 14 rbcL sequences downloaded from GenBank, were then manually
L83@P1: aligned in Mega v 5.2.2 (Tamura et al. 2011). The
L84@P1: subsequent alignment was analyzed in Partitionfinder (Lanfear et al. 2012) to determine the best
L85@P1: fitting model of evolution and data partition. The
L86@P1: analysis resulted in the selection of the General
L87@P1: Time Reversible model plus gamma and a proportion of invariable sites applied separately to
L88@P1-2: each codon position on the basis of the three information criteria, i.e. Akaike information criteri-Peyssonnelia in  the  Azores
L89@P2: 3
L90@P2: on with correction (AICc), Akaike information
L91@P2: criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC). The alignment was analyzed by Maximum likelihood (ML) as implemented by
L92@P2: RAXML v 2.4.4 (Stamatakis 2006) with the
L93@P2: above models and partition scheme with 1000
L94@P2: restarts to find the tree with the lowest likelihood
L95@P2: score and 1000 Bootstrap (BS) replications.
L96@P2:  A Bayesian MCMC (Markov Chain Monte
L97@P2: Carlo) was also applied to the aligned dataset
L98@P2: using MrBayes v. 3.2.5 (Huelsenbeck & Ronquist
L99@P2: 2001; Ronquist & Huelsenbeck 2003). The
L100@P2: Bayesian analysis consisted of two independent
L101@P2: runs of 5 million generations with sampling every
L102@P2: 1,000 generations for a total of 10,002 trees.
L103@P2: Convergence was visualized using Tracer v1.6
L104@P2: (Rambaut & Drummond 2007) and the first 10
L105@P2: percent of the trees of each run was discarded as
L106@P2: the burn-in. The resulting Bayesian Posterior
L107@P2: Probabilities derived from the consensus tree
L108@P2: were mapped on the ML tree. A distance matrix
L109@P2: was also resolved from the branch lengths of the
L110@P2: ML tree using the function cophenetic.phylo of
L111@P2: the APE Package in R (Paradis et al. 2004).
L112@P2:  The resulting distance matrix was used to find
L113@P2: species boundaries in a stand-alone version of
L114@P2: Automatic to Barcode Gap Discovery (ABGD).
L115@P2: General Mixed Yule Coalescence (GMYC) model, as implemented by the Splits Package in R
L116@P2: (Fujisawa & Barraclough 2013) with a single
L117@P2: threshold model, was also used to determine species boundaries. The requisite ultrametric tree for
L118@P2: the GMYC analysis was generated in Beast v
L119@P2: 1.8.1 (Drummond et al. 2012) using a relaxed
L120@P2: log-normal clock with a constant population coalescent as a prior and the best fitting model and
L121@P2: partition as described above. MCMC Chains were
L122@P2: run for 10 million generations with sampling every 1000th generation resulting in 10,000 trees.
L123@P2: The quality of the run was assessed in Tracer v1.6
L124@P2: (Rambaut & Drummond 2007) to ensure that effective sample size (ESS) values were >200 with
L125@P2: the default burn-in (1,000 trees). Tree annotator v
L126@P2: 1.8.1 (Drummond et al. 2012) was used to summarize the resulting 9001 trees after burning, targeting the maximum clade credibility tree with
L127@P2: preserved node heights. A statistical parsimony
L128@P2: method implemented in the TCS 1.21 software
L129@P2: (Clement et al. 2000) was used to infer genealogical relationships among haplotypes. The maximum number of differences resulting from single
L130@P2: substitutions among haplotypes was calculated
L131@P2: with 95% confidence limits, treating gaps as
L132@P2: missing data.
L133@P2: RESULTS
L134@P2: The final dataset was composed of 34 rbcL sequences of Peyssonneliaceae, 20 of which were
L135@P2: newly generated, with 31 sequences representing
L136@P2: hitherto confirmed Peyssonnelia species and three
L137@P2: sequences of Sonderopelta capensis ((Montagne)
L138@P2: Krayesky, 2009) and S. coriacea (Womersley &
L139@P2: Sinkora, 1981) (Table 1). Sonderopelta (Womersley & Sinkora, 1981) was selected as an outgroup
L140@P2: based on previous studies that established the
L141@P2: genus as a sister taxon of Peyssonnelia (Kato et
L142@P2: al. 2006; Krayesky et al. 2009; Dixon & Saunders
L143@P2: 2013). [Note: Sonderopelta has been viewed to be
L144@P2: an illegitimate name by Wynne (2011); however,
L145@P2: Sonderophycus (Denizot, 1968) is not a valid
L146@P2: name (Womersley & Sinkora 1981) in agreement
L147@P2: with Article 41.5 of the International Code of
L148@P2: Botanical Nomenclature (2012, Melbourne
L149@P2: Code)].
L150@P2:  The results of the ABGD and GMYC analyses
L151@P2: showed the existence of six species of
L152@P2: Peyssonnelia within the dataset: P. replicata
L153@P2: (Kützing, 1847), P. bornetii (Boudouresque &
L154@P2: Denizot, 1973), P. rubra and three closely related
L155@P2: species initially identified as P. squamaria (Fig.
L156@P2: 2). All Azorean collections belonged to P. squamaria, along with Mediterranean representatives
L157@P2: from Catalonia (Spain), Sicily (Italy), Malta and
L158@P2: Greece (not shown). Its two sister clades were
L159@P2: only observed in the Mediterranean, and are here
L160@P2: referred to as P. coriacea from Malta and P. polymorpha from Sicily. Sequence JX969797, referred to as P. squamaria by Dixon & Saunders
L161@P2: (2013), corresponds to material of Sicily that has
L162@P2: a unistratose hypothallus layer in contrast to the
L163@P2: 2-layered hypothallus of P. squamaria (Boudouresque & Denizot, 1975).

L239@P6: CONCLUSION
L240@P6: Only a single, true Peyssonnelia species is confirmed for the Azorean Archipelago, in contrast to
L241@P6: the five previously reported (Parente 2010). Although the populations in the Azores are genetically different from those occurring in the Mediterranean, this separation might be relatively recent, since the archipelago emerged about 8 My
L242@P6: ago (Rumeu et al. 2011). Further studies including more islands and more samples are necessary
L243@P6: to assess the variability of the species within the
L244@P6: archipelago and the connection between its populations (Gabriel et al. 2014).
