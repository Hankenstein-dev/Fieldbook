L91: ### Editorial Note
L92: 
L93: This species was treated by Bean in synonymy with Callianthe vexillaria (É.Morren) Donnell (syn. Abutilon vexillarium É.Morren), a situation that obtained until very recently (see e.g. cite13†Donnell, Ballard & Cantino 2012 ). However, the two are now regarded as separate species.
L94: 
L95: The text below is derived from Bean, with updated taxonomy but otherwise unedited.
L96: An evergreen shrub of lax, graceful habit, to 6 ft high. Leaves ovate, cordate at the base, 2 to 4 in. long, slender-pointed, coarsely toothed. Flowers pendulous, borne singly in the leaf-axils from April onwards; calyx inflated, rich red, about 1 in. long; corolla yellow, about 1⁄2 in. longer than the calyx. The stamens and stigmas form a club-shaped stalked cluster that stands out well beyond the corolla. Bot. Mag., t. 5717.
L97: Native of Brazil. Although by no means hardy, it will survive most winters on a warm, sheltered wall, and is certainly one of the most attractive subjects for such a situation.

L109: ### 'Variegata'
L110: 
L111: A brightly, blotchily, yellow-variegated clone, created in the 1860s by Lemoine, who grafted the well-known contagious C. striata ‘Thompsonii’ onto C. megapotamica with the inevitable result that Abutilon mosaic virus was transmitted (cite14†Reid 1914 ). Bean (cite15†1914 ) thought that the leaves were ‘charmingly blotched and tessellated with bright yellow’; G.S. Thomas (cite16†1992 ) considered the variegation ‘a doubtful advantage.’
L112: Callianthe megapotamica 'Variegata' trained against a fence in York, UK, still flowering prolifically through the gloom of December (2021). Image John Grimshaw.
