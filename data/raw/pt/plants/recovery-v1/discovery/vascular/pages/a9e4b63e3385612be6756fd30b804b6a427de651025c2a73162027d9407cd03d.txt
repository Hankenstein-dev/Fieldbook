L209: cite45†  L210: 
L211: As I understand it (based substantially on insights from cite29†@yuri_pirogov above):
L212:   * The iNat taxon Iris × hybrida corresponds to Iris × hybrida hort., which encompasses bearded Irises of various parentage. There are about 2,000 iNat observations ID'ed as this taxon.
L213:   * The plants identified as Iris × hybrida definitely do not correspond to Iris × germanica, either as used on iNat, or as defined by POWO with the hybrid formula I. pallida × I. variegata. Those are not the parents for the Iris × hybrida plants on iNat.
L214:   * So, regardless of how we address Iris × hybrida, it would be entirely wrong to merge it into Iris × germanica. The cite75†proposed taxon swap should be cancelled.
L215:   * Botanical nomenclature requires published nothotaxa to have a precisely identified combination of parents to be valid. The long history of horticultural Iris breeding and the multiple crosses involved mean that identifying exact parentage is very challenging. In order to group plants usefully, Iris specialists have made use of horticultural hybrid names that encompass all hybrids from a particular set of parents.
L216:   * While future research and taxon descriptions may identify additional wild species and clarify hybrid parentage, it seems unlikely that we will ever get to a point where botanical nothospecies can be described for most of the plants contained within Iris × hybrida.
L217: So how do we deal with the iNat taxon currently named Iris × hybrida? Here are some options:
L218: 1. Leave Iris × hybrida as a nothospecies. Create a deviation from POWO. With this we accept that there's more value to iNat users in retaining this name than in trying to force it into POWO's taxonomy.
L219: 2. Create a different taxon that accommodates our imprecise knowledge of parentage for these plants, such as "Iris × hybrida complex". From one perspective, a species complex might seem ideal, because it is purposely intended to include "sibling groups of species". From another, it may be wholly flawed because a complex is not intended to be a "leaf taxon" and we probably cannot name the (notho)species that should be within the complex.
L220: Also, we would need to decide whether to include the wild parent species. Include them and we imply that they're hybrids; exclude them and our complex is not monophyletic.
L221: 3. Swap Iris × hybrida into section Iris. This would "resolve" the issue of having a nothospecies of imprecise parentage. But it would also irreversibly destroy important information for 2,000+ Iris × hybrida observations which would be indistinguishable from the 3,000 observations identified as section Iris. In my view, this doesn't benefit iNat users.
L222: I really favor option 1 here. It does little harm and a lot of good by allowing people to get an accurate ID for a significant fraction of Iris observations. It also allows this hybrid group to be independently tracked in relation to issues such as plant diseases, phenology, invasive tendencies, etc.

L247: cite45†  L248: 
L249: Hi cite76†@upupa-epops . Thanks for engaging with this issue! I do think iNat should find a better approach to deal with "widespread hybrids with imprecise parentage" than the ones currently used. Having worked through the back-story with Iris × germanica and Iris × hybrida. I'll say that I'm also very wary of a one-size-fits-all approach. I think I'd like to see more flexibility but still with some pretty clear guidance.
L250: 
L251: What I'd like to see:
L252:   * A formal policy permitting hybrid taxa to sometimes be added to the iNat taxonomy even when (1) their parentage is not precisely defined and (2) the name may not be accepted by iNat's preferred external taxon authority (e.g. POWO).
L253:   * I would distinguish these "artificial compound hybrids" from "wastebasket taxa". Wastebasket taxa are generally not hybrids, but are artificial assemblages of somewhat similar organisms that haven't yet been accurately placed within the hierarchical taxonomic framework.
L254:   * There should be restrictions on when these "artificial compound hybrids" can be added. I would propose: -- The name is widely used in published sources, e.g. Iris × hybrida, and ideally is supported by some alternative taxonomic source such as cite86†ICNCP†en.wikipedia.org . -- The taxon is identifiable and distinguishable from other taxa in iNat's taxonomy (at least with appropriate photos). -- There is a significant number of (potential) observations of the taxon (e.g. hundreds or thousands, not 10).
L255: A good threshold might be 50 observations as this aligns somewhat with the threshold for inclusion in CV training. -- There is a demonstrable benefit to adding the name to iNat's taxonomy.
L256: Some examples: (1) the hybrid is known to have naturalized in some locations, (2) there is value to distinguishing the hybrid from other (hybrid and/or non-hybrid) taxa so that all of them can be identified more reliably by CV; (3) cultivated observations of the hybrid taxon are frequently added by new iNat users and accurately identifying these can reduce the workload on human identifiers.
L257:   * I definitely think there should be a thorough discussion before any of these "artificial compound hybrids" is added to the taxonomy and I would not oppose some type of staff approval process. Even though plant-breeding has generated many cultivars over several centuries, I would see there being a relatively small number of "artificial compound hybrids".
L258: Rather than adding a separate taxon for each cultivated hybrid, I would prefer that each taxon we add represents a group of cultivars with similar parentage.
