L5@P0: Abstract
L6@P0: Plagiochila is the largest genus of bryophytes of Colombia. A total of 175 species have been recorded from
L7@P0: the country but the majority of these are synonyms. In this paper 57 species are accepted for Colombia. A key
L8@P0: to the Colombian species of Plagiochila is provided and each species is briefly described, with data on types,
L9@P0: synonyms, published illustrations, morphology, geographical distribution and habitat, as well as a brief discussion
L10@P0: of differentiating characters and taxonomic affinity. Among the countries of tropical America, Colombia has the
L11@P0: highest diversity of Plagiochila followed by Ecuador (53 species) and Costa Rica (50 species). More than half of
L12@P0: the Colombian species are widely distributed throughout tropical America and seven of them occur also in Africa
L13@P0: and/or Western Europe. About one fourth of the species are restricted to the northern Andes, which is the centre of
L14@P0: diversity of Plagiochila in the New World. The majority of the species grow as epiphytes in humid Andean forests.
L15@P0: Diversity peaks in the upper montane forest belt between 2000-3000 m. Sixteen species occur in the páramo belt
L16@P0: and five are exclusive to páramo. Eighteen species occur in lowland rainforest. Plagiochila eggersii, recorded from
L17@P0: lowland rainforest of the Chocó, is new to Colombia.
L18@P0: Key words: Andean forest, bryophytes, Colombia, elevational distribution, liverworts, páramo, Plagiochila, taxonomy.

L40@P0: Introduction
L41@P0: Plagiochilaceae are a large family of the liverworts
L42@P0: (Marchantiophyta) with an estimated 450 species in nine
L43@P0: genera (Söderström, et al., 2016; Gradstein, 2015a). The
L44@P0: species grow mainly as epiphytes and are abundant in
L45@P0: tropical montane forests where bryophyte layers are often
L46@P0: dominated by Plagiochilaceae (e.g., Wolf, 1994). About
L47@P0: 95% of the species of Plagiochilaceae belong to Plagiochila
L48@P0: (Dumort.) Dumort., which has an almost worldwide
L49@P0: distribution and is the only genus in the family occurring
L50@P0: in tropical America. Gradstein, et al. (2001) recognized
L51@P0: three further Neotropical genera, Plagiochilion S.Hatt.
L52@P0: with two Neotropical species and Steereochila Inoue and
L53@P0: Szweykowskia Gradst. & E.Reiner with one species each.
L54@P0: However, these four species are now included in Plagiochila
L55@P0: and the genera Steereochila and Szweykowskia are no longer
L56@P0: recognized (Heinrichs, 2002).

L220@P3: Among neotropical countries, Colombia has the largest
L221@P3: number of Plagiochila species followed by Ecuador (53
L222@P3: species), Costa Rica (50 species) and Brazil (34 species)
L223@P3: (Table 1). The high Plagiochila richness of Colombia may

L228@P3: in Brazil, a country eight times larger than Colombia, is

L232@P3: diversity (ca. 80 species), followed by Colombia (Table
L233@P3: 1). Species richness generally decreases towards higher
L234@P3: latitudes with 25 species being found in New Zealand, 21
