L43@P3: ABSTRACT
L44@P3: The red algal tribe Chondrieae F. Schmitz & Falkenberg (Rhodomelaceae, Rhodophyta)
L45@P3: currently contains seven free-living genera and two parasitic genera. The type genus is
L46@P3: Chondria C. Agardh which currently includes more than 80 certain species. The tribe
L47@P3: Chondrieae is superficially similar to the tribe Laurencieae F. Schmitz, but differs in the
L48@P3: number of pericentral cells, origin of tetrasporangia and shape of spermatangial branches.
L49@P3: Since the introduction of molecular tools for macroalgal systematics, some taxa of the
L50@P3: Rhodomelaceae have been studied in attempts to clarify relationships among genera and
L51@P3: species, especially in the tribe Laurencieae. In contrast, the Chondrieae almost has not
L52@P3: been drawn attention, especially in terms of molecular and phylogenetic studies.
L53@P3: Therefore, the current classification of the Chondrieae is still based almost entirely on
L54@P3: morphological characters. This study was carried out to produce the comprehensive
L55@P3: molecular phylogeny of Chondria as the type genus of the Chondrieae and to verify the
L56@P3: current classification of Chondria by morphological analyses, using specimens mainly
L57@P3: from Japan.
L58@P3: The molecular phylogenetic analyses were conducted based on RuBisCO large
L59@P3: subunit (rbcL), small subunit of nuclear ribosomal rRNA (SSU rRNA) and
L60@P3: mitochondrial cytochrome oxidase subunit 1 (cox1) gene sequences; new sequences
L61@P3: were generated for12 species. While the Chondrieae was moderately supported and
L62@P3: sister to the Laurencieae, the genus Chondria did not form a clade, being intermingled
L63@P3: with the genera Acanthophora J.V. Lamouroux and Acrocystis Zanardini (rbcL and SSU
L64@P3-4: rRNA trees). Morphological characters which have been adopted to identify Chondria3
L65@P4: species in previous works (e.g. shape of branchlets and apices, male or female
L66@P4: reproductive morphologies) were evaluated whether these characters reflect their
L67@P4: molecular phylogeny or not. These taxonomic characters did not correspond to the
L68@P4: phylogenetic trees. The phylogenetic trees were shown to be not support the subgenera
L69@P4: Euchondria, Coelochondria and Platychondria in the genus Chondria.
L70@P4: Two species previously identified as a member of the genus Chondria, the
L71@P4: Japanese ‗Chondria capillaris‘ and ‗Chondria nidifica‘, were segregated from Chondria
L72@P4: and clustered in the same clade isolated from other species of the tribe Chondrieae in
L73@P4: rbcL and SSU r RNA trees. The clade of the two species was sister to the clade of the
L74@P4: Laurencieae and other species of the Chondrieae with moderate supports. The new genus
L75@P4: Neochondria was proposed to accommodate these two species: Neochondria ammophila
L76@P4: S. Sutti, M. Tani, Y. Yamagishi, T. Abe & K. Kogame sp. nov. (= Japanese C.
L77@P4: capillaris) and Neochondria nidifica (Harvey) S. Sutti, T. Abe, K.A. Miller & K.
L78@P4: Kogame comb. nov. (= C. nidifica). Neochondria strikingly resembles Chondria in gross
L79@P4: morphology and shares several characters, but it can be distinguished from the latter by
L80@P4: the presence of adventitious elongate cells that form densely compact layers between the
L81@P4: pericentral cells and surround the axial strand even in ultimate branchlets.
L82@P4: Specimens formerly identified as Chondria dasyphylla (Woodward) C. Agardh
L83@P4: in Japan were reexamined using morphological and molecular phylogenetic analyses. It
L84@P4: was revealed that the specimens consisted of multiple species: Chondria acuminata sp.
L85@P4: nov., Chondria cf. curdieana (Harvey ex J.Agardh) De Toni and an unidentified species
L86@P4: (Chondria sp. 1). Chondria acuminata sp. nov. is characterized by its distinctively
L87@P4-5: acuminate branchlets. Chondria cf. curdieana, which was firstly reported from Japan, 4
L88@P5: shares most morphological characters with the Australian Chondria curdieana. The last
L89@P5: taxon, Chondria sp. 1 did not resemble any described Chondria species from Japan.
L90@P5: Morphological and molecular data (rbcL, SSU and cox1) demonstrated the identity of
L91@P5: these three species, suggesting that they are members of the genus Chondria and are
L92@P5-6: distinct from the authentic C. dasyphylla.5

L117@P8: GENERAL INTRODUCTION
L118@P8: The red algae (phylum Rhodophyta) are a distinct group of eukaryotic organisms
L119@P8: characterized by the following combination of characteristics: the complete absence of
L120@P8: any flagellate stages; the presence of accessory photosynthetic pigments called
L121@P8: phycobilins (phycoerythrin and phycocyanin); the occurrence of nonaggregated
L122@P8: photosynthetic lamellae with phycobilisomes within the chloroplast; so-called floridean
L123@P8: starch as food reserve; the existence of oogamous sexual reproduction involving
L124@P8: specialized female cells termed carpogonia and male gametes termed spermatia but
L125@P8: sexuality is apparently lacking in some members (Dixon 1973; Bold & Wynne 1985;
L126@P8: Lee 1999). Since two of those characteristics are also recognized in the cyanobacteria,
L127@P8: some authors have suggested that red algae are primitive and related to the cyanobacteria
L128@P8: (Bold & Wynne 1985). However, later molecular phylogenetic findings using nuclear
L129@P8: gene sequences suggested the conflicting results, red algae were rather related to
L130@P8: glaucophytes, green algae and land plants; comprising the super-group called
L131@P8: Archaeplastida (Bhattacharya & Medlin 1995; Moreira et al. 2000; Nozaki et al. 2003;
L132@P8: Adl et al. 2005). Red algae are a marine and freshwater assemblage (mainly marine)
L133@P8: predominate in extensive areas of the continental shelves in tropical, temperate, and
L134@P8: cold-water regions (Dixon 1973; Bold & Wynne 1985; Freshwater & Rueness 1994; Lee
L135@P8: 1999).
L136@P8: Traditionally, Rhodophyta wasconsidered to include two classes, Bangiophyceae
L137@P8: and Florideophyceae (Dixon 1973; Bold & Wynne 1985; Lee 1999). However, after the
L138@P8-9: upheaval introduced by molecular phylogenetic studies, this outdated classification has 8
L139@P9: been revised several times (Adl et al. 2005, 2007, 2012). At present, Rhodophyta
L140@P9: includes 7 accepted classes comprising Bangiophyceae, Compsopogonophyceae,
L141@P9: Cyanidiophyceae, Florideophyceae, Porphyridiophyceae, Rhodellophyceae and
L142@P9: Stylonematophyceae (Adl et al. 2012; Guiry & Guiry 2018).
L143@P9: Florideophyceae is the most complex and elaborate class in Rhodophyta
L144@P9: (Freshwater 2000; Guiry & Guiry 2018). Members of the Florideophyceae have pit
L145@P9: connections, apical growth, and sexual reproduction with a triphasic life cycle (Dixon
L146@P9: 1973; Lee 1999). Some species are used directly by humans for food, while cell wall
L147@P9: polysaccharides are extracted from others for use as gels, and additives in food and
L148@P9: cosmetic products (Freshwater 2000). Currently, Florideophyceae includes certain 31
L149@P9: orders: Acrochaetiales, Acrosymphytales, Ahnfeltiales, Atractophorales, Balbianiales,
L150@P9: Balliales, Batrachospermales, Bonnemaisoniales, Catenellopsidales, Ceramiales,
L151@P9: Colaconematales, Corallinales, Entwisleiales, Gelidiales, Gigartinales, Gracilariales,
L152@P9: Halymeniales, Hapalidiales, Hildenbrandiales, Nemaliales, Nemastomatales, Palmariales,
L153@P9: Peyssonneliales, Pihiellales, Plocamiales, Rhodachlyales, Rhodogorgonales,
L154@P9: Rhodymeniales, Sebdeniales, Sporolithales and Thoreales (Guiry & Guiry 2018).
L155@P9: The Rhodomelaceae (Ceramiales, Rhodophyta) is the largest family of the
L156@P9: Florideophyceae, currently includes 20 tribes (Alsidieae, Amansieae, Bostrychieae,
L157@P9: Chondrieae, Cladureae, Dipterosiphonieae, Herposiphonieae, Heterocladieae,
L158@P9: Laurencieae, Lophosiphonieae, Lophothalieae, Neotenophyceae, Ophidocladeae,
L159@P9: Polysiphonieae, Polyzonieae, Pterosiphonieae, Rhodomeleae, Sonderelleae,
L160@P9: Streblocladieae and Thaumatelleae) with more than a thousand species and
L161@P9-10: approximately 150 genera recognized (Diaz-Tapia et al. 2017; Guiry & Guiry 2018). 9
L162@P10: The present number of species and genera is probably underestimated since new genera
L163@P10: and new species within the family have been continuously proposed based on both
L164@P10: morphological and molecular data, e.g. Lampisiphonia H.-G. Choi, Díaz Tapia &
L165@P10: Barbara (Barbara et al. 2013), Coronaphycus Metti (Metti et al. 2015), Ohelopapa F.
L166@P10: Rousseau, Martin-Lescanne, Payri & L.Le Gall (Rousseau et al. 2017), Wilsonosiphonia
L167@P10: D. Bustamante, Won & T.O. Cho (Bustamante et al. 2017). The great number of
L168@P10: rhodomelean species reflects their high morphological diversity, especially the
L169@P10: vegetative structures. Thallus form widely varies in the family, from simple structure
L170@P10: types (without medullary nor cortical layers) to more complex pseudoparenchymatous
L171@P10: types. The Rhodomelaceae is distinguished from the other families of the order
L172@P10: Ceramiales by having a polysiphonous structure (axial cell surrounded by several
L173@P10: pericentral cells) with monopodial growth; 4–24 pericentral cells which are cut off in
L174@P10: alternating sequence; colorless and deciduous vegetative trichoblasts, monosiphonous
L175@P10: holoblastic branches which develop from the axial cells and are usually present on
L176@P10: subapical cell; tetrahedrally divided tetrasporangia borne on pericentral cells, or on
L177@P10: cortical cells in some genera; spermatangial organs on modified trichoblasts, which are
L178@P10: terete branches or flat plates; a (3-) 4 celled carpogonial branch, with a lateral sterile
L179@P10: group; auxiliary cell that is cut off from the supporting cell after fertilization
L180@P10: (Hommersand 1963; Womersley 2003). Fundamental of the current taxonomy of the
L181@P10: Rhodomelaceae was established by Falkenberg (1901). His excellent monograph
L182@P10: provided the massive and integrative detail of the family. Kylin (1956) mentioned that
L183@P10: the Rhodomealceae is the most advanced group within the order Ceramiales. Later,
L184@P10-11: Hommersand (1963) provided more details on the classification of the Rhodomelaceae. 10
L185@P11: However, Rhodomelaceae still includes the groups of taxa that are incomplete in
L186@P11: systematics; taxonomic revisions have been conducted using molecular phylogenetic
L187@P11: analyses as well as morphological analyses.
L188@P11: The tribe Chondrieae F. Schmitz & Falkenberg is one of the tribes in the
L189@P11: Rhodomelaceae and currently contains seven free-living genera (Acanthophora J.V.
L190@P11: Lamouroux, Acrocystis Zanardini, Chondria C. Agardh, Cladymenia Harvey,
L191@P11: Coeloclonium J. Agardh, and Husseya J. Agardh) and two parasitic genera (Benzaitenia
L192@P11: Yendo, and Ululania K.E. Apt & K.E. Schlech) (Kurihara et al. 2010; Diaz-Tapia et al.
L193@P11: 2017; Guiry & Guiry 2018). The type genus of the tribe is Chondria (Schmitz &
L194@P11: Falkenberg 1897). Most genera within the tribe have terete, or flattened branched thalli
L195@P11: with a clear axial and five pericentral cells surrounded by a broad, compact, cellular
L196@P11: cortex (except in Coeloclonium) (Womersley 2003). The tribe Chondrieae is
L197@P11: characterized by the following features; the five pericentral cells are elongate and
L198@P11: parallel to the axial cells; pericentral and inner cortical cells in many species are
L199@P11: distinctive with wall thickenings. Gametophytes are dioecious; spermatangial organs are
L200@P11: developed from trichoblasts, forming discs with sterile-cell margins; procarps are borne
L201@P11: on lower cells of trichoblasts, with the supporting cell bearing a 4-celled carpogonial
L202@P11: branch and 2 sterile groups; cystocarps are lateral, ovoid to slightly urceolate;
L203@P11: tetrasporangia are formed in branchlets, occasionally in axillary clusters, cut off from
L204@P11: pericentral cells, subspherial and tetrahedrally divided (Hommersand 1963; Stegenga et al.
L205@P11: 1997).
L206@P11: The tribe Chondrieae is superficially similar to the tribe Laurencieae F. Schmitz
L207@P11-12: and molecular phylogenetic studies have supported the close relationship of these tribes 11
L208@P12: (Kurihara et al. 2010; Diaz-Tapia et al. 2017). However, the Chondrieae differs in the
L209@P12: number of pericentral cells (5 in Chondrieae, 2 or 4 in Laurencieae); tetrasporangia
L210@P12: borne on pericentral cells while in the Laurencieae, they borne inside the cortical cells;
L211@P12: and male gametophytes bear spermatangial plates which develope from trichoblasts in
L212@P12: the Chondrieae while in the Laurencieae, spermatangia are borne on modified
L213@P12: trichoblasts or filaments immersed in apical depressions (Hommersand 1963;
L214@P12: Womersley 2003; Diaz-Tapia et al. 2017). Since the introduction of molecular tools for
L215@P12: macroalgal systematics, some taxa of the Rhodomelaceae have been studied in attempts
L216@P12: to clarify relationships among genera, especially in the Laurencieae (Nam et al. 2000;
L217@P12: Nam 2006; Abe et al. 2006; Martin-Lescanne et al. 2010; Cassano et al. 2012; Metti et al.
L218@P12: 2015; Francis et al. 2017). In contrast, the Chondrieae has been almost neglected,
L219@P12: especially in terms of molecular and phylogenetic studies (Diaz-Tapia et al. 2017).
L220@P12: Therefore, the current classification of the Chondrieae is still based almost entirely on
L221@P12: morphological characters.
L222@P12: I focused on genus Chondria because althouh it has been recognized as the
L223@P12: largest genus of the tribe Chondrieae (Guiry & Guiry 2018), an intensive study on this
L224@P12: genus was missing especially molecular phylogenetic study. Molecular phylogenetic
L225@P12: analyses as well as morphological analyses in order to verify its current classification
L226@P12: were conducted using specimens mainly from Japan. The present thesis consists of three
L227@P12: chapters. In Chapter 1, the molecular phylogeny of the genus Chondria was investigated
L228@P12: using RuBisCO large subunit (rbcL), small subunit of nuclear ribosomal rRNA (SSU
L229@P12-13: rRNA) and mitochondrial cytochrome oxidase subunit 1 (cox1) gene sequences. 12
L230@P13: Morphological characters of each Chondria species were evaluated whether the
L231@P13: characters reflect their molecular phylogeny or not.
L232@P13: In Chapter 2, the Japanese ‗Chondria capillaris (Hudson) M.J. Wynne (=
L233@P13: Chondria tenuissima (Withering) C. Agardh)‘, was reexamined to revise its
L234@P13: classification. Molecular data based on rbcL, SSU rRNA and cox1 indicated that the
L235@P13: Japanese ‗Chondria capillaris‘ not only differs from the typical C. capillaris but also is
L236@P13: not closely related to any Chondria species and is even distinct from the tribe
L237@P13: Chondrieae. Furthermore, some morphological characters strengthened the dissimilarlity
L238@P13: between the Japanese ‗C. capillaris‘ and the typical C. capillaris, and also the separation
L239@P13: of the Japanese ‗C. capillaris‘ from the genus Chondria. Based on both molecular and
L240@P13: morphological results, the new genus Neochondria was established and Neochondria
L241@P13: ammophila sp. nov. was proposed to accommodate the Japanese ‗C. capillaris‘.
L242@P13: Morphology of pericentral cells and the presence of the adventitious cells between 5
L243@P13: pericentral cells were evaluated as the taxonomic characters to distinguish Neochondria
L244@P13: from Chondria.
L245@P13: In Chapter 3, three uncertain species regarded as ‗Chondria dasyphylla
L246@P13: (Woodward) C. Agardh‘ in Japan were investigated. Chondria dasyphylla (Japanese
L247@P13: name ‗Yanagi nori‘) has been reported from Japan since 1896. Okamura (1936) gave a
L248@P13: description of ‗C. dasyphylla‘ in Japan, which agrees well with other descriptions of the
L249@P13: typical C. dasyphylla. However, Okamura‘s description did not provide much details of
L250@P13: this taxon. My survey of specimens from the Herbarium SAP (Faculty of Science,
L251@P13: Hokkaido University) and my samples from Japan using molecular analyses suggested
L252@P13-14: three unidentified species in the specimens identified as C. dasyphylla. Further 13
L253@P14: investigations including comparison of these species with similar species were carried
L254@P14: out, and they were attributed to Chondria acuminata sp. nov., Chondria cf. curdieana
L255@P14-15: (Harvey ex J. Agardh) De Toni and Chondria sp. 1.14

L258@P15: INTRODUCTION
L259@P15: The genus Chondria C. Agardh nom. cons. was circumscribed by C. Agardh (1817)
L260@P15: based on Chondria tenuissima (Withering) C. Agardh (basionym Fucus tenuissimus
L261@P15: Goodenough & Woodward) (Wynne 1991), originally comprising 29 species. The genus
L262@P15: currently contains 80 species (Guiry & Guiry 2018) that can be found in various
L263@P15: habitats, attached to other algae, shells or rocks (Hollenberg 1945; Abbott & Hollenberg
L264@P15: 1976; Gordon-Mills 1987; Lee & Yoon 1996; Tani & Masuda 2003; Tani et al. 2003).
L265@P15: Chondria species were characterized by having young branches (branchlets) basally
L266@P15: constricted, apices of branches rounded or tapering, the subapical cells cutting off five
L267@P15: pericentral cells in alternating order and branched trichoblasts (Harvey 1853; Womersley
L268@P15: 2003). Five pericentral cells remain clear throughout the thallus. Pericentral cells (and
L269@P15: often the inner cortical cells) of most species develop various forms of wall thickenings
L270@P15: which can be the characteristics of the species (Gordon-Mills 1987).
L271@P15: Gametophytes of Chondria species are dioecious. In female gametophytes,
L272@P15: procarps are produced on a lower cell of trichoblasts, with the fifth-formed pericentral
L273@P15: (supporting) cell bearing a 4-celled carpogonial branch and lateral and basal sterile cell
L274@P15: groups (Womersley 2003). Carposporophytes have a basal fusion cell and branched
L275@P15: gominoblasts with clavate terminal carposporangia. Cystocarps are ovoid to slightly
L276@P15-16: urceolate cystocarps, in some species with a basal spur developed from trichoblast cells 15
L277@P16: above the supporting cell. Male gametophytes form spermatangial plates which are flat,
L278@P16: discoid or slightly lobed, developed from the whole or a basal branch of trichoblast, with
L279@P16: a sterile margin of 1–3 cell broad. Tetrasporophytes produce tetrasporangia which are
L280@P16: developed on pericentral cells of lesser branchlets (Hommersand 1963; Gordon-Mills
L281@P16: 1987; Lee & Yoon 1996; Tani & Masuda 2003; Womersley 2003).
L282@P16: Most significant characters for classification of Chondria species are vegetative
L283@P16: forms. Falkenberg (1901) divided Chondria into three subgenera based on their thallus
L284@P16: forms. The subgenus Euchondria Falkenberg was characterized by a cylindrical thallus
L285@P16: with acute apices such as the ones found in Chondria capillaris (Hudson) M.J. Wynne
L286@P16: (as Chondria tenuissima (Withering) C. Agardh) while the subgenus Coelochondria
L287@P16: Falkenberg was defined by a cylindrical thallus but with obtuse apices as observed in
L288@P16: Chondria dasyphylla (Woodward) C. Agardh (Weber-van Bosse 1923), and the last
L289@P16: subgenus, Platychondria Falkenberg, was established for the species which have a
L290@P16: flattened thallus such as Chondria viticulosa A.J.K. Millar & M.J. Wynne (Millar &
L291@P16: Wynne 1992). However, this system was not accepted by many researchers and was not
L292@P16: adopted in recent works (Gordon-Mills 1987; Lee & Yoon 1996; Tani el al. 2003).
L293@P16: The other vegetative characters that have been adopted for classification of
L294@P16: Chondria species are habit, size and color of thalli when alive, branching pattern, the
L295@P16: shape of superficial epidermal cells and the presence (or absence) and shape of lenticular
L296@P16: cell wall thickenings (Setchell & Gardner 1924; Yamada 1935; Hollenberg 1945;
L297@P16: Dawson 1963; Gordon-Mills 1987; Lee & Yoon 1996; Tani el al. 2003; Bacci 2005).
L298@P16: Some gametophytic characters, such as the number and shape of sterile cell rows
L299@P16-17: surrounding a spermatangial plate and the presence or absence of a cystocarpic spur, 16
L300@P17: have been used for species identification (Dawson & Tözün 1964; Gordon-Mills 1987;
L301@P17: Millar & Wynne 1992; Maggs & Hommersand 1993; Lee & Yoon 1996; Tani & Masuda
L302@P17: 2003; Womersley 2003). However, species identification in the genus Chondria is
L303@P17: sometimes difficult due to overlapping characters and considerable intraspecific
L304@P17: variation (Bacci 2005).
