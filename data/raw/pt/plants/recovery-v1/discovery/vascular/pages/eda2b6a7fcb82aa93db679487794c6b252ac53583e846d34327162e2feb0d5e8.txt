L32@P1: SPECIES DISTRIBUTION LISTINGS
L33@P1: Species Distribution Listings are being developed to encourage and promote the conservation of local native animals and plants.
L34@P1: The listings are developed for legally defined geographic areas, and larger bodies of water. The listings include species reported
L35@P1: as having been observed in or reported from the described area. Exotic and non-local landscaped plants are not included in the
L36@P1: listings unless they have become naturalized into the surrounding native environment. Neither “Man” nor our domesticated
L37@P1: animals, except for feral animals, have been included in the listings of species; however, they have had an impact on all natural
L38@P1: areas, the future degree of this impact must be managed in order to restore and provide for the continuation of the natural
L39@P1: interrelationships between all species.
L40@P1: Due to the continuing addition of species, the listings should be considered works in progress. In the listings, and most often in
L41@P1: the listing of animals, species may have been included based on general distribution mapping and/or statements and not on an
L42@P1: observation made in a specific location. It is recommended that we consider a species “confirmed” as occurring in a township or
L43@P1: general listing area only after we have at least three recorded sightings cited in the footnotes with no more than one of those
L44@P1: records being based on general distribution mapping for the species. Note that the Southwest Environmental Information
L45@P1: Network (SEINet) *85* may have several collections recorded for a species within any given township or listing area, and that
L46@P1: the date shown in parentheses is a date of the search of their records and not a date of recorded sighting. Note also that many of
L47@P1: the individual species collection records found in SEINet include additional associated species. For assistance with the
L48@P1: identification of a plant, contact the University of Arizona Herbarium (520-621-7243; FAX: 520-621-7186; P.O. Box 210036
L49@P1: Herring Hall, 1130 East South Campus Drive, Tucson, Arizona 85721).
L50@P1: The species are presented alphabetically by division, class, family and genus within their kingdoms. Unlike the usage by most
L51@P1: authors, all common names have been capitalized, to normalize simply return uppercase letters, except for those used in proper
L52@P1: names, to lowercase letters. Common names presented in languages other than English may lack the inclusion of certain
L53@P1: characters/phonetic symbols because of an inability to be reproduce them. The vernacular names included by Daniel F. Austin in
L54@P1: his book Baboquivari Mountain Plants: Identification, Ecology, and Ethnobotany are noted “
L55@P1: 140” with synonyms (dialectic
L56@P1: variants or alternate transcriptions) printed in angle brackets <> or variations printed in brackets []. An attempt has been made to
L57@P1: identify the range in mature heights reported for the plants. Whenever possible the flowering period is reported to early month
L58@P1: (1st-10th), mid-month (11th-20th) and late month (21st-end of the month). The individual species records include a general
L59@P1: description of the habitat which is provided to help visualize the types of natural habitats the species may be found in. These
L60@P1: descriptions have been developed, in part, from herbarium records and general descriptions of habitat found in literature, and
L61@P1-2: should not be considered limiting as to the type of habitat that a plant might occupy. The terms “streambed”, “creekbed”, 3
L62@P2: “riverbed” or “lakebed” refer to their dry aspects. Plants reported as occurring in recently burned areas were observed in that area
L63@P2: within one year following a fire. The range in elevation has been rounded off to the nearest 100 feet up for the higher elevation,
L64@P2: or down for the lower elevation. Species reported as being within 0 to 100 feet are recorded as occurring “from sea level”. The
L65@P2: reporting of the ecological formations generally follows the mapping presented in the “Biotic Communities of the Southwest” by
L66@P2: David E. Brown and Charles H. Lowe, August 1980, with the exception of the “wetlands” which are being reported as an
L67@P2: ecological formation in the listings; footnotes: Species not considered to be native to Arizona are shown as being EXOTIC,
L68@P2: printed in red. Exotic plants are not recommended for use in landscaping or restoration projects. Plants that may be an attractive
L69@P2: component of a restored native habitat are so noted. Plants reported as having been used by native peoples of North America and
L70@P2: which might be investigated to determine their value as a home garden or commercial crop may be so noted, much of this
L71@P2: information is based on the records of the Native American Ethnobotany website [University of Michigan - Dearborn], footnote
L72@P2: *127*. When describing the “native range” of plants in North America northwestern refers to Alaska, northern refers to northern
L73@P2: Canada (the Yukon Territory, Northwest Territories and Nunavut), northeastern refers to Greenland, central refers to southern
L74@P2: Canada (north-central: British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, Newfoundland and Labrador, New
L75@P2: Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island) and the United States (south-central), and southern refers to Mexico, below
L76@P2: which is Central America and South America. In the footnotes, the source(s) used for the inclusion of the species in the listing is
L77@P2: printed in either green *00* (indicating that the entry is based on an actual sighting) or blue *00* (indicating that the entry is
L78@P2: based on a general distribution description and/or mapping). Plants listed in the book “Livestock-Poisoning Plants of Arizona” by
L79@P2: Ervin M. Schmutz, Barry M. Freeman and Raymond E. Reed and published in 1968 (80) as being either “Major Poisonous Range
L80@P2: Plants” or “Secondary Poisonous Range Plants” are further identified by their listing heading being printed in red in the
L81@P2: footnotes; plants considered to be “Rarely Poisonous and Suspected Poisonous Range Plants” and “Poisonous Cropland and
L82@P2: Garden Plants” have also been noted.
L83@P2: Local native plants are recommended for use in landscape and restoration projects. Once established many native species require
L84@P2: little, if any, irrigation. The inclusion of a plant in the township listing does not necessarily mean that the plant is suitable for the
L85@P2: site in which you want to plant it. Ideally restoration should include those species of plants that were native to the property. The
L86@P2: source material, of plants and seed, used in the project should be as local as possible. In order to determine what plants were
L87@P2: native try to locate photographs of the area prior to clearing or look for natural areas and remnant populations and plants adjacent
L88@P2: to where the restoration is to take place. Plants should be planted in their approximate original habitat and density and taking into
L89@P2: consideration the original local native site and elevation of occurrence.
L90@P2: The use of local native plants in landscape and restoration projects encourages native animals to remain in the area and helps us
L91@P2: to retain the area’s natural beauty and unique identity and heritage.

L146@P3: TOWNSHIP NOTES
L147@P3: LOCATION: This township is located in northeastern Pima County in south-central Arizona. This township is bounded on the
L148@P3: north by the alignment for Ina Road, on the south by the alignment for Grant Road and on the west by the alignment for First
L149@P3: Avenue. A portion of the City of Tucson is located within the southwestern half of this township.
L150@P3: Historic Neighborhoods: The Historic Community of Binghampton is located in this township.
L151@P3: LANDMARKS: A southwestern portion of the Santa Catalina Mountains is located within this township. Named washes and
L152@P3: creeks include the Arcadia Wash, Geronimo Wash, Pantano Wash, Pima Wash (crosses the northwest corner) and Tanque Verde
L153@P3: Creek.
L154@P3: ELEVATION: Elevations range from approximately 2,315 feet in the Rillito Creek on the west township line to approximately
L155@P3: 3,828 feet at the top of an unnamed peak located just south of the north township line about one and one-half miles west of the
L156@P3-4: northeast corner (1). 5
L157@P4: PHYSIOGRAPHIC PROVINCE: This township is located within the Sonoran Desert Section of the Basin and Range
L158@P4: Physiographic Province (2).
L159@P4: SOILS: Soils have been described as being Thermic (hot) Arid Soils (soils with mean annual temperatures of 59 degrees to 72
L160@P4: degrees Fahrenheit (15 degrees to 22 degrees Centigrade) and 5 to 10 inches (13 to 25 cm) mean annual precipitation) and/or
L161@P4: Thermic (hot) Semiarid Soils (soils with mean annual temperatures of 59 degrees to 72 degrees Fahrenheit (15 degrees to 22
L162@P4: degrees Centigrade) and 10 to 16 inches (25 to 41 cm) mean annual precipitation) of the Grabe-Gila-Pima Association (deep soils
L163@P4: of the floodplains), Mohave-Tres Hermanos-Anway Association (deep, arid soils on the valley plains), Pinaleno-Nickel-Palos
L164@P4: Verdes Association (deep, arid, gravelly soils on deeply dissected uplands), Rillino-Latene-Cave Association (deep to very
L165@P4: shallow, arid calcareous soils on uplands) and the Rock Outcrop-Lampshire-Cellar Association (rock outcrop and very shallow
L166@P4: and shallow semiarid soils of the mountains and foothills) (3).
L167@P4: BIOTIC COMMUNITY: This township is located within the Arizona Upland Subdivision of the Sonoran Desertscrub Regional
L168@P4: Formation of the Desertscrub Formation and associated Wetlands (4).
