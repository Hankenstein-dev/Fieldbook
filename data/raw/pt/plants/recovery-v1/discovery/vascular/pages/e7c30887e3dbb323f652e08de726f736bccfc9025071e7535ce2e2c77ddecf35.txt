L4@P0: Introduction
L5@P0: Vitis L. (Vitaceae) is a genus mainly distributed in the
L6@P0: Northern Hemisphere, which includes approximately 60
L7@P0: species with major centers of diversity in China and North
L8@P0: America (Ren & Wen, 2007). It contains woody lianas,
L9@P0: with leaves alternate, simple, often lobed, sometimes
L10@P0: palmately compound, opposite to tendrils, inflorescence
L11@P0: a thyrse composed by flowers 5-merous. Vitis vinifera
L12@P0: L. (grapevine) is one of the world’s most important fruit
L13@P0: crops, and its cultivation has led to a significant increase
L14@P0: in genetic diversity via sexual reproduction. In Europe, the
L15@P0: interspecific hybridization of Vitis which took place mainly
L16@P0: between the late 19th and the mid-20th centuries to address
L17@P0: the phylloxera crisis (Daktulosphaira vitifoliae [Fitch,
L18@P0: 1855], Phylloxeridae, accidentally introduced from North
L19@P0: America to France in the second half of the 19th century
L20@P0: with plant material), resulted in the creation of hybrids and
L21@P0: rootstocks, ultimately contributing to an increase in the
L22@P0: diversity of plant material. Recently, some of these hybrids
L23@P0: have been formally described for Italy (Ardenghi et al., 2014,
L24@P0: 2015a, b) and Spain (Vázquez & García, 2017) according
L25@P0: to the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi,
L26@P0: and plants (Turland et al., 2018). Currently, four species (V.
L27@P0: labrusca L., V. riparia Michx., V. rupestris Scheele, and V.
L28@P0: vinifera L.) and seven nothospecies (V. ×bacoi Ardenghi,
L29@P0: Galasso & Banfi, V. ×gallica F.M.Vázquez, V. ×goliath
L30@P0: Ardenghi, Galasso & Banfi, V. ×instabilis Ardenghi, Galasso,

L43@P0: Abstract – In the present paper, based on field, herbarium and bibliographic research, an update concerning the genus Vitis in Campania is presented.
L44@P0: Five taxa, including three species and two nothospecies, are recorded. Vitis labrusca (casual), Vitis riparia (casual), Vitis ×instabilis (casual) and Vitis
L45@P0: ×koberi (naturalized) are reported for the first time in this region. The agricultural activities and artificial green areas are confirmed as starting points
L46@P0: for the process of invasion of alien plants in southern Italy.
L47@P0: Keywords: Alien species; Floristic records; Grapevines; Nothotaxa; Vascular flora

L50@P1: Italian vascular flora (Galasso et al., 2018). However, for
L51@P1: some areas little is known about their distribution or their
L52@P1: invasiveness.
L53@P1: Although the grapevines has been cultivated in Campania
L54@P1: since the 4th century BC by the Etruscans with the techniques
L55@P1: named “piantata” and “alberata” (Buono & Vallariello, 2003),

L57@P1: appears sporadic. In fact, in this region only V. vinifera was
L58@P1: reported (Bartolucci et al., 2018; Galasso et al., 2018).
L59@P1: The aim of this paper is to update the geographical
L60@P1: distribution of the taxa within the genus Vitis occurring in
L61@P1: Campania, reporting for the first time the presence of alien
L62@P1: units and their invasion status, and thereby contributing
L63@P1: to the knowledge of this still under-investigated genus of
L64@P1: vascular plants.

L203@P3: Conclusions
L204@P3: The genus Vitis in Campania currently comprises three
L205@P3: species and two nothospecies. Four of these units, all nonnative, were first discovered in the region during the present
L206@P3: study. As shown in Fig. 1A, Vitis ×koberi is the most frequent
L207@P3: alien taxon and the only one regarded here as naturalized.
L208@P3: As in other Mediterranean regions (such as Sicily: Ardenghi
L209@P3: & Cauzzi, 2015), this may be related to the greater drought
L210@P3: resistance and limestone-based soils of this hybrid which has
L211@P3: thus been most widely cultivated in the study area. Although
L212@P3: Vitis vinifera is considered a native plant in Campania, all its
L213@P3: recent discoveries and all other taxa examined have escaped
L214@P3: from cultivation. Therefore, agricultural activities and
L215@P3: artificial green areas are confirmed as starting points for the
L216@P3: process of invasion of alien plants in southern Italy (Stinca et
L217@P3: al., 2017; Motti et al., 2018). However, further investigations
L218@P3: are indispensable to define the distribution range of these
L219@P3: taxa and their invasiveness in Campania.
