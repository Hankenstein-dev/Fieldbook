L134@P5-6: ivINTRODUCTION
L135@P6: The old world and largely sub-Saharan genus Moraea
L136@P6: Mill. (Iridaceae: Iridoideae) comprises some 200 species
L137@P6: of cormous geophytes. Although florally diverse, Moraea
L138@P6: is recognized in Iridoideae by a bifacial, channelled (rarely
L139@P6: terete) leaf blade and corms of a single internode derived
L140@P6: from a lateral bud. Most species have iris-like flowers with
L141@P6: flattened, petaloid style branches, with filaments at least
L142@P6: partially united. Other floral types make exact definition
L143@P6: of the genus difficult. Moraea is most diverse in southern
L144@P6: Africa and has a marked concentration of species in the
L145@P6: winter rainfall region of western South Africa and adjacent southwestern Namibia, the likely area of origin of
L146@P6: the genus (Goldblatt et al. 2002). Here we describe three
L147@P6: new species. Moraea pearsonii from Hottentotskloof,
L148@P6: near Karoopoort in the Ceres District, was re-collected in
L149@P6: 2007 for the first time since its discovery 99 years ago,
L150@P6: when H.H.W. Pearson gathered fragmentary material in
L151@P6: November 1908. A late flowering species, it blooms in
L152@P6: November and late December, and the small, stellate flowers have unusual, filiform style branches each divided to
L153@P6: the base. This style conformation appears to have evolved
L154@P6: independently four times in the genus. Moraea tanquana
L155@P6: from the Tankwa National Park, is allied to M. deserticola
L156@P6: Goldblatt from southern Namaqualand and M. speciosa
L157@P6: (L.Bolus) Goldblatt from the Western Karoo, and shares
L158@P6: with these species, cup-like, blue to mauve flowers with
L159@P6: subequal tepals. Although superficially resembling M.
L160@P6: deserticola, it differs from that species in the short anthers
L161@P6: exserted from the floral cup and short style dividing opposite the anther bases so that the style branches emerge
L162@P6: between the anthers. In section Acaules, M. longipes from
L163@P6: Namaqualand stands out in its early flowering, aerial stem
L164@P6: consisting of a single long internode reaching well above
L165@P6: the ground, short style and unusually long anthers. Of
L166@P6: these new species all but M. longipes are currently known
L167@P6: from a single locality but we suspect that collecting nearby
L168@P6: at the appropriate times of the year, will show them all to
L169@P6: have wider ranges.
L170@P6: We take this opportunity to reduce Moraea jarmilae
L171@P6: J.J.Halda, described in 2002 from Ox Bow in Lesotho,
L172@P6: to synonymy in M. albicuspa Goldblatt, and to report
L173@P6: significant range extensions for several southern African
L174@P6: species. Populations of the southwestern Cape species M.
L175@P6: elsiae Goldblatt and M. tricolor Andrews have been discovered in the vicinity of Stilbaai in the southern Cape,
L176@P6: range extensions of 100 km or more from their next
L177@P6: nearest stations. The Stilbaai populations of M. tricolor
L178@P6: also exhibit a shift in flowering time and habitat, unprecedented in Moraea. The widespread eastern southern
L179@P6: African M. spathulata (L.f.) Klatt is now known from
L180@P6: the higher mountains of the Great Karoo, a surprising
L181@P6: record for a plant of otherwise mesic coastal and montane grassland habitats. A population that we refer to M.
L182@P6: vespertina Goldblatt & J.C.Manning, a species until now
L183@P6: known from two localities on the Bokkeveld Plateau
L184@P6: west of Calvinia, has been discovered in the Tankwa
L185@P6: National Park, some 200 km to the south. Although differing in some respects from the Bokkeveld populations,
L186@P6: notably their slightly larger flowers and drier habitat, the
L187@P6: Tankwa plants seem best included here for the moment.

L199@P6: ABSTRACT
L200@P6: Three new species are described in the largely sub-Saharan genus Moraea Mill. (± 200 spp.), all from its centre of diversity in the winter rainfall region of southern Africa. Moraea pearsonii, from Hottentotskloof near Ceres in Western Cape,
L201@P6: flowers in late November and December when its leaves are ± dry, and has small, pale lilac, stellate flowers with the style
L202@P6: branches each divided to the base into filiform arms. Moraea tanquana, from the Tankwa River Basin in Northern Cape,
L203@P6: resembles the southern Namaqualand M. deserticola but has broad, plane leaves, short anthers exserted from a shallower
L204@P6: floral cup and a short style. In section Acaules, M. longipes from Namaqualand stands out in its early flowering habit, a stem
L205@P6: consisting of a single long internode reaching well above the ground, short style and unusually long anthers. Moraea jarmilae
L206@P6: described from Ox Bow, Lesotho in 2002, is conspecific with M. albicuspa and is reduced to synonymy. Significant range
L207@P6: extensions are reported for M. elsiae, M. falcifolia, M. pseudospicata, M. spathulata, M. tricolor, M. vegeta, M. verecunda, M.
L208@P6: vespertina and M. vlokii. A yellow-flowered morph, local in the Perdebont Valley of the Little Karoo, is reported for the first
L209@P6: time in typically blue- to violet-flowered M. bipartita, as well as the occurrence of a hybrid swarm, rare in Moraea, between
L210@P6: M. bipartita and M. polyanthos.
