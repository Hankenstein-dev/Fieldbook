L11@P0: Summary. An updated synonymized checklist of the Compositae of Bolivia is provided. The checklist is based
L12@P0: on the only catalogue of the Bolivian flora, that of Foster dating from 1958, a literature survey, together with
L13@P0: additional herbarium work whilst naming many recent J. R. I. Wood collections; it does not represent a
L14@P0: complete herbarium survey. Currently, 13 tribes are represented by 1071 species in 227 genera with an
L15@P0: estimated 302 endemic species, which is equivalent to 28.2% endemism. Keys to tribes, and genera of the
L16@P0: tribes, are provided, with keys to taxa within some genera.

L48@P0-1: References ..................................................................................................................................................................... 631INTRODUCTION
L49@P1: Floristic works vary from to the basic species list to comprehensive research floras. Davis & Heywood (1963)
L50@P1: suggested that the species list served a ‗very limited purpose‘ except for a poorly known Flora, and
L51@P1: highlighted several drawbacks. During the last 15 years great steps have been made in catalogueing the
L52@P1: Floras of several South American countries. Checklists have been compiled by Zuloaga & Morone (1999)
L53@P1: (Argentina), Jørgensen & León-Yánez (1999) (Ecuador), Boggan et al. (1996) (Guianas – Guyana, Surinam and
L54@P1: French Guiana), Brako & Zarruchi (1993)(Peru), allowing a useful comparison between Floras, highlighting
L55@P1: where future work is needed, and paving the way to fuller floristic treatments. Indeed, floras of each of the
L56@P1: countries mentioned is either complete or underway, sometimes falteringly, rarely approaching the ‗research
L57@P1: floras‘ considered by Davis & Heywood as being the most comprehensive, one step below the monograph.
L58@P1: Amongst the Andean countries, Bolivia clearly stands out as having a relatively poorly known
L59@P1: phanerogamic flora and not having a current checklist. The last complete listing of the ferns and flowering
L60@P1: plants of Bolivia was that of Foster‘s ‗Catalogue ...‘ (Foster, 1958). Foster‘s hope had been to produce a ‗Flora
L61@P1: of Bolivia‘ but the realization that it would not be completed in his lifetime prompted him to publish the
L62@P1: ‗Catalogue’. The dicotyledons totalled 1252 genera and the largest family of flowering plants was the
L63@P1: Compositae with approximately 900 species (represented by 970 taxa) in 125 genera, in 12 tribes. Clearly, a
L64@P1: modern checklist of the family, as a precursor to a flora is highly desirable. To this end the present
L65@P1: ‗Preliminary Checklist of the Compositae of Bolivia‘ was embarked on.
L66@P1: Fortuitously, the Compositae have been treated reasonably well in the literature. In their bibliography Funk
L67@P1: & Mori (1989) have summarized the state of published information on collectors of Bolivian plants to that
L68@P1: date. Some (few) collectors were highlighted in their attempts to begin regional floras that included Bolivia,
L69@P1: such as Weddell‘s Chloris Andina, whose first volume (1855–57) treated the family, Fries (1906) treated the
L70@P1: Compositae amongst his own collections from Bolivia, Cabrera (1952) and Koster (1945, 1947) treated many
L71@P1: of the Compositae amongst Herzog‘s collecting trips, and Buchtien (1910) catalogued his own collections.
L72@P1: Revisions and accounts of Bolivian plants include some by B. L. Robinson (1920, 1932 – Stevia, 1922 – Mikania,
L73@P1: 1932 – Eupatorium s.l.), Gleason (1923 – Vernonia), Cabrera (1985 – Senecio) and Müller (2006 – Baccharis).
L74@P1: There are of course many mongraphs of groups that include Bolivian species, these too numerous to mention
L75@P1: here.
L76@P1: Coverage
L77@P1: The geographical coverage of the present checklist is the present delimitation of Bolivia as shown in ‗The
L78@P1: Times ... Atlas‘ (Times Books, 2007).
L79@P1: Material
L80@P1: This ‗Preliminary Checklist ...‘ is primarily a literature based list. The primary list was based on Foster‘s
L81@P1: Catalogue (Foster 1958). Names cited by Foster are marked with an asterisk (‗*‘). For those where there were
L82@P1: clearly mis-identifications, explanations are provided as to the possible reasons, perhaps because of
L83@P1: problems concerning critical taxa, or noting upon which accounts, or material, this was based. In some cases
L84@P1: subsequent reassessment has shown that the names are inapplicable to Bolivian material. This was then
L85@P1: augmented using monographs and revisions of taxa whose range included Bolivia, neighbouring floras and
L86@P1: checklists, original literature providing descriptions of taxa described from Bolivia, and assessments of a
L87@P1: number of accounts of collectors‘ itineraries and determinations of their collections (e.g. Bang, Buchtien,
L88@P1: Fiebrig, R. E. Fries, Herzog, E. W. D. & M. M. Holway, Mandon, Perkins, Rusby, Steubel, Weddell, R. S.
L89@P1: Williams). In addition the few available tribal revisions (e.g. Eupatorieae, Liabeae, Vernonieae), with their
L90@P1: accompanying nomenclators, were abstracted these providing confirmation of the presence of many taxa.
L91@P1: Also used were a number of papers (e.g. Blake, 1930; Cabrera, 1959a, b, 1960, 1973; Freire & Iharlegui, 2000;
L92@P1: Pizarro, 1960; Pruski, 1998) that provided information on historical collections, types, and a proposed
L93@P1: synonymy of several names with relevant new combinations. At all stages a fully cross-referenced list was
L94@P1: built up including as complete a synonymy of the accepted names as possible. The resultant synonymy
L95@P1: representing a global synonymy with the intention that this list will provide the basis for a ‗research flora‘
L96@P1: (sensu Davis & Heywood, 1963: 296); it is clearly incomplete in some instances as the status of many
L97@P1: infraspecific names needs to be checked thoroughly. With this aim in mind full type citations were added for
L98@P1-2: all names appearing in the list. To the accumulating list was added a number of names based on 3
L99@P2: determination of J. R. I. Wood et al.‘s many hundreds of Compositae collections made over the last 15 years
L100@P2: throughout the country; this has added a number of new generic and species records for Bolivia;
L101@P2: representative material is cited where relevant. The format of the Preliminary Checklist is outlined in detail
L102@P2: below. A limited check, largely because of imposed time constraints, has been made on the presence of
L103@P2: additional type material present in the Herbarium of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew. At the initial stage no
L104@P2: concerted attempt was made to add authenticated material at the end of each accepted species entry;
L105@P2: however, some cited material has been added during the second phase of the project when an ongoing
L106@P2: assessment of herbarium material was started. Although there are still a significant number of poorly known
L107@P2: taxa, ill-represented in herbaria, an attempt has been made to provide geographical, ecological and
L108@P2: phytosociological information, together with altitudinal ranges and flowering time, for many taxa. This is an
L109@P2: ongoing process and will take some time to complete.
L110@P2: The format of the present Checklist
L111@P2: The following synonymic checklist is in alphabetical order, with subbordinate taxa in the ranking order
L112@P2: indicated by the most recent International Code of Botanical Nomenclature (Vienna Code)[= Code], 2006
L113@P2: (McNeill et al. 2006), and taxa at the same rank in strict alphabetical order. Unranked taxa are placed
L114@P2: alphabetically under the most appropriate next highest level. Accepted taxa at all ranks are given in
L115@P2: emboldened text, synonymic entries are in italics, and valid names of taxa not present in Bolivia are in
L116@P2: ordinary Roman text, as are taxa whose presence in Bolivia is in some doubt. Tribal, generic and species
L117@P2: concepts are those currently accepted by the author (q.v. Hind, 2007 – tribal, and generic concepts used in
L118@P2: my new generic arrangement of the Compositae in K). The original spelling of names is indicated, or
L119@P2: corrected, within the provisions of the most recent Code. For each accepted generic name author/s citation
L120@P2: (following Brummitt & Powell, 1992), publication details (following Botanico Periodicum Huntianum, its
L121@P2: supplement and second edition for journals and Taxonomic Literature, ed. 2 – TL2 – for books) and publication
L122@P2: date (following the available information, with appropriate notes if these differ from those usually provided,
L123@P2: and pre-print dates, or actual dates, are provided in square brackets). Author/s citations have been checked,
L124@P2: and in many cases corrected, having examined all but a very rare minority of the source publications; any
L125@P2: publication not examined by the author is clearly marked. Publication details are expanded to give issue,
L126@P2: part or number details for journals by way of completeness and accuracy, regardless of whether pagination
L127@P2: is continuous between issues, parts or numbers. Generic synonymy is provided in chronological order for
L128@P2: each genus as far as is known, each also provided with publication details and type citations;
L129@P2: lectotypification details are provided whenever possible. Generitypes for the accepted generic names are
L130@P2: cited and lectotypification details are also provide whenever possible. Generitypes and types of synonymic
L131@P2: genera present in the Bolivian flora are emboldened. A compilation of relevant literature is provided for
L132@P2: each genus, including revisions as well as relevant neighbouring flora accounts, and critical taxonomic or
L133@P2: nomenclatural papers. In many cases a key to species has been provided, although in some genera this is still
L134@P2: required and will be added in future editions or on the web version of this Checklist. For each accepted
L135@P2: species author/s citation, publication details and ‗type‘ information is provided as well as for all relevant
L136@P2: synonyms, the synonyms appearing in chronological order. It is quite probable that many infraspecific
L137@P2: synonyms need to be added at some future date, but only when relevant protologues and type material have
L138@P2: been examined. The type information is provided in the classical sense – the protologue citation within
L139@P2: single quotes, even when this is strictly just ‗habitat‘ information, with additions from the type material, or
L140@P2: the complete specimen label information, in square brackets ‗[...]‘. No attempt has been made to translate
L141@P2: text, units, or modify dates to adopt to a ‗journal style‘; the style used should allow direct comparison with
L142@P2: type material when relevant. Rarely, in some protologues, information was originally provided within
L143@P2: square brackets – this is left in square brackets, and often noted as such. Collectors‘ names are as provided in
L144@P2: the protologues except that those provided in Roman text are italicized for easier recognition, and those
L145@P2: abbreviated (or contracted) are often exanded (in the same type face) within square brackets. The
L146@P2: protologues, recent revisions, and Index Herbariorum – List of Collectors, were sources of this information as
L147@P2: well as several virtual herbaria. When relevant, additional notes have been provided concerning cited
L148@P2: material, or its location. There are several instances where, in the case of citation of material by Rusby, for
L149@P2: example, that a single collection was not cited and the wording of the protologue clearly indicates another
L150@P2: collection was considered at the same time. This has often been misinterpreted, although the location of
L151@P2: some material is unclear. A historical perspective is required, and Rusby‘s introductory paper to his
L152@P2: coverage of the Bang collections (Rusby, 1893) explains where some of the material is to be found. Britton
L153@P2-3: visited the Herbarium of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew during a European trip and much material was seen, explaining the citation of much Bridges or Pearce material etc. in the protologues of Rusby‘s plants.
L154@P3: Clearly, very many more lectotypifications are needed, but this is certainly not something to embark on
L155@P3: without conisderable extra research. In cases where it is clear no holotype was stated, rather than add the
L156@P3: location of known duplicate type material (which might lead to erroneous lectotypification), the location of
L157@P3: material is sometimes withheld, e.g. this is frequently the case with George Gardner‘s Brazilian collections.
L158@P3: This situation is also true of the location of type material, if extant, of many eighteenth and early nineteenth
L159@P3: century protologues. It should also be remembered that in several instances the original material used by
L160@P3: botanists to write their protologues remained unmounted for many decades. When eventually mounted,
L161@P3: curators were often never of a mind to mark sheets ‗1 of 2‘, etc., and duplication of labels was often poor. The
L162@P3: result has often been that some names are represented by two or more sheets in the botanist‘s host
L163@P3: herbarium and these sheets have been given sequential, or quite disparate, accession numbers, without
L164@P3: recognizing that they were ‗original material‘ or even holotypes. Care will have to be taken in assessing these
L165@P3: collections – e.g. those of R. A. Philippi. Species distribution is provided by country (in alphabetical order),
L166@P3: sometimes in a general manner, and within Bolivia is given on a Department by Department basis (in
L167@P3: alphabetical order). Since some protologue citations provided minimal locality information, the original
L168@P3: specimens were void of such information, or information given cannot be located on maps available,
L169@P3: departmental detail is provided as ‗Bolivia (?)‘. The dubious occurrence of taxa in Bolivia is usually noted.
L170@P3: Whilst admittedly far from complete, details on habitat/ecology, altitude (or elevation if preferred) and
L171@P3: flowering time have been added for many taxa. Additional notes are also provided where problems have
L172@P3: been discovered with literature citations, type citations, typifications, etc. Vernacular, or common, names
L173@P3: have been provided for some taxa. In many instances the location of type material is provided, where
L174@P3: known, although this is an ongoing compilation and is certainly far from complete. It is clearl that a search
L175@P3: through many virtual herbaria has revealed that the digitization process is very much on-going as many
L176@P3: types known to be in certain institutions do not appear on their digital catalogues, yet. As the Latin American
L177@P3: Plant Initiative (LAPI) has been running parallel to the completion of this list the addition of barcode
L178@P3: numbers for type material at K has been omitted until the project is complete.
L179@P3: All synonyms are indexed separately, provided with a full publication reference, and equated with their
L180@P3: currently accepted name and its authority. Should any reader have a differing taxonomic view it should be
L181@P3: straightforward to find the relevant synonymy for the taxa concerned.
L182@P3: Three tables are provided. Table 1 shows the distribution of genera within tribes (in alphabetical order) and
L183@P3: the numbers of genera per tribe, number of species per genus, number of endemic species per genus, and
L184@P3: totals of genera, species and endemic species, together with the percentage of endemic species. Table 2
L185@P3: shows the tribes in descending order of size based on the number of species (with the number of genera in
L186@P3: brackets). Table 3 shows the ten largest genera (their tribal placement) and the number of species (with the
L187@P3: number of endemic species in brackets).
L188@P3: Although much relevant literature is provided after each genus there is a wealth of additional literature that
L189@P3: has been used in producing this checklist, such as works on collectors‘ itineraries, more general accounts, etc.
L190@P3: Full bibliographic references to many of these whole papers are provided at the end, together with
L191@P3: bibliographic information not normally met with. Some of this information clarifies, or sometimes corrects,
L192@P3: citations often incorrectly provided in the literature, especially in the case of preprints.
L193@P3: Summary of the Preliminary Checklist – Version 2
L194@P3: In this first phase an estimated total of c. 1071 species of 227 genera in 13 tribes of the Compositae was
L195@P3: recorded. An estimated total of 302 endemic species indicates 28.2% endemism; there are no genera endemic
L196@P3: to Bolivia. There are a number of dubious records from Foster‘s Catalogue which remain to be reconfirmed. It
L197@P3: is also clear that a number of nomina nuda provided by Schultz Bipontinus, based on Mandon‘s collections
L198@P3: need to be reassessed and their current identity ascertained. A separate list of Mandon‘s collections, and their
L199@P3: current determinations, will be published in due course.
L200@P3: Emendments and amendments will be made available to the International Plant Names Index as all aspects of
L201@P3: the publication of the names involved have been checked against the Code, for their validity and legitimacy.
L202@P3-4: The results will eventually be fed into the Global Compositae Checklist and iPlants – the World’s Plants Online5
