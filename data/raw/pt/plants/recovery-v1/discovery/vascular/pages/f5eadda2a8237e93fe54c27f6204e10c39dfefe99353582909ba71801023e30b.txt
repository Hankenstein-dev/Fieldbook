L7@P0: Abstract
L8@P0: Chamaedorea delicata (Arecaceae: Arecoideae: Chamdoreeae), a new species from cultivation
L9@P0: at the San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance, San Diego, California, U. S. A., is named, described,
L10@P0: illustrated, discussed, and compared to related species. The new species is a clustered,
L11@P0: dioecious palm, forming open clumps to 4 m tall and wide of several to numerous, very slender,
L12@P0: erect to leaning, bamboo-like stems each topped with a few, small, long-pinnate leaves,
L13@P0: lending a dainty or delicate nature to the plant.
L14@P0: Introduction
L15@P0: In March, 2025 co-author Hodel visited the San Diego Zoo and Wildlife Alliance (The Zoo) to assist
L16@P0: staff with identifying their holdings of Chamaedorea (Arecaceae: Arecoideae: Chamdoreeae) as
L17@P0: part of their efforts to inventory its palms. During the course of their work in the Fern Canyon
L18@P0: area of The Zoo, they encountered one taxon composed of five plants that Hodel was unable to
L19@P0: place with a known species. We made several collections from three of the plants at that March
L20@P0: visit and then additional collections of other plants at subsequent visits in June, 2025. With the
L21@P0: material at hand, Hodel wrote up a description of the taxon and compared it to related species,
L22@P0: culminating in the decision that the taxon in question was a species new to science. Here this
L23@P0: new species is named, described, illustrated, and compared to related species, and we discuss its
L24@P0: origin and history at The Zoo. The description is from fresh, undried, living material.

L165@P14: Discussion
L166@P14: Chamaedorea includes over 100 species of dwarf to moderate, solitary of clustered, pinnate- or
L167@P14: simple (bifid apically and pinnately ribbed)-leaved, mostly understory palms with bamboo-like
L168@P14: stems from Mexico to northern South America. Denizens of moist to wet, undisturbed forests,
L169@P14: the genus is especially rich and diverse from 500 to 1000 m elevation in southern Mexico and
L170@P14: adjacent Guatemala and again in Costa Rica and adjacent Panama. Hodel (1992a) provided a
L171@P14: comprehensive, detailed, and copiously illustrated monograph of the genus and he and others
L172@P14: added additional species and information in subsequent publications (Bernal et al. 2004;
L173@P14: Cascante-Marin and Muller 2020; Grayum 1998; Hodel 1992a, 1995, 1996, 1997, 2013, 2022;
L174@P14: Hodel et al. 1995, 1997; and Pérez-Farrera et al. 2021).
L175@P14: By the nature of its solitary, staminate flowers with valvate petals free and spreading apically and
L176@P14: solitary staminate inflorescences at a node, Chamaedorea delicata falls within the informal
L177@P14: grouping of subgenus Chamaedoropsis (Hodel 1992a). Several recent molecular-based
L178@P14: phylogenies (Cano 2018, Cuenca and Asmussen-Lange 2007, Thomas et al. 2006) do not fully
L179@P14: support the morphological basis for Chamaedoropsis and a few of the other informal subgenera,
L180@P14: especially the closely related subgenus Chamaedorea, that I had outlined (Hodel 1992a);
L181@P14: however, the character traits I used to define these subgenera are, in most cases, still useful in
L182@P14: helping to identify species.
L183@P14: Within Chamaedoropsis, Chamaedorea delicata falls within an informal sub-group I termed

L243@P16: Cultivation
L244@P16: All plants of Chamaedorea delicata are in moderate to dense shade on mostly sloping, moist,
L245@P16: clay-loam soil with abundant, natural mulch derived from fallen leaves from Fern Canyon’s
L246@P16: canopy trees.
L247@P16: San Diego has a Mediterranean climate with warm, dry, mostly sunny summers and cool, mostly
L248@P16: moist winters (Kottek et al. 2006). Average monthly temperatures are 14.1 C in January, the
L249@P16: coolest month, and 22 C in August, the warmest month. Average high/ low daily temperatures
L250@P16: are 18 C/10 C in January and 26 C/19.5 in August (CR 2025, WRCC 2025, WU 2025).
L251@P16: Night and morning low clouds, commonly referred to as the “marine layer” or “May gray, June
L252@P16: gloom,” are a significant weather feature especially from May through August but can occur in
L253@P16: any month of the year (MUCSD 2025, UT 2025). This phenomenon helps to moderate
L254@P16: temperatures and increase humidity. Temperatures rarely exceed 32 C and even more rarely
L255@P16: drop to O C.
L256@P16: Annual average relative humidity ranges throughout the day from 76 percent in the early
L257@P16: morning to 58 percent at 1:00 pm (WU 2025).
L258@P16: Precipitation averages about 30 cm annually, most from November through March (WRCC 2025).
L259@P16: The precipitation deficit is made up by irrigation through overhead sprinklers Plants are not
L260@P16: fertilized.

L262@P16: somewhat similar to those found in moist, tropical, mountain forests where Chamaedoreas
