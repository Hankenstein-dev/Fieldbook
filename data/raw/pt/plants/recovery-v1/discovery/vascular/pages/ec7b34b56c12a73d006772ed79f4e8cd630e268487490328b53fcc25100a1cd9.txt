L40@P3: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L41@P3: Capacity Development in the Water Science Sector
L42@P3: It is recognised that there is a need for Transformation and Redress, as well as
L43@P3: Capacity Development, in the Water Science Sector in South Africa. However,
L44@P3: students from some universities are currently not being offered sufficient opportunities
L45@P3: to develop skills in aquatic (freshwater) research that are desperately needed for the
L46@P3: protection of South Africa’s freshwater ecosystems and the sustainable long-term
L47@P3: utilisation of freshwater resources. The majority of the emphasis in the development
L48@P3: of water scientists is currently on the utilisation of water as a resource for agricultural,
L49@P3: industrial and domestic use in the water and sanitation sector. The ecological and
L50@P3: biological components of water science are less emphasised and opportunities for
L51@P3: research in these areas may not be offered to students at higher education institutions
L52@P3: (HEIs). Therefore, exploring opportunities for linking monitoring of aquatic ecosystems
L53@P3: to University science programmes (such as BSc. Honours research projects) could
L54@P3: create an interest in ecological based research of freshwater ecosystems and the
L55@P3: experiences gained could equip (and inspire) students involved to pursue careers in
L56@P3: ecological research of freshwater ecosystems.
L57@P3: The primary objective of this Technical Report is to explore how a baseline
L58@P3: assessment of the Krom River, Olifants-Doring River System, Western Cape, prior to
L59@P3: the river being treated with the piscicide Rotenone could be used to integrate human
L60@P3: capacity development into an ecological project with the intention of developing a BSc
L61@P3: Honours level programme to integrate aquatic research and monitoring techniques
L62@P3: into post-graduate and supervisor training at HEIs. The specific objectives were to:
L63@P3: x Generate baseline data for the Krom River that CapeNature plans to treat with
L64@P3: Rotenone in the near future,
L65@P3: x Integrate post-graduate student projects into a long-term monitoring framework to
L66@P3: develop national capacity in freshwater fish and invertebrate monitoring.
L67@P3: Krom River Baseline Monitoring
L68@P3: The Krom River rises in the Cederberg Mountains at an elevation of ~1500 m and
L69@P3: flows in an easterly direction for approximately 20 km before joining the Matjies River
L70@P3: which then flows into the Doring River. Surveys were conducted to document the
L71@P3: baseline condition of the Krom River before the planned treatment with Rotenone to
L72@P3: remove non-native fishes. Fish populations, water quality and habitat parameters were
L73@P3: recorded at 29 sites in the Trout, Rock catfish, Treatment and Sawfin zones on the
L74@P3: Krom River over two field trips: January-February 2017 and January-February 2018.
L75@P3: Fish abundance at each site was estimated using three independent methods: fyke
L76@P3: nets, underwater video analysis (UWVA) and snorkel surveys. Underwater Video
L77@P3: Analysis was carried out using GoPro® HD Hero® cameras. Cameras were placed in
L78@P3: each site and recorded footage for a minimum of 15 minutes and then viewed to obtain
L79@P3: estimates of fish abundance and diversity. Snorkel surveys used the two pass method
L80@P3-4: where all fish encountered by the observer were counted on each pass and an iv
L81@P4: estimate of abundance is derived from the mean of the two counts. Fyke nets were
L82@P4: set overnight and cleared the next morning.
L83@P4: Invertebrate monitoring was conducted at 20 monitoring sites within the Fishless,
L84@P4: Trout, Rock Catfish, and Treatment zones of the Krom River over two field trips:
L85@P4: January-February 2017 and January-February 2018 by kick sampling following the
L86@P4: guidelines set out by Weyl et al. (2016) and involved both SASS5 methods as well as
L87@P4: species specific estimates of abundance.
L88@P4: Baseline monitoring of fish and invertebrate communities in the Krom River
L89@P4: demonstrated that:
L90@P4: • The distribution of fish in the Krom River has not changed from earlier studies in
L91@P4: 2006 and 2007. The only native fish in the upper catchment is the Clanwilliam rock
L92@P4: catfish Austroglanis gilli, while sawfin Cheilobarbus serra and Clanwilliam
L93@P4: yellowfish Labeobarbus seeberi were found in the lower reaches in the Matjies
L94@P4: River floodplain. Clanwilliam yellowfish were not recorded from the Krom River in
L95@P4: previous studies.
L96@P4: • Three non-native fish species were identified from the Krom River catchment:
L97@P4: rainbow trout Oncorhynchus mykiss in the whole river, bluegill Lepomis
L98@P4: macrochirus in the river from just above the barrier weir at Krom River Farm to the
L99@P4: confluence with the Matjies River as well as the farm dams, and largemouth bass
L100@P4: Micropterus salmoides in at least one of the farm dams.
L101@P4: • Assessments of invertebrate communities demonstrated that communities were
L102@P4: distinct for each of the zones delineated for the Krom River likely responding to
L103@P4: gradient change along the river length in keeping with the River Continuum
L104@P4: Concept. There was also inter-annual variation in the invertebrate assemblages
L105@P4: between the two years of the study.
L106@P4: • There were variations in water quality between the zones of the Krom River. The
L107@P4: upper reaches differed from the lower reaches below the Krom River Farm
L108@P4: complex by increased turbidity and electrical conductivity.

L116@P4: one BSc. Honours student and one Diploma student from the Universities of Fort

L123@P4-5: University in March 2018. v

L308@P13: CHAPTER 1. INTRODUCTION
L309@P13: 1.1 Introduction
L310@P13: It is recognised that there is a need for Transformation and Redress, as well as
L311@P13: Capacity Development, in the Water Science Sector in South Africa. However,
L312@P13: students are often unable to access sufficient opportunities to develop skills in aquatic
L313@P13: (freshwater) research desperately needed for the protection of South Africa’s
L314@P13: freshwater ecosystems and the sustainable long-term utilisation of freshwater
L315@P13: resources. Therefore, exploring opportunities for linking monitoring of aquatic
L316@P13: ecosystems to BSc. Honours research projects could create an interest in ecological
L317@P13: based research of freshwater ecosystems and the experiences gained could equip
L318@P13: students involved to pursue careers in this field
L319@P13: The current K5/2538 project follows two previous projects (K9/822 and K5/2261) that
L320@P13: monitored the impact of river rehabilitation using the piscicide Rotenone on the nearby
L321@P13: Rondegat River (Woodford et al., 2012; Weyl et al., 2016). These projects provided
L322@P13: comprehensive species level assessments of invertebrate and vertebrate distributions
L323@P13: both prior to, and for three years after, the Rotenone treatment. Overall these research
L324@P13: projects demonstrated that Rotenone treatment was effective at removing smallmouth
L325@P13: bass from the treatment zone (Weyl et al., 2013) and that native invertebrate and fish
L326@P13: communities are recovering after treatment (Weyl et al., 2014; Bellingan et al., 2015).
L327@P13: Despite this evidence, the development of National Policy requires additional
L328@P13: information, particularly on the effects of Rotenone on lentic environments, such as
L329@P13: dams and wetlands, before considering the registration of Rotenone as a national tool
L330@P13: for river rehabilitation. The opportunity to address these knowledge gaps was provided
L331@P13: by CapeNature’s plan to treat two off channel dams (Chalet Dam in Krom River and
L332@P13: Kranskloof Dam in the Northern Cape) in 2017 in addition to collecting base-line data
L333@P13: in anticipation for the treatment of the Krom River as part of the CAPE Alien Fish
L334@P13: Eradication pilot project. However, delays in receiving the General Authorisation from
L335@P13: the Department of Water and Sanitation for the treatment of the Krom River and in
L336@P13: receiving funding from DEA: NRMP during the 2018/19 financial year prevented the
L337@P13: treatment of the Krom River from occurring at the time of completing this project and
L338@P13: the scope of the project was thus reduced to collecting base-line data for the Krom
L339@P13: River and monitoring of the treatment and recovery of the two off-channel dams.
L340@P13: The current project also provided the opportunity to incorporate capacity building in
L341@P13: aquatic ecosystem monitoring by including some of South Africa’s higher education
L342@P13: institutions (HEIs), including the Universities of Fort Hare, Venda, the Western Cape
L343@P13: and Stellenbosch. By partnering with these HEIs, the project offered opportunities for
L344@P13: exposure to aquatic research and experts in this field to students whom might not
L345@P13: otherwise have access to similar opportunities at their home institutions by linking the
L346@P13: monitoring of aquatic ecosystems to B Sc. Honours research projects. These
L347@P13: partnerships between SAIAB and the HEIs contributes to the achievement of national
L348@P13: transformation goals and increased interest and opportunities for students at HEIs to
L349@P13-14: pursue careers in aquatic ecology. 2
L350@P14: The greater aims of the current project were therefore two-fold: (1) to support policy
L351@P14: development through robust monitoring of ecosystem responses to management
L352@P14: interventions (e.g. conservation, rehabilitation and monitoring) and (2) developing
L353@P14: appropriate methods to integrate post-graduate students in longer term monitoring
L354@P14: projects. These two components are reported separately as PART 1: IMPACT AND
L355@P14: RECOVERY OF NATIVE BIOTA IN ONE RIVER AND TWO DAMS FOLLOWING
L356@P14: ALIEN FISH REMOVALS USING ROTENONE and PART 2: KROM RIVER BASELINE MONITORING AND CAPACITY DEVELOPMENT (this report).
L357@P14: 1.2 Aims and Objective
L358@P14: The primary objective of this Technical Report is to explore using baseline monitoring
L359@P14: of the Krom River to integrate human capacity development into an ecological project.
L360@P14: The Krom River is scheduled for alien fish eradication by CapeNature in the near
L361@P14: future. As is standard practise, a pre-treatment monitoring baseline assessment prior
L362@P14: to Rotenone treatment is necessary (Woodford et al., 2012; Weyl et al., 2016). The
L363@P14: techniques used in the baseline monitoring could be used to develop a B Sc. Honours
