L4@P0: The taxonomy of the genus Micranthes Haw. in Europe is reviewed and the morphological characters that
L5@P0: can be used to discriminate between it and Saxifraga L. are discussed. A new classification of Micranthes
L6@P0: stellaris (L.) Galasso, Banfi & Soldano is proposed, based on recent phylogeographical studies. In it, the
L7@P0: species is divided into three subspecies, (a) subsp. stellaris, distributed from eastern N. America,
L8@P0: Greenland and northern Europe, south and west to the Massif Central, the Iberian peninsula and
L9@P0: ?Corsica; (b) subsp. hispidula (Rochel) Gornall, ranging from the Carpathians south to the Balkan
L10@P0: peninsula, and (c) subsp. robusta (Engl.) Gornall, confined to the Alps. The first has four varieties, var.
L11@P0: stellaris, var. gemmifera (D.A. Webb) Gornall, var. paucidentata Gornall and var. obovata (Engl.) Gornall.
L12@P0: The last has two varieties, var. acaulis (Haller f.) Gornall and var. prolifera (Sternb.) Gornall. A new
L13@P0: combination is also made for the viviparous morphs of M. clusii (Gouan) Fern. Prieto, Vázquez, Vallines &
L14@P0: Cires, viz. M. clusii subsp. lepismigena (Planellas) Gornall.
L15@P0: Keywords: Micranthes stellaris, Micranthes clusii, nomenclature, phylogeography, Saxifraga, taxonomy
L16@P0: Introduction
L17@P0: Although Saxifraga L. shows considerable variation in
L18@P0: its vegetative morphology and cytology (Webb &
L19@P0: Gornall, 1989), most botanists have followed Linnaeus
L20@P0: in adopting a broad interpretation of the variation and
L21@P0: have been content to recognise a single, large genus,
L22@P0: largely on account of the remarkably constant gross
L23@P0: floral morphology which consists of a pentamerous
L24@P0: calyx and corolla, ten stamens and a bicarpellate
L25@P0: ovary. It is true that a few species deviate from this formula in having more or fewer petals, stamens or carpels, but they are a very few (Gornall et al., 2012).
L26@P0: An alternative view involves breaking up the genus
L27@P0: into segregates, an approach that was pioneered by
L28@P0: Haworth (1812, 1821) in the early nineteenth century
L29@P0: but which did not gain acceptance. In the early twentieth century, Small & Rydberg (1905) revived
L30@P0: Haworth’s segregate genera and, for a while, some
L31@P0: botanists adopted the practice of running the two taxonomies side by side, e.g. Losina-Losinskaja (1928),
L32@P0: Komarov (1929), and Hara (1939), but this practice
L33@P0: soon faded out and Linnaeus’ broad concept prevailed
L34@P0: once more (Gornall, 1987b; Webb & Gornall, 1989).
L35@P0: Early in another century, there is now solid evidence
L36@P0: from numerous gene sequences, both plastid and
L37@P0: nuclear (Deng et al., 2015; Soltis et al., 1996), to support some limited surgery on Saxifraga, whereby two
L38@P0: of the sections, section Merkianae (Engl.) Hara and
L39@P0: section Micranthes (Haworth) D. Don (together largely
L40@P0: corresponding to section Boraphila Engler) are combined and elevated to genus rank as Micranthes
L41@P0: Haworth. Analysis of the sequence evidence demonstrates that Saxifraga is polyphyletic if these sections
L42@P0: are included within it. Splitting them off, however, renders both Micranthes and the rest of Saxifraga as reassuringly monophyletic taxa. This approach has now
L43@P0: been adopted in floristic accounts of North America
L44@P0: (Brouillet & Gornall, 2007, 2008), the Arctic (Elven
L45@P0: & Murray, 2008), Asia (Akiyama & Gornall, 2011;
L46@P0: Akiyama et al., 2012), and Europe, where eight species
L47@P0: of Micranthes have been recognised (Prieto et al., 2010,
L48@P0: 2013) (Table 1).
L49@P0: The aim of this paper is to discuss the morphological
L50@P0: characters by which Micranthes may be distinguished
L51@P0: from Saxifraga and to update the nomenclature of those
L52@P0: infraspecific taxa that require recognition in Europe.
