L161: Mentha suaveolens
L162: cite91†Ehrh. L163: A variant of apple mint, pineapple mint, displays white variegation on its leaves, cite92†Pierce County, Washington L164: Mentha suaveolens, the apple mint, pineapple mint, woolly mint or round-leafed mint (cite93†synonyms M. rotundifolia, Mentha macrostachya, Mentha insularis),^{cite94†[1] }^{cite95†[2] } is a member of the mint family cite88†Lamiaceae . It is native to southern and western cite96†Europe including the cite97†Mediterranean region . It is a cite98†herbaceous , upright cite99†perennial plant that is most commonly grown as a culinary herb or for ground cover.
L165: ## Description
L166: 
L167: [cite100†edit ]
L168: Apple mint typically grows to a height of from 40 to 100 centimetres (16 to 39 in) tall and spreads by cite101†stolons to form cite102†clonal colonies. The foliage is light green, with the opposite, wrinkled, sessile cite103†leaves being oblong to nearly ovate, 3 to 5 cm (1.2 to 2.0 in) long and 2 to 4 cm (0.8 to 1.6 in) broad. They are somewhat hairy on top and downy underneath with serrated edges.
L169: The flowers develop in terminal spikes 4 to 9 cm (1.6 to 3.5 in) long and consisting of a number of whorls of white or pinkish flowers. Apple mint flowers in mid to late summer. The plant is aromatic with a fruity, minty flavour.^{cite104†[3] }

L183: ### Varieties and cultivars
L184: 
L185: [cite111†edit ]
L186: 
L187: There are several cite112†varieties and cite113†cultivars commonly available^{cite114†[4] } in horticulture:
L188:   * M. suaveolens 'Variegata' – common name pineapple mint – with variegated leaves and mauve flowers.^{cite114†[4] }
L189:   * M. suaveolens 'Pineapple' – common name pineapple mint – with variegated leaves and creamy-white flowers.^{cite114†[4] }
L190:   * M. suaveolens var. crispa 'Mojito' – with curled leaves and mauve-pink flowers.^{cite114†[4] }
L191:   * Mentha suaveolens subsp. timija, an endemic Moroccan variety,^{cite115†[5] } is known for its evergreen foliage and thrives along rivers in plains and mountains, displaying green leaves with a hairy texture.
L192: ## Distribution
L193: 
L194: [cite116†edit ]
L195: 
L196: Apple mint is native to southern and western Europe and is naturalised in central and northern parts of Europe. It is found in damp and wet locations.^{cite104†[3] }
L197: ## Cultivation and uses
L198: 
L199: [cite117†edit ]
L200: 
L201: An attractive herb, apple mint is often used as an cite118†ornamental plant . It is hardy and easy to grow, preferring full sun to lightly shady conditions. The leaves of this plant can be used to make apple mint cite119†jelly , as well as a flavoring in dishes such as apple mint cite120†couscous . It is also often used to make a cite121†mint tea , as a cite122†garnish , or in cite123†salads .^{cite124†[6] }
L202: Pineapple mint (Mentha suaveolens 'Variegata') is a cite113†cultivar of apple mint that has leaves which are banded with white. A cite107†hybrid derived from it is grapefruit mint (Mentha suaveolens × piperata).
L203: 
L204: Apple mint has been used for medicinal purposes for thousands of years in many parts of the world, including Africa, Europe, Asia, and the Americas.
