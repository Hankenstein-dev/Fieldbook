L11@P0: A bstract: Twelve species of the genus Cladophora occur along the South African East Coast.
L12@P0: Detailed descriptions and illustrations are presented. Four species are recorded for the first time in
L13@P0: South Africa: C. catenata, C. vagabunda, C. horii and C. dotyana-, the last two are also new records
L14@P0: for the Indian Ocean. A comparison of the South African C. rugulosa specimens with specimens of
L15@P0: C. proliferaixom South Africa and other regions have shown that these species are not synonymous
L16@P0: as previously considered, leading to the resurrection of C. rugulosa which is probably a South
L17@P0: African endemic.
L18@P0: Key w ords: Cladophora, C. catenata, C. dotyana, C. horii, C. prolifera, C. rugulosa, C. vagabunda,
L19@P0: South Africa, KwaZulu-Natal.
L20@P0: Introduction
L21@P0: Cladophora Kützing is one of the largest green-algal genera and has a worldwide
L22@P0: distribution. Within the class Cladophorophyceae the genus Cladophora is characterized
L23@P0: by its simple thallus architecture: branched, uniseriate filaments of multinucleate
L24@P0: cells. Eleven different architectural types (sections) are distinguished in the genus
L25@P0: (van den Hoek 1963, 1982; van den Hoek & Chihara 2000). Recent studies based on
L26@P0: morphological and molecular data have proven that Cladophora is polyphyletic (van
L27@P0: den Hoek 1982; Bakker et al. 1994; Hanyuda et al. 2002). The available molecular
L28@P0: data are not sufficient yet to confirm (or reject) van den Hoek’s morphological
L29@P0: sections. A phylogeny of the Cladophorophyceae based on 18S rRNA (Bakker et al.
L30@P0: 1994) suggests two lineages in the class: one containing predominantly tropical species
L31@P0: including siphonocladean taxa and some Cladophora species, the other one containing
L32@P0: mostly warm- to cold-temperate species of Cladophora. The addition of several

L427@P5: Section Repentes Kiitzing
L428@P5: 1. C ladophora coelothrix Kiitzing, 1843: 272 Fig. 2
L429@P5: Type locality: Golfo di Genova, Italy (leg. Meneghini, L 937/278/392).
L430@P5: References: Borgesen (1939: 72-73, fig. 15); Sartoni (1992: 300, fig. 5B); van den
L431@P5: Hoek (1963: 40-43, pi. 5, figs 55-67, pi. 6, figs 68-71, pi. 7, figs 72-77, pi. 8, fig.
L432@P5: 78; 1982: 47-52, figs 11-29); van den Hoek & Chihara (2000: 36-40, fig. 14).
L433@P5: Description:
L434@P5: Thallus dark green, forming cushion-like mats, composed of interwoven, densely
L435@P5: branched filaments; loosely attached to the substratum by unbranched or branched,
L436@P5: uni- or multicellular rhizoids developing from the basal poles of the short cells of
L437@P5: the basal, stolon-like filaments. Basal filaments giving rise to the upright branch
L438@P5: systems. Growth mainly by division of conspicuous apical cells, and by intercalary
L439@P5: cell division lower down; cells becoming barely longer and broader in basipetal
L440@P5: direction. Upright filaments either unbranched and composed of 3-5 cells, or branched
L441@P5: with a branch-system up to the 2nd order and a feebly acropetal organisation. Maximum of one lateral per cell; newly formed laterals often without cross-walls at their
L442@P5: base; in older laterals cross-walls steeply inclined to the parent cell; angle of
L443@P5: ramification 20°-50°; ultimate filaments and apical cells often curved. Apical cells
L444@P5: cylindrical with rounded tip, 57-75 pm in diam., 1/w ratio 5-15; cells of ultimate
L445@P5: filaments cylindrical, 57-93 pm in diam., 1/w ratio 8-12; basal, prostrate cells
L446@P5: cylindrical, 75-120 pm in diam.; 1/w ratio 2.5-5. Cell walls ca. 1-3 pm thick in
L447@P5: apical cells and ultimate filaments, up to 15 pm thick in basal cells.
L448@P5: Ecology: intertidal to subtidal (to 20 m deep).
L449@P5: Specimens examined: Adlams Reef, Sodwana Bay: KZN 321 (9/08/1999); Mabibi:
L450@P5: KZN 2186 (13/02/2001).
L451@P5: Geographic distribution: C. coelothrix is widely distributed in the tropical to warmtemperate seas (van den Hoek & Chihara 2000: 37). Along the East African coast it
L452@P5: has been recorded for Somalia (Sartoni 1992), Mozambique (Coppejans et al. 2002)
L453@P5: and South Africa (Transkei) (Bolton & Stegenga 1987: 168).
L454@P5: Note: The possible absence of cross-walls at the base of newly formed laterals is a
L455@P5: result of the delay in their formation, a phenomenon also occuring in C. socialis and
L456@P5: C. catenata. C. coelothrix may be confused with Cladophoropsis sundanensis Reinbold
L457@P5: [recently recorded from South Africa (Leliaert et al. 2001)] due to similarities in
L458@P5: growth form, presence of hapteroidal rhizoids, delayed cross-wall formation and
L459@P5: comparable cell dimensions. C. sundanensis can be distinguished by its light green
L460@P5: thallus and cells which occasionally divide by segregative cell-division.

L463@P6: 2. C ladophora sodalis Kiitzing, 1849: 416 Fig. 3
L464@P6: Type locality: Tahiti (L 937/253/440).
L465@P6: References: B orge sen [1940: 36-37, fig. 12 (as C. patentiramea (Montagne) Kiitzing
L466@P6: forma longiarticulata Reinbold in Weber-van Bosse)]; Egerod (1974: 137-138, figs
L467@P6: 17-21); Jaasund [1976: 9, fig. 17 (as C. patentiramea f. longiarticulata)]; Sartoni
L468@P6: (1986: 361, 363, fig. 4A; 1992: 302-304, figs 6A,B); van den Hoek (1963: 43, 46-
L469@P6: 47, pi. 8, figs 79-85, pi. 9, figs 86-91; 1982: 52-57, figs 30-40); van den Hoek &
