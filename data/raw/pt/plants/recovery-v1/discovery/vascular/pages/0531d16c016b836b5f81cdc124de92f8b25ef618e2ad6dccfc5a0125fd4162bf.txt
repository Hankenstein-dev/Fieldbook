L174: Alyssum is a cite108†genus of over a hundred species of flowering plants in the family cite104†Brassicaceae , native to temperate Europe, Asia, and northern Africa,^{cite107†[1] } with the highest species diversity in the Mediterranean region. The genus comprises cite109†annual and cite110†perennial cite111†herbaceous plants or (rarely) small cite112†shrubs , growing to 10–100 cm tall, with oblong-oval leaves.
L175: Alyssum flowers are characteristically small and grouped in terminal clusters;^{cite113†[2] } they are often yellow or white colored but can be pink or purple.
L176: The genera cite114†Lobularia , cite115†Aurinia and cite116†Odontarrhena are closely related to Alyssum and were formerly included in it. The widely cultivated species popularly known as "sweet alyssum" (Alyssum maritimum) is cite83†Lobularia maritima . The common rockery plant (Alyssum saxatile) is cite117†Aurinia saxatilis .
L177: 
L178: Alyssum foliage is used as food by the cite118†caterpillars of certain cite119†Lepidoptera , including the cite120†Gem (Orthonama obstipata). However, rabbits will not eat it.^{cite121†[3] }
