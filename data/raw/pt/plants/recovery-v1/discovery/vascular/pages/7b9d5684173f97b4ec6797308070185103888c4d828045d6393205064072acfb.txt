L50@P3: After the publication of Seed plants of southern Africa: families and genera (Leistner 2000) the Executive of SABONET
L51@P3: expressed the wish to have the scope of this work extended to cover all 10 countries in which the organisation is active. In
L52@P3: response to that wish the present project was undertaken. As indicated on the title page, it is a supplement to Leistner (2000) and
L53@P3: it will generally be necessary to consult the two works together. During the compilation it was apparent how many plant groups
L54@P3: are still poorly known. The present work should therefore be considered no more than a small tentative step towards that grand
L55@P3: goal—a Flora africana.
L56@P3: Area covered
L57@P3: The area covered is referred to as southern tropical Africa (abbreviated: sthn trop. Afr.). Five countries are included: Angola,
L58@P3: Zambia, Zimbabwe, Malawi, and Mozambique which lie in a broad belt from the Atlantic to the Indian Oceans to the north of
L59@P3: the five countries in the southernmost part of Africa dealt with in Leistner (2000), namely Namibia, Botswana, South Africa,
L60@P3: Swaziland and Lesotho.
L61@P3: Arrangement and circumscription of families and genera
L62@P3: As in Leistner (2000) the families are alphabetically arranged within the three groups: (1) Gymnosperms or non-angiosperm
L63@P3: seed plants (pp. 26–29), (2) Dicotyledons or Magnoliopsida (pp. 30–374) and (3) Monocotyledons or Liliopsida (375–458).
L64@P3: The Magnoliids, Ceratophyllales and the Basal families Cabombaceae and Nymphaeaceae are again treated within the alphabetical system of the dicotyledons.
L65@P3: Relationships between families are shown in the simplified cladograms taken from Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG II)
L66@P3: (2003), on p. 3. The position of every family is also given in brackets underneath the family name in the text treatment: (1) in
L67@P3: the first line according to the system of Cronquist (1988) as given in Mabberley (1997), which, in practice at least, has not yet
L68@P3: quite replaced (2) the system of APG II, which is shown in the second line.
L69@P3: Compilation and main sources
L70@P3: This work was compiled almost exclusively from literature consulted up to October 2003. Information was freely copied, often verbatim, from the cited references. The main sources of information were Lebrun & Stork (1991–1997 and 2003), an unpublished checklist
L71@P3: of families and genera of the region compiled by Dr Neil Brummitt and placed at my disposal by the author with kind permission of the
L72@P3: Director of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, Kew Records and the following Floras: Flora zambesiaca, Conspectus florae angolensis,
L73@P3: Flora de Moçambique, Flora of tropical east Africa, Flora of west tropical Africa, Flore d’Afrique centrale, Flore du Gabon and Flore
L74@P3: du Cameroun. The most frequently consulted journals were Kew Bulletin and Bulletin du Jardin Botanique National de Belgique.
L75@P3: Descriptions and notes
L76@P3: Descriptions of families and genera dealt with in Leistner (2000) are generally not repeated in this book. At the beginning of
L77@P3: these families is a reference to that publication starting with the name of the compiler of the family account and giving the page
L78@P3: on which it starts. Important family characters recorded for southern tropical Africa but not for southern Africa, are noted. The
L79@P3: aim in compiling both family and genus descriptions was to reflect characters found in local representatives, rather than those
L80@P3: found outside the region. Distribution and size of families and genera on a worldwide basis are given only for taxa not dealt
L81@P3: with in Leistner (2000). Occurrence within the region is expressed in terms of the five countries covered. The presence of a
L82@P3: genus in southern Africa is also generally recorded even though this fact is immediately visible by the absence of a description
L83@P3: in the text. A genus recorded for both southern tropical and southern Africa with a single representative in southern tropical
L84@P3: Africa is not necessarily represented in southern Africa by the same species. The number of genera in the region and the number
L85@P3: of species by which they are represented is given.
L86@P3: Exotics
L87@P3: Full treatment is again given to genera represented by one or more exotic species considered to be growing spontaneously in
L88@P3: natural vegetation and therefore likely to be taken as indigenous. Information on this issue is very sparse and there are likely to
L89@P3: be many more naturalised exotics than listed here. Entirely exotic families, genera, and species are marked with an asterisk.
L90@P3: Synonyms
L91@P3: Synonyms cited are mainly those found in commonly used literature. Generic synonyms do not necessarily embrace the entire
L92@P3: content of the current genus.
L93@P3: Identification of genera
L94@P3: Genera of families represented by only one genus will obviously key out in the key to families. In families with more than one
L95@P3: genus there is either a key to genera, or notes describing how the genus can be keyed out using the key in Leistner (2000). In
L96@P3: families in which all genera are also dealt with in Leistner (2000), no identification aids are given.

L99@P4: Number of taxa represented
L100@P4: The flora of the five countries, as reflected here, comprises 228 families, 2,032 genera, and 11,637 species. These figures are
L101@P4: fairly similar to those for the five countries of southern Africa with regard to families and genera, but strikingly different with
L102@P4: regard to species: 237 families, 2,242 genera, 20,677 species (Germishuizen & Meyer 2003). The vast difference in species
L103@P4: numbers can be ascribed mainly to two factors: (1) the species-rich Cape Flora in southern Africa, and (2) the less complete
L104@P4: knowledge of the flora of parts of the northern countries. The 18 largest genera in the Cape Flora are represented by 2,687
L105@P4: species (Goldblatt & Manning 2000). Of the same genera five are absent from southern tropical Africa and the rest comprise a
L106@P4: mere 221 species. The 18 largest genera in southern tropical Africa contain a total of 1,993 species. The same genera are all well
L107@P4: represented in southern Africa by a total of 1,444 species. Genera in southern tropical Africa are mostly smaller than in the
L108@P4: region to the south, with an average of 5.7 species to the genus compared to 9.2.
