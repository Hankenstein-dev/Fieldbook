L64@P7: Started in 1994 and being inspired by the realisations of the Romanian bryologist Simeon Ştefan
L65@P7: Radian, my bryological studies were focused especially on hornworts and liverworts, under the guidance
L66@P7: of professors Ioan Cristurean, Simona Mărgăritescu and Gheorghe Dihoru, and continued with the
L67@P7: doctoral thesis “Taxonomical and ecological research on the liverworts (Marchantiophyta) from
L68@P7: Romania”, supervised by Mircea Oltean and Marin Falcă, resultting in a large volume of field data
L69@P7: regarding the distribution of these species in Romania, included in the present Atlas.
L70@P7: The Hornwort (Anthocerotophyta) and Liverwort (Marchantiophyta) Atlas of Romania could not
L71@P7: be compiled without the efforts of several researchers, during more than 200 years, from Romania and
L72@P7: other countries, professionals or amateurs, who published during this time hundreds of scientific papers
L73@P7: concerning bryophyte species from Romania, and collected thousands of samples that are now deposited
L74@P7: in different herbariums spread across the globe.
L75@P7: A special support in the realisation of this Atlas was received from my colleagues. From Paulina
L76@P7: Anastasiu, Dan Cogălniceanu, Gina Cogălniceanu, Simona Mihăilescu, Mihaela Paucă-Comănescu,
L77@P7: Adriana Jalbă, Claudia Nicolae, Marilena Onete, Oliviu Pop, Cristina Fiera, Nicoleta Şerpoiu, Kinga
L78@P7: Öllerer I received important bryophytic material collected in different parts of the country. Gavril
L79@P7: Negrean and Richard Wallfisch processed some of the chorological data. Elena Oltu helped in the
L80@P7: processing of the collected material. Kinga Öllerer arranged some of the data and helped with the

L82@P7: Atlas.

L94@P7-9: December 2008BRYOLOGICAL RESEARCHES IN ROMANIA
L95@P9: The knowledge about bryophytes has very old routes in Romania, these can be found also in some
L96@P9: of the names given for hornworts (Anthoceros - lepra lupului pătat, corneţel), liverworts (Marchantia -
L97@P9: muşchiul de fântână, fierea-pământului, coada rândunicii, colbează, funuces or triercii) or mosses
L98@P9: (Sphagnum – coada mâţei de baltă).
L99@P9: The first studies about bryophytes in Romania were done in the beginning of the 19th century. In
L100@P9: this starting period for the Romanian bryology, the first published contributions were lists or
L101@P9: enumerations in botanical papers or in papers dedicated especially for the study of cryptogams.
L102@P9: The first paper in which we can find information on liverwort and moss species from Romania is:
L103@P9: “Enumeratio stirpium magno Transsylvaniae Principatui” by Baumgarten J.C.G. from 1846,
L104@P9: published posthumously by Fuss M. (Baumgarten 1846:1-236). An older contribution, but for which there
L105@P9: was no possibility to find any connection with the citation of Romanian bryophytes is “Enumeratio
L106@P9: plantarum Galiciae & Bucowinae”, published in 1835 by Zawadzki A.
L107@P9: The first publication of specialty, regarding the cryptogams from the Arpaşului Valley, Făgăraş
L108@P9: Mountains, appeared in 1853, by the author Ludwig Ritter von Heufler, in which also the first figures of
L109@P9: some Romanian liverworts were published (Heufler 1853: plate IV) (Fig. 1). In the second half of the
L110@P9: 19th century several publications have appeared in Romania in which hornwort, moss or liverwort
L111@P9: species are mentioned. In this period the following authors have published: Schur F. (1852, 1853, 1858,
L112@P9: 1866, 1885), Heufler L. (1853), Juratzka J. (1859, 1860, 1862, 1863), Pančić J. (1861), Hampe E.
L113@P9: (1861), Knauer B. (1863), Müller B. (1863), Hoffmann U. (1864), Fuss M. (1864-1869, 1872, 1878),
L114@P9: Barth J. (1868, 1871, 1877, 1883, collected mosses and liverworts from the Vlădeasa Massive, but these
L115@P9: data were not published - 1892:34), Grecescu D. (1869, 1876a, 1876b, 1880), Simonkai L. (1872, 1878,
L116@P9: 1890, 1892, 1893), Schimper W.Ph. (1876), Brândză D. (1876a, 1876b, 1883), Venturi G. (1880),
L117@P9: Kanitz A. (1881), Demeter K. (1884-1886, 1888), Hazslinszky F. (1866, 1868, 1885), Csató J. (1885,
L118@P9: 1894), Breidler J. (1890a, 1890b), Mágócsy-Dietz A. (1892), Pax F. (1896, 1898), Péterfi M. (1896-
L119@P9: 1900), Loitlesberger K. (1898, 1900), Zahlbruckner A. (1900).
L120@P9: Fig. 1. Porella platyphylla (Heufler 1853: Pl. IV, Fig. 1).
L121@P9-10: 9The beginning of the 20th century is dominated by the Romanian bryologist Simeon Ştefan
L122@P10: Radian, who describes the genus Bucegia, with the species B. romanica, from the Cerbului Valley in the
L123@P10: Bucegi Mountains, the species being confirmed by Schiffner V. in 1903 and Müller K. in 1906. Because
L124@P10: of the author’s special flair, the correctitude with which he described this species and because of the fact
L125@P10: that it was included in the work of reference entitled “Die Lebermoose Deutschland, Österreich u. d.
L126@P10: Schweiz mit Berücksichtigung der übrigen Länder Europas” (Müller 1906-1911), Bucegia romanica
L127@P10: Radian remained untouched by the later attempts of some bryologists for renaming the species (as
L128@P10: Radiania rumaenica or Bucsecsia).
L129@P10: After obtaining his diploma in natural sciences in 1894, Radian immediately started his bryological
L130@P10: studies in the Bucegi Mountains. In 1901 he published two synthesis works for that period regarding the
L131@P10: bryophytes from Romania (Radian 1901a, Radian 1901b), together with a list of mosses collected by
L132@P10: Popovici A. Bâznoşanu from Stânca-Ştefăneşti, Botoşani county (Procopianu-Popovici 1901:27, annex).
L133@P10: In 1903, he described the species Bucegia romanica in the paper entitled “Sur le Bucegia nouveau genre
L134@P10: d'Hépatiques a Thalle”. In 1915 and 1920 the contributions III and IV to the Romanian liverwort flora
L135@P10: were published, where liverwort species are cited for the first time, some of them confirmed by us after
L136@P10: 89 years since the first publication (Asterella gracilis), some still unfound (Riccia warnstorfii). In 1922 he
L137@P10: presented the work entitled “Anatomia aparatului vegetativ de la Bucegia romanica Radian” (The
L138@P10: anatomy of the vegetative apparatus of Bucegia romanica Radian) in order to obtain the doctoral title, this
L139@P10: being the first doctoral thesis in the domain of bryology in Romania (Radian 1923).
L140@P10: The first half of the 20th century is marked by the apparition of several important bryological
L141@P10: publications. In this period the following authors have published: Degen A. (1901), Röll J. (1902, 1903),
L142@P10: Matouschek F. (1903, 1905), Péterfi M. (1901-1904, 1906, 1908-1910, 1918), Györffy I. (1903-1912,
L143@P10: 1914-1917), Barth J. (1905), Wolcsánszky J. (1905), Grinţescu G.P. (1907), Prodan I. (1907), Schiffner
L144@P10: V. (1908), Pax F. (1908), Gusztáv M. (1910), Lilienfeldówna F. (1911), Szurák J. (1912), etc.
L145@P10: Another Romanian researcher who activated in the field of bryology for 48 years (1924-1972) and
L146@P10: who published in this domain about 90 scientific papers in and outside the country was Constantin Papp.
L147@P10: Born on the 1st of January 1896, Papp C. has studied at the Faculty of Sciences (botany) of the “Al.
L148@P10: I. Cuza” University and also at the Faculty of Medicine (pharmacy), obtaining his diploma certificate both
L149@P10: in natural sciences (1921) and in pharmacy (1922). In 1926 he defended his doctoral thesis “Contribuţiuni
L150@P10: la studiul briofitelor din Moldova” (Contributions to the study of bryophytes in Moldova), that represents
L151@P10: the second doctoral thesis in bryology from Romania.
L152@P10: Most of his publications in bryology are referring to different regions from Moldova (56 titles), but
L153@P10: he has also conducted research in Dobrogea, Bucovina, Transylvania, Banat etc.
L154@P10: He studied the bryoflora of the Ceahlău, Rarău, Bucegi and Leaota Mountains, from Cheile Turzii,
L155@P10: Cheile Mari ale Bicazului and from Cumpătul.
L156@P10: A special merit of prof. Papp C. in the field of bryology is represented by the publication of the
L157@P10: first guide for cryptogams, “Plante vasculare cuprinzând şi chei dichotomice pentru determinarea până la
L158@P10: genuri a briofitelor din împrejurimile oraşului Iaşi” (Vacular plants, containing also dichotomous keys for
L159@P10: the determination till the genus of the bryophytes from the surroundings of the city of Iaşi) (Papp &
L160@P10: Răvăruţ 1938), “Flora pentru determinarea briofitelor cunoscute în Moldova dintre Carpaţi şi Prut” (Flora
L161@P10: for the determination of bryophytes known in Moldova, between the Carpathians and the Prut River)
L162@P10: (Papp 1943), “Briofitele din Republica Socialistă România (determinator)” (The bryophytes from the
L163@P10: Romanian Socialist Republic - guide) (Papp 1967a) (Mititelu & Barabaş V. 1971:913).
L164@P10: Papp C. collaborated at the editing of the Exssicatae and the Schedae from Romania (Cluj, Iaşi,
L165@P10: Constanţa, Bacău), he determined and verified the bryological materials from geobotanical works. He
L166@P10: organized the Botanical Department of the University in Iaşi, a collection with a rich bryological material
L167@P10: (Ştefureac 1973a:467-472; Ştefureac 1973e:664-667).
L168@P10: The most prolific Romanian researcher in the domain of bryology was Traian I. Ştefureac, who in
L169@P10: 1940 defended his doctoral thesis entitled “Cercetări sinecologice şi sociologice asupra bryophytelor din
L170@P10: Codrul Secular Slătioara (Bucovina)” (Sinecological and sociological studies on the bryophytes from the
L171@P10: Slătioara Ancient Forest (Bucovina) (Ştefureac 1941), the first doctoral thesis in the field of bryophyte
L172@P10: cenology from Romania (Plămadă 1987a:335-337).
L173@P10: During his 52 years of activity he published over 110 papers in the field of bryology in the
L174@P10: specialty journals from Romania and outside the country. Between his published works there are several
L175@P10: synthesis works with monographic character, such as: “Studii briologice în unele formaţiuni de vegetaţie
L176@P10-11: 10din România (sărături, sfagnete, păduri)” (Bryological studies in some vegetation formations from
L177@P11: Romania (saltings, sphagniherbosa, forests) (Ştefureac 1969a), “Conspectul briofitelor din Dobrogea”
L178@P11: (The epitome of the bryophytes from Dobrogea) (Ştefureac, Mihai & Pascal 1970), “Conspectul
L179@P11: briofitelor din Moldova” (The epitome of the bryophytes from Moldova) (Ştefureac & al. 1973),
L180@P11: “Conspectul briofitelor din Bucovina” (The epitome of the bryophytes from Bucovina) (Ştefureac & al.
L181@P11: 1981) etc.
L182@P11: In the domain of palynology, he published a paper on the particularities of the hornwort, liverwort
L183@P11: and moss spores, (Ştefureac 1971d:21-42), and also other papers in collaboration (Ştefureac, Ploaie P. &
L184@P11: Ploaie G. 1971: 75-79; Ştefureac, Ploaie G. & Ploaie P. 1979: 279-286).
L185@P11: Ştefureac T. was member of the IAB (International Association of Bryologists), of GTCBE
L186@P11: (Groupe de Travail pour la Cartographie du Bryophytae de l’Europe) and reviewer at “Revue
L187@P11: Bryologique et Lichénologique”. Also, prof. Ştefureac T. coordinated several doctoral theses at the
L188@P11: botanical department of the Biology Faculty – University of Bucharest (Cristurean I., Dihoru
L189@P11: Alexandrina, Dihoru G., Eftimie Elena, Mihai G., Plămadă E. etc).
L190@P11: Studies on the muskegs in Romania were conducted especially by Pop Emil (13.04.1897-
L191@P11: 14.07.1974), whose contribution to the knowledge on the distribution of bryophytes in Romania is very
L192@P11: important. He cited 267 bryophyte species in his papers (32 liverworts and 235 mosses). He had a
L193@P11: significant contribution to the chorology of the glacial relict Odontoschisma sphagni (Dicks.) Dumort.
L194@P11: (Ştefureac 1976b:1017-1018; Ştefureac 1979b:183-188; Boşcaiu 1999; Boşcaiu, Toniuc & Purdelea 1999).
L195@P11: Bryological researches in Transylvania were conducted by Péterfi M., author of several works, in
L196@P11: which also a new species for Romania is published: Geocalyx graveolens. Also in Transylvania, Borza
L197@P11: A., Györffy I., Nyárády E.I., Nyárády A., Soó R. and others analyzed and published materials collected
L198@P11: from different sites. An important contribution to the knowledge of Transylvanian bryoflora was that of
L199@P11: Boros Á. and Vajda L., who published several papers in which they list an important number of new
L200@P11: species for Romania.
L201@P11: During the 20th century, important contributions to the knowledge of the Romanian bryoflora had
L202@P11: also some foreign bryologists, such as: Debreczy Z., Degen A., Düll R., Grolle R., Hargitai Z., Herzog
L203@P11: T., Igmángy J., Jakucs P., Járai-Komlódi Magda, Karczmarz K., Kazimierz R., Mickiewicz J.,
L204@P11: Muñoz J., Müller K., Novotný I., Ochyra R., Orbán S., Pax F., Podpěra J., Pócs T., Sass-Gyarmati
L205@P11: Andrea, Schiffner V., Schuster R.M., Simon T., Szepesfalvi J. and Váňa J.
L206@P11: Mihai Gheorghe studied the bryoflora of Moldova, especially in the Başeului valley, but he also
L207@P11: had an important contribution to the bryoflora of Dobrogea, Muntenia and even Transylvania. In 1983 he
L208@P11: published the list of liverworts from Romania, completed by Dihoru G. (1986:98) with the following taxa:
L209@P11: Marsupella emarginata subsp. aquatica (under M. aquatica), Marchantia paleacea, Scapania compacta
L210@P11: and S. brevicaulis (under S. degenii).
L211@P11: Mohan Gheorghe, a disputed bryologist, published about several species, among which also the
L212@P11: liverworts Marchantia paleacea and Scapania compacta, of which presence in Romania has been
L213@P11: infirmed especially by Plămadă E. (Plămadă 1979b:379-382; Plămadă 1992:223-235, + annex 422-487).
L214@P11: Dihoru Gheorghe had an important contribution to the bryoflora of Dobrogea, Siriu and Cozia
L215@P11: Mountains, he published several papers on nomenclature. His name is also connected to a new species for
L216@P11: Romania, Scapania paludosa. During his research at the Institute of Biology in Bucharest, he mapped the
L217@P11: species from the Anthocerotaceae and Pelliaceae families.
L218@P11: Lungu Lucia conducted research on and published the bryophyte of peatbogs from the Neagra
L219@P11: Boştenilor valley and contributed to the knowledge of the Orthotrichum genus.
L220@P11: Plămadă Emanoil has an important contribution to the knowledge of bryoflora from Romania,
L221@P11: publishing several new species: Pleurocladula albescens var. islandica, Kurtzia pauciflora, Tritomaria
L222@P11: polita, Cephaloziella elegans and Scapania glaucocephala, the latter one being a wrongly identified
L223@P11: species.
L224@P11: For this domain, important contributions had also the following researchers: Eftimie Elena,
L225@P11: Pascal P., Páll Ş., Barabaş Victoria, Wallfisch R. etc.
L226@P11: In the last five years, several papers regarding the bryoflora of Romania were published, a new
L227@P11: moss species was described, Dicranum transsylvanicum (Lüth 2002: 17-21), and Goia Irina has defended
L228@P11: her doctoral thesis entitled „Brioflora de pe substrat lemnos din bazinul superior al Arieşului. Studii
L229@P11: briotaxonomice, corologice, ecologice şi cenologice” (The woody substrate bryoflora from the superior
L230@P11-12: 11basin of the Arieş river. Bryotaxonomical, chorological, ecological and coenological studies) (Goia
L231@P12: 2001).
L232@P12: In Romania, karyological studies have been realized by Eftimiu (1933) on Bucegia romanica,
L233@P12: Tarnavschi (1941) on Buxbaumiales, Ştefureac (1957b) on Poytrichum norvegicum and on Oligotrichum
L234@P12: hercynicum (Ştefureac 1962c).
L235@P12: The most important bryophyte collections can be found in the herbaria from Bucharest (BUC, with
L236@P12: over 22.000 samples, of which over 4.000 are liverworts; BUCA, around 20.000 de samples, of which
L237@P12: over 5.000 are liverworts), Cluj (CL), with over 65.000 samples, of which over 6.500 are liverworts and
L238@P12: Iaşi (I), with over 3.000 samples (Ştefureac 1978c:565).
L239@P12: The bryophytes were described in several systematic botany and geobotany contributions, in which
L240@P12: hornworts, liverworts and mosses were approached (Iacobescu 1920; Zitti 1946:205-219; Guşuleac 1947-

L243@P12: 1970e:30).
L244@P12: Different liverwort classification systems were presented and proposed for acceptance (Ştefureac
L245@P12: 1970e:28-29), and in some botanical papers only the words mosses (Livescu 1970:271; Nemeş & Lungoci
L246@P12: 1972:275) or bryophyte appear (Ştefureac 1972d:56; Ştefureac 1975d:53).
