L193: Scabiosa atropurpurea (cite74†syn. Sixalix atropurpurea), the mourningbride, mournful widow, pincushion flower, or sweet scabious, is an cite76†ornamental plant of the genus cite71†Scabiosa in the family cite70†Caprifoliaceae .^{cite77†[2] } It is native to southern Europe.^{cite78†[3] }

L206: ## Description
L207: 
L208: [cite81†edit ]
L209: 
L210: Scabiosa atropurpurea has cite82†cauline leaves that are cite83†pinnately dissected ,^{cite78†[3] } plants have red to purple flowers. It is native to southern Europe. It propagates by seeds which are dispersed by animals.^{cite84†[4] }
L211: ## Cultivation
L212: 
L213: [cite85†edit ]
L214: 
L215: It prefers cooler climates; high temperatures and humidity negatively impact its growth. Scabiosa atropurpurea grows best in rich, alkaline soils high in organic matter with good drainage.^{cite86†[5] } It makes a good cut flower. Scabiosa atropurpurea 'Beaujolais Bonnets' is a cultivar grown for its long blooming period and burgundy flowers.^{cite87†[6] }
L216: ## Invasive species
L217: 
L218: [cite88†edit ]
L219: 
L220: It has become an cite89†invasive species outside of its native range, including the US state of California where it has invaded grasslands.^{cite78†[3] } It ranks as one of South Australia's most commonly recorded weeds.^{cite90†[7] }
