L358@P3: Introduction
L359@P3: SCOPE: All species of native, naturalized, and frequently cultivated plants known to occur
L360@P3: outdoors in Florida, USA are intended to be included in this flora. This perspective emphasizes
L361@P3: the plant species that are available for ecological interactions outdoors with animals, fungi,
L362@P3: bacteria, etc. Cultivated species are primarily limited to those having numerous observations
L363@P3: from different places (excluding indoor and potted plants, though some may trickle into the
L364@P3: flora). It is impossible to include all cultivated species and there is no intention here to do so.
L365@P3: Using botanical garden, herbarium, and iNaturalist records, Franck & Farid (2020) roughly
L366@P3: estimated ~7,500 plant species that existed in Florida solely from cultivation.
L367@P3: The boundaries of the flora are those of the state of Florida, USA, which are its terrestrial and
L368@P3: riverine (Perdido, Chattahoochee, St. Marys) borders with Alabama and Georgia, on the Gulf
L369@P3: Coast to 3 leagues (9 nautical miles, ~9°) from shore, and on the Atlantic Coast to 3 geographic
L370@P3: miles (~3°) from shore. For legal purposes, the Atlantic Ocean includes the waters east of the
L371@P3: peninsula and south of the Florida Keys until 24.583°, -83° (just SW of the Dry Tortugas), thus
L372@P3: including the Florida Straits; the Gulf of Mexico then includes the waters south of the panhandle
L373@P3: and west of the peninsula, between the Keys and the peninsula, and north and west of 24.583°,
L374@P3: -83°, thus including Florida Bay (United States vs. Florida, 363 U.S. 121, 1960; United States vs.
L375@P3: Florida, 425 U.S. 791, 1975; Florida Constitution, Art. 2, sect. 1; Florida Statues, Ch. 6.081, 6.09;
L376@P3: Jarvis 2003).
L377@P3: PURPOSE: As humans thrive from information, this document is a rich source of information on
L378@P3: the plants of Florida. Knowledge of the flora is essential for the management of resources,
L379@P3: including air, water, and soil quality, agriculture, mining, construction, restoration, recreation, and
L380@P3: conservation. The complex functions and immeasurable benefits of biodiversity (Gascon et al.
L381@P3: 2015; Strayer 2017) derive from a continuum of rare to common species (Mouillot et al. 2013;
L382@P3: González-del-Pliego et al. 2024).
L383@P3: CATEGORIES (see Essl et al. 2018; Pereyra 2019; Warren 2021).

L385@P3-4: diversification. These are listed in bold italics.-2-

L408@P4: Brief History of Florida, its Environment, Humans, and Botany (herbarium acronyms follow
L409@P4: Thiers 2025).
L410@P4: Approximately 700–450 million years ago (mya), the basement rocks underlying the Florida
L411@P4: platform were located in the Gondwana continent in the southern latitudes, during which time
L412@P4: the so-called Cambrian explosion occurred (~530 mya). Around 350–500 mya, many major
L413@P4: terrestrial lineages arose or diversified, including embryophytes (land plants), dikarya, hexapods,
L414@P4: arachnids, and tetrapods, at a time when atmospheric O2 levels were probably <20% by volume
L415@P4: (Mills et al. 2023). The amalgamation of Baltica, Laurentia, and Gondwana formed Pangea ~320–
L416@P4: 400 mya (Wu et al. 2021). Atmospheric O2 levels were probably >30% (Mills et al. 2023), just
L417@P4: before the Permian-Triassic extinction event (~250 mya). The Florida basement moved
L418@P4: northward through the equator ~200–300 mya and detached from the Africa and South America
L419@P4: landmasses to reach its current position as a peninsula ~100–200 mya (Veevers 2004; Hine et
L420@P4: al. 2017). The Turonian period, ~90–94 mya, marked the highest sea level (~+150–200 m) and
L421@P4: hottest temperatures of the past 500 million years (Miller et al. 2011; Judd et al. 2024). The large
L422@P4: asteroid impact at Chicxulub, ~66 mya, caused a mass extinction event (Morgan et al. 2022). A
L423@P4: sea level highstand as much as +100 m or more probably completely submerged all parts of the
L424@P4: Florida platform ~50 mya (Miller et al. 2011). At least some parts of the Florida platform have
L425@P4: probably remained emergent since the past 20–30 million years (James 1961). Continued
L426@P4: depositions of carbonates, evaporites, and siliciclastics formed the modern terrestrial land
L427@P4-5: (Bostick 2018; Upchurch 2019). Modern humans (Homo sapiens) arose roughly 300,000–-3-
L428@P5: 800,000 years ago in Africa, perhaps also partly in Eurasia (Bergström et al. 2021). The most
L429@P5: recent sea level highstand at +6 m was ~120,000 years ago (Georgiou et al. 2024), submerging
L430@P5: much of the southern Florida peninsula. Indelible, artistic depictions of animals were made by
L431@P5: humans as early as 50,000 years ago in Indonesia (Brumm et al. 2021; Oktaviana et al. 2024). The
L432@P5: last glacial maximum ~20,000–26,000 years ago had sea levels -120 m, exposing much more of
L433@P5: the Florida platform, and the emergence of Beringia allowed humans to colonize North America
L434@P5: (Bennett et al. 2021), during the coolest global temperatures in the past 500 million years (Judd
L435@P5: et al. 2024).
L436@P5: Knowledge of the Florida flora began in the panhandle as early as 15,000 years ago, when
L437@P5: humans occupied the Aucilla River region (Brown 1994; Halligan et al. 2016; Smith 2018; Faught
L438@P5: & Pevny 2019) and the Florida landmass was significantly larger, with sea levels of -80 m. Pollen
L439@P5: of Corylus and Picea was present (Watts et al. 1992; Carr 2012). Shortly after humans arrived,
L440@P5: numerous large animals went extinct or were extirpated (e.g. American lion, armadillo, bison,
L441@P5: camelids, carcara, dire wolf, ground sloth, horse, jaguar, mammoth, mastodon, peccary, sabertoothed tiger, spectacled bear, tapir; Webb 1974). Sea level rose rapidly, reaching ca. -10 m
L442@P5: ~7,000 years ago. Around this time or shortly thereafter, shell mound architecture may have
L443@P5: started in Florida (Marquardt 2010; Saunders & Russo 2011).
L444@P5: About 5,000–6,000 years ago, some of the earliest extant illustrations of plant species
L445@P5: appeared in Eurasia (e.g. Chogha Cheshmeh pottery, Battlefield Palette, Abydos tombs). Some
L446@P5: of the earliest extant botanical texts (or herbals) are traced to Assyrian and Babylonian
L447@P5: cuneiform, ~4,500 years ago (Thompson 1924; Borchardt 2002), and later examples include the
L448@P5: Ebers Papyrus (~3,500 years ago) and The Hippocratic Corpus (~2,500 years ago). Some 2,000
L449@P5: years ago, books with plant illustrations began to appear (Chavannes-Mazel 2023).
L450@P5: Humans reached the Florida Keys by 2,500 years ago (LeFebvre et al. 2022). By the early
L451@P5: 1500s, hundreds of thousands of peoples probably inhabited Florida across 100s of villages and
L452@P5: towns, subsisting from wild foods and cultivated crops in some areas, including beans, corn, and
L453@P5: cucurbits. Modern names for peoples historically inhabiting Florida include the Ais, Apalachi,
L454@P5: Calusa, Chacato, Hobe, Jaega, Jororo, Mayaca, Mayaimi, Matecumbe, Pansacola, Tequesta,
