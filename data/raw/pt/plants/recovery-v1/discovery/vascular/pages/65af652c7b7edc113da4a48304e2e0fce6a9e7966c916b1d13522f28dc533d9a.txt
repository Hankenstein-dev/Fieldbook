L429: cite365†Asteraceae or Compositae is a cite366†family of cite367†flowering plants (anthophytes) in the cite368†order cite369†Asterales .
L430: Asteraceae (commonly referred to as the aster, daisy, composite,^{cite370†[1] } or sunflower family), is a very large and widespread family,^{cite371†[2] }^{cite372†[3] } which includes over 32,000 currently accepted species, in over 1,900 genera (cite373†list ) in 13 subfamilies.^{cite374†[4] } In terms of numbers of species, the Asteraceae are rivaled only by the cite375†Orchidaceae .^{cite371†[2] }^{cite376†[5] } Nearly all Asteraceae bear their flowers in dense heads (capitula or pseudanthia) surrounded by cite377†involucral bracts .
L431: When viewed from a distance, each cite378†capitulum may appear to be a single flower. Enlarged outer (peripheral) flowers in the capitula may resemble petals, and the involucral bracts may look like a calyx. The name Asteraceae comes from the type genus cite379†Aster , from the cite380†Ancient Greek cite381†ἀστήρ†en.wiktionary.org , meaning star, and refers to the star-like form of the inflorescence.
L432: The alternative name Compositae is still valid under the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants.^{cite382†[6] } It refers to the "composite" nature of the capitula, which consist of a few or many individual flowers.
L433: Most members of Asteraceae are cite383†annual or cite384†perennial herbs, but a significant number are also shrubs, vines, or trees. The family has a cite385†cosmopolitan distribution , with species ranging from subpolar to tropical regions, colonizing a wide variety of habitats. The largest proportion of the species occur in the cite386†arid and semiarid regions of subtropical and lower temperate latitudes.^{cite387†[7] }
L434: 23,420 species of vascular plant have been recorded in South Africa, making it the sixth most species-rich country in the world and the most species-rich country on the African continent. Of these, 153 species are considered to be threatened.^{cite388†[8] } Nine cite389†biomes have been described in South Africa: cite390†Fynbos , Succulent cite391†Karoo , cite392†desert , cite393†Nama Karoo , cite394†grassland , cite395†savanna , cite396†Albany thickets , the cite397†Indian Ocean coastal belt , and cite398†forests .^{cite399†[9] }
L435: The 2018 cite400†South African National Biodiversity Institute 's cite401†National Biodiversity Assessment plant checklist lists 35,130 taxa in the phyla cite402†Anthocerotophyta (hornworts (6)), cite403†Anthophyta (flowering plants (33534)), cite404†Bryophyta (mosses (685)), cite405†Cycadophyta (cycads (42)), cite406†Lycopodiophyta (Lycophytes(45)), cite407†Marchantiophyta (liverworts (376)), cite408†Pinophyta (conifers (33)), and cite409†Pteridophyta (cryptogams (408)).^{cite410†[10] }
L436: 329 cite411†genera are represented in the literature. Listed cite412†taxa include cite413†species , cite414†subspecies , cite415†varieties , and cite416†forms as recorded, some of which have subsequently been allocated to other taxa as cite417†synonyms , in which cases the accepted taxon is appended to the listing.^{cite410†[10] } Multiple entries under alternative names reflect cite418†taxonomic revision over time.
