L23@P2: 1. Introduction
L24@P2: Phytoplankton are increasingly being used to monitor the ecological quality and health
L25@P2: of the water environment and also to measure the effectiveness of management or
L26@P2: restoration programmes or regulatory actions.
L27@P2: The European Water Framework Directive (2000/60/EC) requires member states to
L28@P2: monitor phytoplankton abundance and composition and a uniform procedure has been
L29@P2: developed by CEN.
L30@P2: The following guidance has been developed with reference to the CEN standard
L31@P2: “Water quality - Guidance standard on the enumeration of phytoplankton using inverted
L32@P2: microscopy (Utermöhl technique)” (CEN 2004), Test Methods and Procedures:
L33@P2: Freshwater Phytoplankton NRA (1995) and “PL100 Quantitative and qualitative
L34@P2: phytoplankton analysis” (SYKE) as well as reference texts such as Utermöhl (1958)
L35@P2: and Lund , Kipling and LeCren (1958).
L36@P2: Analysis should be carried out using sedimentation chambers with an inverted
L37@P2: microscope (Utermöhl technique).
L38@P2: This method is suitable for studies investigating the abundance, composition and
L39@P2: biovolume of phytoplankton in rivers and lakes.
L40@P2: 2. Terms and definitions
L41@P2: The terms and definitions used are as those as described in “Water quality - Guidance
L42@P2: standard on the enumeration of phytoplankton using inverted microscopy (Utermöhl
L43@P2: technique)” CEN 2004.
L44@P2: 3. Principles
L45@P2: The quantitative analysis described here includes the identification, enumeration and
L46@P2: calculation of biovolumes of Lugol’s iodine preserved water samples.
L47@P2: The preserved sample is thoroughly mixed and a sub-sample of known volume is
L48@P2: placed in a sedimentation chamber. When the algae have settled to the bottom of the
L49@P2: chamber, they are counted and identified using an inverted microscope.
L50@P2: The statistical reliability of the analysis depends upon the distribution of algal units/cells
L51@P2: within the sedimentation chamber and assumes that the algae are randomly distributed
L52@P2: within the chamber. If the algae are randomly distributed (and comply with a Poisson
L53@P2: distribution) then a 95% confidence limit of +_ 20% can be achieved by counting about
L54@P2: 100 algal units (Lund, Kipling and LeCren, 1958). Note that random distributions are
L55@P2: not always achieved in sedimentation chambers so alternative protocols or methods
L56@P2: may have to be used.
L57@P2: The counts for individual taxa are converted to algal biomass by using the cell/unit
L58@P2: volume of the count units. The volumes are based on measurements made during
L59@P2-3: counting. Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 3
L60@P3: 4. Equipment
L61@P3: Sedimentation chambers of 5 to100ml capacity (Hydro-Bios plankton chambers or
L62@P3: similar are recommended). Sedimentation chambers with volumes greater than
L63@P3: 10mls are usually combination chambers and consist of a base plate and upper
L64@P3: removable column which is slid aside once the algae have settled.
L65@P3: Inverted microscope with phase contrast (and/or DIC/Normarski) including:
L66@P3: - long working distance condenser with numerical aperture of >0.5
L67@P3: - 10x or 12.5x binocular eyepieces, one with a square grid e.g. Whipple
L68@P3: eyepiece graticule, Miller Square or similar, and another with a cross-hair
L69@P3: graticule (Figure 4.1)
L70@P3: - low power objective (5x or 10x)
L71@P3: - 10x, 20x, 40x and 100x oil immersion, phase &/or DIC objectives
L72@P3: - ideally the microscope should be fitted with a (digital) camera
L73@P3: - a mechanical stage
L74@P3: Figure 4.1 Eyepiece graticules
L75@P3: (a) Whipple graticule (b) cross-hair graticule
L76@P3: Variety of pipettes with wide bore tips
L77@P3: Glass cylinders for initial sedimentation
L78@P3: Supply of ultra high purity or membrane filtered water is required for topping up,
L79@P3: diluting and general cleaning.

L81@P3: iodide) in 1 l of distilled water then adding 50g I (iodine). Shake until all dissolved
L82@P3: and add 100g of glacial acetic acid. Store in dark bottle.

L98@P4: 5. Preparation of samples
L99@P4: 5.1 Acclimatisation.
L100@P4: Stored and preserved samples, sedimentation chambers and all equipment used
L101@P4: should be allowed to acclimatise to the same (room) temperature for at least 12 hours
L102@P4: (preferably 24 hours). This has been found to be one of the most important factors in
L103@P4: achieving a random distribution of algal cells in the chambers.
L104@P4: 5.2 Sample mixing.
L105@P4: Just before taking a sub-sample to fill the sedimentation chamber, the sample must be
L106@P4: thoroughly mixed. It is recommended that the mixing is done manually and that this is
L107@P4: standardised; the sample should be mixed using a combination of alternating
L108@P4: horizontal rolling and vertical tumbling (turning upside down) of the sample bottle for 2
L109@P4: minutes. These actions should be gentle and not involve any vigorous shaking.
L110@P4: 5.3 Sub-sample preparation and setting up chambers.
L111@P4: After thorough mixing, a known volume of sample is used to fill the sedimentation
L112@P4: chamber. The method and care taken to fill the chambers is crucial as it determines
L113@P4: the final distribution of settled algae in the chamber. If care is taken then a random
L114@P4: distribution allows uniform counting strategies and statistical methods to be used. If a
L115@P4: random distribution is not achieved then alternative and often more complex methods
L116@P4: must be employed.
L117@P4: The exact volume of sample used to fill the chamber depends on the phytoplankton
L118@P4: density. Large volumes of up to 100 ml may be required for oligotrophic waters whilst
L119@P4: at high phytoplankton densities dilution may be required.
L120@P4: Ideally, enough sample should be taken to completely fill the chamber in one addition,
L121@P4: either directly pouring from the sample bottle or using a wide-bore pipette. Fill a little
L122@P4: more than needed and allow a little to over-spill the chamber when you slide the lid
L123@P4: across.
L124@P4: This recommendation, to fill the chamber in one addition, raises a number of difficulties
L125@P4: for samples with either very low or very high phytoplankton densities. A number of
L126@P4: options are available for dealing with varying densities of phytoplankton:
L127@P4: 1) Use a sedimentation chamber of an appropriate size depending on how abundant
L128@P4: the algae are (chlorophyll concentrations may be used as a guide if available). For
L129@P4: example use a 2.5 ml chamber if densities are high or a 10 ml chamber if densities are
L130@P4-5: low.Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 5
L131@P5: 2) For very low densities, a pre-concentration step may be necessary. Let sample
L132@P5: settle in a measuring cylinder - usually 250 ml is sufficient. Leave for 3 days, then
L133@P5: draw off top water leaving 25 ml at bottom of cylinder (i.e. x10 concentration). If
L134@P5: needed this can be repeated with up to 4 250 ml cylinders and the 4 lots of 25 ml then
L135@P5: poured into a 100 ml measuring cylinder for a second pre-concentration to 10 ml (i.e.
L136@P5: x100 concentration).
L137@P5: 3) For very high densities, where 2.5 or 5 ml of sample is too much it may be
L138@P5: necessary to add a smaller measured volume. Use an accurate wide-bore pipette and
L139@P5: add 0.5 or 1 ml of sample to the chamber, then top up with distilled water. You must be
L140@P5: very careful not to add too much water - so none spills over. The alternative is to count
L141@P5: fields at x100 magnification.
L142@P5: A general rule is to aim for about four counting units per field of view at high
L143@P5: ( 400) magnification.
L144@P5: The following points should be noted:
L145@P5: - ensure all equipment and sample are acclimatised to room temperature and be
L146@P5: as constant as possible.
L147@P5: - place the sedimentation chamber on a horizontal flat surface – a perspex or thin
L148@P5: acrylic board (which is a poor heat conductor) is ideal – and it should be placed
L149@P5: away from strong heat, light and vibration sources.
L150@P5: - take enough sample, either directly from the bottle or with a pipette, to
L151@P5: completely fill the chamber in one addition.
L152@P5: - close the chamber with a thick cover slip, making sure air bubbles are avoided.
L153@P5: - make a note of the sample volume, sample site and date next to the chamber
L154@P5: or label the flat sedimentation board.
L155@P5: - chamber volumes should be measured accurately as their volume rating is only
L156@P5: a guide (5 ml chambers can range from 4.7 - 5.2 ml). To measure chamber
L157@P5: volume, weigh the chamber and lid whilst empty, then fill with distilled water and
L158@P5: re-weigh. The weight in grammes is equivalent to volume in ml. Repeat three
L159@P5: times and record the average.
L160@P5: - allow contents to settle, undisturbed, for at least 4 hours per cm height of
L161@P5: chamber. For 10 ml HydroBios chambers settle for at least 12 hours and for 50
L162@P5: ml chambers at least 48 hours settling time is recommended.
L163@P5: - if there are large numbers of buoyant cyanobacteria present you can add either
L164@P5: a drop of diluted detergent or glacial acetic acid to the chamber before closing
L165@P5: the chamber with the cover slide.
L166@P5: - after sedimentation if combination chambers are used, then slide the chamber
L167@P5: column aside and replace it with a thick cover slide. With both combination
L168@P5: chambers and 5 or 10 ml HydroBios type chambers, check for and try to avoid
L169@P5: introducing any air bubbles at this stage. This can be eliminated by carefully
L170@P5: topping up with UHP or membrane filtered water from a dropper pipette whilst
L171@P5-6: sliding the cover slide back into place. Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 6
L172@P6: - the sedimentation chamber should be gently moved to the microscope stage.
L173@P6: Open chambers should not be moved as the settled algae will be easily
L174@P6: disturbed.
L175@P6: After the appropriate settlement period and before counting two checks need to be
L176@P6: made:
L177@P6: 1. the overall distribution pattern of particles should be checked using a stereo zoom
L178@P6: or inverted microscope at very low power (4x or 10x objectives). A random
L179@P6: (Poisson) distribution is required and this is recognised by the irregular pattern,
L180@P6: often with open spaces. If particles are not randomly distributed and for example
L181@P6: are concentrated in one area of the chamber or found in concentric rings towards
L182@P6: the edge of the chamber then a new sample should be set up. The distribution of
L183@P6: particles/algal cells or units should be checked from time to time and this can be
L184@P6: done using the methods outlined in Annex F of the CEN method. The simplest of
L185@P6: these being to undertake a count of one taxa and calculate the variance to mean
L186@P6: ratio – this approximates the Chi squared distribution for n-1 df – the result is then
L187@P6: checked using a goodness to fit test for Chi squared.
L188@P6: 2. If the algal density is too low or too high then another sample should be set up and
L189@P6: the volume adjusted accordingly. It can sometimes be extremely difficult to judge
L190@P6: the correct volume but the general advice is
L191@P6: if there are too many particles then they may not settle independently and pile
L192@P6: up, also it can be very difficult to count and can lead to inaccuracies from
L193@P6: “fatigue”
L194@P6: if there are too few particles, the errors increase especially when counting
L195@P6: random fields or transects and large areas of the chamber need to be
L196@P6: observed. The density of detritus or non-algal particles is also important
L197@P6: especially if algal densities are low, and skill is needed to judge the ideal
L198@P6: volume to sediment.
L199@P6: 6. Counting
L200@P6: 6.1 General
L201@P6: The counting procedure involves recording the taxa observed and the number of algal
L202@P6: units (objects) for each taxon in a known area of the counting chamber. As the volume
L203@P6: of sample added and area of the whole chamber observed is recorded, the
L204@P6: concentration of each individual taxon can then be calculated.
L205@P6: The observed taxa are identified to the required taxonomic level (see section 6.3). It is
L206@P6: very important to remember that it is better to correctly identify algae to lower
L207@P6: taxonomic level than misidentify to a higher level.
L208@P6: It is useful to scan the sample at a variety of magnifications before the quantitative
L209@P6: analysis is undertaken and to compile a taxa list before beginning the count.
L210@P6: If there is evidence of significant benthic contamination or littoral taxa present
L211@P6: (eg periphyton) such that the open water taxa are obscured, then it may not be
L212@P6: worth undertaking a full count.
L213@P6: Where small numbers of littoral or benthic taxa such as Surirella and Nostoc, are
L214@P6-7: present, they should not be counted. Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 7
L215@P7: 6.2 Counting procedure
L216@P7: The count should be carried out in the following manner;
L217@P7: a low magnification (e.g. x 40 or x100), whole chamber count to pick up large taxa,
L218@P7: followed by;
L219@P7: transect counts at an intermediate magnification ( x250), which are helpful to
L220@P7: enumerate “intermediate-sized” taxa that are too small for the low-magnification
L221@P7: count but too large to be reasonably counted using fields of view at high
L222@P7: magnification, followed by;
L223@P7: a high magnification count (x400 or greater) using fields of view. This picks up the
L224@P7: small taxa. Aim to count 100 fields of view (i.e. about 400 units assuming the
L225@P7: recommended sample concentration)
L226@P7: Details are provided in sections 6.2.1 to 6.2.3 below.
L227@P7: 6.2.1 Counting the whole chamber at low magnification for large taxa.
L228@P7: Working at low power (x40 to x100) the whole chamber should be scanned in a series
L229@P7: of horizontal transects (figure 6.1) and the larger taxa (e.g. Ceratium), large colonial or
L230@P7: filamentous forms (e.g. Microcystis, Fragilaria) counted. A cross-hair graticule eyepiece
L231@P7: (figure 4.1) is used when counting the whole chamber. Algae that lie between the two
L232@P7: horizontal lines are counted as they pass the horizontal line. Algal objects that cross
L233@P7: the top line are included whilst those crossing the bottom line are not and will be
L234@P7-8: counted on the next transect (or vice versa).Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 8
L235@P8: Figure 6.1 Counting method for whole chamber.
L236@P8: 6.2.2 Counting transects.
L237@P8: Algal objects larger than approximately 20 µm (small Cryptomonas) can be counted at
L238@P8: a magnification of approximately x200 in 3 - 5 randomly chosen diameter transects of
L239@P8: the counting chamber (figure 6.2). The cross-hair eyepiece and method for counting
L240@P8: algal objects described in the section above is used also. The chamber is rotated
L241@P8: between transect to randomly chosen positions.
L242@P8: Figure 6.2 Counting method for diameter transects.
L243@P8: 6.2.3 Counting randomly selected fields.
L244@P8: Small algae, less than about 20 µm (e.g. Rhodomonas, small centric diatoms), should
L245@P8: be counted in 100 (or more) randomly selected fields at x400 magnification (or greater)
L246@P8: using a square or Whipple graticule, Miller Square or similar in the ocular eyepiece to
L247@P8: delineate the counting area. Fields can be selected either in a pseudo-random way by
L248@P8: the counter or using a mechanical stage with a vernier that allows random positions to
L249@P8: be found from random number coordinates or using an electronic stage with built in
L250@P8-9: random position control. Guidance_Phytoplankton counting_Feb2014 9

L252@P9: identified algal units (cells, colonies or filaments).

L254@P9: whether algal objects lying across the grid lines are counted in or out. A simple rule
L255@P9: should be adopted as described in the CEN method (2004) e.g. algal objects (cells,
L256@P9: colonies or filaments) crossing both the top and the left hand side of the grid are not
L257@P9: counted whilst those crossing the bottom or right hand side of the grid are counted

L262@P9: 6.2.4 Point to consider when counting
L263@P9: Algal objects and counting units: Algal objects or counting units are independent
L264@P9: algal cells, colonies or filaments/trichomes. One species or taxa may be present in
L265@P9: the sample as different counting units and may be counted at different
L266@P9: magnifications. For example, Microcystis colonies will probably be counted in the
L267@P9: whole-chamber or transect but individual Microcystis cells (which may be present if
L268@P9: colonies are disintegrating) will be counted in random fields. Similarly Dinobryon
L269@P9: colonies are most likely to be counted in diameter transects and single Dinobryon
L270@P9: cells will be counted in random fields.
L271@P9: Other examples of counting/algal units include:
L272@P9: Colonies e.g. Aphanothece, Coelosphaerium, Sphaerocystis
L273@P9: Algal cells which can occur as single cells but also form colonies, e.g. Aulacoseira,
L274@P9: Dinobryon, Melosira.
L275@P9: Colonies which have more or less permanent cell numbers, e.g.
L276@P9: Desmodesmus/Scenedesmus (2, 4 or 8 cells), Pandorina (16 cells) Crucigenia (4
L277@P9: cells)
L278@P9: Filaments or trichomes e.g. Anabaena, Aphanizomenon, Oscillatoria, Planktothrix
L279@P9: Colonies where the size and shape vary e.g. Microcystis

L282@P9: filaments should be treated as individual algal objects or units as described above.

L295@P10: For estimating biovolumes of colonies or coenobia:-
L296@P10: Using cell volumes – make direct counts of cells in ‘sub-colonies’ or small
L297@P10: areas. These can then be multiplied up by number of ‘sub-colonies’ or the
L298@P10: ratio of small area to whole colony to get the total cell numbers, e.g.
L299@P10: Microcystis, Woronichinia, etc.
L300@P10: Using colony/coenobium measurements – measure colony width and depth
L301@P10: e.g. Pediatrum, Microcystis (using Reynolds & Jaworski’s formula
L302@P10: embedded in counter spreadsheet)
L303@P10: For estimating biovolumes of filaments:-
L304@P10: Using filament measurements – calculate mean dimensions by measuring
L305@P10: the length and diameter of at least 30 filaments. For high-magnification
L306@P10: random field of view counts, only the lengths of the filaments lying within the
L307@P10: grid should be measured. For whole chamber or transect counts at low or
L308@P10: intermediate magnification whole filament lengths can be measured.
L309@P10: Using cell volumes – combine counting of filaments with the mean numbers
L310@P10: of cells per filament, e.g. Aphanizomenon
L311@P10: Count the number of filaments in the normal way (transects or random
L312@P10: fields) and measure the length of at least thirty filaments to calculate the
L313@P10: average length (see above for difference in measuring filament length
L314@P10: between transect and random fields approaches)
L315@P10: From up to 10 filaments, calculate the average number of cells per unit
L316@P10: length (e.g. 20 µm). This can be measured at a higher magnification if
L317@P10: the cells are small or hard to distinguish easily (e.g. some species of
L318@P10: Oscillatoria).
L319@P10: Then the number of cells per filament is calculated by multiplying up the
L320@P10: average filament length by the average number of cells per unit length.
L321@P10: Where the algae form spiral filaments e.g. Anabaena circinalis, the average
L322@P10: number of cells per gyre is counted and then the number of gyres per
L323@P10: filament is estimated. The two numbers are multiplied together to give the
L324@P10: estimated number of cells per filament.
L325@P10: 6.3 Identification and coding
L326@P10: Appendix A provides a list of taxa which is to be used to guide the required level of
L327@P10: identification. It includes Whitton Codes, accepted names, biovolume formulae and
L328@P10: biovolume ranges, where available. If taxa can be identified but are not included within
L329@P10: this list, photographs and drawings (including measurements) should be taken and the
L330@P10: inclusion of the ‘new’ taxa to the list should be checked with the Project Manager.
L331@P10: The standard flora for identification is the Freshwater Algal Flora of the British Isles
L332@P10: (Whitton et al., 2003) but other identification guides are also available and may be
L333@P10: used if they prove more helpful for certain taxonomic groups (see Section 10).

L335@P10: lower taxonomic level than misidentify to a higher level.

L366@P11: 7. Calculation of phytoplankton biovolume
L367@P11: Biovolumes must be measured for all taxa and is done by assigning simple geometric
L368@P11: shapes to each cell, filament or colony, measuring the appropriate dimensions and
