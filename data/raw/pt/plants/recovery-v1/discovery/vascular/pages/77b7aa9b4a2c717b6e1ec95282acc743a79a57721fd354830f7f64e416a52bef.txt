L273: One touch… and you are hooked! 😍

L282: Crassula rogersii is a small Crassula that wins you first through touch and immediately afterwards through colour. Thick oval leaves are covered in fine velvety hairs that soften the green and make the surface resemble fabric. Upright stems branch into a miniature shrub and, with sufficient light, edges may take on copper, reddish or warm brown tones. The result is rich and warm, ideal for a bright position needing texture rather than simply another green leaf.
L283: Its appearance has strong retail appeal, but the story remains authentically botanical: this is an accepted South African species rather than an unnamed hybrid. Over time it develops a more mature shrubby form, making it a plant that evolves beautifully with a collection. For customers who enjoy tactile plants, warm colour and low but precise care, it is one of the most satisfying and distinctive choices in the category.

L341: #### Light and position
L342: 
L343: Provide strong filtered light and gradually add morning or late-afternoon sun. Low intensity causes long internodes and greener sparse growth; abrupt scorching exposure can burn the hairy leaves.
L344: #### Watering correctly
L345: 
L346: Wait for the root zone to dry fully, then water deeply around the base rather than over the hairs. Allow complete drainage. If lower leaves soften while the mix is damp, inspect roots rather than adding water.
L347: 
L348: #### Air and humidity
L349: 
L350: Leaf hairs retain droplets, so airflow is important after watering. Avoid misting and dense contact with neighbouring foliage.
L351: #### Temperature and seasonal rhythm
L352: 
L353: Active growth coincides with adequate light and mild to warm temperatures. It may slow under low winter light or extreme heat, reducing water use accordingly.
L354: 
L355: #### Substrate
L356: 
L357: Use a porous Crassula mix with mineral components and a pot with a clear drainage hole. Support the base without burying lower leaves.
L358: #### Feeding, repotting and handling
L359: 
L360: Feed lightly while new leaf pairs appear. In fresh Sybasoil, wait about two months before diluted Cactus & Succulent Focus.

L364: #### Accepted name Crassula rogersii
L365: 
L366: Crassula rogersii Schönland is an accepted species in Crassulaceae. It is not a cultivar, and its scientific epithet honours the collector or botanist associated with its description.
L367: #### Native to the Cape Province
L368: 
L369: Its natural range lies in South Africa’s Cape Province. Cultivated lines come from nursery propagation and should not be confused with recent wild collection.
L370: #### Xeric shrubland ecology
L371: 
L372: The species belongs to flora adapted to strong light, seasonal moisture and rapidly aerated soils. Hairy leaves are part of that adaptation.
L373: 
L374: From natural habitat to care: Velvet does not mean a need for atmospheric humidity; it is xeric epidermal protection. Cape Province origin translates into quality light, clean airflow and meaningful watering cycles followed by complete drying.

L376: #### Hairs alter appearance
L377: 
L378: Fine pubescence diffuses light, reduces shine and creates a softer colour impression.
L379: 
L380: #### Opposite leaf pairs
L381: 
L382: Leaves develop in opposite pairs, each new pair turning to build a rhythmic four-ranked structure.
L383: 
L384: #### Stems become woody
L385: 
L386: The base hardens with age, allowing the plant to form a small persistent shrubby crown.
L387: #### CAM water economy
L388: 
L389: This metabolic adaptation limits daytime water loss but does not remove the need for healthy oxygenated roots.
L390: 
L391: Greenleaf Tip: Clean the hairs only with a dry soft brush. A wet cloth mats them, leaves marks and unnecessarily raises surface moisture.
