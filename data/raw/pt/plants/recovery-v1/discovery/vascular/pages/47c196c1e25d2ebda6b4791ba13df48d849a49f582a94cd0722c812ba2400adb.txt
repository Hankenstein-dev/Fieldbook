L18@P0: Introduction
L19@P0: In the subtribe Aveninae (Poaceae, Poeae), recent molecular and morphological studies have segregated three smaller genera: Helictochloa Romero Zarco, Trisetopsis Röser
L20@P0: & A.Wölk, and Tzveleviochloa Röser & A.Wölk from Helictotrichon Besser (RomeroZarco 2011, Wölk and Röser 2013, Wölk and Röser 2017). The genus Tzveleviochloa is
L21@P0: a native to South-east Asia (Wölk and Röser 2017, Soreng et al. 2022), comprising five
L22@P0: species: T. burmanica (Bor) Röser & A.Wölk, T. parviflora (Hook.f.) Röser & A.Wölk,
L23@P0: T. potaninii (Tzvelev) Röser & A.Wölk, T. schmidii (Hook.f.) E.A.Kellogg, and T. yadavii D.Prasad & P.Agnihotri, of which four species are found in India, two in China,
L24@P0: and one in Bhutan and Myanmar (Wölk and Röser 2017, Prasad et al. 2021a, b). It is
L25@P0: morphologically intermediate between Trisetum Pers. and Helictotrichon s. str. and has
L26@P0: two aristae at the lemma apex (like Trisetum), lodicule asymmetrically lobed with one
L27@P0: side lobe, and ovary densely hirsute at the apex or upper half (like Helictotrichon s. str.)
L28@P0: (Wölk and Röser 2017).
L29@P0: Helictorichon uniyalii was described by Kandwal and Gupta (2010) based on specimens collected from the Dhakuri top, Pindari Valley, Uttarakhand (India). It was recognized by its 1 mm long ligules, relatively narrow panicle, 6–7 mm long spikelets,
L30@P0: mucronate glumes, approximately 6 mm long and keeled lower glumes, and approximately 2 mm long anthers (Kandwal and Gupta 2010). The original description of H.
L31@P0: uniyalii did not include the morphological features of the lemma apex, lodicule, and
L32@P0: hair type of the ovary, which are key features for placing the species among the newly

L48@P1: Material and methods
L49@P1: In the present work, morpho-taxonomic studies were done.
L50@P1: Specimens were examined at BSD (Botanical Survey of India,
L51@P1: Northern Circle, India) and LWG (CSIR-National Botanical
L52@P1: Research Institute, India). The protologue and relevant literature of H. uniyalii were studied intensively. In addition,

L54@P1: habitats where it was likely to be found. We prepared herbarium specimens in the laboratory and then deposited them

L61@P1: Morphology
L62@P1: In the protologue, Helictotrichon uniyalii was described
L63@P1: to have ‘lemma 6.0–6.5 mm long, 5-nerved, narrowly

L66@P2: lanceolate, bilobed at the tip, awned; lodicules inverted
L67@P2: common in shape; ovary hairy at the top’ by Kandwal and
L68@P2: Gupta (2010). Furthermore, the illustrations provided by
L69@P2: Kandwal and Gupta (2010) demonstrated that the lower
L70@P2: glume is almost equal to the upper glume and completely
L71@P2: covers the florets, and lodicules are almost entire, creating ambiguity in the identity of species. However, in
L72@P2: Helictotrichon (South-east Asian species) the lower and upper
L73@P2: glumes are never of the same length, and the lower glume
L74@P2: is always shorter than the adjacent lemma. After examining
L75@P2: specimens, we found that the lower glume of H. uniyalii is
L76@P2: almost half the lemma length, and the ratio of lower glume
L77@P2: to upper glume is 0.58–0.62. As previously stated, the taxonomic position of H. uniyalii was ambiguous due to lack
L78@P2: of information on morphological characters such as lemma
L79@P2: apex, lodicule shape, and ovary hairs. However, the examined specimens show that H. uniyalii has two aristae which
L80@P2: are unequal in length (0.28–0.67 mm) at the lemma apex,
L81@P2: lodicules asymmetrically lobed with side lobes 0.2–0.4 mm
L82@P2: long, and ovary densely hirsute at the upper half, which fit
L83@P2: well to the circumscription of Tzveleviochloa. Therefore, we
L84@P2: assign H. uniyalii to Tzveleviochloa and propose the new
L85@P2: combination, T. uniyalii.
