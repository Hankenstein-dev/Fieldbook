L118: Abstract
L119: The integration of fine-scale taxonomic results into a comprehensive phylogenetic classification remains an ongoing challenge in modern systematics, particularly for infraspecific variants and interspecific hybrids, which are often overlooked. To address the resulting nomenclatural gaps and instabilities, this study provides a significant update by introducing numerous nomenclatural novelties for selected vascular plant genera, with a special focus on nothotaxa.
L120: In total, this work validates 452 nomenclatural novelties across 31 families, including Aizoaceae, Amaranthaceae, Amaryllidaceae, Apiaceae, Apocynaceae, Asteraceae, Berberidaceae, Cactaceae, Cistaceae, Crassulaceae, Cupressaceae, Cyperaceae, Equisetaceae, Fabaceae, Gesneriaceae, Melanthiaceae, Orchidaceae, Poaceae, Ranunculaceae and Saxifragaceae, ect.
L121: Among them are the establishment of 12 new nothogenera: × Osteolaena [Amaranthaceae], × Chrysania, × Cicuca, × Ozodes and × Psepaurea [Asteraceae], × Turdenicarpus [Cactaceae], × Astoxytropis [Fabaceae], × Eucongia [Gesneriaceae], × Pseudillium [Melanthiaceae], × Batepalum [Orchidaceae], × Rankwithia [Ranunculaceae] and × Mitarella [Saxifragaceae]; 59 nothoranks [five nothosubgenera, 42 nothosections, six nothosubsections and six nothoseries] under seven nothogenera [× Anacamptorchis, × Dactylocamptis, × Dactylodenia, × Gymnanacamptis, × Orchidactylorhiza, × Orchigymnadenia and × Orchimantoglossum] for the first time and as additions to five natural genera [Cattleya, Dactylorhiza, Paphiopedilum, Phalaenopsis and Pulsatilla], respectively; moreover, 360 new nomenclatural acts are proposed, covering 139 new combinations, 209 new combinaton and status, six new status, and six new replacement names; besides, nine names are synonymized under eight taxa.
L122: These changes reconcile hybrid taxonomy with contemporary phylogenetic insights, stabilize nomenclature and provide a crucial reference to support accurate identification, biodiversity documentation, and conservation efforts.

L136: 1. Introduction
L137: Building upon the broadly established phylogenetic framework at higher taxonomic levels, the continuous publication of new studies at the genus and species level necessitates the integration of their results into a complete classification down to the lowest ranks. This process creates a demand for new nomenclatural combinations, especially for infraspecific variations (subspecies, varieties, forms) and hybrids, which are typically overlooked in molecular phylogenetic studies.
L138: These often poorly differentiated microtaxa can possess significant local heritage value that risks being lost when they are subsumed into broader taxa. Furthermore, updating major nomenclatural databases requires incorporating findings from these numerous publications.
L139: Despite phylogenetic classification becoming standard in modern biology, the practical integration of fine-scale nomenclatural results remains an incomplete and challenging task, highlighting the need to validate new names for these overlooked ranks to support future identification and conservation.
L140: The establishment of a stable nomenclature is particularly critical and challenging for nothospecies (interspecific hybrids), given their significant practical importance. Their value spans the creation of ornamental horticultural plants, the development of improved food crops, and their potential as medical resources.
L141: However, their nomenclature is frequently destabilized by ongoing taxonomic revisions driven by advances in molecular systematics, which routinely redefine genera through lumping and splitting. This instability underscores an urgent need for comprehensive compilations that reconcile hybrid taxonomy with contemporary phylogenetic classifications.
L142: Systematic efforts to document nothogeneric names and establish new nomenclatural arrangements are therefore essential to provide a reliable reference framework and accommodate newly described hybrids.
