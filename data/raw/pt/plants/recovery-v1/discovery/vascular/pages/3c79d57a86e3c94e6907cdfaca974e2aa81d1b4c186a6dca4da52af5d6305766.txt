L161@P3: There are at least 10 species world-wide in the genus
L162@P3: Athalamia, with A. spathysii the only representative in
L163@P3: southern Africa, A. pulcherrima (Steph.) Hatt. being from
L164@P3: Ethiopia and distinguished by larger, hyaline ventral scales

L169@P3: hyalina (Sommerf.) Hatt. In A. spathysii the radial walls

L171@P3: response to growing in sunlight, as was found in cultivation studies by Miiller (1951-1958). Shimizu & Hattori
L172@P3: (1954), however, reported for this genus, that they had
L173@P3: observed pores with both thickened radial walls (stellate)
L174@P3: or with thin walls (not stellate) on the same plant!
L175@P3: According to Muller (1939), Amell (1963), Vanden
L176@P3: Berghen (1968) and Volk (1979) oil bodies are absent in
L177@P3: the genus Athalamia. I also found them to be absent in
L178@P3: A. spathysii, but fresh material was not available for study.
L179@P3: Shimizu & Hattori (1954), however, reported oil cells in
L180@P3: Athalamia glauco-virens as well as in A. nana (Shimizu
L181@P3: & Hattori 1955). Schuster (1992) states that sparse, scattered oil cells may be present or absent.
L182@P3: Asexual reproduction is said to be absent. Stephani
L183@P3: (1895) suspected that adventitious growth in the form of

L186@P3: of the thallus continues to grow and increase in length,
L187@P3: beyond the archegoniophore after the latter has differentiated.

L189@P3: midrib of A. pinguis, the hyphae having penetrated the
