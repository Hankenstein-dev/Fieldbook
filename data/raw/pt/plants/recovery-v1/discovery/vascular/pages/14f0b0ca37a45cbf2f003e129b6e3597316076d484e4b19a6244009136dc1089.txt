L165: Spartina is a cite87†section of the genus cite79†Sporobolus , plants in the cite77†grass family , frequently found in coastal salt marshes. It was first established as a separate genus in 1789,^{cite88†[2] } and reduced to a section in 2014.
L166: Species are commonly known as cordgrass or cord-grass,^{cite89†[3] } and are native to the coasts of the Atlantic Ocean in western and southern Europe, north-western and southern Africa, the cite90†Americas and the islands of the southern Atlantic Ocean; one or two species also occur on the western coast of North America and in cite91†freshwater habitats inland in the Americas. The highest species diversity is on the east coasts of North and South America, particularly cite92†Florida .
L167: They form large, often dense cite93†colonies , particularly on coastal cite94†salt marshes , and grow quickly. The species vary in size from 0.3–2 m tall. Many of the species will produce cite95†hybrids if they come into contact.
L168: ## Taxonomy
L169: 
L170: [cite96†edit ]
L171: 
L172: In 2014, the taxon Spartina was subsumed into the genus Sporobolus and reassigned to the taxonomic status of cite87†section ,^{cite97†[4] } but it may still be possible to see Spartina referred to as an accepted genus. In 2019, an interdisciplinary team of experts from all continents (except for Antarctica) coauthored a report published in the journal Ecology supporting Spartina as a genus.^{cite98†[5] }
L173: The section name Spartina is derived from σπαρτίνη (spartínē), the cite99†Greek word for a cord made from Spanish broom (cite100†Spartium junceum ).^{cite101†[6] }

L203: ## Ecology
L204: 
L205: [cite120†edit ]
L206: 
L207: Species of the section Spartina are used as food plants by the cite121†larvae of some cite122†Lepidoptera species including the cite123†Aaron's skipper , which feeds exclusively on cite103†smooth cordgrass , and the cite124†engrailed moth.
L208: Some species of the section Spartina are considered as ecosystem engineers that can strongly influence the physical and biological environment.^{cite125†[7] }^{cite126†[8] } This is particularly important in areas where invasive Spartina species significantly alter their new environment, with impacts to native plants and animals.^{cite127†[9] }
L209: ### As an invasive species
L210: 
L211: [cite128†edit ]
L212: 
L213: Three of the species placed in Spartina have become cite129†invasive plants in some countries. In cite130†British Columbia , Sporobolus anglica, also known as English cordgrass, is an aggressive, aquatic alien that invades mud flats, salt marshes and beaches, out-competing native plants, spreading quickly over mud flats and leaving large Spartina meadows.^{cite131†[10] } It is also invasive in China and California.^{cite127†[9] }
L214: Sporobolus montevidensis and Sporobolus pumilus have become invasive on the Iberian Peninsula and the west coast of the United States^{cite127†[9] }^{cite132†[11] }^{cite133†[12] }
L215: 
L216: Sporobolus alterniflorus and its hybrids with other Spartina species are invasive in numerous locations around the globe, including China, California, England, France, and Spain.^{cite127†[9] }^{cite134†[13] }
L217: ## Cultivation
L218: 
L219: [cite135†edit ]
L220: 
L221: Species of the section Spartina have been planted to reclaim cite136†estuarine areas for cite137†farming , to supply cite138†fodder for cite139†livestock , and to prevent cite140†erosion . Various members of the genus (especially Sporobolus alterniflorus and its derivatives, Sporobolus anglicus and Sporobolus × townsendii) have spread outside of their native boundaries and become cite129†invasive .
L222: Big cordgrass (S. cynosuroides) is used in the construction of bull's eye targets for sports archery. A properly constructed target can stop an arrow safely without damage to the arrowhead as it lodges in the target.^{cite141†[14] }
