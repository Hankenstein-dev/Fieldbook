L137: Santiago Castroviejo y Bolíbar (abbreviation Castrov.; 27 August 1946 in Tirán, cite47†Moaña – 30 September 2009 in cite44†Madrid ) was a Spanish cite46†botanist . He is credited with identifying and naming 63 species in the cite48†IPNI (International Plant Names Index). The abbreviation "Castrov." is used to indicate Santiago Castroviejo as an authority in the scientific description and classification of species.[ 1 ]
L138: ## Biography
L139: 
L140: [cite49†edit ]
L141: He earned his PhD in cite50†Biology from the cite45†Complutense University of Madrid in 1972.[ 2 ] Santiago Castroviejo was a research professor at the Real Jardín Botánico (cite51†Spanish National Research Council ), Director of the cite52†Real Jardín Botánico de Madrid between 1984 and 1994.
L142: He was the scientific director of the Coiba Project at the cite53†Coiba Biological Station (cite54†Panama ), principal investigator of the cite55†Flora iberica Project, principal investigator of the Anthos Project – Information System on Spanish Plants, member of the executive committee and the Steering Committee respectively of the projects: Euro+Med PlantBase and Species Plantarum Project-Flora of the World, President of the cite56†Spanish Royal Society of Natural History , Academician of the cite57†Real Academia de Ciencias Exactas, Físicas y Naturales and Médaille du Conseil de la cite58†Société Botanique de France , among other important scientific distinctions.
L143: He was awarded the National Research Prize in 2009. He supervised 19 doctoral theses[ 3 ] and authored more than 150 research papers published in national and international scientific journals.
L144: The standard cite59†author abbreviation Castrov. is used to indicate this person as the author when cite60†citing a cite61†botanical name .[ 4 ]
