L63:   * cite27†Pulicaria inuloides (Poir.) DC. L64: ### According to Flora of Iraq
L65: 
L66: Ghazanfar, S. A., Edmondson, J. R. & Hind, D. J. N. (Eds). (2019). Flora of Iraq, Volume 6: Compositae.Kew Publishing
L67: Morphology General Habit
L68:     Annual or perennial herb 30–50 cm tall with a much-branched  stem,  side  branches making an angle of 45° to main stem and often overtopping it, green to reddish, pilose and glandular with hairs finally evanescent
L69: Morphology Leaves
L70:     Leaves oblong to oblanceolate, sessile or with a petiolate base, 1.25–6 × 0.2–4 cm, entire, pilose and sessile-glandular on both surfaces
L71: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences
L72:     Inflorescence consisting of remote capitula on 1–3.5 cm long pedicels
L73: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences Bracts Involucre
L74:     Involucre 5–7 × 9–12 mm; phyllaries linear, herbaceous, glandular with long spreading hairs
L75: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Florets
L76:     Female florets 2–4 mm long, disc florets 2–3 mm long, lobes glandular and papillose
L77: Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
L78:     Achenes ellipsoid, 1.25–1.5 mm long, pilose; outer pappus a toothed cup, inner of 6–10 slender setae 2.5– 3.5 mm.
L79: Ecology
L80:     In damp places, near streams and wells, marshy fields and sides of ditches, on silty, sandy and clay soils; alt. 3–250(–1450) m;
L81: Phenology
L82:     flowering & fruiting: (Apr.-)Jun.-Aug.(-Sep.).
L83: Distribution
L84:     Occasional in the mountain region of Iraq, more frequent in the steppe region and also on the irrigated alluvial plain in the desert region. Aegean Is., Cyprus, Palestine, Jordan, Sinai, Egypt, Syria, Lebanon, Turkey, Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Saudi Arabia, C Asia (Turkmenia), N Africa (Algeria, Morocco), Mauritania.
L85: cite28†[FIQ] L86: ### According to Flora of Oman
L87: 
L88: Shahina A. Ghazanfar (2015). Flora of Oman, Volume 3: Loganiaceae - Asteraceae. National Botanic Garden of Belgium
L89: Vernacular
L90:     lqat, abu ‘ain safra.
L91: Morphology General Habit
L92:     Aromatic annual to pauciennial herb, to 70 cm high, glandular and with long hairs, usually with several, erect, leafy and already basally branched stems
L93: Morphology Stem
L94:     Stem leaves up to 90 × 12 mm, spatulate, obtuse to rounded, entire to denticulate, base semiamplexicaul
L95: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences Capitulum
L96:     Capitula radiate, at the end of the stems in a loosely corymbiform synflorescence, rarely single
L97: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences Bracts Involucre
L98:     Involucre 5–6 mm long, cup-shaped to hemispherical, 6–12 mm in diameter, with spreading hairs and glandular; phyllaries many, very narrowly linear, outer ones herbaceous, inner ones with scarious margin
L99: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers
L100:     Flowers yellow; ray flowers pistillate, ray lamina 1–2 mm long, disk flowers perfect, tubular
L101: Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
L102:     Achenes ± 1 mm long, fusiform, somewhat hairy
L103: Morphology Reproductive morphology Seeds Pappus
L104:     Pappus of a corona 0.2–0.3 mm long and 8–12 bristles 2.5–3 mm long.
L105: Phenology
L106:     Flowering and fruiting: September to April(?).
L107: Ecology
L108:     In and along wadis and irrigation channels, near water.
L109: Altitude
L110:     200(?)–600(?) m.
L111: Distribution
L112:     Distributed from Morocco across N Africa (and extending to the southern Saharan mountain ranges and the Horn of Africa), the SE Mediterranean and the Arabian Peninsula to Iraq, Iran and Afghanistan. Elsewhere in the Arabian Peninsula found in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Yemen. Northern Oman, Hajar mountains and Southern Oman, Dhofar.
L113: Note
L114:     Pulicaria arabica has been distinguished by Gamal-Eldin (1981) from P. inuloides because of its life cycle, mostly divaricate branching, mostly straw-coloured stems, silky indumentum of non-tubercle-based hairs and mostly shorter leaves. P. inuloides however flowers in the first year, and we fail to see clear distinguishing characters between the two taxa. Gamal-Eldin admitted that in many cases it is difficult to place a plant in either taxa. Therefore P.
L115: arabica is here accepted in a wider sense, including P. inuloides, which may be distinguished at subspecies level as P. arabica subsp. inuloides (Poir.) Maire, if appropriate. The photo in Collenette (Wildflowers of Saudi Arabia, 1999: 211) of P. inuloides is misidentified and is actually that of P. schimperi. The name Pulicaria inuloides (Poir.) DC. is illegitimate because of the existence of an older homonym that is a synonym of P. vulgaris Gaertn. and would have to be replaced by P. longifolia Boiss.

L132: 
L133: M. Thulin et al. Flora of Somalia, Vol. 1-4 [updated 2008] https://plants.jstor.org/collection/FLOS
L134: Morphology General Habit
L135:     Perennial herb 5–90 cm high; branches often reddish, scabrid
L136: Morphology Leaves
L137:     Leaves lanceolate, 2–9´0.4–1 cm, dentate, obtuse, pubescent to scabrid, glandular, sticky
L138: Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences
L139:     Capitula in lax corymbs; involucre 5–6 mm long; phyllaries lanceolate, acute, scabridulous and glandular
L140: Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Corolla
L141:     Corolla yellow or cream; ray florets 4–6 mm long, of which ray 2–5 mm; disk florets 4–4.5 mm long, with hairy apex

L144: Distribution

L146: Ecology
