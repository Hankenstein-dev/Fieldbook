L12: cite8†What is a Genus (pl. Genera)? L13: 
L14: In botanical taxonomy, a genus (plural genera) is a rank used to group closely related species within a family. In the hierarchy, genus sits below family and above species.
L15: 
L16: Genera are defined by shared morphological, anatomical, and genetic characteristics (for example, features of flowers, fruits, seeds, or leaves) that indicate a close evolutionary relationship among the species they contain.
L17: Each genus can include one or more species. Examples include Rosa (roses) and Solanum (nightshades, including tomato and eggplant).

L23: 
L24: cite6†Suggest a correction! L25: Biscutella, a Mediterranean-centered genus of Brassicaceae, comprises about 52 accepted species; it is typified by Biscutella laevigata (POWO, 2024). Plants are mostly small herbs of open, calcareous rock, scree, and alpine habitats, with centers of diversity in the western and central Mediterranean and substantial endemism in the Alps and the Iberian Peninsula (Kiefer & Heubl, 2014; GBIF, 2024).
L26: The silicle is diagnostic: two compressed, bivalvar, laterally oriented valves that yield a lenticular–scutellate (“eyeshield”) outline and a short to well-developed persistent beak, separating Biscutella from most allies that bear transversely oriented fruits (Franz et al., 2010; Machlatzky & Koch, 2014). Basal leaves are rosulate, often with simple and glandular hairs; cauline leaves, when present, are generally reduced.
L27: Inflorescences are terminal racemes with typically yellow or white tetradynamous flowers; fruits are silicles that vary in the degree of wing development on the valves and the beak. Despite variation in stamen filament indumentum and petal size, pollination is generalized and largely melittophilous; seeds are small, occasionally winged, and dispersed by wind or gravity (Kiefer & Heubl, 2014). Basal chromosome number is x = 8, although polyploidy is frequent in the B.
L28: laevigata complex (Kiefer & Heubl, 2014).
L29: Within the genus, Biscutella subgenus Biscutella (including sect. Biscutella) encompasses most taxa, with many species delimited by combination of fruit morphology, habitat, and ploidy; the B. laevigata complex remains a main arena for hybridization and reticulate evolution (Franz et al., 2010; Machlatzky & Koch, 2014).
L30: A broader circumscription that merges Dethawia and Heldreichia into Biscutella was proposed on morphological–phylogenetic grounds (Kiefer & Heubl, 2014), but standard checklists retain a narrower treatment and continue to list these as genera (POWO, 2024; WFO, 2024); thus species boundaries and generic limits remain unsettled in some sections. The genus has modest horticultural relevance as rock-garden ornamentals; it is not a major crop, timber source, or invasive group.
L31: Conservation concerns reflect the vulnerability of narrowly endemic alpine and Mediterranean montane taxa to climate warming and habitat disturbance, and outstanding phylogenetic and biosystematic work is still needed to resolve reticulation and refine species limits (Kiefer & Heubl, 2014).
