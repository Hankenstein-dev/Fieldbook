L55: Origin and Habitat: Crassula rogersiiSN|27403]]SN|27403]] is endemic to South Africa in the Eastern Cape (Province from Willowmore to Port Elizabeth and Port Alfred)
L56: Habitat and ecology: Dry river valleys in Albany thicket and Succulent Karoo, among rocks and scrub.

L77: Common Names include:
L78: CHINESE (中文): 若歌诗
L79: Description: Crassula rogersiiSN|26686]]SN|27403]] is a small, much-branched, decumbent to erect succulent bush with fleshy red stems and club-shaped leaves. The leaves are covered with short white hairs giving them a velvety appearance and a felt-like texture. In its natural habitat, Crassula rogersiiSN|27403]]SN|27403]] forms a small, tight cushion to 15-30 cm tall. The plant remains green in shady spots but is at its best with plenty of sunlight, where the leaves become tipped with red.
L80: The tiny star-shaped flowers are yellow, and are held in small clusters at the end of thin stems from mid-summer to autumn. There are several hybrids of this plant.
L81: Derivation of specific name: This member of the Crassulaceae family was given this name by Frederick A. Rogers (1876- 1944), English missionary and amateur botanist, lived in Republic of South Africa from 1904.
L82: Roots: Fibrous.
L83: Branches: Succulent, erect and later decumbent, green, older brancheas becoming woody and reddish-brown, 3.5-5 mm in diameter, old leaves not persistent.
L84: Leaves: Opposite, oblanceolate to club-shaped or sub-semi-cylindrical, 15-23 mm long, 7-13 mm broad, ﬂattened, ascending and twisted sideways, hairy, both faces convex, green to yellowish green, margin reddish, horny, base cuneate, tip obtuse to rounded. Hairs fine, soft, and white.
L85: Inflorescence: Elongated thyrses to 20 cm long, with several globular dichasia (clusters).
L86: Flowers. Pale yellow. Sepals triangular, to 2.5 mm long, obtuse, margin ciliate. Corolla tubular, yellowish, the petals to 4 mm long, fused shortly at the base and with ovoid dorsal appendage. The anthers are dark brown.
L87: Blooming season: Flowers mid-summer to autumn.
L88: Similar species: Crassula rogersiiSN|27403]]SN|27403]] is easily confounded with hairy forms of Crassula cultrataSN|27403]]SN|26686]], but has smaller leaves which are convex below. The two species when occurring together are distinct and C. cultrata appears to be much more erect and (usually) glabrous.

L100: Cultivation and Propagation: Crassula rogersiiSN|27403]]SN|27403]] develops into a small, dense cushion but does not seem to spread, making it particularly useful for pot cultivation or nooks in a rockery. Like most succulents, become very attractive and highly coloured when grown in shallow containers and exposed to all weather conditions, but for rapid growth good rich soil is necessary.
L101: Exposition: It does best in bright light with some morning- and/or evening sun. A half shaded place will be tolerated. Afternoon sun and high heat in summer can cause the leaves become tipped with red.
L102: Water: Drench the soil well, water running out of the pot’s hole must be removed after a few minutes. Wet legs can cause rotten roots. Before adding water next allow to dry.
L103: Pests and bugs: Crassulas are sensitive to mealybugs. Large clumps of Crassula rogersiiSN|27403]]SN|27403]] should be lifted and divided in the winter to prevent the clumps from rotting.
L104: Propagation: Leaf cuttings, offsets.
