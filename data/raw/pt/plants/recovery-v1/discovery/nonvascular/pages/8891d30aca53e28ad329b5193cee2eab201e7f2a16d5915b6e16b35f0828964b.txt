L62@P4: Abstract: Keys for the identification of all bryophytes presently known to occur in
L63@P4: Hungary are presented. The three groups: Hornworts (2 taxa), Liverworts (149
L64@P4: taxa), and Mosses (541 taxa) are treated separately. Bryophyte identification using
L65@P4: these keys proceeds in two steps: 1. Artificial keys to the genera, 2. Keys from
L66@P4: genera to species, arranged systematically according to recent taxonomy. Each
L67@P4: species of the Hungarian bryophyte flora is assigned to one of six frequency
L68@P4: classes (very common: cc, common: c, widespread: w, rare: r, very rare: rr, not
L69@P4: seen: n.s.). A glossary explaining the technical terms used in the keys and an index
L70@P4: of genera are included.
L71@P4: Keywords: Genera, species, glossary, mosses, liverworts, hornworts
L72@P4: INTRODUCTION
L73@P4: Identification of Hungarian bryophytes up to now had to rely on
L74@P4: either keys in Hungarian published 68 and 38 years ago,
L75@P4: respectively, long out of print (Boros 1953, Orbán and Vajda 1983),
L76@P4: and out-of-date in many respects, or on keys written in various
L77@P4: languages for other countries or regions (e.g. for liverworts and
L78@P4: hornworts: Smith (1991), Paton (1999), Damsholt (2002),
L79@P4: Schumacker and Váňa (2005), Frey et al. (2006), Casas et al.
L80@P4: (2009), Atherton et al. (2010) in English, Gradstein and van Melick
L81@P4: (1996), Siebel and During (2006) in Dutch, Müller (1905–1916,
L82@P4: 1951–1958), Frahm and Frey (1992, 2004), Frey et al. (1995),
L83@P4: Nebel and Philippi (2005) in German; for mosses: Smith (1978,
L84@P4: 2004), Casas et al. (2006), Frey et al. (2006), Atherton et al. (2010)
L85@P4: in English, Touw and Rubers (1989), Siebel and During (2006) in
L86@P4-5: Dutch, Frahm and Frey (1992, 2004), Frey et al. (1995), Nebel andActa Biol. Plant. Agriensis 9(2): 3–260

L88@P5: Philippi (2000, 2001) in German, Brugués et al. (eds.) (2007),
L89@P5: Brugués and Guerra (eds.) (2015), Guerra et al. (eds.) (2006, 2010,
L90@P5: 2014, 2016) in Spanish, to name just a few). None of these
L91@P5: treatments by itself allows the identification of all bryophytes
L92@P5: presently known in the Hungarian bryophyte flora, except Frey et
L93@P5: al. (2006). Therefore, time is overdue to present an accurate and
L94@P5: updated tool for naming all bryophyte taxa of Hungary, including
L95@P5: the many that have been discovered as new members of the
L96@P5: Hungarian bryoflora in recent years. This need became particularly
L97@P5: urgent during the first stages of the Hungarian bryophyte recording
L98@P5: project (Erzberger 2012, Erzberger and Németh 2016, Erzberger
L99@P5: 2020) when new contributors to the project had to be guided in
L100@P5: improving their identifications skills. To remedy this, the author
L101@P5: then compiled a preliminary version of identification keys, which
L102@P5: was circulated among the participants of the recording project and
L103@P5: continuously improved according to the accumulating experience.

L107@P5: European and Hungarian checklists (Hodgetts et al. 2020,
L108@P5: Erzberger and Papp 2020). Here authorities to all names can be
L109@P5-6: found; they have been deliberately omitted in this treatment. To ERZBERGER (2021): Identification keys for Hungarian bryophytes

L111@P6: facilitate the use of older works, the names used in older checklists
L112@P6: (Erzberger and Papp 2004, Papp et al. 2010) are included as
L113@P6: synonyms. To give an overwiew of the underlying taxonomy, a
L114@P6: conspectus of classification has been included. A glossary
L115@P6: explaining all technical terms used in the keys has also been
L116@P6: compiled.
L117@P6: As a consequence of recent taxonomic developments, mainly
L118@P6: based on molecular evidence, many species have changed their
L119@P6: generic names and many genera have been transferred to different
L120@P6: families. Thus the conventional approach of three steps in
L121@P6: bryophyte identification – keys to families, keys to genera of each
L122@P6: family, keys to species of each genus – is no longer practical, since
L123@P6: many families lack easily observable morphological defining
L124@P6: characters. I have therefore followed Guerra et al. (eds.) (2016) and
L125@P6: Casas et al. (2006, 2009) in first giving a key to genera and then
L126@P6: within each genus (containing more than one species in Hungary) a
L127@P6: key to species.
L128@P6: However, for families with a ± clear-cut morphological
L129@P6: definition, keys are provided directly to all or nearly all species of
L130@P6: the familiy (Table 1).

L197@P7: This approach, although at first glance seeming to lack
L198@P7: systematic rigour, should provide the possibility to enter the
L199@P7: process of identification at various taxonomic levels (family, group
L200@P7: of morphologically similar genera, genus) and thus enable a
L201@P7: comparison among taxa that could easily be confounded, although
L202@P7: in some cases not related taxonomically. A similar purpose is
L203@P7: intended by various notes mentioning differences between related
L204@P7: or unrelated taxa.
L205@P7: The keys have been adapted from the references mentioned
L206@P7: above and others (e.g. Watson 1981), and from additional
L207@P7-8: treatments of special groups which are indicated at the families or ERZBERGER (2021): Identification keys for Hungarian bryophytes
L208@P8: 7
L209@P8: genera, including personal experience of the author. In general,
L210@P8: only taxa from the most recent Hungarian checklist (Erzberger and
L211@P8: Papp 2020) have been considered, plus three species added to the
L212@P8: Hungarian bryoflora after the publication of the checklist
L213@P8: (Hydrogonium croceum, Orthothecium rufescens, Rhytidiadelphus
L214@P8: loreus), their names are printed in bold, but some taxa that might
L215@P8: be expected (or have been doubtfully recorded) have in a few cases
L216@P8: been included (printed in normal case).
L217@P8: As an additional information the frequency of recent
L218@P8: occurrences of taxa (based on records since 1974, i.e. after the era
L219@P8: of Á. Boros) is included in the following categories: very common:
L220@P8: cc, common: c, widespread: w, rare: r, very rare: rr, not seen: n.s.
L221@P8: (i.e. no record after 1973). These estimates are based on a
L222@P8: preliminary evaluation of the recording project (Erzberger and
L223@P8: Németh 2016, Erzberger 2020) which will be published elsewhere,
L224@P8: and their accuracy should not be overestimated, since coverage of
L225@P8: the country is far from complete; in particular the lowlands are
L226@P8: sorrowfully under-represented. The frequency may help mainly
L227@P8: beginners to check the result of their identification, excluding very
L228@P8: rare and rare species, which are unlikely to be found without
L229@P8: experience.
