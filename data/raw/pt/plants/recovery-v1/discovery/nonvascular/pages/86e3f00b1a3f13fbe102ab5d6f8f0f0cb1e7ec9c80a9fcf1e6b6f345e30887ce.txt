L13@P0: Abstract: The Charales (stoneworts) are an ancient monophyletic group with conserved features same in ancesters
L14@P0: as well as in present representatives. They are considered to be closely related to the ancestors of land plants. The
L15@P0: taxonomy of Charales was delimitated on the basis of morphology and later by fossil characters as well. Nowadays,
L16@P0: the higher taxa are relatively well defined and their delimitation is supported by molecular analyses. However, the
L17@P0: situation is more problematic at subgeneric levels. The appearance often depends on the environmental conditions
L18@P0: that can produce broad morphological variation. The last report on Charales in Bohemia was a checklist of taxa
L19@P0: and their distribution along with ecological requirements (Vilhelm 1914). Although about the research history
L20@P0: and the species diversity in Bohemia from the 19th century are well recorded, there is little information from the
L21@P0: 18th and 20th centuries. We have compiled here all historical and current data about the distribution of all members
L22@P0: of Charales in the Czech Republic along with information on their ecological preferences. This paper presents a
L23@P0: list of 26 species of the four genera of charophytes found in waters of the Czech Republic. The species have been
L24@P0: distributed in over 1000 localities.
L25@P0: Key words: charophytes, Characeae, Chara, Nitella, Tolypella, determination, distribution, taxonomy
L26@P0: Introduction
L27@P0: The Characeae (Charophyceae, Charophyta),
L28@P0: commonly known as stoneworts, are ancient
L29@P0: monophyletic group with conserved features in
L30@P0: their morphology (Garbary et al. 1993, McCourt
L31@P0: et al. 1996, Feist & Feist 1997). The organization
L32@P0: of body and life cycle of the Characeae are
L33@P0: distinctive (Printz 1927, Dangeard 1933, Irvine
L34@P0: & John 1984), but plants, particularly in the genus
L35@P0: Chara Vaillant, have often been mistaken for
L36@P0: aquatic phanerogams, especially for underwater
L37@P0: horsetail. The species and infraspecific concepts
L38@P0: have been delimitated on the basis of vegetative
L39@P0: morphology and characteristic oospore wall
L40@P0: ornamentations (Wood 1962, Leicht et al. 1990).
L41@P0: Currently the taxonomy of Charales is well
L42@P0: supported by molecular analyses (Sakayama et al.
L43@P0: 2002, Sakayama 2008).
L44@P0: This paper is presenting main concepts in
L45@P0: the taxonomy of charophytes. Based on the data
L46@P0: in the literature, two complementary approaches
L47@P0: are presented: the first, concerning charophyte
L48@P0: morphology and life history and the second,
L49@P0: current molecular achievements.
L50@P0: We have compiled all historical and current
L51@P0: data about the distribution of localities reported for
L52@P0: all members of Characeae in the Czech Republic
L53@P0: along with information on their basic ecological
L54@P0: preferences.

L88@P1: Taxonomic delimitation
L89@P1: In the 20th century, Charophyta were classified
L90@P1: as green algae (Chlorophyta). The investigations
L91@P1: of Charophyta by Stewart & Mattox (1975),
L92@P1: based on morphology and comparative cytology,
L93@P1: showed that the group of Chlorophyta should
L94@P1: be divided into two classes: the Chlorophyceae
L95@P1: and Charophyceae. According to their study, the
L96@P1: Charophyceae included not only the Charales
L97@P1: but also other groups of green algae such as the
L98@P1: Zygnematales and Coleochaetales. Nowadays the
L99@P1: Charophyta group is referred as the Streptophyta
L100@P1: including orders Chlorokybales, Klebsormidiales,
L101@P1: Coleochaetales and Charales (Mattox &
L102@P1: Stewart 1984). On the basis of morphological,
L103@P1: ultrastructural and molecular analyses, particularly
L104@P1: the Charales and Coleochaetales are considered to
L105@P1: be closely related to the ancestor of land plants
L106@P1: (Grambast 1974, Mishler & Churchill 1985,
L107@P1: Surek et al. 1993, McCourt 1995, Melkonian
L108@P1: & Surek 1995, Martín-Closas 2003). However,
L109@P1: their relationships with higher plants have still
L110@P1: not been fully resolved (Mattox & Stewart 1984,
L111@P1: McCourt et al. 1995). The molecular analyses
L112@P1: (18S rRNA, 5S rRNA and Rubisco (rbcL) gene)
L113@P1: supported only the position of the Charales as a
L114@P1: sister taxon of land plants (Graham 1993, Surek
L115@P1: et al. 1993, McCourt et al. 1996, Karol et al.
L116@P1: 2001).
L117@P1: The order Charales contains one family with
L118@P1: six genera divided into two tribes Chareae [Chara
L119@P1: Linnaeus, Lamprothamnium J. Groves, Nitellopsis
L120@P1: Hy, Lychnothamnus (Rupr.) Leonhardi] and
L121@P1: Nitelleae [Nitella Agardh, Tolypella (A. Br.)
L122@P1: Leonhardi]. This system was created on the
L123@P1: basis of the morphology (Wood 1962, Wood &
L124@P1: Imahory 1965) and later supported by analyses
L125@P1: of fossil characters, especially by the appearance
L126@P1: of fossil gametangia (gyrogonites) (Grambast
L127@P1: 1974). Subsequently some authors expressed
L128@P1: doubts about the delimitation (validity) of some
L129@P1: genera within Characeae, e.g. doubts about the
L130@P1: monophyly of the genus Tolypella or about the
L131@P1: relationships among Chara, Lamprothamnium and
L132@P1: Lychnothamnus (Soulié-Märsche 1989, Feist &
L133@P1: Grambast-Fessard 1991). According to McCourt
L134@P1: et al. (1996) the members of the tribe Chareae
L135@P1: form monophyletic group, whereas Nitella and
L136@P1: Tolypella do not correspond to the monophyletic
L137@P1: tribe Nitelleae. On the basis of the morphology,
L138@P1: molecular analyses and chromosome studies
L139@P1: Tolypella is a distinct genus (Sawa 1974, Sawa
L140@P1: & Frame 1974, McCourt et al. 1996, Meiers et
L141@P1: al. 1999) and although Lychnothamnus is a sister
L142@P1: taxon to Nitellopsis, it is not closely related to
L143@P1: Chara (McCourt et al. 1996).
L144@P1: More problematic is the classification at
L145@P1: lower species level (Wood & Imahory 1965)
L146@P1: mainly considered oospore wall ornamentations
L147@P1: to be a crucial diagnostic feature at the species and
L148@P1: infraspecific levels. On the basis of morphological
L149@P1: similarities, they reclassified many taxa that had
L150@P1: been previously accepted as a species to the
L151@P1: infraspecific rank. The current studies indicate that
L152@P1: some of the infraspecific taxa defined by Wood &
L153@P1: Imahory (1965) should be recognized as distinct
L154@P1: species (John et al. 1990, Mukherjee & Ray 1993,
L155@P1: Ray et al. 2001, Mandal & Ray 2004, Sakayama et
L156@P1: al. 2006). Although there are a lot of evidence that
L157@P1: oospore wall ornamentation in the genus Nitella is
L158@P1: a useful guide to species determination, it is not so
L159@P1: sufficient taxonomic criterion for species of Chara
L160@P1: (John & Moore 1987, Casanova 1991, Casanova
L161@P1: 1997, Sakayama et al. 2002, Abrol & Bhatnagar
L162@P1: 2006, Sakayama et al. 2006, Casanova 2007). The
L163@P1-2: problem of delimitation at lower taxonomic levels should be solved using combination of scanning
L164@P2: electron microscopy (SEM) applied on the oospore
L165@P2: morphology, general morphology and molecular
L166@P2: analyses based on 18S rRNA and Rubisco (rbcL)
L167@P2: gene sequences (John & Moore 1987, Sakayama
L168@P2: et al. 2002, Mandal & Ray 2004, Kapralov &
L169@P2: Filatov 2007, Sakayama 2008).
L170@P2: Diagnostic features
L171@P2: In spite of modern approaches the morphological
L172@P2: features (see in Figs 1–12) still play crucial role
L173@P2: in determination of Charales. The two tribes
L174@P2: Chareae and Nitelleae are distinguished by the
L175@P2: number and arrangement of the coronula cells at
L176@P2: the apex of the oogonium. The coronula consists
L177@P2: of five cells arranged in one tier in the Chareae,
L178@P2: whereas two tiers (each with five cells) are typical
L179@P2: for the Nitelleae. The second difference between
L180@P2: Chareae and Nitelleae is the construction of rays
L181@P2: in the whorls. In the tribe Chareae the main axis
L182@P2: supports whorls of simple branchlets at the nodes,
L183@P2: in case of Nitelleae the whorls are made up of
L184@P2: rays which may be simple or divided. Another
L185@P2: distinguishing feature for Chareae and Nitelleae
L186@P2: Figs 1–12. The morphology of Charales: the main stem is composed of nodes with whorls of branchlets and internodes (Fig. 1;
L187@P2: after Krause 1997), basal part is formed of rhizoids (Fig. 2; after Dąmbska 1964). Surface of the stem is covered with cortical
L188@P2: and spine cells (Fig. 3; after Krause 1997). The stipuloide cells (Fig. 4; after Dąmbska 1964) are arranged at the basis of the
L189@P2: branchlet whorls at the nodes. The presence of the features mentioned above varies from species to species. The cortex of the
L190@P2: stem is formed of longitudinally arranged rows of cells with the spine cells organized solitarily (Fig. 4) or in clusters (Fig. 5;
L191@P2: after Krause 1997). The cells are of various appearances (e.g. elongated, shortened). The unique reproductive organs (Fig. 6;
L192@P2: after Krause 1997), antheridia (Fig. 7; after Dąmbska 1964) and oogonia (Fig. 8; after Dąmbska 1964) occur in association with
L193@P2: bracteoles at the whorls of branchlets. Both gametangia are wrapped in protective cells with specific ornamentation. Mature
L194@P2: oogonia (Fig. 9; Krause 1997) are brown or black and contain only one egg cell, whereas antheridia are bright orange due
L195@P2: to the carotenoid layer and contain thousands of biflagellate spermatozoids. The zygote with protective cells is formed after
L196@P2: the fertilization. As another type of reproduction is usage of special vegetative branchlets originated in either nodal complex
L197@P2: or white star-shaped (Fig. 10; Dąmbska 1964) or spherical structures (bubils; Fig. 11; after Dąmbska 1964). The prochara
L198@P2: (prothallus; Fig. 12; after Vilhelm 1914) is formed after the fertilization or the asexual reproduction.

L220@P3: General ecological demands
L221@P3: Members of Charophyta are recorded from both
L222@P3: freshwater habitats and brackish waters (Krause
L223@P3: 1997). However, according to some studies (e.g.
L224@P3: Elliott 1984) some of the ancestral forms (e.g.
L225@P3: Karspinskya) were marine. At the present time,
L226@P3: salinity is regarded as limiting factor for survival
L227@P3: of charalean algae (Dąmbska 1964). Charophytes
L228@P3: form part of submerged vegetation in permanent or
L229@P3: temporary lakes, pools, streams and rivers. They
L230@P3: also can be found in calceorus rich fens (Gąbka
L231@P3: 2007, Urbaniak et al. 2008). The majority of the
L232@P3: species are known from the temperate zone, but they
L233@P3: endure polar conditions as well. Environmental
L234@P3: factors (e.g. water temperature and chemistry,
L235@P3: light and altitude) have a considerable impact on
L236@P3: the distribution of Charophytes (e.g. Dąmbska
L237@P3: 1964, Krause 1981, Haas 1994, Blindow 1992,
L238@P3: Blindow & Langangen 1995, Pełechaty et al.
L239@P3: 2004, Gąbka et al. 2007, Boszke & Bociąg 2008).
L240@P3: Much attention in present research is paid to the
L241@P3: interrelation of charophytes with phytoplankton
L242@P3: (Scheffer 1989, Scheffer & Jeppensen 1998,
L243@P3: Scheffer 2001). In shallow waters charophytes
L244@P3: meadows suppress growth of phytoplankton and
L245@P3: influence water quality, especially impacting on
L246@P3: transparency (Van Den Berg et al. 1998, Van Den
L247@P3: Berg et al. 1999).
L248@P3: Charophytes occur in both mildly acid
L249@P3: and alkaline waters (pH 7–8). The pH limit
L250@P3: ranges from 5 (Chara globularis, Olsen 1944)
L251@P3: to 9.5 (Chara braunii, Corillon 1975). Nitelleae
L252@P3: prefer pH 6–8 and Chareae prefer the pH range
L253@P3: 7–9 (Dąmbska 1964). Phosphorus, iron oxide
L254@P3: and calcium carbonate are other abiotic factors
L255@P3: influencing charophyte growth and abundance.
L256@P3: However, the knowledge about sediment and
L257@P3: elemental requirements of Charophyta is poor
L258@P3: (Vilhelm 1914, Dąmbska 1964, Grant et al.
L259@P3: 1971, Grant 1990, Haas 1994, Torn et al. 2004).
L260@P3: Generally, the appearance of the thallus or plant
L261@P3: depends on the environmental conditions under
L262@P3: which they are growing (Migula 1897, Wood &
L263@P3: Imahory 1965, Corillion 1975, Bonnis & Grillas
L264@P3: 2002, Boszke & Bociąg 2008).
