L154: Trentepohlia is a cite76†genus of filamentous cite68†chlorophyte cite77†green algae in the family cite70†Trentepohliaceae , living free on terrestrial supports such as tree trunks and wet rocks or cite78†symbiotically in cite79†lichens .^{cite80†[1] } The filaments of Trentepohlia often have a strong orange colour (photograph at right) caused by the presence of large quantities of cite81†carotenoid pigments which mask the green of the cite82†chlorophyll .
L155: ## Nomenclature
L156: 
L157: [cite83†edit ]
L158: Organisms belonging to the genus Trentepohlia were first described by cite84†Linnaeus in 1759; he named his species Byssus aureus (currently known as Trentepohlia aurea).^{cite85†[2] } The genus was cite86†circumscribed by cite71†Carl Friedrich Philipp von Martius in Fl. Crypt. Erlang. on page 351 in 1817. The genus name of Trentepohlia is in honour of Johann Friedrich Trentepohl (1748–1806), who was a German clergyman and cite87†botanist .
L159: He worked as a lecturer and Pastor in various places in cite88†Wesermarsch .^{cite89†[3] } Martius' name was cite90†conserved in favor of the moss genus Trentepohlia and the cite91†Brassicaceae genus Trentepohlia both published by cite92†Albrecht Wilhelm Roth ; the names were published earlier, in 1794 and 1800 respectively.^{cite93†[4] }
L160: ## Description
L161: 
L162: [cite94†edit ]
L163: Trentepohlia consists of branched filaments of cells; the filament occurs in two systems, a prostrate and an erect system. Both prostrate and erect systems are variously branched; the branching is typically alternate or irregular, rarely opposite. Occasionally, the erect system of filaments may be reduced.
L164: The ends of filaments may be somewhat attenuated, and end in a cellulose cap or a cylindrical, unicellular hair.^{cite95†[5] } Cells are cylindrical, barrel-shaped or spherical, one to five times longer than broad, with thin or thick and lamellate cite96†cell walls . Within each cell there are multiple, discoid cite97†chloroplasts or a single band-shaped chloroplast; chloroplasts lack cite98†pyrenoids . The chloroplast is typically obscured by carotenoid pigments, giving the cells an orange or red color.^{cite95†[5] }
L165: ### Reproduction
L166: 
L167: [cite99†edit ]
L168: The life cycle of Trentepohlia is poorly known.^{cite85†[2] } It is known to reproduce both cite100†asexually and cite101†sexually . Asexual reproduction is via cite102†zoospores , which are quadriflagellate and produced in terminal sporangia. Sexual reproduction is by biflagellate gametes which are produced in spherical or urn-shaped gametangia. The gametangia are located either at the ends of filaments (terminal) or in at the sides or middle of filaments (lateral, intercalary).^{cite95†[5] }
L169: Trentepohlia is generally hypothesized to undergo an cite103†alternation of generations . In this scheme, it alternates between cite104†haploid cite105†gametophytes which produce biflagellate cite106†isogamous gametes, and cite107†diploid cite108†sporophytes which undergo cite109†meiosis to form quadriflagellate zoospores. However, it has also been reported that biflagellate gametes are able to germinate into new organisms without fusing sexually.^{cite85†[2] }
L170: ## Habitat and ecology
L171: 
L172: [cite110†edit ]
L173: 
L174: Trentepohlia is a subaerial alga, and is commonly found in humid climates. It typically occurs on moist rocks, wood, tree trunks, and leaves. It is easily distinguished due to the orange, felt-like growths.^{cite95†[5] } Patches of Trentepohlia often obscure posts and inscriptions; this is an economic nuisance as it becomes necessary to paint over them.^{cite85†[2] } cite111†Trentepohlia abietina has been reported growing on spiders in cite112†Queensland , Australia.^{cite113†[6] }
L175: Trentepohlia species form close associations with fungal hyphae, and can form what is known as a "protolichen".^{cite95†[5] } They are widespread true cite114†phycobionts in lichens, such as the "secret writing" cite115†crustose lichen genera cite116†Graphis , cite117†Graphina , cite118†Gyalecta and cite119†Opegrapha .^{cite120†[7] }^{cite121†[8] } In some regions, lichens associated with Trentepohlia appear to have a higher level of rarity than lichens with other photobionts.^{cite122†[9] } With cite123†global warming , European lichens with Trentepohlia as phycobionts have increased in recent decades.^{cite124†[10] }
L176: ## Diversity
L177: 
L178: [cite125†edit ]
L179: 
L180: Trentepohlia contains about 40 species, and is present on almost all continents.^{cite126†[11] } It is mostly diverse and common in tropical and subtropical areas^{cite127†[12] } but several species also occur in temperate environments including cite128†Britain and cite129†Ireland .^{cite130†[13] }
L181: Species of Trentepohlia have traditionally been distinguished based on morphological characters, such as the branching pattern, size and shape of cells, morphology and arrangement of reproductive structures, presence of hairs, and substratum. However, the morphology of Trentepohlia is highly variable and often dependent on the external environment.
L182: It is not uncommon to find different morphological character states of Trentepohlia in the same individual; additionally, some individuals may be intermediate in morphology and thus not assignable to a single species. In addition, the genus (and several species within it) are known to be cite131†polyphyletic . Thus, the genus as a whole is in need of cite132†taxonomic revision .^{cite133†[14] }
