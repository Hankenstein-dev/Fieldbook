L68: 
L69: Late autumn is the best time to see this very common species, when it usually bears numerous horizontal capsules, each of which has a lid with a long, narrow beak, as is typical of Rhynchostegium and Rhynchostegiella species. Both generic names stem from the word rhynkhos, which means snout in Greek.
L70: It is not however hard to find at other times of the year if you make yourself familiar with its favoured habitats – deeply shaded, dry but sheltered situations on hard basic substrates. In fact, it will often grow where little else will, as for example at the base of a wall on mortar. It does grow on natural rock too, but seems to really like walls and other man-made habitats.
