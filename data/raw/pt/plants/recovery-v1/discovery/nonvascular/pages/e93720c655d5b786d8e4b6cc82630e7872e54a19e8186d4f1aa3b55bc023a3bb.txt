L142: Mastocarpus papillatus, sometimes called Turkish washcloth, black tar spot,^{cite70†[4] } or grapestone^{cite71†[5] } is a species of cite51†red algae in the family cite54†Phyllophoraceae . It is sometimes confused with the distantly related Turkish towel (cite46†Chondracanthus exasperatus ) which is of a similar texture but larger.
L143: The cite72†specific epithet papillatus ('with cite73†papillae ')^{cite74†[6] } is due to the nipple-like projections on the female cite75†gametophyte which can give the texture of a cite76†terrycloth cite77†washcloth found at a cite78†hamam (Turkish bathhouse).^{cite70†[4] }
L144: ## Distribution
L145: 
L146: [cite79†edit ]
L147: 
L148: Despite the cite80†common name , it grows nowhere near cite81†Turkey . It is instead native to the northeastern cite82†Pacific Ocean and commonly can be found in cite83†California , cite84†Oregon , cite85†Washington , cite86†British Columbia , and cite87†Alaska .^{cite88†[7] }^{cite89†[8] } It has been found growing as far north as the cite90†Commander Islands ^{cite91†[9] } and the cite92†Russian Far East ,^{cite93†[10] } and as far south as cite94†Chile .^{cite95†[11] }^{cite96†[12] }^{cite97†[13] }
L149: ## Habitat
L150: 
L151: [cite98†edit ]
L152: 
L153: It grows on cite99†rocky cite100†substrates in the high- to mid-cite101†intertidal zones and has been found growing at depths of 250 m.^{cite70†[4] } Like many red algae species, it uses cite102†phycoerythrin to cite103†photosynthesize which allows it to grow deeper than plants using other pigments.^{cite70†[4] }
L154: ## Description
L155: 
L156: [cite104†edit ]
L157: 
L158: Turkish washcloth is reddish-brown to almost black, with males paler than females and lacking the signature papillae on the blades.^{cite70†[4] } The blades are branched and small at less than 15 cm long, as one would expect to a plant with a diminutive common name.^{cite70†[4] } The first blades in the spring are thin, purple and lack papillae.^{cite71†[5] } Later in the year the thalli dry out and become cite105†grey and lifeless.^{cite71†[5] }
L159: cite106†Mastocarpus jardinii is a similar species, and can be confused with M. papillatus.^{cite107†[14] } cite108†Mazzaella affinis thalli looks similar in shape and color to male M. papillatus, but are smaller and grow in thick mats and not as individuals.^{cite107†[14] } It also can be confused with other species with Petrocelis phases (see below), such as cite109†Pikea , other cite55†Mastocarpus spp., cite110†Ralfisa , and cite111†Hildenbrandia , thought the last two are thinner than the Turkish towel crust.^{cite107†[14] }
L160: ## Life cycle
L161: 
L162: [cite112†edit ]
L163: M. papillatus has a relatively complex reproductive cycle. Male cite75†gametophytes discharge cite113†nonflagellated sperm to drift in the current until they attach to the cite114†trichogynes of female plants. There the sperm perform cite115†mitosis without cite116†cell division , turning into a cite117†spermatium . cite118†Fertilisation then proceeds through a cite119†fertilization pore between the trichogyne and the spermatium.
L164: The cite120†eggs then grow into cite121†tetrasporophytes which are more or less copies of their parents.^{cite70†[4] }
L165: Alternately, the tetrasporophyte goes through the Petrocelis phase (so named as it was thought to be a distinct genus, cite122†Petrocelis , of cite54†Phyllophoraceae ) and grows into a crust that looks like spilled cite123†tar , inspiring the black tar spot common name. The tetrasporophyte undergoes cite124†meiosis and produce cite125†tetrasporangia which release spores to germinate elsewhere.
L166: Eventually the crust grows into a typical adult plant.^{cite70†[4] } This cite126†crustose phase is shared by all members of the genus cite55†Mastocarpus as well as those in cite109†Pikea .^{cite107†[14] }
L167: ## Ecology
L168: 
L169: [cite127†edit ]
L170: 
L171: Turkish washcloth is food for many animals, including cite128†Echinolittorina ziczac , cite129†Littorina , cite130†Amphipoda , cite131†Lottia , cite132†Pholis crassispina , cite133†Lottia scutum , cite134†Trochidae , cite135†Lacuna vincta , cite136†Stenosoma , cite137†Mopalia swanii , and cite138†Cryptochiton stelleri .^{cite139†[15] }
L172: Like the cite140†Porphyra species used to make cite141†nori , M. papillatus is susceptible to infection by the cite142†parasitic cite143†oomycete cite144†Pythium porphyrae , or red rot disease, which can kill large colonies.^{cite70†[4] } It is also susceptible to infection by cite145†Pythium marinum .^{cite146†[16] }
L173: ## Uses
L174: 
L175: [cite147†edit ]
L176: 
L177: Grapestone is cite148†edible ^{cite70†[4] } and can be cooked in cite149†soups or cite150†stir-fries but care must be taken as overcooking can turn the plant mushy and cite151†gelatinous .^{cite71†[5] } This property can be exploited to use it as a cite152†thickening agent like its close relative cite153†Mastocarpus stellatus .^{cite71†[5] } Grapestone has a similar flavor to cite154†oysters .^{cite155†[17] }
L178: Grapestone is easily foraged from the shore, but like any wild species cite156†overharvesting is possible. By grabbing the longer blades and pulling sideways, the shorter blades and cite157†holdfast may remain on the rock and live on.^{cite71†[5] }
L179: 
L180: M. papillatus is also thought to have cite158†antimicrobial properties.^{cite70†[4] }
