L16@P0: Abstract: The distribution and diversity of the algae of the order Trentepohliales were studied from
L17@P0: field collections made in Gabon, central Africa. Both forest and urban environments were examined
L18@P0: for the presence of trentepohlialean taxa. Based on these field collections fourteen taxa of Trentepohlia
L19@P0: and Printzina were recorded: Printzina bosseae (De Wildeman) Thompson & Wujek, Printzina
L20@P0: lagenifera (Hildebrand) Thompson & Wujek, Printzina sp., Trentepohlia abietina (Flotow) Hansgirg,
L21@P0: Trentepohlia abietina var. corrugata (Leighton) Cribb, Trentepohlia arborum (C.Agardh) Hariot,
L22@P0: Trentepohlia aurea (Linnaeus) Martius, Trentepohlia chapmanii Rindi & López-Bautista, Trentepohlia
L23@P0: dusenii Hariot, Trentepohlia cfr. flintii Sarma, Trentepohlia minima Schmidle, Trentepohlia peruana
L24@P0: (Kützing) Printz, Trentepohlia rigidula (J.Müller) Hariot, Trentepohlia umbrina (Kützing) Bornet.
L25@P0: Among these taxa, five are reported here for the first time from Africa: P. bosseae, T. abietina var.
L26@P0: corrugata, T. chapmanii, T. minima and T. peruana. An undescribed Printzina species was collected
L27@P0: from "Les Monts de Cristal" forest for which more collections and an assessment based on molecular
L28@P0: data are needed to assess the identity in detail. These findings suggest that microbial diversity in
L29@P0: Africa is still grossly understudied, and future biodiversity surveys of subaerial algae may lead to
L30@P0: the discovery of new and undescribed trentepohlialean species.
L31@P0: Key words: Africa, Chlorophyta, Gabon, terrestrial algae, Trentepohliales.
L32@P0: Introduction
L33@P0: The order Trentepohliales consists exclusively of terrestrial algae classified in the
L34@P0: mainly marine class Ulvophyceae (as demonstrated by molecular studies by LópezBautista & Chapman 2003 and Cocquyt et al. 2010). Representatives of this order are
L35@P0: widespread around the world both in temperate and tropical regions. Their abundance,

L38@P1: however, is typically higher in tropical regions with humid climates. Trentepohlialean
L39@P1: algae are usually found growing on a wide range of substrata, such as rocks, tree barks,
L40@P1: leaves, and various man-made constructions (Chapman 1984, Ettl & Gartner 1995,
L41@P1: Thompson & Wujek 1997, Lopez-Bautista et al. 2007, Rindi & Lopez-Bautista 2008,
L42@P1: Rindi et al. 2008). They have also been found growing on animals such as spiders
L43@P1: (Cribb 1964) and hair of sloths (Suutari et al. 2010). Although these organisms are
L44@P1: green algae, they usually show different colors due to the abundance of carotenoid
L45@P1: pigments that color the thallus in orange, red, or even yellow (Geitler 1923). This group
L46@P1: is characterized by several unique characteristics, in particular a sporangial branch
L47@P1: called sporangiate lateral, formed by a zoosporangium borne at the top of an enlarged
L48@P1: cell with a bent neck, the so-called suffultory cell (see fig. 6 in López-Bautista et al.
L49@P1: 2002: http://ucjeps.berkeley.edu/constancea/83/lopez_etal/trent_fig6.html). Previous
L50@P1: studies dealing with Trentepohliales diversity have mainly focused on Europe and
L51@P1: some parts of Asia (Lopez-Bautista et al. 2007). Studies for tropical regions have a
L52@P1: very uneven coverage, and not until recently some investigators have looked at these
L53@P1: algae in North and Central America (Rindi et al. 2008, Thompson & Wujek 1997), in
L54@P1: South America (Akiyama 1971 and Rindi et al. 2007, 2008), and the Hawaiian Islands
L55@P1: (Rindi et al. 2005).
L56@P1: Since the Trentepohliales are one of the major components of the subaerial microbial
L57@P1: flora in tropical regions (Fritsch 1907, Islam 1960, Gaylarde et al. 2006), and
L58@P1: considering that tropical rainforests have been shown to be centers of algal biodiversity
L59@P1: in South America and Asia (Lopez-Bautista et al. 2007, Neustupa & Škaloud 2008,
L60@P1: 2010, Neustupa et al. 2011, Eliáš et al. 2010, Němcová et al. 2011), the tropical
L61@P1: rainforests of central Africa can be expected to host a high trentepohlialean diversity.
L62@P1: Africa is the least studied continent with regard to subaerial green algae, and records
L63@P1: of African Trentepohliales are scattered in the fragmentary literature. Some species
L64@P1: were described by early authors based on collections from Africa (e.g., Montagne
L65@P1: 1846, Massalongo 1861, Reinsch 1877, Wittrock & Nordstedt 1893). Since these
L66@P1: early reports, however, there was only a single major study by Printz (1921). This
L67@P1: author investigated subaerial algae from South Africa, reporting several Phycopeltis
L68@P1: and Trentepohlia species with detailed morphological descriptions. More recently,
L69@P1: other records of Trentepohliales from South Africa and neighboring countries were
L70@P1: documented by Rindi et al. (2006), who recorded some species previously unreported
L71@P1: in Africa. The remaining information is limited to some studies on terrestrial algae
L72@P1: from Algeria, Congo, Morocco and South Africa, which added occasional records of
L73@P1: Trentepohlia species(Hariot 1891, 1913, Gauthier-Lievre 1954, Joska & Bolton 1996)
L74@P1: without further taxonomic or morphological details.
L75@P1: In the course of a recent fieldtrip to Gabon, one of us (HAA) had the opportunity
L76@P1: to make large collections of Trentepohliales. This country is situated on the equator
L77@P1: in the central-western region of Africa. Gabon is characterized by a hot and humid
L78@P1: climate, and is home to a rich and diverse tropical rainforest that covers about 80%
L79@P1: of the country’s total surface area (Alonso et al. 2006). It was therefore considered a
L80@P1: good candidate area to host a rich and diverse trentepohlialean flora. We present here
L81@P1: the results of the survey, which led to many interesting records, some new for the
L82@P1: whole African continent.

L84@P2: Materials and methods
L85@P2: Samples of Trentepohliales were collected in Gabon (West Africa) in late May of 2008. Extensive
L86@P2: collections were made at "Les Monts de Cristal" National Park at the Tchimbélé site (approximately
L87@P2: 0°35'55.4"N, 10°25'14"E). Since its establishment as a national park in 2002 (by a presidential decree)
L88@P2: the site has been a protected area and maintains many native areas in pristine conditions. The park
L89@P2: is covered by a dense rainforest that has not been affected by major human impacts. Other samples
L90@P2: were collected around the capital city Libreville (approximately 0°23'53"N, 9°26'39"E). All samples
L91@P2: were removed with a plastic scrapper from different natural and artificial substrata where yellow,
L92@P2: orange or red patches referable to Trentepohliales were observed by unaided eye. The substrata
L93@P2: sampled included leaves, tree bark, rocks, concrete walls, and metal poles. After the collection, the
L94@P2: samples were air-dried, placed into sealable plastic bags, and shipped back to the laboratory at The
L95@P2: University of Alabama for further analyses.
L96@P2: Upon arrival, the samples were examined using both light and dissecting microscopes. The algae
L97@P2: contained in the samples were identified morphologically using keys and descriptions available in the
L98@P2: literature (i.e., Hariot 1889, De Wildeman 1900, Printz 1939, Cribb 1970, Rindi & López-Bautista
L99@P2: 2007, Rindi et al. 2008). Photographic documentation was made with a CCD Q3 Digital camera
L100@P2: mounted on an Olympus research microscope BX51 and an Olympus dissecting scope SZX7. Voucher
L101@P2: specimens were deposited in the herbarium of the University of Alabama (UNA).

L103@P2: Printzina bosseae (De Wildeman) Thompson & Wujek Figs 1–3
L104@P2: This alga forms a greenish-orange mat on tree bark. Erect axes are up to 700 mm
L105@P2: tall, sparingly branched. The cells are cylindrical, 14–18 mm in width, 1.5–3 times as
L106@P2: long as wide. The cell wall is thicker and colored brownish orange in the older parts.
L107@P2: Sporangiate laterals are common; the zoosporangia are globular, 25–30 mm in diameter,
L108@P2: with the ostiole opposite to the attachment. As typical of this species, growth restarts
L109@P2: from the suffultory cell after the release of the zoosporangium. The apical cells are
L110@P2: blunt, dome-shaped, devoid of pectic caps. Gamentangia are lateral and globular in
L111@P2: shape. After the release, the gametangia leave some saucer-shaped scars on the main
L112@P2: axes and branches.
L113@P2: The alga formed a greenish-orange fur on tree bark and was collected at "Les Monts de
L114@P2: Cristal" National Park at the Tchimbélé site on 21 May 2008. This species is reported
L115@P2: for the first time from Africa.
L116@P2: Printzina lagenifera (Hildebrand) Thompson & Wujek Fig. 4
L117@P2: This alga forms a dense and compact mat, in which individual filaments are densely
L118@P2: mixed. The erect and prostrate parts cannot be distinguished. The cells are very variable
L119@P2: in shape. In some cases they are perfectly cylindrical (7–10 mm in width), but more
L120@P2: often they are elliptical or subglobular (8–12 mm in width). Presumptive gametangia
L121@P2: (15–20 mm in diameter) are quite common and exhibit the morphology typical of this
L122@P2: species. They are flask-shaped with the ostiole borne at the top of a neck, which is
L123@P2: well developed in mature gametangia. They are usually produced in apical, lateral or
L124@P2: intercalary position.
L125@P2: This alga was found forming orange coatings on a wooden wall in "Les Monts de
L126@P2: Cristal" National Park at the Tchimbélé site on 21 May 2008. This species is one of

L135@P3: Printzina sp. Fig. 5
L136@P3: This alga is found forming a bright green layer, very compact, strictly adherent to
L137@P3: the substratum; the structure of the thallus is pseudoparenchymatic, formed by the
L138@P3: encroachment of many entangled filaments. There is no clear distinction between
L139@P3: prostrate and erect parts. The filaments are irregularly branched, strongly bent. The
L140@P3: cells are elliptical, barrel-shaped or globular, rarely cylindrical usually 5–8 mm wide.
L141@P3: Reproductive structures could not be observed.
L142@P3: This alga was collected on a tree bark at "Les Monts de Cristal" National Park at the
L143@P3: Tchimbélé site on 24 May 2008.
L144@P3: Trentepohlia abietina (Flotow) Hansgirg Figs 6–7
L145@P3: This species forms thin orange bushes on bark of trees. Erect axes are up to 250 mm
L146@P3: tall, sparsely or not branched, arising from a limited prostrate system. The cells are
L147@P3: variable in shape, sometimes cylindrical, sometimes swollen or barrel-shaped, 5–10 mm
L148@P3: (mainly ~7.5) and 1–2 times as long as wide. The apical cells are usually longer and
L149@P3: bear a small pectic cap. New branches arise from the upper part or the top corner
L150@P3: of the cell. The cell wall is smooth. Reproductive structures were rarely observed.
L151@P3: Some sporangiate laterals were observed at the top of some thicker erect axes. The
L152@P3: zoosporangia are globular, 14–15 mm in diameter. A few globular or ovoid gametangia
L153@P3: were observed borne apically or laterally on short branchlets, glorbular or subglobular,
L154@P3: 15–20 mm in diameter.
L155@P3: This alga was common on tree barks in "Les Monts de Cristal" National Park at the
L156@P3: Tchimbélé site and in Libreville, where it was collected at several sites in May of 2008.
L157@P3: This species represents another widespread entity in the genus. Originally described
L158@P3: from central Europe, it has been widely recorded in the tropics (De Wildeman 1891,
L159@P3: Cribb 1958, Islam 1960, Akiyama 1971, Islam 1972, Rindi et al. 2005, Rindi and
L160@P3: Lopez-Bautista 2008), and was also reported growing on a spider (Cribb 1964). In
L161@P3: Africa, it was reported for the first time by Rindi et al. (2006) in South Africa.

L186@P5: This alga was by far the most common species of Trentepohliales in Gabon at the time
L187@P5: of the survey, being found growing on a wide range of substrata including tree bark,
L188@P5: wooden fences and rocky cliffs around a waterfall at the Tchimbélé site in "Les Monts de
L189@P5: Cristal" National Park. This species is usually reported from different types of substrata
L190@P5: and is often the phycobiont in specimens of the common lichen genus Coenogonium.
L191@P5: This alga is one of the most widespread species of Trentepohlia and, more generally,
L192@P5: one of the most common terrestrial algae in the tropics worldwide. It has been reported
L193@P5: almost in every study that has concerned the Trentepohliales in tropical regions. In
L194@P5: Africa it was previously reported from Democratic Republic of the Congo (formerly
L195@P5: Zaire) by Sarma (1986).
L196@P5: Trentepohlia aurea (Linnaeus) Martius Figs 12–13
L197@P5: This alga consists of orange, dense tufts formed by erect axes irregularly branched.
L198@P5: The cells are cylindrical (10–13mm wide), 1–3 times as long as wide. The apical cells
L199@P5: are variable in shape, mostly with blunt tips, but occasionally pointed. The cell walls
L200@P5: are variable in thickness and the septa are sometimes remarkably thick.
L201@P5: Presumptive gametangia are numerous and variable in shape and position. They are
L202@P5: globular or flask-shaped, 20–25 mm wide at maturity; sometimes they bear a welldeveloped beak. They are borne apically or laterally; sometimes 2 or 3 gametangia
L203@P5: occur adjacent to each other on adjacent segments.
L204@P5: This alga is the type species of the genus Trentepohlia. It was found growing on a
L205@P5: bark of a tree in the Monts de Cristal national park on 22 May 2008, and in another
L206@P5: collection it was also found growing on a cement pole. It has been reported previously
L207@P5: from various parts of Africa (Congo, Morocco, South Africa, Democratic Republic of
L208@P5: the Congo) by Sarma (1986).

L229@P7: This alga has a thallus sharply separated in erect and prostrate parts. The cells are
