L136: ## Description
L137: 
L138: [cite87†edit ]
L139: Most members of Phyllosiphon are cite88†parasitic green algae that inhabit the leaves of plants in the family cite82†Araceae . When inside the leaves, they cause yellowish-green spots; they remain greenish in color even when the leaf dries. Microscopically, the thallus of Phyllosiphon consists of branched, siphonal filaments which penetrate the space between leaf cite89†parenchyma cells. cite90†Asexual reproduction occurs from the formation of endospores, which fill the filaments.
L140: Endospores are about 3-6 μm in diameter and ellipsoidal.^{cite84†[4] }
L141: A number of species of Phyllosiphon are free-living, but are cite91†phylogenetically in the same cite92†clade as parasitic Phyllosiphon species, and are therefore placed in the genus. When free-living, the cells are solitary and coccoid. Cells contain a single parietal, lobed cite93†chloroplast with^{cite86†[6] } or without cite94†pyrenoids , as well as oil droplets and carbohydrates.^{cite84†[4] }
L142: ## Ecology
L143: 
L144: [cite95†edit ]
L145: 
L146: Illustration of the phytopathogenic green algae Phyllosiphon arisari, which affects the foliage of Arisarum
L147: 
L148: The species cite41†Phyllosiphon arisari Kühn induces necrosis in leaf tissue of cite96†Arisarum , after invading the intracellular space.^{cite85†[5] } Another species, Phyllosiphon ari, parasitizes leaves of Arum italicum.^{cite97†[7] } Five additional species have been described from the tropics, but have not been reported since.^{cite84†[4] }
L149: When free-living, Phyllosiphon species inhabit a variety of terrestrial habitats, in particular subaerial cite98†biofilms on tree bark.^{cite84†[4] }
