L106: ## Summary
L107: 
L108: ### Description
L109: 
L110: Gracilaria multipartita is a translucent, dull purple or reddish-brown algae which has fronds that measure up to 25 cm long. It is cartilaginous, very brittle, and has a compressed stipe.
L111: 
L112: ### Recorded distribution in Britain and Ireland
L113: 
L114: Distribution in the British Isles is restricted to Cornwall, south Devon and Dorset.
L115: ### Global distribution
L116: 
L117: Recorded in Senegal, the Mediterranean, Nova Scotia Canada, Uruguay, the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean. Gracilaria multipartita is probably widely distributed in warmer waters.
L118: 
L119: ### Habitat
L120: 
L121: Grows on rocks or other hard substrata, tolerating a wide degree of wave exposure. Recorded from the upper sublittoral to a depth of 15 m. Gracilaria multipartita is also tolerant of sand and mud cover.
L122: 
L123: ### Depth range
L124: 
L125: -
L126: ### Identifying features
L127: 
L128:   * Translucent algae, dull purple or reddish brown in colour (sometimes bleached).
L129:   * Stipe compressed, expanding gradually into a blade which is up to 1 mm thick and up to 1 cm broad between dichotomies.
L130:   * Stipe eventually branches up to 6 times in the plane of the blade.
L131:   * The margin of the blade is frequently proliferous.
L132: ### Additional information
L133: 
L134: Globally there is some variation in frond-width and the degree of branching, although the appearance is quite uniform in the British Isles. Gracilaria multipartita is commercially used as an agar or agaroid raw material in the U.S.A.
