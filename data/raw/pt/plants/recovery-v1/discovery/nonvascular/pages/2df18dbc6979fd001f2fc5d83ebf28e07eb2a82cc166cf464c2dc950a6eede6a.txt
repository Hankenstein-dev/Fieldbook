L0@P0: SEAWEEDS
L1@P0: Seaweeds are an important marine resource in Namibia. They are harvested worldwide primarily for the
L2@P0: extraction of chemicals that serve as gelling and thickening agents in foods, and for media used in medical
L3@P0: and microbiological work. These chemical agents are classified in 3 major categories: agar, carrageenans,
L4@P0: and alginate. Agar is the most valuable of these compounds because it is the strongest known gelling agent,
L5@P0: is essentially inert, and does not require additional chemicals in order to gel. Carrageenan is a general term
L6@P0: for a group of closely related gelling compounds which are also important gelling and thickening agents but
L7@P0: usually require the presence of ions of potassium or calcium in order to gel. Alginate is a general thickening,
L8@P0: binding, and gelling agent that has a variety of uses in food, textile, and manufacturing industries. Aside from
L9@P0: providing valuable gelling agents, seaweeds are also important for direct consumption by man, as an animal
L10@P0: feed supplement, as food for abalone farms, and as fertilizer.
L11@P0: There are numerous species of seaweeds and algae in Namibia. At least 9 species are of important present or
L12@P0: potential use, and are presented here. They are included in 3 major categories of seaweeds: the red, brown,
L13@P0: and green algae. These 3 divisions are usually distinguishable by the colour that their name suggests, except
L14@P0: red algae can be either red, brown, yellow, or dark in colour.
L15@P0: Reproduction takes place in various ways, including sexual (gametes), asexual (spores), and vegetative
L16@P0: propagation (e.g., fragmentation of tallus). The life cycle may entail 1, 2, or 3 generations that can be morphologically similar or dissimilar and sometimes alternating between macroscopic and microscopic.

L51@P1: the Walvis Bay and Swakopmund area.
