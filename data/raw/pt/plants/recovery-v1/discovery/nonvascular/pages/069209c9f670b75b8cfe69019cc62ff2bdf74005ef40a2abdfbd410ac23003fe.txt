L126: 
L127:   * cite49†Selenastrum bibraianum L128:   * cite50†Selenastrum bifidum L129:   * cite51†Selenastrum densum L130:   * cite52†Selenastrum rinoi L131:   * cite53†Selenastrum subtile L132:   * cite54†Selenastrum westii L133: Selenastrum is a cite55†genus of cite56†green algae in the family cite46†Selenastraceae .^{cite57†[2] } It is common in cite58†freshwater habitats around the world.^{cite59†[3] } Most species prefer temperate or warm-temperate waters.^{cite48†[1] }
L134: 
L135: The genus was cite60†circumscribed by the German phycologist cite61†Paul Friedrich Reinsch in 1866.^{cite48†[1] } The name Selenastrum comes from the Greek roots selene, meaning "moon", and astron, meaning "star".^{cite62†[4] }
L136: ## Description
L137: 
L138: [cite63†edit ]
L139: 
L140: Selenastrum forms colonies of cells, numbering four, eight, 16, or 32. Cells are strongly curved and crescent-shaped, with pointed ends; the cells are attached to each other on their convex sides. Each cell contains a single parietal cite64†chloroplast .^{cite59†[3] }
L141: 
L142: Like other members of the family, Selenastrum reproduces asexually, by forming cite65†autospores .^{cite66†[5] } Zoospores and sexual reproduction have not been observed in this genus.^{cite48†[1] }
L143: ## Taxonomy
L144: 
L145: [cite67†edit ]
L146: 
L147: Selenastrum has had an unstable taxonomic history.^{cite68†[6] } Selenastrum is distinguished from the similar and related genus cite69†Ankistrodesmus by the curvature of the cells; Selenastrum has more strongly curved cells. The two genera have sometimes been considered congeneric,^{cite70†[7] } but cite71†molecular phylogenetic studies have shown them to be found in different clades.^{cite72†[8] }
L148: In 2016, the genera cite73†Messastrum and cite74†Curvastrum were created, differing from Selenastrum mainly in their cite75†18S rDNA and cite76†rbcL gene sequences. Curvastrum additionally differs from Selenastrum in its colony size, forming solitary cells or colonies of up to four cells. Both genera are monotypic, containing the species Messastrum gracile (formerly Selenastrum gracile) and Curvastrum pantanale, respectively.^{cite77†[9] }
