L149@P6: NORTH CAROLINA PLANT LIST – RARE
L150@P6: INTRODUCTION
L151@P6: The North Carolina Natural Heritage Program (NCNHP) conducts and maintains an inventory of known
L152@P6: locations of rare animals and plants in the state as part of its mission to support conservation of natural
L153@P6: areas. The primary functions of this inventory list are its use 1) in determining priorities for protection of the
L154@P6: state’s most important natural areas and the plants and animals contained therein, 2) in determining
L155@P6: priorities for inventory, and 3) by public agencies and private persons for environmental assessment and land
L156@P6: management planning.
L157@P6: The NCNHP is charged under the Nature Preserves Act (G.S. 143B-135.250 to 143B-135.272) with
L158@P6: compiling and maintaining information relevant to the protection of North Carolina’s most important
L159@P6: natural areas. NCNHP takes the lead role in North Carolina in the inventory of the state’s natural diversity and
L160@P6: the identification of important natural areas and rare species habitats. The list incorporates the most recent
L161@P6: federal status designations determined by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) for all animal and
L162@P6: plant taxa native to North Carolina. Prior to 2018, NCNHP included Federal Species of Concern (FSC) as a
L163@P6: federal status. The FSC list was collectively maintained by the North Carolina offices of the USFWS, but FSC
L164@P6: is not an official federal status. Under the guidance of the USFWS Region 4 office, the Raleigh Ecological
L165@P6: Services office has revised their focus toward At-Risk Species and as such the NCNHP no longer reports FSC
L166@P6: as a federal status. The At-Risk Species list is a dynamic list that includes species proposed for listing under
L167@P6: the ESA, Candidate species, and species that have been petitioned for listing. Please refer to the At-Risk
L168@P6: Species Finder at https://www.fws.gov/southeast/finder/#/species/list for more information.
L169@P6: This list also includes North Carolina legal status information from the N.C. Plant Conservation Program
L170@P6: (NCPCP), a unit of the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services and the agency responsible for the
L171@P6: listing and protection of North Carolina’s endangered and threatened plants, under provisions of the North
L172@P6: Carolina Plant Protection and Conservation Act (N.C. General Statutes - Chapter 106 Article 19B). The
L173@P6: NCPCP List of North Carolina’s Endangered, Threatened and Special Concern Plant Species can be found
L174@P6: on the website: http://www.ncagr.gov/plantindustry/plant/plantconserve/. NCWRC is responsible for the
L175@P6: listing and protection of the state’s nongame species of mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, freshwater
L176@P6: fishes, mollusks, and crustaceans, under N.C. General Statutes - Chapter 113 Article 25. The NCNHP,
L177@P6: NCPCP, NCWRC, and USFWS work cooperatively to ensure the continued survival of all of North
L178@P6: Carolina’s rich flora and fauna. Over the past 40 years and continuing in the future, the advice and
L179@P6: protection designations (for example, Endangered, Threatened, and Special Concern) of scientific councils and
L180@P6: committees have been considered in the creation of the list.
L181@P6: Many species that lack formal legal protection are nonetheless imperiled in North Carolina. Therefore,
L182@P6: NCNHP collects data on these species in addition to those classified under the three protection designations
L183@P6: above and places them in two categories: Significantly Rare (SR) and Watch List (W). These species receive
L184@P6: no special legal protection; rather, the designation is used to convey information about the species’ rarity
L185@P6: within the state. The tracking of SR species, along with Endangered, Threatened, and Special Concern
L186@P6: species, plays a critical role in how NCNHP evaluates natural areas for their importance in conservation of the
L187@P6: state’s native biodiversity. These status categories are defined in this list under “N.C. Status.”
L188@P6: NCNHP requests locality and population data on the species listed in this publication. Plant survey forms are
L189@P6: provided online at https://www.ncnhp.org/share-data/contribute-your-data/plant-survey-form. People may
L190@P6: complete the online survey form or, if preferred, download the form and email completed forms to
L191@P6: natural.heritage@ncdcr.gov or mail to: N.C. Natural Heritage Program; MSC 1651; Raleigh, NC 27699-1651.
L192@P6: For the most current information about the range of tracked species in North Carolina, visit www.ncnhp.org.
L193@P6: Information, including maps and datasets, on animals, plants, and communities nationwide may be found on
L194@P6-7: NatureServe Explorer (www.natureserve.org/). 2

L198@P7: Status Codes
L199@P7: In North Carolina, Endangered, Threatened, and Special Concern species have legally protected status
L200@P7: through the North Carolina Plant Conservation Program (NCPCP). The Natural Heritage Program maintains
L201@P7: database and GIS files on state listed and Significantly Rare species, as well as species considered
L202@P7: extirpated in the state. United States Status is designated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) and
L203@P7: the U.S. National Marine Fisheries Service (USNMFS) in accordance with the U.S. Endangered Species Act
L204@P7: of 1973 (USESA), as amended. Plants and plant varieties (including fungi, hornworts, lichens, mosses),
L205@P7: animal species and subspecies, and vertebrate populations are considered for Endangered or Threatened
L206@P7: status according to the criteria established under the USESA. Proposals and determinations to add taxa or
L207@P7: populations to the Lists of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and plants are published in the Federal
L208@P7: Register. Additionally, the USFWS and the USNMFS periodically publish a Notice of Review or Notice of
L209@P7: Reclassification in the Federal Register that presents an updated list of plant and animal taxa that are
L210@P7: regarded as candidates or proposed for possible addition to the Lists of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife
L211@P7: and Plants. Contact the Asheville or Raleigh Ecological Services Field Offices for more information.

L305@P9: Rank Codes
L306@P9: Natural Heritage Programs and the NatureServe Network have developed a consistent method for evaluating
L307@P9: the relative imperilment of both species and ecological communities. These assessments led to the designation
L308@P9: of a conservation rank. These ranks provide an estimate of extinction risk. Conservation rank values have been
L309@P9: assigned over the past 40 years by the NCNHP, NatureServe, and many collaborators in government
L310@P9: agencies, universities, natural history museums, botanical gardens, and other conservation organizations. This
L311@P9: information has been developed primarily to help in guiding conservation and informing environmental
L312@P9: planning and management.
L313@P9: Conservation ranks are either state (S) or global (G) and are based on a one-to-five scale, ranging from
L314@P9: critically imperiled (S1 or G1) to demonstrably secure (S5 or G5). These assessments are based on the best
L315@P9: available information, considering a variety of factors such as abundance, distribution, population trends,
L316@P9: and threats. Global ranks apply to the species throughout its range. The system is widely used by
L317@P9: agencies and organizations nationwide as the best available scientific and objective assessment of a
L318@P9: species’ imperilment throughout its range.
L319@P9: A rank involving two numbers indicates uncertainty. For example, an S2S3 or a G2G3 rank indicates that
L320@P9: the species may be S2 or S3 (or G2 or G3), but that existing data do not allow that determination to be made.
