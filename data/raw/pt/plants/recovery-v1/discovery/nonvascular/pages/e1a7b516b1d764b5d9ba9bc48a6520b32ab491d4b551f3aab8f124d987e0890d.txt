L68@P5: This check-list represents a summary of floristic data collected over
L69@P5: a period of nine months (September, 1971 - July, 1972) in connection with a
L70@P5: study of the jetty at Moss Landing, California. This work has been termed
L71@P5: preliminary, inasmuch as it was envisioned as a baseline for future studies;
L72@P5: therefore, it provides a list of algal species present, along with notations,
L73@P5: and does not represent a compilation of ecological data, although some notes
L74@P5: are of an ecological nature. The purpose of this work, then, is to establish a basic flora upon which ecological analyses can be begun. This compilation is preliminary also because a flora is never complete; it is to
L75@P5: be expected that new discoveries will be made, particularly of the smaller,
L76@P5: physically less significant species; the authors have made several such additions during the last few weeks of the compilation.
L77@P5: Floristic differences were known in advance between the north and the
L78@P5: south side of the jetty; this was to be expected, owing to the differences
L79@P5: in salinity, wave force, exposure, etc., and the differing variations of the
L80@P5: factors. These differ'ences in ftoristic composition have been tabulated (see
L81@P5: Table 1). No attempt has been made, in this study, to detect and measure or
L82@P5: evaluate the various gradients of distribution (i.~., west to east into the
L83@P5: harbor, or vertical intertidal zonation). However, such gradients were observed; analysis of the vertical zonation relative to the horizontal will
L84@P5: likely show an interesting depression of zonation on the south side, for example.
L85@P5: The sources of information for this report include the following: (1)
L86@P5: field work done specifically for this survey; (2) collections made by
L87@P5-6: -1­students in two phycology classes at MLML during the period of the survey; (3)
L88@P6: collections made by earlier classes; (4) the Moss Landing Marine Laboratories
L89@P6: Herbarium. All voucher specimens have been accessioned to the MLML Herbarium,
L90@P6: and a master set of such vouchers has been assembled into a single issue exsiccata, the specimen numbers of which correspond to the species numbers in
L91@P6: the text. All collections are of the authors, except as noted in the text.
L92@P6: The notes provided for the various species are intended to supplement
L93@P6: presently available information; they are therefore uniquely applied to the
L94@P6: plants growing on the jetty. The plants of the jetty appeared and occurred
L95@P6: as described in Smith (1944) excepting as noted in the text, these annotations being morphological or ecological information as added or amended by
L96@P6: the information gathered for the jetty algae. In a similar manner, taxonomic
L97@P6: and nomenclatural information has not been included, excepting where it is
L98@P6: at variance or more recent than that given in the Monterey floras (Smith,
L99@P6: 1944; Hollenberg and Abbott, 1966). The bibliography, then, includes only
L100@P6: those references cited in the annotations and discussions.
L101@P6: In addition to the check-list, three features have been included in the
L102@P6: present ~rk. Table 1 provides a synopsis of the relative abundance and the
L103@P6: location of all of the species detected. Table 2 presents two lists. One
L104@P6: represents plants conspicuously, or unusually, absent from the jetty~ These
L105@P6: are species that, in the opinion of the authors, are commonly found and would
L106@P6: generally be expected to occur at a location such as the jetty. List two is
L107@P6: a compilation of species that the authors expect to be discovered growing on
L108@P6: the jetty in the future. The third feature is a set of keys to all of the
L109@P6: species detected thus far. It is to be noted that while these keys are
L110@P6-7: -2­inclusive, they are not exclusive, that is, a species not included in the key
L111@P7: may still be keyed (incorrectly). Therefore, new discoveries, and plants
L112@P7: from other localities will not satisfactorily be determined using these keys.
L113@P7: In this report, the location commonly referred to as the UNorth Jetty"
L114@P7: has been called "the jetty" (see map p. 35) in order to lessen confusion when
L115@P7: referring to the north or the south side. The term "south jetty" has been
L116@P7: retained for the "South Jetty", where only a few collections have been made,
L117@P7: mainly of Egregia spp.
L118@P7: Several specific locations on the jetty have become defined both by
L119@P7: their physical description and by their floristics. Several of these have
L120@P7: been termed communities, a reference to their floristic composition. These
L121@P7: are as follows (see also map, p. 35, and Table 1): (1) the water-sand-jetty
L122@P7: community, which is comprised of rocks partially and completely buried by
L123@P7: sand. Some floristic components of this community are Petalonia fascia,
L124@P7: Gigartina volans, Gracilaria sjoestedtii, and Gymnogongrus spp. These populations occur where the waterline on the jetty meets the sandy beach at
L125@P7: low tide; (2) the sand spit locality occurs along the slough side of the
L126@P7: jetty toward the harbor (see map, arrow denotes seaward end of sand spit).
L127@P7: Characteristic plants of this area include Cladophora microcladioides,
L128@P7: ~. ovoidea, Chaetomorpha aerea, DIva costata, Gracilaria sjoestedtii (and
L129@P7: its epiphytes Ceramium sp. and Polysiphonia sp.); (6) the harbor end of the
L130@P7: jetty; and "SJ," the region on the south jetty characterized by the presence of a large population of Egregia spp.
L131@P7: A notable limitation upon this study is the length of time the survey
L132@P7: covered; less than a year. Some information has been gathered regarding
L133@P7-8: -3­seasonal variation, for example of morphology or of abundance. However, a
L134@P8: clearer understanding of the jetty flora will be based upon a full annual
L135@P8: perusal of each floristic element_(species, populations, communities), and
L136@P8: better yet, collection of data over a number of years. The latter would detect not only seasonal variation, but an important variation - the yearly
L137@P8: variation. For example, this study covered a portion of a year that was a
L138@P8: "good" year for Gigartina subgenus Mastocarpus species, both from the standpoint of morphological expression and development, and of the abundance of
L139@P8: male plants, which were exceedingly abundant in locations where they were
L140@P8: difficult to find in previous years.
