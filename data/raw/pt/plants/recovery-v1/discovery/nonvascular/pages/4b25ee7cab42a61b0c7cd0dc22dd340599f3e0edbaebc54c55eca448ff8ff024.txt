L216@P8: AN INSPIRATION TO MANY
L217@P8: "He was the best teacher I ever hadl" so have said several of his former students.
L218@P8: A kindly and respected gentleman, Dr. Taft had a distinguished career spanning
L219@P8: four-and-a-half decades in botany at The Ohio State University. An inspiring,
L220@P8: thoroughly dedicated teacher in general botany and the algae, he was highly revered
L221@P8: by his students and honored several times for his excefience in the classroom, As
L222@P8: a research scientist, Taft was a recognized world authority on algal fioristics,
L223@P8: especially the taxonomy of the desmids, and water pollution problems related io
L224@P8: algae, His research was presented in over 50 published scientific papers and books,
L225@P8: as listed in his bibliography, p. 169. As a servant to the general public, he
L226@P8: generously shared his scientific knowledge, thereby helping many communities
L227@P8: improve iheir techniques of monitoring water quality and maintaining healthful
L228@P8: standards of living. Professor Taft exemplified the full mission of the land-grant
L229@P8: university and in so doing, brought honor and respect to his botanical profession
L230@P8: and to The Ohio State University.

L233@P8: for him or the evening of collegiate registration. To make certain he would complete
L234@P8: registration in time for the date, Clarence chose botany, as it had the shortest
L235@P8: line. This event occurred at Michigan State Normal College now Eastern Michigan
L236@P8: University!, Ypsilanti, where he held an undergraduate assistantship in botany,
L237@P8: and obtained a "Teachers Life Certificate" in 1927 and an A.R. degree in the
L238@P8: natural sciences in 1929.

L240@P8: by George Nichols at The University of Michigan Biological Station on Douglas
L241@P8: Lake near Pellston, Michigan. That summer he met Aaron J. Sharp, who was an
L242@P8: assistant in bol.any at the University of Oklahoma. Following this meeting, Dr.
L243@P8: Paul B. Sears, Chairman, offered an assistantship at that University, and Taft
L244@P8: earned his M.S. degree in botany there in 1931, His thesis was on the desmids

L246@P8: Survey Bul!erin.
