L9@P1: Abstract
L10@P1: The first overview of the bryophytic vegetation of the Mediterranean temporary ponds in Umbria region is reported. Phytosociological relevés
L11@P1: were carried out in a scattered system of ephemeral pools, where seasonal fluctuation in the water level is the main driving factor. By applying
L12@P1: Braun-Blanquet's approach to sample the bryo-communities and multivariate analysis tools to analyse data, the identification of some bryophyte
L13@P1: communities was possible, one of which is here described as new association. It is Entosthodono fascicularidis-Archidietum alternifolii ass. nova,
L14@P1: referable to the class Psoretea decipientis. The other bryo-communities have been framed into the classes Cladonio digitatae-Lepidozietea reptantis,
L15@P1: Ceratodonto purpurei-Polytrichetea piliferii and Psoretea decipientis again.
L16@P1: Key words: biodiversity, conservation, habitat 3170*, moss vegetation, phytosociology.
L17@P1: Introduction
L18@P1: The temporary ponds are "usually small (<10 ha in
L19@P1: area) and shallow wetlands which are characterized by
L20@P1: an alternation of flooded and dry phases, and whose
L21@P1: hydrology is largely autonomous. They occupy depressions, often endorheic, which are flooded for a
L22@P1: sufficiently long period to allow the development of
L23@P1: hydromorphic soils and wetland-dependent aquatic
L24@P1: or amphibious vegetation and fauna communities"
L25@P1: (Ramsar Convention, 1971; Ramsar Convention Bureau, 2002). Another important component is that the
L26@P1: flooded period typically alternates with dry phases, a
L27@P1: condition that prevents the development of species and
L28@P1: communities from the permanently wet habitats.
L29@P1: These peculiar habitats are characterized by specific
L30@P1: environmental and hydroecological conditions, where
L31@P1: a basic role is played by the length of the flooded period together with the waterdepth, the pond size and
L32@P1: its slope. Different ecological conditions give rise to
L33@P1: different types of temporarily wet habitats, from waterlogged soils and flat ponds, which get totally dry
L34@P1: in early spring, to basin-shaped ponds, whose bottom
L35@P1: keeps wet till the start of summer (Gigante et al., 2007,
L36@P1: 2013b; Ernandes & Marchiori, 2013). Deil (2005)
L37@P1: proposed several ecological models, distinguishing
L38@P1: waterlogged, amphibic, shallow and deep submerged
L39@P1: aquatic conditions on the ground of the amplitude of
L40@P1: water-level fluctuations in relation to soil surface. In
L41@P1: large and deep ponds, water can persist till full summer and different types of habitats, from temporarily
L42@P1: to permanently flooded, can often be found in contact
L43@P1: with each other, depending on the length of the wet
L44@P1: period.
L45@P1: The ephemeral wetland habitats have a very wide
L46@P1: distribution all over the world, although they are always rather localized and represented by very restricted areas of occurrence (Deil, 2005).
L47@P1: Similar ephemeral wetlands are widespread in most
L48@P1: of the world’s Mediterranean-climate regions: the west
L49@P1: coasts of North and South America, the Mediterranean
L50@P1: Basin, South Africa, and Southern Australia (Barbour
L51@P1: et al., 2005). Their distributional range, according to
L52@P1: Deil (2005), includes the Mediterranean Basin and the
L53@P1: sub-humid areas on both sides of the Tropic of Cancer.
L54@P1: In the subtropical regions, the wet season is mostly
L55@P1: spring and the pools desiccate in early summer (seasonal pool habitat).
L56@P1: Temporary ponds are present in Mediterranean and
L57@P1: Submediterranean Italy, however, their actual distribution is restricted and very localized, due to their peculiar ecology as well as to human impact and environmental changes and climate change (Gigante et al.,
L58@P1: 2007, 2013b; Bagella et al., 2010; Bagella & Caria,
L59@P1: 2012, 2013; Poponessi et al., 2014).
L60@P1: The Mediterranean temporary wet habitats are fragile
L61@P1: environments, being closely dependent on water fluctuations and frequently localized in small surface areas. Even though recently there is an increasing awareness of the importance of these types of environments,
L62@P1: not always appropriate safeguard measures are being
L63@P1: taken (Grillas, 2004a).
L64@P1: The Mediterranean temporary ponds represent a very
L65@P1: important biotope for bryophytes. One of the features

L68@P2: that allows them to succeed in these environments is
L69@P2: the ability to tolerate long periods of dehydration without suffering any physiological damage. Bryophytes
L70@P2: also have the ability to protect the soil from excessive
L71@P2: dehydration and erosion, thus promoting the establishment of vascular species, particularly amphibious
L72@P2: species of the Isoëto-Nanojuncetea class. Indeed, the
L73@P2: vegetation of European ephemeral wetlands has been
L74@P2: assigned to the class Isoëto-Nanojuncetea Br.-Bl. et
L75@P2: Tüxen ex Westhoff, Dijk et Passchier 1946 (Pietsch,
L76@P2: 1973; Philippi, 1977; Brullo & Minissale, 1998; Deil
L77@P2: et al., 1999; Deil, 2005), often neglecting the prominent role of the bryophytic taxa. In fact, in many botanical studies the cryptogams are skipped, although
L78@P2: this taxonomic group is very important just because
L79@P2: it contributes considerably to the total biomass of the
L80@P2: ephemeral wetland vegetation by even modifying the
L81@P2: vascular germination conditions (Deil, 2005).
L82@P2: The plant species present in the temporary ponds
L83@P2: depend to a large extent on the type of substrate beneath the pond, on the water depth and the duration of
L84@P2: flooding. In general they are all conditioned by a short
L85@P2: life cycle and are able to produce seeds and spores (in
L86@P2: the case of bryophytes) in the short favourable season
L87@P2: (Ruiz, 2008). The ecological factors that mostly influence the affirmation and diversity of the bryophytic
L88@P2: populations in temporary wetlands are temperature,
L89@P2: oligotrophy and the water regime (Cogoni et al.,
L90@P2: 2009).
L91@P2: In Italy, the first studies on the bryophytic vegetation have been carried out by several researchers in
L92@P2: different time periods (e.g. Giacomini, 1939; Hèbrard,
L93@P2: 1978; Cortini Pedrotti, 1982; Cortini Pedrotti, 1985;
L94@P2: Privitera, 1989, 1990; Lo Giudice, 1991; Aleffi, 1992a;
L95@P2: Aiello et al., 1997; Puglisi et al., 1991; Puglisi, 1995;
L96@P2: Puglisi et al., 2006) with reference to scattered areas in
L97@P2: Sicily, Abruzzo and Calabria. A strong improvent to the
L98@P2: knowledge of the phytocoenotic aspects of bryophytes
L99@P2: has been given by a huge syntaxonomic overview for
L100@P2: the whole of Italy (Puglisi & Privitera, 2012) and further developments have been added by the recent European Vegetation Checklist (Mucina et al., 2016) that
L101@P2: takes into account also the bryophytic syntaxa.
L102@P2: In Umbria Region, the bryophytic component of
L103@P2: these habitats has been studied only with reference
L104@P2: to the flora (Aleffi, 1992b; Cortini, 1985; Ellis et al.,
L105@P2: 2015; Ellis et al., 2016; Poponessi et al., 2016; Ellis et
L106@P2: al., 2017) while an overview of the bryophytic communities is still missing, to date. This is the first contribution focusing on bryophytic assemblages in this
L107@P2: type of ecosystems.
L108@P2: Aims of the present study are: i) to improve the
L109@P2: knowledge about the bryo-vegetation of the Mediterranean temporary ponds in central Italy and ii) to point
L110@P2: out the species with a prominent role in colonizing the
L111@P2: different micro-environments.

L113@P2: Study area
L114@P2: The study was carried out in the lowlands west of
L115@P2: Lake Trasimeno in Umbria (inland central Italy) at
L116@P2: Piana di Ferretto (N: 43°9.957', S: 11°59.667'), a flat
L117@P2: area near Castiglione del Lago, which is very particular in terms of its ecology (Fig. 1). The area belongs to
L118@P2: the Mediterranean Bioclimate with transitional traits
L119@P2: to the sub-Mediterranean variant of the temperate bioclimate (Gigante & Venanzoni, 2007; Pesaresi et al.,
L120@P2: 2014). The substratum is represented by sandy-clayey
L121@P2: Plio-Pleistocenic sediments which generate oligotrophic soils. The dominant soils are represented by
L122@P2: leached decarbonated sandy-argillaceous types (Giovagnotti et al., 2003).
L123@P2: The investigations focused on a system of Mediterranean temporary ponds categorized as a priority habitat
L124@P2: under the EU Directive 92/43/EEC and listed in Annex I with the code 3170*. The site has been selected
L125@P2: as a SAC (Special Area of Conservation) belonging to
L126@P2: Natura 2000 (IT5210020). These pond systems are exclusively powered by rainwater, distributed in an area
L127@P2: of more than two hectares, where crops, pig farms and
L128@P2: oak forests are alternated (Gigante et al., 2007). Data
L129@P2: on the bryological vegetation are here provided for the
L130@P2: first time, specifically regarding the Mediterranean
L131@P2: temporary pond systems of this area.
L132@P2: Materials and methods
L133@P2: The bryophytic vegetation surveys were carried out
L134@P2: in the spring period of the years 2015 and 2016. The
L135@P2: sampling sites were scattered in the system of temporary ponds of the study area, taking into account
L136@P2: the different micro-habitats characterized by different eco-hydrological conditions. The ponds locations
L137@P2: were known and already investigated with reference
L138@P2: to their vascular component (Gigante et al., 2007,
L139@P2: 2013b). As concerns the sampling methodology, the
L140@P2-3: Fig. 1 - The study area, located in the western sector of Umbria Region.Bryophytic vegetation in central Italy 33
L141@P3: phytosociological approach of Braun-Blanquet (1979)
L142@P3: was adopted, based on the collection of complete lists
L143@P3: of species and estimates of the related cover values,
L144@P3: by way of a 9-values alpha-numerical scale (Westhoff
L145@P3: & Van Der Maarel, 1978), adopting the subdivision
L146@P3: of the value "2" into three distinct ranges: "2m", "2a"
L147@P3: and "2b" (Barkman et al., 1964). A total amount of 59
L148@P3: phytosociological relevés were carried out in standard
L149@P3: 10x10 cm2 areas. The relevés took into account only
L150@P3: the bryophytic component. The occurrence of vascular species, generally represented only by the first
L151@P3: leaves due to the phenological shift compared to the
L152@P3: bryophytes, has been annotated and reported as total
L153@P3: number. For each plot, basic geographic and ecological details of the sampling areas have been recorded:
L154@P3: geographic coordinates, slope, exposure, total cover of
L155@P3: the vegetation, total cover of the bryophytes. Additionally, the number of total species per relevé has been
L156@P3: calculated and reported. The relevés have been stored
L157@P3: in the vegetation plot database VegItaly (Gigante et al.,
L158@P3: 2012; Landucci et al., 2012; Venanzoni et al., 2012).
L159@P3: After a first critical review of the 59 carried out relevés, seven of them have been removed since they referred to extremely species-poor communities, in some
L160@P3: cases monospecific, and not appropriate to be used for
L161@P3: a phytosociological characterization. For this reason
L162@P3: they have not been included in the multivariate analyses and they are only briefly commented in the results.
L163@P3: For the statystical analyses, 52 relevés have been used
L164@P3: to produce a "21 rows (species) x 52 columns (relevés)" matrix, after a transformation of the cover values according to Westhoff & Van Der Maarel (1978).
L165@P3: The differences among relevés have been measured by
L166@P3: applying the complete link algorithm (Orloci, 1978)
L167@P3: and the Euclidean distance as dissimilarity measure.
L168@P3: The analyses have been performed by using the SynTax 5.02 package (Podani, 1995). Groups of similar
L169@P3: relevés have been identified based on the dendrogram
L170@P3: clusters and interpreted on the ground of a cross-check
L171@P3: of their floristic composition with the available literature. They have been referred to bryo-phytosociological units at the association level. When no correspondence to already described syntaxa was possible, new
L172@P3: associations or sub-associations have been proposed,
L173@P3: complying with the rules of the International Code of
L174@P3: Phytosociological Nomenclature (Weber et al., 2000).
L175@P3: For the syntaxonomic framing of the bryo-communities, we followed the standards proposed by Puglisi &
L176@P3: Privitera (2012) and the most recent updates provided
L177@P3: by Mucina et al. (2016). The cited nomenclature of
L178@P3: vascular syntaxa follows Biondi & Blasi (2013) and
L179@P3: Biondi et al. (2014). The statistical significance of the
L180@P3: differences between variables (number and cover of
L181@P3: bryopytic vs. vascular species) in the identified groups
L182@P3: of relevés was analyzed by applying nonparametric
L183@P3: statistical methods (Mann-Whitney U-Test) by using
L184@P3: the software Analyst Soft StatPlus, mac v2009.
L185@P3: For each detected bryo-community, a description is
L186@P3: hereafter reported which includes also notes on the
L187@P3: co-occurrence of vascular taxa. For the Bryophytes
L188@P3: the used nomenclature follows Ros et al. (2007) for
L189@P3: Marchantiophyta and Ros et al. (2013) for Bryophyta.
L190@P3: For the critical genera we used Aleffi & Schumacker
L191@P3: (1995) and Hodgetts (2015). For the vascular species,
L192@P3: nomenclature is updated according to the most recent
L193@P3: reviews (Conti et al., 2005) and matches with the database AnArchive (Lucarini et al., 2015).

L209@P3: Vegetation of the class Psoretea decipientis Mattick

L211@P3: ENTOSTHODONO FASCICULARIDIS-ARCHIDIETUM ALTERNIFOLIUM ass. nova
L212@P3: (Holotypus: rel. n. 2, Tab.1; Cluster I)
L213@P3: This community is the most widespread and abundant in the temporary pools of Ferretto. It was found on
L214@P3: waterlogged soil, on very changeable slopes (around
L215@P3: 18±7° on average). It is characterized by the abundant
L216@P3: presence of Archidium alternifolium and Entosthodon
L217@P3: fascicularis, both acrocarpous mosses which give the
L218@P3: community its typical physiognomy. These two species are typical of the Mediterranean temporary ponds.
L219@P3: Some thalloid and small leafy liverworts are associated to this community, such as Riccia sorocarpa, R.
L220@P3: subbifurca, Fossombronia pusilla. The cover is high,
L221@P3: ranging between 60-90% (87% on average), and the
L222@P3: number of bryophytic species varies from 3 to 5. Archidium alternifolium was often observed colonizing
L223@P3: the bottom of the pond, after the disappearance of
L224@P3: surface water, lasting also during the dry period and
L225@P3: still detectable in August. Sometimes, the bottom of
L226@P3-4: the ponds is first colonized by species of Algae (uni-34 S. Poponessi et al.
L227@P4: dentified), showing a fast, ephemeral phenology and
L228@P4: completely disappearing when the top sediment gets
L229@P4: dry. Soon after, Archidium alternifolium colonizes the
L230@P4: microhabitat and can persists, coexisting with the annual vascular phytocoenoses which colonize the same
L231@P4: micro-environment in late spring.
L232@P4: Archidium alternifolium and Entosthodon fascicularis are indicated as characteristic species of the new
L233@P4: association. Both are typical elements of the dwarf
L234@P4: moss communities that colonize temporary pools (Grillas, 2004a). From an ecological point of view, both the
L235@P4: dominant species prefer acid-subneutrophilic and temporarily flooded soils (Dierßen, 2001). On the whole,
L236@P4: this ephemeral community behaves as terricolous and
L237@P4: sub-acidophilic. The vascular presence is rather irrelevant, both in number and cover values (Figs. 3 and
L238@P4: 4), mostly due to young individual fronds of Isöetes
L239@P4: histrix L. and initial leaves of Solenopsis laurentia (L.)
L240@P4: C. Presl.
