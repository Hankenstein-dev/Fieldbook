L6@P0: Abstract
L7@P0: Two species of charophytes are reported from Sarandë in Albania, Chara vulgaris
L8@P0: and C. corfuensis, the latter is a new species for Albania and is endemic to the
L9@P0: Balkan Peninsula. As the number of species is lower in Albania than in many of the
L10@P0: neighboring countries, a survey of brackish and freshwater localities is expected to
L11@P0: increase the number of species in the future.
L12@P0: Përmbledhje
L13@P0: Dy specie të harofiteve janë raportuar nga Saranda në Shqipëri, Chara vulgaris dhe
L14@P0: C. corfuensis, kjo e fundit është një specie e re për Shqipërinë dhe është endemike
L15@P0: për Gadishullin Ballkanik. Meqënëse numri i llojeve është më i vogël në Shqipëri se
L16@P0: në shumë vende fqinje, një vrojtim si në zonat me ujë të njelmët ashtu dhe me ujë të
L17@P0: ëmbël pritet që të rritë numrin e specieve në të ardhmen.
L18@P0: Key words: Albania, Sarandë, charophytes, Chara vulgaris, Chara corfuensis.
L19@P0: Introduction
L20@P0: Sarandë district is in the southernmost part of Albania. Three localities have
L21@P0: been visited, two freshwater lakes and one brackish water lake. The localities
L22@P0: are listed in Table 1.
L23@P0: The charophyte flora of Albania is still only partly known, as can be seen on
L24@P0: the distribution maps in Blaženčić & Blaženčić (2002) and Blaženčić et al.
L25@P0: (2006a). Filarszky (1931) reports four species of charophytes from Albania.
L26@P0: Kashta (1994,1999, 2009, 2013) reports seven species, most from northern
L27@P0: and north eastern parts of the country. Blaženčić & Blaženčić (2003) only
L28@P0: lists 14 species from Albania, few species compared to the 47 species which
L29@P0: have been recorded from the Balkans (Blaženčić & Blaženčić, 2006b).
L30@P0: Material and methods
L31@P0: This work is based on material collected in the localities in Albania on 25
L32@P0: and 26 June 2014. The specific conductivity of the water was measured with
L33@P0: a Milwaukee, SM 301 EC meter, range 0-1990 µm/cm and Martini EC 60
L34@P0: Meter, range 0- 20 mS/cm. Calcium and chloride content was measured with
L35@P0: Aquamerck test kits. Charophyte nomenclature is in accordance with Krause
L36@P0: (1997). Specimens collected are deposited at the Botanical Museum,

L78@P3: A short description of Chara corfuensis (Figure 4)
L79@P3: Specimens were up to 30 cm long. Plants monoecious. Relatively heavily
L80@P3: encrusted. They had stipulodes in two tiers (diplostephanous). Cortex 2-3-
L81@P3: corticate, diplostichous – isostichous. Spine-cells long, solitary or in pairs
L82@P3: (some), up to 3x the stem diameter. Branchlets, some ecorticate or with
L83@P3-4: cortex only on the 1-2 lowest internodes. Bract-cells elongate, verticillate.10 BSHN(UT) 19/2015

L85@P4: Discussion/Conclusions
L86@P4: The charophyte flora in Albania is only known in part. According to
L87@P4: Blaženčić & Blaženčić (2006b) of the known 47 species of charophytes in
L88@P4: the Balkan Peninsula, only 23 are found in Albania. These finds are
L89@P4: concentrated in Shkodër district with Lake Shodër (Kashta 1994, 1999,
L90@P4: 2009) and the area around Ochrid Lake on the border with Macedonia
L91@P4: (Kostic, 1937; Blaženčić & Blaženčić, 2002; Kashta et al., 2013). This can
L92@P4: be seen very clearly in figure 1 of Blaženčić & Blaženčić (2006a).
L93@P4: Some of the species found in Albania are endemic to the Balkans (Chara
L94@P4: ochridana) and some rare, as Chara hydropitys and C. corfuensis, the last
L95@P4: new to Albania and reported in this article. Many of the species found in
L96@P4: neighboring countries will probably be found in Albania, but this will require
L97@P4: a more systematic survey of the brackish- and fresh water localities of the
L98@P4: country.
