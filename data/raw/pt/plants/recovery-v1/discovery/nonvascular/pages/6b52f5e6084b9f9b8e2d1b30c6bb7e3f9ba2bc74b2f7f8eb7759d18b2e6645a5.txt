L137: Grimmia is a cite67†genus of cite61†mosses , originally named by cite68†Jakob Friedrich Ehrhart ^{cite69†[1] } in honour of cite70†Johann Friedrich Carl Grimm , a cite71†physician and cite72†botanist from cite73†Gotha , cite74†Germany .
L138: ## Geographic distribution
L139: 
L140: [cite75†edit ]
L141: 
L142: Although predominantly occurring in the moderate zones, representatives of the cite76†cosmopolitan cite67†genus Grimmia may be found in all parts of the world, from cite77†Alaska to the most southern point of cite78†Chile , and from cite79†Siberia to cite80†South Africa , though in cite81†tropic regions , e.g. cite82†Hawaii and cite83†Indonesia . Grimmia species can only be found at high altitudes, mainly mountains.
L143: ## Identification
L144: 
L145: [cite84†edit ]
L146: Grimmia is a notoriously difficult genus in terms of cite85†identification , and in the majority of cite86†herbaria a considerable number of species was found misidentified. The cite87†American cite88†bryologist cite89†Geneva Sayre ^{cite90†[2] } (1911–1992), who worked for many years on a cite91†monograph of the North American Grimmias, indicated in an original way these difficulties, as she said: "it contains an ambigua, a varia, a decipiens, a controversa, a revisa and at least two anomalas".^{cite92†[3] }
L147: ## Publications
L148: 
L149: [cite93†edit ]
L150: 
L151: In the cite94†Index Muscorum ,^{cite95†[4] } the genus Grimmia is represented with 800 names of cite96†published cite97†species . As since cite98†Loeske (1930),^{cite99†[5] } no revision of the cite100†European species had been carried out.
L152: 
L153: The cite101†Dutch bryologist H. C. Greven started in 1990 with Grimmia cite102†fieldwork and a revision of the Grimmia collections from important European herbaria. The results were published in "Grimmia Hedw. (Grimmiaceae, Musci) in Europe".^{cite103†[6] }
L154: After examining the Grimmia collections from herbaria in cite104†North- and cite105†South America , cite80†South Africa , cite106†Australia and cite107†New Zealand , and about 50 Grimmia collecting trips in all cite108†continents , Grimmias of the World was published.^{cite109†[7] } An important contribution was the revision of Grimmia in North America.^{cite110†[8] }
L155: In the past decades, some more bryologists became interested in the genus Grimmia. Jesús Muñoz published a herbaria revision of Grimmia in Latin America,^{cite111†[9] } and Maier studied herbarium cite112†specimens of Grimmia from the cite113†Himalaya .^{cite114†[10] }
