L11: CHALLENGE
L12: 
L13: Predicting habitat suitability under climate change is vital to conserving biodiversity but our current species distribution models rely on coarse scale climate data and may therefore lack precision.
L14: 
L15: We need finer, more accurate data to truly understand and protect vulnerable species.

L20: CLIMATE CHANGE

L22: AIMS
L23: BryoMicroClim focuses on understanding the vulnerability of Hedwigia striata, a near-threatened moss species in Europe, to climate change.
L24: 
L25: By studying its microclimate at fine-scale resolutions and long time-scales, we aim to provide more robust predictions of biological responses to changing climate and determine the resilience of microclimate refugia, bridging the gap between broad climate patterns and small-scale environments.
L26: 
L27: APPROACH
L28:   * Partnering with bryologists across Europe
L29:   * Collect and process in situ microclimate measurements from sampling sites across Europe for a target species
L30:   * Use of field sensors to capture detailed microclimate temperature and humidity data during 1 year
L31:   * Data analysis to identify fine-scale patterns of ecologically relevant microclimate variables for bryophytes
L32: 
L33: BRYOPHYTES
L34: are small, slow-growing plants, commonly known as mosses, whose water content varies with ambient moisture. This characteristic makes them highly sensitive to environmental conditions and therefore uniquely positioned to study the impacts of climate change.

L51: OBJECTIVES
L52: 
L53:   * Deploy an innovative network of microclimate sensors across Europe
L54:   * Uncover how microclimates vary over time and space for our target species
L55: 
L56:   * Compare local microclimates with broader climate trends
L57:   * Understand the species' limits and tolerance regarding temperature and humidity
L58:   * Explore how microclimate variation might help our target species adapt to climate change
