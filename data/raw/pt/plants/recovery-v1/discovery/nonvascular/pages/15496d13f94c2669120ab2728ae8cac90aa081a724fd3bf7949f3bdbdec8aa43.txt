L100: East Sussex, and its location within the cite38†United Kingdom L101: In England, cite39†Sites of Special Scientific Interest (SSSIs) are designated by cite40†Natural England , which is responsible for protecting England's natural environment. Designation as an SSSI gives legal protection to the most important wildlife and geological sites.^{cite41†[1] } As of March 2019, there are 65 sites designated in cite42†East Sussex , 45 of which have been designated for their biological interest, 15 for their geological interest, and 5 for both biological and geological interest.^{cite43†[2] }
L102: Six sites are cite44†Special Areas of Conservation , two are cite45†Special Protection Areas , two are cite46†Ramsar sites , twenty are cite47†Geological Conservation Review sites, ten are cite48†Nature Conservation Review sites, five are cite49†national nature reserves , ten are cite50†local nature reserves , six are in cite51†Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty , two are on the cite52†Register of Historic Parks and Gardens and three contain cite53†scheduled monuments .
L103: Nine sites are managed by the cite54†Sussex Wildlife Trust and one by the cite55†Royal Society for the Protection of Birds .
L104: cite42†East Sussex is a county in cite56†South East England . It is bordered by cite57†Kent to the north-east, cite58†West Sussex to the west, cite59†Surrey to the north and the cite60†English Channel to the south.^{cite61†[3] } It has an estimated population of 757,600 within an area of 1,795 km^{2} (443,554.2 acres), therefore making it the cite62†28th largest ceremonial county in the cite38†United Kingdom .^{cite63†[4] }
