L44:     (Gigartinaceae, Rhodophyta) from Pacific North America. Phycologia 47: 124–155. DOI: 10.2216/07-36.1
L45:     Species boundaries in the Gigartinaceae are poorly defined due to the high degree of phenotypic plasticity exhibited by
L46:     the thallus, with the result that many superfluous taxa have been described. To clarify the taxonomy of species of
L47:     Chondracanthus Kützing reported from the Gulf of California and Pacific coast of North America, morphological and
L48:     molecular phylogenetic studies were performed. Phylogenetic analyses of chloroplast and nuclear DNA yielded nearly
L49:     congruent hypotheses. DNA from type material was examined from select species to validate the correspondence
L50:     between modern and historically important collections. Monographic observations on species from Pacific North
L51:     America were included to clarify species boundaries. Analyses identified two species from the Gulf of California and
L52:     nine from the Pacific coast. The following statements are supported: (1) Gigartina serrata Gardner is independent of
L53:     Chondracanthus canaliculatus (Harvey) Guiry, and Chondracanthus serratus (Gardner) comb. nov. is being proposed. (2)
L54:     Thalli genetically close to C. canaliculatus but morphologically similar to G. serrata represent a new species of
L55:     Chondracanthus from Pacific Baja California, Mexico, Chondracanthus bajacalifornicus sp. nov. (3) Pinnate thalli
L56:     previously referred to C. canaliculatus but larger and closely related to C. canaliculatus and C. bajacalifornicus represent
L57:     a new species of Chondracanthus from southern California, Chondracanthus kjeldsenii sp. nov. (4) Gigartina californica J.
L58:     Agardh is conspecific with C. corymbiferus (Kützing) Guiry. (5) Gigartina boryi Setchell &amp; Gardner is a heterotypic
L59:     synonym of C. harveyanus (Kützing) Guiry. (6) Chondracanthus spinosus (Kützing) Guiry is a polymorphic species that
L60:     includes G. armata J. Agardh, G. asperifolia J. Agardh, G. echinata Gardner, G. eatoniana J. Agardh, and G. farlowiana
L61:     J. Agardh. (7) Chondracanthus exasperatus (Harvey &amp; Bailey) Hughey encompasses a range of forms of varying size,
L62:     margin shape, pigmentation, and thickness. (8) Gigartina acicularis sensu Dawson from the Gulf of California, C.
L63:     johnstonii (Dawson) Guiry, C. macdougalii (Dawson) Guiry, and G. pectinata Dawson are conspecific with C.
L64:     squarrulosus (Setchell &amp; Gardner) Hughey, P. C. Silva &amp; Hommersand. (9) Chondracanthus intermedius is tentatively
L65:     confirmed in the Gulf of California.
L66:     KEY WORDS: Chondracanthus, Gigartinaceae, Gulf of California, Pacific North America, Rhodophyta, species,
L67:     taxonomy, rbcL, ITS
L68:     INTRODUCTION
L69:     Chondracanthus Kützing (1843) is one of seven genera
L70:     currently recognized in the red algal family Gigartinaceae.
L71:     The genus contains approximately 18 species that are
L72:     distributed in New Zealand, Japan, Pacific North and
L73:     South America, and the North and South Atlantic Ocean
L74:     with a present center of distribution in the eastern North
L75:     Pacific Ocean (Hommersand et al. 1993). Thalli are soft to
L76:     firmly cartilaginous and variously branched to bladelike
L77:     and bear reproductive structures on ordinary branches,
L78:     pinnules, or spines, the latter for which the generic name
L79:     was proposed (Greek khondros, cartilage + acantha, spine).
L80:     Chondracanthus is currently restricted to Gigartinaceae
L81:     having auxiliary cells that form few gonimoblast initials
L82:     that are directed inwardly and surrounded by a compact
L83:     envelope divisible into inner vacuolated and outer cytoplasm-rich layers, with the gonimoblast filaments penetrating the inner layer and linking to the outer envelope by
L84:     secondary pit connections and with carposporangia borne
L85:     in short chains interspersed among the sterile gonimoblast
L86:     * Corresponding author (jhughey@hartnell.edu).
L87:     124
L88:     and envelope cells (Hommersand et al. 1993). Species are
L89:     distinguished based on thallus shape, size, color, and
L90:     intertidal position, although these characters show great
L91:     variability.
L92:     A tally of the species shows a marked discrepancy
L93:     between the number of taxa originally described and those
L94:     currently recognized. Nineteenth-century authors (especially J. Agardh, Harvey, &amp; Kützing) described about 70
L95:     species that were assigned to Gigartina Stackhouse (1809)
L96:     or to the segregate genera established by Kützing, namely,
L97:     Chondracanthus, Chondroclonium Kützing (1845), Chondrodictyon Kützing (1849), Mastocarpus Kützing (1843), and
L98:     Sarcothalia Kützing (1849). Of the nearly 40 species
L99:     recognized by Kützing (1849), 6 were recorded from North
L100:     America, 1 placed in Chondroclonium, 2 in Gigartina, and 3
L101:     in Mastocarpus. These segregate genera were all referred to
L102:     Gigartina by J. Agardh (1851 [1851–1863]), who assigned 23
L103:     species to the genus. J. Agardh (1876) later increased the
L104:     number to 39, of which 7 came from the eastern north
L105:     Pacific. In his final revision of the genus, J. Agardh (1899)
L106:     recognized 69 species of which 19 were from the Pacific
L107:     coast of North America. Gardner (1927) and Setchell &amp;
L108:     Gardner (1933) augmented the list, describing six new
L109:     species from the Pacific coast. In their final but preliminary
L110:     Hughey &amp; Hommersand: Systematics of Chondracanthus
L111:     survey of Gigartina, Setchell &amp; Gardner (1933) treated
L112:     Kützing’s segregate genera as subgenera and recognized 88
L113:     species worldwide, recording 3 from the Gulf of California
L114:     and 27 from the Pacific coast. Dawson (1944, 1946)
L115:     increased the number, describing 3 new species from to
L116:     the Gulf of California and 1 from the Pacific coast.
L117:     Consolidation of the numerous species attributed to the
L118:     Pacific coast of North America was initiated by Dawson
L119:     (1961) in his account of the Gigartinaceae of Pacific
L120:     Mexico. He recognized 17 species and reduced 8 previously
L121:     recognized species to synonymy. Gigartina binghamiae J.
L122:     Agardh (1899) was synonymized with G. corymbifera
L123:     (Kützing) J. Agardh (1876) [Mastocarpus corymbiferus
L124:     Kützing 1847] and G. boryi Setchell &amp; Gardner (1933) with
L125:     G. harveyana (Kützing) Setchell &amp; Gardner (1933) [M.
L126:     harveyanus Kützing 1847]. More important was his
L127:     perception that G. armata J. Agardh (1899), G. echinata
L128:     Gardner (1927), G. spinosa (Kützing) Harvey (1853) [M.
L129:     spinosus Kützing 1847], G. eatoniana J. Agardh (1899), and
L130:     G. asperifolia J. Agardh (1899) ’are variations of an
L131:     exceedingly polymorphic complex that defies specific
L132:     segregation’. This complex was treated as a single species,
L133:     G. spinosa, by Abbott (1972) and Abbott &amp; Hollenberg
L134:     (1976). Plants from the Gulf of California that were
L135:     interpreted by Setchell &amp; Gardner (1924) as G. chauvinii
L136:     were placed in synonymy with G. pectinata Dawson (1944)
L137:     by Dawson (1961). In the same monograph, Dawson (1961)
L138:     merged G. leptorhynchos forma caespitosa Dawson (1949)
L139:     with G. multidichotoma Dawson (1946). Abbott &amp; Hollenberg (1976) further condensed the list of species from
L140:     California to 10, treating G. multidichotoma as synonymous
L141:     with G. leptorynchos J. Agardh (1885), G. serrata Gardner
L142:     (1927) as conspecific with G. canaliculata Harvey (1841),
L143:     and G. californica J. Agardh (1899) as conspecific with G.
L144:     exasperata Harvey &amp; Bailey (1851). After observing
L145:     morphological variation in cultured G. exasperata and
L146:     studying reproductive development in Gigartinaceae, Kim
L147:     (1976) finalized the consolidation by merging G. exasperata,
L148:     G. spinosa, G. californica, G. harveyana, and G. corymbifera
L149:     under the name G. corymbifera.
L150:     Working with cultures, Polanshek &amp; West (1977) showed
L151:     that certain populations of G. papillata (C. Agardh) J.
L152:     Agardh (1846) [Sphaerococcus papillatus C. Agardh 1821]
L153:     comprise sexual plants that alternate with a tetrasporangial
L154:     crust previously treated as an independent genus, Petrocelis.
L155:     Since most species of Gigartina (including the type species)
L156:     have isomorphic reproductive stages, those species with an
L157:     alternation of heteromorphic stages appeared to warrant
L158:     generic separation. Guiry &amp; West (1983) showed that G.
L159:     stellata (Stackhouse) Batters (1902) [Fucus stellatus Stackhouse in Withering 1796] had a life history similar to that of
L160:     G. papillata. Because G. mamillosa (Goodenough &amp; Woodward) J. Agardh (1851) [Fucus mamillosus Goodenough &amp;
L161:     Woodward 1797], which was designated the type species of
L162:     Mastocarpus by Setchell &amp; Gardner (1933), is a taxonomic
L163:     synonym of G. stellata, it follows that Mastocarpus was
L164:     available to accommodate species of Gigartina having
L165:     a heteromorphic alternation of generations. Accordingly,
L166:     Mastocarpus was reinstated by Guiry et al. (1984) and
L167:     assigned to its own family, the Petrocelidaceae, named for
L168:     Petrocelis, the tetrasporangial stage. Molecular studies by
L169:     125
L170:     Fredericq &amp; Ramirez (1996) established that Mastocarpus
L171:     forms a well-supported clade within the Phyllophoraceae,
L172:     and the most recent treatment (Guiry &amp; Guiry 2007) places
L173:     Mastocarpus in the Phyllophoraceae.
L174:     On the basis of reproductive morphology and an analysis
L175:     of rbcL gene sequences, Hommersand et al. (1993, 1994)
L176:     reorganized the genera within the Gigartinaceae. Among
L177:     the species remaining in the Gigartinaceae from the eastern
L178:     North Pacific, Hommersand et al. (1993) referred G. volans
L179:     (C. Agardh) J. Agardh (1846) [Sphaerococcus volans C.
L180:     Agardh 1821] and G. leptorynchos to Mazzaella G. Detoni
L181:     f. (1936). Only 5 species from the Pacific coast were retained
L182:     in Chondracanthus – C. canaliculatus (Harvey) Guiry in
L183:     Hommersand et al. (1993), C. corymbiferus (Kützing) Guiry
L184:     in Hommersand et al. (1993), C. harveyanus (Kützing)
L185:     Guiry in Hommersand et al. (1993), C. spinosus (Kützing)
L186:     Guiry in Hommersand et al. (1993), C. tepidus (Hollenberg)
L187:     Guiry in Hommersand et al. (1993) [G. tepida Hollenberg
L188:     1945] – and 2 from the Gulf of California – C. johnstonii
L189:     (Dawson) Guiry in Hommersand et al. (1993) [G. johnstonii
L190:     Dawson 1944] and C. macdougalii (Dawson) Guiry in
L191:     Hommersand et al. (1993) [G. macdougalii Dawson 1944].
L192:     Two names were reinstated, one from the Pacific coast, C.
L193:     exasperatus (Harvey &amp; Bailey) Hughey in Hughey et al.
L194:     (1996), and the other from the Gulf of California, C.
L195:     pectinatus (Dawson) L. Aguilar &amp; R. Aguilar (1997) [G.
L196:     pectinata Dawson 1944]. Chondracanthus pectinatus, however, was recently treated as a heterotypic synonym of C.
L197:     squarrulosus [Grateloupia squarrulosa Setchell &amp; Gardner
L198:     1924] (Hughey et al. 2001). A summary of the nomenclatural history of species previously referred to as Gigartina
L199:     from the eastern North Pacific is provided in Table 1.
L200:     At present, four species are recognized from the Gulf of
L201:     California and six from the Pacific coast (Hommersand et
L202:     al. 1993, 1994, 1999). The purpose of this study is to
L203:     investigate the taxonomic revisions proposed for Chondracanthus by earlier (Kützing 1847, 1849; Harvey 1841, 1853;
L204:     J. Agardh 1899; Setchell &amp; Gardner 1933) and later
L205:     (Dawson 1944, 1961; Abbott 1972; Abbott &amp; Hollenberg
L206:     1976; Kim 1976; Hommersand et al. 1993, 1994, 1999)
L207:     workers. To address the taxonomy, the plastid rbcL gene
L208:     (large subunit of ribulose-1,5-biphosphate carboxylase/
L209:     oxygenase) and the Internal Transcribed Spacer (ITS)
L210:     regions one and two and the 5.8 S gene (nrDNA) were
L211:     analyzed. Where necessary, the ITS 1 region of the original
L212:     material was sequenced to confirm correspondence between
L213:     type and recent collections (Hughey et al. 2001). In
L214:     addition, a diagnosis of each species, lists of synonyms,
L215:     references to previous studies and illustrations, and details
L216:     of the morphological characters are included for species
L217:     from Pacific North America.

L497:     Alignment and choice of outgroup
L498:     The boundaries of the ITS regions were determined from
L499:     published sequences (Goff et al. 1994; Van Oppen et al.
L500:     1995). Alignment of rbcL sequences was performed visually
L501:     using PAUP* 4.0.0b4, E Smithsonian Institution (Swofford
L502:     2000). The ITS 1, 2, and 5.8 S regions were preliminarily
L503:     aligned with the Clustal W 1.61 alignment program and
L504:     then optimized manually in PAUP. The following Clustal
L505:     parameters were used to generate the initial sequence
