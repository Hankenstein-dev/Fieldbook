L12: cite8†What is a Genus (pl. Genera)? L13: 
L14: In botanical taxonomy, a genus (plural genera) is a rank used to group closely related species within a family. In the hierarchy, genus sits below family and above species.
L15: 
L16: Genera are defined by shared morphological, anatomical, and genetic characteristics (for example, features of flowers, fruits, seeds, or leaves) that indicate a close evolutionary relationship among the species they contain.
L17: Each genus can include one or more species. Examples include Rosa (roses) and Solanum (nightshades, including tomato and eggplant).

L24: cite6†Suggest a correction! L25: Fabronia is a globally distributed genus of pleurocarpous mosses in the family Fabroniaceae (order Hypnales), currently comprising about 120–150 species (IGS, 2017; Goffinet and Shaw, 2018). It occurs in warm temperate to tropical regions, with a concentration of species in the Neotropics and in South and East Asia, commonly on bark in humid forests and occasionally on rock or soil in shaded, often calcareous sites. The generic name is conserved, and the type species of Fabronia is F.
L26: pusilla (Wijk et al., 1960;(Index of Moss Names, 2024).
L27: The genus is recognized by its usually complanate, feathery shoots and ovate to lanceolate leaves with well-defined, single costae and toothed to entire margins, often bearing papillae or short basal prorulae. The perichaetial leaves are typically differentiated, and the double peristome bears 16 more or less equal exostome teeth and a weakly developed endostome with short or vestigial segments; capsules are inclined to horizontal and usually smooth (Buck and Ireland, 1989; Allen, 2002).
L28: Ovary/placentation are referable to the standard pleurocarpous pattern, and the capsule dehisces by an operculum, with elater-like structures frequently absent in Fabronia (Buck and Ireland, 1989).
L29: Diversity peaks in the Neotropics and in tropical Asia, with several regionally endemic species, including narrow Island endemics in the Pacific and New Caledonia. Habitats are typically epiphytic on trunks and branches in lowland to montane humid forests, sometimes extending into limestone or serpentine outcrops. Several taxa in arid-zone clades are adapted to seasonally desiccating microhabitats, reflecting broad ecological amplitude within warm regions (Allen, 2002; Goffinet and Shaw, 2018).
L30: Intrinsic biology: sexual condition is often autoicous; spores are wind-dispersed, and asexual reproduction is rare. Chromosome reports are sparse and variably reported for Fabronia (e.g., n=10 in some studies) and are best regarded as poorly settled without firm phylogenetic support; therefore, the base number is not reported here.
L31: Taxonomy and phylogeny: Fabronia is monophyletic within Fabroniaceae in recent molecular work (Goffinet and Shaw, 2018; Olsson et al., 2009), and species-level limits are under active revision in key regions. The genus has not been consistently divided into widely accepted subgenera or sections in modern treatments, though historical treatments applied subgeneric ranks.
L32: Alternative classifications in regional accounts may recognize segregate taxa in Eriodictyon or Plagiorhabdus, and these delimitation differences require careful comparative work; consequently, the circumscription remains species- rather than sectional-driven at present (WFO, 2024; Gradstein, 2023).
L33: Human relevance: a few Fabronia species are cultivated in terrarium or greenhouse setups as epiphytic mosses; most occur naturally in shaded, humid forest settings and have no significant economic uses. No taxa are widely recognized as invasive.
L34: 
L35: Conservation and outlook: many narrowly endemic species are under-collected and occur in small, threatened habitats; standardized IUCN assessments, targeted floristic revisions, and phylogenomic analyses are needed to secure taxonomy and red list priorities.
L36: Sources: Buck and Ireland (1989); Goffinet and Shaw (2018); Olsson et al. (2009); Wijk et al. (1960, updated Index of Moss Names, 2024); Gradstein (2023); WFO (2024); IGS (2017).
