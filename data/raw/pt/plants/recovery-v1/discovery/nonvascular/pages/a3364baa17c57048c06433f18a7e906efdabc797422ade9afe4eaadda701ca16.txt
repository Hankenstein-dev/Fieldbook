L181: This is a list of red cite116†seaweeds (cite117†Domain : cite118†Eukaryota , cite119†Division : cite120†Rhodophyta ) recorded from the cite121†oceans bordering South Africa. This list comprises locally used cite122†common names , cite123†scientific names with cite124†author citation and recorded cite125†ranges . Ranges specified may not be the entire known range for the species, but should include the known range within the waters surrounding the cite126†Republic of South Africa .
L182: List ordering and cite127†taxonomy complies where possible with the current usage in cite128†Algaebase , and may differ from the cited source, as listed citations are primarily for range or existence of records for the region. Sub-taxa within any given cite129†taxon are arranged alphabetically as a general rule. Details of each species may be available through the relevant internal links. cite130†Synonyms may be listed where useful.
L183: cite131†Red algae , or Rhodophyta (cite132†/roʊˈdɒfɪtə/ , cite132†/ˌroʊdəˈfaɪtə/ ; from cite133†Ancient Greek cite134†ῥόδον†en.wiktionary.org (rhódon) 'rose' and cite135†φυτόν†en.wiktionary.org (phutón) 'plant'), make up one of the oldest and largest cite136†phyla of cite137†eukaryotic cite138†algae , containing over 7,000 recognized species within over 900 cite139†genera amidst ongoing taxonomic revisions.
L184: The majority of species (6,793) are in the class cite140†Florideophyceae , and mostly consist of cite141†multicellular marine algae, including many notable cite142†seaweeds . Red algae are abundant in marine habitats. Approximately 5% of red algae species occur in freshwater environments, with greater concentrations in warmer areas. This may be due to an cite143†evolutionary bottleneck in which the last common ancestor lost about 25% of its core genes and much of its evolutionary plasticity.
L185: Red algae form a distinct group characterized by eukaryotic cells without cite144†flagella and cite145†centrioles , cite146†chloroplasts without external cite147†endoplasmic reticulum or unstacked (stroma) cite148†thylakoids , and use cite149†phycobiliproteins as cite150†accessory pigments , which give them their red color.
L186: Despite their name, red algae can vary in color from bright green, soft pink, resembling brown algae, to shades of red and purple, and may be almost black at greater depths and are only called red algae due to the phycobiliproteins and the reddish members. Unlike green algae, red algae store sugars as food reserves outside the chloroplasts as cite151†floridean starch , a type of starch that consists of highly branched cite152†amylopectin without cite153†amylose . Most red algae cite154†reproduce sexually .
L187: The life history of red algae is typically an cite155†alternation of generations that may have three generations rather than two. cite156†Coralline algae , which secrete cite157†calcium carbonate and play a major role in building cite158†coral reefs , belong there.
L188: Red algae such as cite159†Palmaria palmata (dulse) and cite160†Porphyra species (cite161†laver /cite162†nori /cite163†gim ) are a traditional part of cite164†European and cite165†Asian cuisines and are used to make products such as cite166†agar , cite167†carrageenans , and other cite168†food additives .
