L45@P3: SUMMARY
L46@P3: This report provides full documentation of the new proposals for EUNIS habitats up to level
L47@P3: 5 for marine habitats found under the MeshAtlantic project. Three case studies were presented in
L48@P3: detail, the Algarve region and the Southwestern Coast in Portugal and the Basque Country in Spain,
L49@P3: plus other contributions from Brittany (France). Overall, there were 45 new habitat proposals. This
L50@P3: represents a contribution to a comprehensive typology for the marine habitats of the European
L51@P3: Atlantic coast.
L52@P3: INTRODUCTION
L53@P3: Marine protection has been emphasized through global and European conventions which
L54@P3: highlighted the need for the establishment of special areas of conservation. Classification and habitat
L55@P3: mapping have been developed to enhance the assessment of marine environment and improve
L56@P3: spatial and strategic planning of human activities and to help on the implementation of ecosystem
L57@P3: based management. European Nature Information System (EUNIS) is a comprehensive habitat
L58@P3: classification system to facilitate the harmonised
L59@P3: description and collection of habitat and biotopes
L60@P3: that has been developed by the European
L61@P3: Environment Agency (EEA) in collaboration with
L62@P3: experts from institutions throughout Europe.
L63@P3: The EUNIS system is a hierarchical classification
L64@P3: which comprises at least five levels of complexity
L65@P3: depending on the substrate type, ecological
L66@P3: zones (e.g. infralittoral, circalittoral), coastal wave
L67@P3: exposure and biological communities (Connor et
L68@P3: al. , 2004) (Figure 1).
L69@P3: •BIOTOPE: A biotope is defined as the combination of a habitat and its associated
L70@P3: community of species (Olenin and Ducrotoy, 2006).
L71@P3: OBJECTIVES
L72@P3: Under the MeshAtlantic project, partners committed to identify habitats and biotopes at
L73@P3: a broad and fine scale based on historical and new surveys data. Surveys were to assess Marine
L74@P3: Protected Areas (MPAs) and others natural areas with great potential to integrate the marine
L75@P3: Natura 2000 network sites. These campaigns were carried out in a concerted manner to produce
L76@P3: habitat maps compliant with best practices and operational procedures developed by previous
L77@P3: Interreg projects (Mesh, Balance). This project will also allow to improving the EUNIS classification
L78@P3: for southern Atlantic Area regions. The present report aimed to prepare the MeshAtlantic proposal
L79@P3: of new habitat types found for the EUNIS classification in the European Atlantic coast.

L96@P4: INTRODUCTION
L97@P4: The EUNIS classification has been applied to the North-East Atlantic and Mediterranean
L98@P4: coast but still excludes most of the south Atlantic coast of Europe. Thus it is important to test its
L99@P4: application and effectiveness in the European south coast where it remains less used. The EUNIS
L100@P4: application to this region should lead to an improvement of the classification as a whole.
L101@P4: Since 2003 several surveys have been planned and focused on the characterization and
L102@P4: mapping of marine habitats and biotopes of the south coast of Portugal (e.g. Gonçalves et
L103@P4: al., 2010). These surveys were designed to produce new habitat maps that could facilitate the
L104@P4: execution of the Habitats Directive and future implementation of the Water and Marine Strategy
L105@P4: Framework Directives. Those historical data and more recent biological data that derived from the
L106@P4: new MeshAtlantic surveys were used for the implementation of the EUNIS habitat classification for
L107@P4: the Algarve coast.
L108@P4: The objective of this case study is to illustrate the classification process of marine habitats
L109@P4: of the south coast of Portugal in view of new biological data available. Seven provisional biotopes
L110@P4: for the classification are also presented in detail as a case study of a new proposal for the EUNIS
L111@P4: classification from the south coast of Portugal (Algarve).
L112@P4: APPLICATION OF THE EUNIS CLASSIFICATION
L113@P4:  STUDY AREA
L114@P4: Historical data and new surveys have been used for habitat characterization and EUNIS
L115@P4: classification of the south coast of Portugal (Algarve). The main biological data set and study
L116@P4: area were derived from the previous RENSUB project (Gonçalves et al., 2004, 2007, 2008, 2010).
L117@P4: The most recent biological data came from the new surveys carried out under the MeshAtlantic
L118@P4: assignment (Monteiro et al., 2012a; Monteiro et al., 2013; unp. data). Sampling was stratified by
L119@P4: depth and bottom substrate, and used underwater visual census, Van Veen grab, beam trawl and
L120@P4: quadrates method to estimate fauna densities and macroalgae coverage. Important additional
L121@P4: data was obtained by ROV surveys carried out by UALG/CCMAR or in cooperation with the NGO
L122@P4: OCEANA. The Rensub project (2003-2010) was carried out at the central off Algarve coast between
L123@P4: an area close to the cape of Santa Maria (Faro) and the Ponta da Piedade cape (Lagos) at west
L124@P4: side. This marine area is part the National Underwater Ecological Reserve that extends from the
L125@P4: coastline to 30 metre depth and is 3 km wide in the west and over 10 km wide in the eastern part
L126@P4: (Figure 2). On the other hand, new surveys within the MeshAtlantic framework were carried out
L127@P4: in the coast of Sagres, between 0 and 90 m depth and in an offshore area of Portimão between
L128@P4: 60 and 90 m (Figure 2). Both surveys began with a previous acoustic survey of the seabed by
L129@P4: means of Side Scan Sonar and Multi-beam, followed with sediment type classification and ground
L130@P4-5: truthing validation using grab sampling, Beam trawl and ROV surveys (Figure 2). 3
L131@P5:  DATA ANALYSIS AND MAPPING
L132@P5: The upper EUNIS hierarchy classifications (EUNIS levels 1, 2 & 3 or 4) were based on the
L133@P5: commonly referred to as the top-down approach. Upper classifications were computed on the
L134@P5: Raster Calculator (ArcMap/ESRI Spatial Analyst extension) and were basically driven by combining
L135@P5: environmental raster variables required by the EUNIS classification (Connor et al., 2004), such as:
L136@P5: bottom type (e.g. rock, sand, and mud), biological zone (e.g. infralittoral, circalittoral) and energy
L137@P5: level (e.g. high energy).
L138@P5: Environmental variables:
L139@P5: Bottom Type: information of the type of substrate present at the seabed is essential for
L140@P5: marine communities and for the EUNIS classification. Before computation habitat classification
L141@P5: substrate type was classified according to the Folk (1974) simplified classification used in the
L142@P5: MeshAtlantic project (Rock, Sand, Sandy mud, Muddy sand, Mud, Mixed sediment and Coarse
L143@P5: sediment).
L144@P5: Biological Zone: The fraction of light reaching the seabed was used to define the limit
L145@P5: between ecological zones. This fraction of light is one of the main physical factors commonly
L146@P5: correlated to the abundance of macrophytes and macroalgae species (e.g. Peres and Picard, 1964).
L147@P5: Studies have stated that the value of 1% of surface light is required for kelp and Posidonia oceanica
L148@P5: for the North Sea and Mediterranean sea, respectively (Ballesta et al., 2000; McBreen and Askew,
L149@P5: Figure 2. Study area within Rensub Project (a) and under the MeshAtlantic project: Portimão area (b) and Sagres area
L150@P5: (c).
L151@P5: c) b)
L152@P5-6: a)4
L153@P6: 2011). Following previous assessment of the macroalgae distribution in the Algarve coast under
L154@P6: MeshAtlantic (Monteiro et al., 2012b) the value of 1% was adopted as a proxy to define the lower
L155@P6: infralittoral limit given the broad scale of the classification.
L156@P6: Energy level: Following an analysis of the wave energy and currents data the almost entire
L157@P6: Algarve shelf coast was considered moderately exposed. These sites generally include open
L158@P6: coasts facing away from prevailing winds and without a long fetch but where strong winds can be
L159@P6: frequent (Connor et al., 2004).
L160@P6: To predict upper habitat classification level both raster variables were assigned to a set
L161@P6: of code values (Table I). These code values make easier the computation method of combining
L162@P6: environmental raster variables required by the EUNIS classification. After the addition the combined
L163@P6: resulted code was renamed by the actual EUNIS habitat classification.
L164@P6: Level 4 (and lower) habitat classification
L165@P6: To set up the lower level of the classification, a statistical analysis of species records were
L166@P6: made. Biological data set falling in the upper levels (Level 3 or 4) were analysed using the analytical
L167@P6: techniques available in the PRIMER 6 package (Clarke & Warwick, 2001). CLUSTER (Hierarchical
L168@P6: cluster analysis) and nMDS (Non-metric Multi-Dimensional Scaling) analysis based on species
L169@P6: matrices listing individual counts on each sampling were carried out. The similarity/dissimilarity
L170@P6: among data sets from every provisional typologies or habitat was assessed by using the ANOSIM
L171@P6: (Analysis of similarities) (Clarke & Warwick, 2001).
L172@P6: The total species composition of each habitat derived from previous data analysis was then
L173@P6: checked using SIMPER analysis. Particular attention was given to the set of species contributing to
L174@P6: the similarity and dissimilarity. Following Connor et al. (2004), species contributing with more than
L175@P6: 1% to the overall similarity of the records within the data set were defined as characterising the
L176@P6: habitat. Characteristic or indicator species of each biotope were also determined in our case study

L201@P7:  RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
L202@P7: The present study allowed the identification of seven habitats on rocky and soft bottom.
L203@P7: These habitats are present on the infralittoral and circalittoral biological zones of the Algarve
L204@P7: coast between the shoreline and 30 meter depth (Figures 3 and 4). According to the statistical
L205@P7: analysis there are confidently typology differences and therefore, from our point of view, these
L206@P7: habitats are distinctive and in need of proper classification. Besides that, when these habitats were
L207@P7: compared to those existing on the EUNIS habitat database, it was possible to verify the existence
L208@P7: of considerable differences in the characteristics species. This is not surprising since most of the
L209@P7: current EUNIS classification is based on data collected from the Northern European Coast (North
L210@P7: Sea and Baltic Sea). The list of the EUNIS habitats identified in the south coast of Portugal (Algarve)
L211@P7: as well as the new propositions for the EUNIS classification scheme are presented in table II.

L253@P9: DESCRIPTION
L254@P9: This habitat is located in circalittoral rocky areas at depths of 20 metres and
L255@P9: markedly goes on to greater depths. The rocky areas are predominantly regular
L256@P9: and are clearly different from other coastal habitats. The mud sediment that in
L257@P9: some extent covers the seabottom is one of the main reasons. This habitat also
L258@P9: differs from other coastal habitats due to the density of several seafans species,
L259@P9: especially six gorgonian species (Eunicella labiata, Eunicella verrucosa, Eunicella
L260@P9: gazella, Leptogorgia sarmentosa and Leptogorgia lusitanica). It is also characterised
L261@P9: by large density of others species such as cnidarians (Parazoanthus axinellae,
L262@P9: Actinothoe sphyrodeta) and sponges (Axinella polypoides, Axinella damicornis,
L263@P9: Crella fusifera, Crella elegans, Cliona celata, Adreus fascicularis and Phorbas fictitius).
L264@P9: Some bryozoans species (e.g Adeonella calveti, Pentapora foliacea), ascideans
L265@P9: (Stolonica socialis, Smithia cervicornis) and gastropodes (Bolma rugosa, Hexaplex
L266@P9: trunculus) are also indicator species. Two fish species are also characteristic of
L267@P9: this habitat (Gobius gasteveni and Serranus hepatus) and only few coralline algae
L268@P9: species can be found (e.g. Lithophyllum incrustans and Mesophyllum lichenoides).
L269@P9: According to the IUCN (2013) the gorgonian Eunicella verrucosa is facing a high risk
L270@P9: of extinction in the wild in the medium-term future. The sand goby Pomatoschistus
L271@P9: minutus under protection by the Bern Convention can be found in this habitat.

L301@P10: This habitat can be found on infralittoral rocky areas at depths between 15 and
L302@P10: 30 meters. The rocky bottom is predominantly regular in terms of rugosity
L303@P10: but rocky slopes between one and two meters are often distinguishable. Near

L347@P11: DESCRIPTION
L348@P11: This habitat may be found on infralittoral rocky areas at depths between 0 and 30
L349@P11: meters. The rocky bottom is characterized by a low rugosity although rocky slopes,
L350@P11: between one and two meters high, are commonly seen here. The wave energy
L351@P11: and currents effect, as well as the fraction of light that reaches the sea bottom
L352@P11: are very important ecological features for this habitat. The algae species are
L353@P11: predominant here (Plocamium cartilagineum, Lithophyllum incrustans, Halopteris
L354@P11: filicina, Mesophyllum lichenoides, Sphaerococcus coronopifolius, Peyssonnelia
L355@P11: rubra), especially two species: Dictyota dichotoma and Cystoseira usneoides. This
L356@P11: habitat is generally located near the coastal sheltered bays and small isles with
L357@P11: distinct features. In general, this habitat is also characterized by a large abundance
L358@P11: of invertebrates such as anemones (Anemonia sulcata, Aiptasia diaphana),
L359@P11: echinoderms (Paracentrotus lividus, Holothuria arguinensis, Holothuria mammata,
L360@P11: Ophioderma longicauda), molluscs (Gibbula cineraria, Clavagella melitensis) and
L361@P11: bryozoa (Schizobrachiella sanguinea and Pentapora foliacea). Some fish species
L362@P11: such as Parablennius pilicornis, Diplodus vulgaris, Gobius xanthocephalus and two
L363@P11: wrasses species (Coris julis, Symphodus bailloni) are very common in this habitat
L364@P11: and can be considered as good indicative species. According to the IUCN (2013),
L365@P11: the sea fan Eunicella verrucosa, also presented in this habitat, is facing a high risk
L366@P11: of extinction in the wild in the medium-term future. The sand goby Pomatoschistus
L367@P11: minutus under protection by the Bern Convention may also be found in this habitat.
