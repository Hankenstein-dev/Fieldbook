L37@P1: INTRODUCTION
L38@P1: The only previous records of Pedobesia from the broad expanse of the Indian
L39@P1: Ocean have been those of P. clavaeformis (J. Agardh) MacRaild et Womersley ( 1974)
L40@P1: from Western Australia by Womersley (1984) and Huisman & Walker (1990). South
L41@P1: African records of Derbesia ryukyuensis Yamada et T. Tanaka f = Pedobesia ryukyuensis
L42@P1: (Yamada et T. Tanaka) Kobara et Chihara] by Bolton & Stegenga (1987) and Anderson
L43@P1: & Stegenga ( 1989) were later recognized by Stegenga et al. ( 1997) to be Derbesia marina
L44@P1: (Lyngbyc) Sober. Thus, the identification of Pedobesia lamourouxii (J. Agardh) Feldmann
L45@P1: et al.1 in collections made from the Sultanate of Oman and from Socotra Island, Yemen,
L46@P1: represent a noteworthy addition to the fiora of this region of the northern Arabian Sea.

L56@P1: In the course of this study we recognized that the name Bryopsis simplex
L57@P1: Meneghini ex Kiitzing (1843), which has been applied to a taxon that has been

L59@P1: epithet-bearing name over Derbesia lamourouxii (J. Agardh) Sober (1846). The basionym
L60@P1: of this latter name is Bryopsis balbisiana var. lamourouxii J. Agardh ( 1842), which has
L61@P1: priority only at the varietal level (see Art. 11.2 of the ICBN, Greuter et al., 1994). The first
L62@P1: person to propose this taxonomic synonymy was Kiitzing (1849), who was followed,
L63@P1: among others, by Hauck (1882-1885), Ardissone (1886), DeToni (1889), and Pignatti
L64@P1: (1962).

L115@P3: incompta Meneghini in Zanardini (1865) as conspecific with Bryopsis lamourouxii with

L117@P3: with that of B. lamourouxii, whereas the erect axes measure only about one-eighth of the
L118@P3: width of the axes known for Pedobesia simplex (= P. lamourouxii). This discrepancy may
L119@P3: be the result of an error in the magnification given by Zanardini. On the other hand, after
L120@P3: an examination of 22 collections of this taxon in GENT and MICH and in the loan from

L122@P3: Meneghini (1845) recognized four varieties of Bryopsis simplex: var. nuda

L137@P4: Vegetative morphology
L138@P4: Thallus grass-green, 4-5 cm high, usually in tufts (Fig. 9) composed of erect,
L139@P4: scarcely branched, siphonous filaments (Fig. 10), 440-800 pm in diameter in the middle
L140@P4: part (narrower at the base: 300-350 pm diameter) (Fig. 5), obtuse to slightly tapering at
L141@P4: the apex. Basal rhizoids much branched, 45-100 pm in diameter. Cell walls 3-10 pm
L142@P4: thick. Chloroplasts numerous, 2.5—4.0 pm in length, lacking pyrenoids. Prostrate disc
L143@P4: calcified with numerous pores (Fig. 11).
L144@P4: Reproductive morphology
L145@P4: Sporangia bome subapically on the erect filaments, 1-6 per filament (Figs 6-8,
L146@P4: 10), arranged on one side of the filament or all around, mature sporangia spherical to
L147@P4: reniform, 300-500 pm in diameter, length/width: 0.8-1.0.
L148@P4: Ecology
L149@P4: The Socotran specimen was found on the Nogid (southern) coast of the island,

L152@P4: of the Socotra Archipelago, is subject to upwelling of colder, nutrient-rich waters during
L153@P4: the southwest monsoon between April and October. This biotope is covered by a rich and
L154@P4: unique assemblage of macroalgal species along with soft and hard corals. Some of the
L155@P4: other algae are Caulerpa racemosa (Forsskâl) J. Agardh, C. scapelliformis (Turner) C.

L157@P4: Kylin, Solieria robusta (Greville) Kylin, Portieria hornemannii (Lyngbye) P. Silva.
L158@P4: The Omani specimens were collected at depths of 6 to 12 m in a rocky habitat

L186@P6: Taxonomy and nomenclature
L187@P6: As early as 1839 Montagne proposed that this alga under discussion be
L188@P6: segregated as a distinct genus and gave a diagnosis but without providing a generic name.
L189@P6: As discussed by Schiffner (1935), J. Agardh (1887) greatly confused the situation by
L190@P6: treating his var. disticha and var. lamourouxii to be one and the same species, Bryopsis
L191@P6: balbisiana Lamouroux ex C. Agardh (1823). Feldmann (1937) rectified the earlier
L192@P6: taxonomic confusion about the status of B. balbisiana and determined that the only
L193@P6: specimen in Caen bearing a label in the hand of Lamouroux with the name ‘Bryopsis
L194@P6: Balbisiensis' was a specimen that corresponded to the concept of Bryopsis, not to
L195@P6: Derbesia lamourouxii. Despite Feldmann’s observation and the later acceptance of B.
L196@P6: balbisiana by Koster (1941) and Boudouresque & Perret (1977), Pignatti (1962) treated
L197@P6: this name as a synonym of Derbesia lamourouxii. Although Lamouroux (1813), when
L198@P6: publishing the name Bryopsis balbisiana, did not give a description, his figure (pi. 13, fig.
L199@P6: 2) shows a habit (with branching to 3 or 4 orders) foreign to that of Pedobesia simplex,
L200@P6: and thus we cannot agree with Pignatti.
L201@P6: Ecology
L202@P6: Feldmann ( 1937, as Derbesia lamourouxii) observed that Pedobesia simplex
L203@P6: occurs on rocks in shaded stations close to the wat£j surface. Athanasiadis (1987) reported
L204@P6: it as occurring sublittorally in the Aegean Sea in shaded habitats from 1 to 6 m depth.
L205@P6: Ardré (1970) observed this species on the coast of Portugal, where it occurs in exposed
L206@P6: and sheltered habitats, particularly at the extreme low water mark on shaded rocks, rarely
L207@P6: in illuminated tide pools. Abélard (1982) stated the habitat for this species is shaded rocks
L208@P6: at the low-water mark. An examination of nine specimens (in GENT) from the
L209@P6: Mediterranean collected by E. Coppejans showed that Pedobesia simplex commonly
L210@P6: occurred either at the infralittoral fringe or in the subtidal from 25 to 35 m in depth.

L229@P8: Historical perspective and floristic affinities
L230@P8: Although the recent catalogue of benthic marine algae for the Indian Ocean by
L231@P8: Silva et al. (1996) did not include any records from Socotra Island, a short list of marine
L232@P8: and fresh-water taxa was included by Dickie (1888) in Balfour’s “Botany of Socotra”.
L233@P8: Dickie’s list was repeated in Forbes (1903), along with a list of algae from nearby
L234@P8: Abd-el-Kuri identified by E. M. Holmes. Recently, the United Nations Development
L235@P8: Programme (UNDP) started to show interest in the conservation and sustainable use of the
L236@P8: biodiversity (both marine and terrestrial) of the Socotra Archipelago (UNDP/GEF Project
L237@P8: YEM/96/G32). The Proceedings of the First International Symposium on Socotra Island,
L238@P8: which arose from the UNDP project, included a paper on seaweeds (Banaimoon, 1998),
L239@P8: but the list of species was based on mainland Yemeni collections. Kemp ( 1998a) reported
L240@P8: the occurrence of the distinctive fucoid Nizamuddinia zanardinii (Schiffner) Silva from
L241@P8: Socotra, reflecting the floristic affinity between this archipelago and the monsoonimpacted fiora of the Yemeni and Omani coasts of the northern Arabian Sea. The
L242@P8: unexpected occurrence of the kelp Ecklonia radiata (C. Agardh) J. Agardh on the Arabian
L243@P8: coast (Lüning, 1990) reflects upwelling from the seasonal monsoon, which contributes to
L244@P8: a diversified flora with a mixture of tropical, subtropical and warm temperate elements.
L245@P8: Hiscock et al. (1984) have characterized the littoral algal cover of southern Oman as
L246@P8: undergoing « drastic changes » because of the differences in the local weather conditions
L247@P8: between pre- and post-monsoon periods.
L248@P8: Up lo now there has been little known on the affinities of the Socotran seaweed
L249@P8: flora. Previous zoogeographic studies (Salm, 1993; Kemp, 1998b) showed that the coral
L250@P8: reef communities and associated fish fauna of the Socotran Archipelago mostly closely
L251@P8: resembled those of the southern Arabian peninsula. The zoogeographic distribution of
L252@P8: these corals and fishes in the southern Arabian area is mainly influenced by the seasonal
L253@P8: Somali Current with its associated upwelling of cold nutrient-rich water. Despite the
L254@P8: resemblance in coral reef communities, there are also some major differences. Those
L255@P8: zoogeographic studies also showed that an east African influence is evident in Socotra,
L256@P8: which is minimal on the mainland coast of Arabia.
