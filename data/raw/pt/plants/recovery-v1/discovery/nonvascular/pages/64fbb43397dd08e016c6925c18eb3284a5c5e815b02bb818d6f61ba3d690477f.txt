L192@P6: This project on non-geniculate coralline algae had
L193@P6: its genesis more than a decade ago. In the early
L194@P6: 1990s Bill Woelkerling and I discussed how we could
L195@P6: begin to document New Zealand’s non-geniculate
L196@P6: flora and increase recognition of the importance of
L197@P6: these species in coastal ecosystems. The gaps in our
L198@P6: knowledge about the coralline algae of New Zealand
L199@P6: had been obvious for many years but the technical
L200@P6: difficulties involved in working with these calcified
L201@P6: red algae, as well as the history of confused taxonomy
L202@P6: and nomenclature for New Zealand species, meant
L203@P6: that there were significant hurdles to be overcome.
L204@P6: The priority of this research work, which has been
L205@P6: funded by the Ministry of Fisheries Biodiversity
L206@P6: Programme (ZBD2001/05), has been to develop a
L207@P6: reliable and durable reference, which can be used in
L208@P6: fisheries and coastal management, as well as in marine
L209@P6: resource protection and conservation. We wanted to
L210@P6: make information accessible to marine scientists and
L211@P6: resource managers, and to improve understanding of
L212@P6: these algae, both through the production of this guide
L213@P6: and through improved reference collections lodged in
L214@P6: the herbaria at Te Papa (WELT), Auckland Museum
L215@P6: (AK), and Landcare Research (CHR).
L216@P6: The taxonomic research using morphological and
L217@P6: anatomical characters which forms the basis of
L218@P6: this guide has been complemented with data from
L219@P6: molecular sequencing, carried out at the University
L220@P6: of Otago by Dr Judy Broom and Darren Hart. While
L221@P6: this work is not discussed in this guide, it forms a
L222@P6: very important and exciting area of research making
L223@P6: use of new tools and approaches to better understand
L224@P6: species relationships and identity.
L225@P6: In treatments of coastal communities, even within
L226@P6: recent publications, non-geniculate coralline algae
L227@P6: have been referred to as “pink paint”, completely
L228@P6: overlooking the diversity that may be present and
L229@P6: the complex ecological roles particular species may
L230@P6: play. We hope that the production of this guide and
L231@P6: the underlying research that has been supported by
L232@P6: the Ministry of Fisheries will cause people to look
L233@P6: more closely, and to recognise and document coastal
L234@P6: diversity including these key organisms, and that this
L235@P6: will lead to a better understanding of the dynamic
L236@P6: relationships between these algae and the coastal
L237@P6: flora and fauna.

L243@P7: dentifying non-geniculate corallines poses certain
L244@P7: challenges, particularly for the non-specialist. Many
L245@P7: species can vary considerably in growth form and
L246@P7: external appearance, thereby making reliable sightrecognition of most species difficult or impossible.
L247@P7: Two individuals belonging to the same species can
L248@P7: look quite different depending on age, habitat, and
L249@P7: other factors. Likewise, specimens of two species that
L250@P7: are quite different taxonomically often cannot be told
L251@P7: apart solely on the basis of growth form and external
L252@P7: appearance. Thus, without further information,
L253@P7: it generally is not possible to identify 17 of the 20
L254@P7: species included in this guide through immediate
L255@P7: use of the keys (Chapters 8 & 10) or a quick scan
L256@P7: of the species profiles (Chapter 12). Rather, coralline
L257@P7: identification is based on vegetative and reproductive
L258@P7: characters (detailed in Chapter 7), and use of the keys
L259@P7: relies on observation of these important anatomical
L260@P7: and morphological features.

L361@P8: GENERAL LIMITATIONS OF THE GUIDE
L362@P8: In using this guide, it is very important to remember
L363@P8: its limitations.
L364@P8:  This guide is designed to help users identify
L365@P8: specimens of non-geniculate coralline algae
L366@P8: collected in central New Zealand. It is a prelude
L367@P8: to monographic studies, but should not be
L368@P8: considered a replacement or substitute.
L369@P8:  Most tetrasporangial (and bisporangial) specimens
L370@P8: of the 20 species of non-geniculate corallines
L371@P8: covered in this guide should be identifiable
L372@P8: using the techniques and keys provided. Not all
L373@P8: specimens, however, will be identifiable. Most
L374@P8: sterile specimens (i.e., without reproductive
L375@P8: structures) cannot be identified, and most male
L376@P8: or female specimens cannot be fully identified in
L377@P8: the absence of tetrasporangial (or bisporangial)
L378@P8: individuals (see Figure 7.5). This limitation also
L379@P8: applies to monographic studies.
L380@P8:  The sampling programme upon which this guide
L381@P8: is based was extensive, but only a small fraction of
L382@P8: the total central New Zealand coast was covered,
L383@P8: and most sampling sites were visited only once.
L384@P8: This means that other species occurring in the
L385@P8: region may have escaped detection.
L386@P8:  This guide is designed and based on collections
L387@P8: from central New Zealand. It has not been tested
L388@P8: on specimens collected outside this region. If
L389@P8: using the guide for specimens from other parts of
L390@P8: New Zealand, keep in mind that such specimens
L391@P8: may not be identifiable because they represent
L392@P8: species not included in the guide or not present in
L393@P8: central New Zealand. Such specimens should not
L394@P8: be discarded but rather should be deposited in a
L395@P8: registered New Zealand herbarium with suitable
L396@P8: notes (listed in item 14 on p. 28) so that they can
L397@P8: be further assessed by specialists, particularly in
L398@P8: the context of monographic studies.
L399@P8:  The characteristics of species given in the species
L400@P8: profiles (Chapter 12) are based on specimens
L401@P8: examined during the present study. New
L402@P8: specimens may show greater variation in some
L403@P8: characters (particularly measured characters such
L404@P8: as conceptacle size) than has been recorded here.
L405@P8:  Published records for the entire New Zealand
L406@P8: region for both geniculate and non-geniculate
L407@P8: corallines have been summarised by Woelkerling
L408@P8: & Nelson (2004), and are not repeated here. The
L409@P8: accuracy of previously published records requires
L410@P8: assessment in the context of monographic work,
L411@P8: and experience in southern Australia (Woelkerling
L412@P8: 1997) suggests that many of these previously
L413@P8: published records are based on misidentifications
L414@P8: or are otherwise untrustworthy.
L415@P8:  The names used for species in this guide have
L416@P8: been based (with one exception) on the study of
L417@P8: type material. Nevertheless, it is possible that
L418@P8: older names for some of the species included here
L419@P8: will be discovered during monographic studies,
L420@P8: thus necessitating adoption of a different name.
L421@P8-10: 89Chapter 2. Introduction
L422@P10: New Zealand shores are rich in coralline red
L423@P10: algae. Most plants are easily recognised as
L424@P10: corallines because they form calcified pinkish crusts
L425@P10: or stony growths on a variety of substrates, including
L426@P10: rocks, other algae, seagrasses, molluscs, and other
L427@P10: animals. Throughout New Zealand, corallines are
L428@P10: common and often conspicuous both in intertidal and
L429@P10: subtidal habitats wherever suitable substrates occur.
L430@P10: Some species can also grow unattached and can form
L431@P10: extensive localised beds, made up of thousands of
L432@P10: individuals, on the seafloor. Unattached plants are
L433@P10: often called rhodoliths.
L434@P10: Some corallines have upright branches that consist
L435@P10: of alternating calcified and uncalcified segments
L436@P10: (Figure 7.2, A & B). Such corallines are said to be
L437@P10: geniculate. Others lack branches or have branches
L438@P10: that are entirely calcified and thus lack uncalcified
L439@P10: joints or nodes (Figure 7.2, C–H). Such corallines
