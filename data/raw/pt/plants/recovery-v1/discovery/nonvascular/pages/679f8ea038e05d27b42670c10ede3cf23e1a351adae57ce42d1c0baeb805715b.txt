L38@P0: Distribution and status in the Baltic Sea region
L39@P0: Main distribution area of Lamprothamnium papulosum is the western Baltic Sea. Besides some historical
L40@P0: records in Germany, it has never been found east of the Darss Sill. Outposts can be found in Norway,
L41@P0: France, Italy, Spain, Ireland, and Great Britain.
L42@P0: All recent and former records of the species are restricted to Denmark, Germany and the west coast of
L43@P0: Sweden. In Sweden, there are some rather recent records along the west coast but mostly the newest

L69@P2: Habitat and ecology
L70@P2: L. papulosum is a characteristic macrophyte in shallow, sheltered bays, fjords and coastal lagoons. It
L71@P2: grows on sandy bottoms that can have a mixture of silt, gravel and/or pebbles but unlikely on pure silt
L72@P2: or clay. In contrast to many other charophyte species, L. papulosum is a typical brackish water species
L73@P2: with no records in freshwater environments. In Sweden it has been reported at a salinity range between
L74@P2: 9.5 and 25 psu, in Denmark between 8 and 18 psu and in Germany between 10 and 18 psu for recent
L75@P2: records (8 and 18 psu for historical records). The lower salinity boundary for distribution seems to be
L76@P2: around 8–10 psu. It typically occurs in depth ranges between 0.5 and 2 m. All recent records in Germany
L77@P2: are no deeper than 1 m. L. papulosum often grows together with other charophytes such as Chara
L78@P2: baltica and C. canescens and higher plants like Ruppia spp., Zannichellia palustris and Zostera noltii but
L79@P2: seems to avoid densely vegetated areas. It never builds up dense populations. Usually, it is found as
L80@P2: single specimens, and it may easily be overlooked. The species is annual but overwinters with globular
L81@P2: bulbils in the sediment.
L82@P2: Description of major threats
L83@P2: The observed declines are probably caused mainly by increased eutrophication. As Lamprothamnium
L84@P2: papulosum prefers vegetation stands with low coverage, it is likely to be sensitive to the increasing
L85@P2: number of ephemeral algae due to eutrophication. Also, coastal constructions and physical disturbance
L86@P2: due to increased tourism has led to intensified degradation of shallow water habitats like coastal
L87@P2: lagoons, bays and fjords. In the future climate change may alter the habitat conditions and also
L88@P2: increasing aquaculture in bays may deteriorate the habitats.
L89@P2: Assessment justification
L90@P2: Lamprothamnium papulosum has been assigned to the Endangered category in 2024 Red List II under
L91@P2: criteria B2b(i,ii,iii,iv,v) c(iv) (previously in 2013 as Endangered).
L92@P2: Species data was gathered via HELCOM Red List II project’s species data call (form 30.11.2022-2.5.2023)
L93@P2: together with the Contracting Parties national red lists with their species parameter info (e.g. generation
L94@P2: length, maturity, decline etc.).
L95@P2: Species data and all available parameter information were combined to create a Baltic Sea wide dataset.
L96@P2: The data was run through the Swedish assessment tool EDIT, that follows the IUCN guidance and
L97@P2: calculates, based on the available data, automated species threat categories.
L98@P2: To optimize the use of the EDIT tool, estimates for Area of Occupancy (AOO) and Extent of Occurrence
L99@P2: (EOO) are needed. For this purpose, HELCOM developed a regional tool which produces the calculations,
L100@P2: as well as the underlying maps based on the data available in the HELCOM Biodiversity Database.
L101@P2: The initial assessment results and deliverables by the EDIT tool were reviewed, during workshops held
L102@P2: per species group, by the Contracting Parties nationally nominated experts.
L103@P2: The geographic range of the species is considered restricted and continuingly declining, mainly due to
L104@P2: eutrophication (Andersson et al. 2004; Swedish Species Information Centre 2010). All recent and former
L105@P2: occurrences are restricted to the west coast of Sweden, Denmark and Germany. The population is
L106@P2: severely fragmented due to the occurrence in very few separate bays and lagoons without any
L107@P2: connection to each other.
L108@P2: Recommendations for actionsto conserve the species
L109@P2: Combatting local sources of nutrients (mainly agriculture) causes eutrophication. Conservation
L110@P2: measures, such as restriction of coastal construction, dredging and beach tourism in shallow coastal
L111@P2-3: lagoons, bays and fjords.SPECIES INFORMATION SHEET Lamprothamnium papulosum
