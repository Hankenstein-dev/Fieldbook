L68: 
L69: This rare and quite robust moss of raised acid mires and calcareous flushes (only in Scotland) really ought to be straightforward to identify, with its clearly wrinkled-looking and relatively straight leaves.
L70: However, the best characters for identification lie in microscopy, where it may be unequivocally separated from more common lookalikes (D. bonjeanii and D. scoparium especially). Mount some well-grown leaves on a slide and examine the cells in the upper leaf. Are they mostly quite short, chaotically arranged and without pores in their walls? This would be correct for D. undulatum, but not D. bonjeanii or D.
L71: scoparium, both of which have longer, porose upper leaf cells aligned parallel to each other and with overlapping ends.
