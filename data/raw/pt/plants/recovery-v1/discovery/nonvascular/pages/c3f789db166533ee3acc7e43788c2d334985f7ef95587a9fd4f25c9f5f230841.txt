L94@P4: Abstract
L95@P4: A total 72 freshwater chlorophycean algae belonging to 33 genera, 12 families and 7 orders have
L96@P4: been reported from Raja-Rani wetland. Desmids were found dominant by 64%. Genus Cosmarium
L97@P4: had maximum number of species (24%), followed by Staurastrum (10%), Euastrum,Scenedesmus
L98@P4: and Micrasterias (each 6%).
L99@P4: Key words: Bambusina brebissonii, Chlorophyceae, Cosmarium, Rare species, Seasons
L100@P4: Introduction
L101@P4: Green algae (Chlorophyceae) are aquatic,
L102@P4: photosynthetic organism which lack true roots,
L103@P4: stems, leaves, vascular tissue and have simple
L104@P4: reproductive structure. It is the largest and most
L105@P4: diverse group of algae representing by more than
L106@P4: 8,000 species in the world (Guiry, 2012). They are
L107@P4: cosmopolitan in distribution and able to grow in wide
L108@P4: range of habitats. They are common in moist tropical
L109@P4: regions. Their size ranges from microscopic unicell
L110@P4: to large pseudo-parenchymatous structure exceeding
L111@P4: 50 m in length.
L112@P4: Algae are being the topic of global interest
L113@P4: nowadays. As world is facing a problem of energy
L114@P4: crisis, scientists are working for the production of
L115@P4: biofuels from algae as an alternative source of
L116@P4: energy. Algae are widely using in the field of
L117@P4: agriculture, medical science, industries and as
L118@P4: laboratory tools in genomics, proteomics and other
L119@P4: researches (Graham et al., 2016). Algae are also
L120@P4: using as biomonitors, both to assess the pollution of
L121@P4: river streams and to decide the environmental
L122@P4: conditions of the past. On the other hands, some
L123@P4: produce nuisance blooms causing a great threat to
L124@P4: the aquatic ecosystem of the world.
L125@P4: Literature revealed that the freshwater green algae
L126@P4: of Nepal have been studied by various authors
L127@P4: (Sahay et al., 1992; Kargupta & Jha, 1997; Rai,
L128@P4: 2009). Among the green algae, the desmids are the
L129@P4: most studied algae contributed by Bando et al.,
L130@P4: 1989; Habib & Chaturvedi 1997; Rai, 2014. The
L131@P4: chlorococcales from various localities of Nepal have
L132@P4: been studied by Nakano & Watanabe (1988) and Rai
L133@P4: & Misra (2012). Algal flora of Raja-Rani wetland
L134@P4: has not been studied hitherto. Hence, an endeavour
L135@P4: is made to explore the green algae of this wetland as
L136@P4: a preliminary work.
L137@P4: Materials and Methods
L138@P4: Study area
L139@P4: Raja-Rani wetland (Dhimal Pokhari) (26°44.9’22'’N,
L140@P4: 87°28.9’10'’E, altitude 470 m msl) is situated in
L141@P4: Churia hill (Dhimal Danda), west from Chisang river
L142@P4: at Letang-Bhogateni municipality 4, Morang district,
L143@P4: Nepal (figure 1). The wetland lies within the RajaRani community forest (1700 ha.) and covers about
L144@P4: 133 ha. The wetland has 3 ponds viz., Raja, Rani
L145@P4: and Rajkumari which altogether covers only about
L146@P4: 20 ha. Based on the area covered, Rani Pond is large,
L147@P4: situated in the east and Raja Pond is quite small,
L148@P4: situated in the west side; both approximately 7 m
L149@P4: depth in rainy season. South to the Rani Pond, there
L150@P4: are two small Rajkumari ponds with 2 m depth in
L151@P4: rainy season.
L152@P4: This wetland is important religious and historical
L153@P4: place particularly for Dhimal community. The area
L154@P4: has dense mixed-forest dominated by Shorea robusta
L155@P4: and is rich in biodiversity (MoFSC, 2012). The
L156@P4-5: J. Pl. Res. Vol. 16, No. 1, pp 1-17, 20182
L157@P5: 2018 Journal of Plant Resources Vol.16, No. 1
L158@P5: aquatic macrophytes are: floating speciesEichhornia cracipes, Pistia stratiotes, Spirodella
L159@P5: polyrhiza etc. and submerged speciesCeratophyllum demersum, Ottelia alismoides,
L160@P5: Hydrilla verticillata etc.
L161@P5: The climate of Raja-Rani wetland is sub-tropical
L162@P5: type with hot and humid summers, intense monsoon
L163@P5: rain and cold dry winters. The average annual
L164@P5: minimum and maximum temperature ranges from
L165@P5: 12°-19°C, and 22°-30°C, respectively. The total
L166@P5: annual rainfall in this region varies from 1,138 mm
L167@P5: to 2,671 mm (FRA/DFRS, 2014).
L168@P5: Plant collection and identification
L169@P5: A total 36 algae samples were collected from 12 sites
L170@P5: (9 from Raja and 3 from Rani lake) on the periphery
L171@P5: of the lakes. Three seasonal trips were made at an
L172@P5: interval of 3 months i.e., Falgun (winter), Jestha
L173@P5: (summer) and Bhadra (rainy), 2072/073 BS.
L174@P5: Epiphytic algae were collected by squeezing
L175@P5: submerged leaves, stems and roots of macrophytes;
L176@P5: free floating planktonic microalgae were collected
L177@P5: using plankton net (mesh size 0.5 mm) and benthic
L178@P5: forms were collected by scrubbing the substratum
L179@P5: like stone, pebbles etc. Finally, the collected samples
L180@P5: were preserved in 4% formaldehyde solution in air
L181@P5: tight polythene bottles with proper tagging and
L182@P5: labeling (sample no, sampling site, date of collection
L183@P5: etc.)

L242@P6: algae belonging to 33 genera, 12 families and 7
L243@P6: orders have been enumerated from 12 sites of RajaRani wetland, Bhogateni, Morang, Nepal (table 1).
