L303: ## Abstract
L304: Molecular phylogenetic studies based on Sanger sequences have shown that Cyperaceae tribe Fuireneae s.l. is paraphyletic. However, taxonomic sampling in these studies has been poor, topologies have been inconsistent, and support for the backbone of trees has been weak. Moreover, uncertainty still surrounds the morphological limits of Schoenoplectiella, a genus of mainly small, amphicarpic annuals that was recently segregated from Schoenoplectus.
L305: Consequently, despite ample evidence from molecular analyses that Fuireneae s.l. might consist of two to four tribal lineages, no taxonomic changes have yet been made. Here, we use the Angiosperms353 enrichment panel for targeted sequencing to (i) clarify the relationships of Fuireneae s.l. with the related tribes Abildgaardieae, Eleocharideae, and Cypereae; (ii) define the limits of Fuireneae s.s., and (iii) test the monophyly of Fuireneae s.l. genera with emphasis on Schoenoplectus and Schoenoplectiella.
L306: Using more than a third of Fuireneae s.l. diversity, our phylogenomic analyses strongly support six genera and four major Fuireneae s.l. clades that we recognize as tribes: Bolboschoeneae stat.nov., Fuireneae s.s., Schoenoplecteae, and Pseudoschoeneae tr. nov. These results are consistent with morphological, micromorphological (nutlet epidermal cell shape), and embryo differences detected for each tribe.
L307: At the generic level, most sub-Saharan African perennials currently treated in Schoenoplectus are transferred to Schoenoplectiella. Our targeted sequencing results show that these species are nested in Schoenoplectiella, and their treatment here is consistent with micromorphological and embryo characters shared by all Schoenoplectiella species. Keys to recognized tribes and genera are provided.
L308: ## 1 Introduction
L309: Since the first molecular phylogenies of sedges (Cyperaceae) over 20 years ago, our knowledge of relationships within the family has improved at an ever-astonishing pace.
L310: Initially, these analyses made use of just a few plastid markers (e.g., rbcL, ndhF, trnL-F; Muasya et al., cite30†1998 ; Yen & Olmstead, cite31†2000 ) and nuclear markers (e.g., ITS; Starr et al., cite32†1999 ), but they immediately confirmed the suspicions of many authors on the basis of differences in macro- and micromorphological features (e.g., Schuyler, cite33†1971 ; Wilson, cite34†1981 ; Goetghebeur, cite35†1986 ; Bruhl, cite36†1995 ), including embryo types (Van der Veken, cite37†1965 ; Goetghebeur, cite35†1986 ), that many generic and tribal circumscriptions within the family did not reflect their evolutionary history.
L311: Unfortunately, these early molecular analyses did not have a major impact on family classification due to poor taxonomic sampling or topologies that were either insufficiently resolved or inadequately supported to make lasting taxonomic changes (e.g., Simpson et al., cite38†2003 ).
L312: However, as taxonomic sampling increased and more molecular markers were developed, statistical support for trees improved, and a series of new genera (e.g., Zameioscirpus Dhooge & Goetghebeur, Dhooge et al., cite39†2003 ; Calliscirpus C.N.Gilmour, J.R.Starr & Naczi, Gilmour et al., cite40†2013 ; Afroscirpoides García-Madrid & Muasya, García-Madrid et al., cite41†2015 ) was described.
L313: Changes to tribal (Léveillé-Bourret et al., cite42†2018 ; Léveillé-Bourret & Starr, cite43†2019 ; Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ; Larridon et al., cite45†2021a ), generic (Léveillé-Bourret et al., cite46†2020 ), and subgeneric (Villaverde et al., cite47†2020 ; Roalson et al., cite48†2021 ) classifications in the family are now taking place at a rapid pace, owing in part to advances in sequencing technology. However, one major problem has yet to be resolved: the status and classification of tribe Fuireneae Rchb. ex Fenzl.
L314: Fuireneae s.l. is a cosmopolitan tribe consisting of 153 species assigned to six genera: Bolboschoenus (Asch.) Palla, Fuirena Rottb., Actinoscirpus (Ohwi) R.W.Haines & Lye, Pseudoschoenus (C.B.Clarke) Oteng-Yeb., Schoenoplectus (Rchb.) Palla, and Schoenoplectiella Lye (Figs. cite49†1 , cite50†2 ).
L315: They are found in tropical to temperate wetlands where they purify water and provide essential food and habitat for wildlife (Fassett, cite51†1957 ; Smith & Coops, cite52†1991 ; Kim et al., cite53†2013 ; Mishra et al., cite54†2015 ; Naczi et al., cite55†2018 ), mitigate flooding, and even protect coastal shorelines from erosion (Albert et al., cite56†2013 ).
L316: They are also useful to humans as food, building materials, and medicines (Simpson & Inglis, cite57†2001 ); however, many are among the world's worst weeds, especially in rice fields (e.g., Bolboschoenus maritimus (L.) Palla, Schoenoplectiella mucronata (L.) J.Jung & H.K.Choi; Bryson & Carter, cite58†2008 ).

L328: Like many sedge genera, all six currently placed in Fuireneae are segregates of Scirpus L. s.l., a heterogeneous entity now corresponding to c. 50 natural genera and over 250 species (Koyama, cite62†1958 ; Goetghebeur, cite63†1998 ). Among the segregates of Scirpus s.l., Fuireneae s.l. species are particularly interesting, because they share a series of morphological and embryo features that seem to mark them as a natural group. All Fuireneae s.l.
L329: species have terete spikelets with spirally arranged glumes, bisexual flowers with or without perianth parts, and embryos with a distinctive mushroom-like (fungiform) shape. As embryo characters often provide key features for circumscribing sedge tribes and genera (e.g., Léveillé-Bourret & Starr, cite43†2019 ; Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ), Goetghebeur (cite63†1998 ) considered these distinctive embryos of the Bolboschoenus- or Schoenoplectus-type as evidence that Fuireneae s.l.
L330: could represent a specialized offshoot within the family. Nevertheless, from the first molecular phylogeny of Cyperaceae (Muasya et al., cite30†1998 ), it was not only clear that Scirpus s.l. consisted of numerous, often distantly related genera, as many had suggested (e.g., Van der Veken, cite37†1965 ; Schuyler, cite33†1971 ; Wilson, cite34†1981 ), but even Fuireneae s.l. could consist of multiple independent lineages.
L331: Molecular studies have since retrieved four distinct lineages within Fuireneae s.l.: (i) a clade comprising species from the subcosmopolitan genus Bolboschoenus (15 species; Govaerts et al., cite64†2020 ); (ii) a clade consisting of the largely American and African, tropical to warm temperate genus Fuirena (55 species; Govaerts et al., cite64†2020 ); (iii) a clade with the monotypic, (sub)tropical, south Asian–Australasian genus Actinoscirpus as sister to the cosmopolitan, mainly temperate genus Schoenoplectus (27 species, hereafter the Schoenoplectus Clade; Govaerts et al., cite64†2020 ), and (iv) the monotypic, South African endemic genus Pseudoschoenus as sister to the cosmopolitan, mainly tropical genus Schoenoplectiella (52 species, hereafter the Schoenoplectiella Clade; Govaerts et al., cite64†2020 ), which is a segregate of Schoenoplectus (Lye, cite65†2003 ).
L332: Among the four larger, widespread genera, Bolboschoenus and Fuirena are easily defined morphologically and they have always been monophyletic in previous molecular studies (Muasya et al., cite66†2009a ; Escudero & Hipp, cite67†2013 ; Hinchliff & Roalson, cite68†2013 ; Glon et al., cite69†2017 ; Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ). In contrast, the separation and relationships of Schoenoplectus and Schoenoplectiella have not been so clear.
L333: As Scirpus s.l. was progressively dissolved, the focus for large genera like Schoenoplectus was to provide an infrageneric classification that reflected evolutionary relationships while dividing the genus into workable groups (Oteng-Yeboah, cite70†1974a ; Raynal, 1976a, 1976cite71†b ; Smith & Hayasaka, cite72†2001 ). Authors were basically unanimous that either three or four major taxonomic divisions adopted from Scirpus s.l.
L334: could be made within Schoenoplectus, either at the subgeneric level, as subgenera Schoenoplectus, Malacogeton (Ohwi) Oteng-Yeboah, and Actaeogeton (Reichenb.) Oteng-Yeboah, or at the sectional level, as sections Schoenoplectus (=Scirpus section Pterolepis Pfeiff.), Malacogeton (Ohwi) S.G.Smith & Hayasaka, Actaeogeton (Reichenb.) J.Raynal, and Supini (Cherm.) J.Raynal (Raynal, cite73†1976a ; Smith & Hayasaka, cite72†2001 ; Smith & Hayasaka, cite74†2002 ).
L335: Moreover, it was clear that when treated as sections, taxa could be divided into two distinct groups: (i) sections Actaeogeton and Supini were typically densely tufted plants with sculptured, black nutlets and entire glume apices, whereas (ii) sections Schoenoplectus and Malacogeton were rhizomatous plants with smooth, yellow to dark brown nutlets and glumes entire to emarginate (Raynal, cite71†1976b ; Smith & Hayasaka, 2001, cite74†2002 ).
L336: Moreover, embryo features supported this division with the embryo scutellum of sections Actaeogeton and Supini being umbonate or pileate like a mushroom cap versus a turbinate to rhomboid scutellum in sections Schoenoplectus and Malacogeton (Van der Veken, cite37†1965 ). When the molecular analysis of Muasya et al. (cite30†1998 ) placed two small, tropical annuals, S. articulata (L.) Palla and S. junceus (Willd.) J.Raynal, in a clade separate from the type for Schoenoplectus, S.
L337: lacustris (L.) Palla, Lye (cite65†2003 ) decided to segregate 26 species into Schoenoplectiella. This new genus was roughly meant to elevate Schoenoplectus section Supini to generic status, a section defined by Raynal (cite73†1976a ) to contain the small, tufted annuals of Schoenoplectus whose bristles were reduced or absent.
L338: In addition, the species of section Supini also stood out, because beyond sharing other features such as rugulose to highly sculpted nutlets, most displayed amphicarpy, a rare condition in Cyperaceae where pistillate flowers at culm bases develop larger nutlets than the nutlets produced in aerial inflorescences (Raynal, cite73†1976a ; Bruhl, cite75†1994 ).
L339: Nonetheless, in a molecular analysis of Korean Scirpus s.l., Jung & Choi (cite76†2010 ) expanded the genus to include some species from Scirpus section Actaeogeton, which does not display amphicarpy, after results suggested they formed a clade with Schoenoplectiella species separate from Schoenoplectus s.s. (i.e., sections Schoenoplectus and Malacogeton).
L340: Hayasaka (cite77†2012 ) subsequently redefined Schoenoplectiella to include all species from sections Actaeogeton and Supini, which resulted in a genus composed of small, amphicarpic annuals and large, single-fruit-type perennials. Unfortunately, this merger meant that all the characters used to separate Schoenoplectiella from Schoenoplectus were overlapping.
L341: Moreover, discontinuous characters could not be found for the separation of Schoenoplectiella sections Actaeogeton and Schoenoplectiella (=Supini), even though they were still assumed to be natural groups.
L342: Although our understanding of Fuireneae s.l. has progressed enormously over the past 50 years, and a pattern pointing toward a grade of multiple major clades has emerged, the most recent molecular analyses are equivocal about the way to proceed from a taxonomic point of view. Recent molecular studies of Fuireneae s.l.
L343: have retrieved the tribe as a grade of three (Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ) or four clades (Muasya et al., cite66†2009a ; Escudero & Hipp, cite67†2013 ; Hinchliff & Roalson, cite68†2013 ; Glon et al., cite69†2017 ) branching off after an Abildgaardieae–Eleocharideae Clade and paraphyletic with respect to tribe Cypereae.
L344: Depending on the markers used, analyses place either Bolboschoenus (Glon et al., cite69†2017 ; Spalink et al., cite78†2018 ) or Fuirena (Glon et al., cite69†2017 ) as branching first, and the Schoenoplectiella Clade (Escudero & Hipp, cite67†2013 ; Shiels et al., cite79†2014 ) or the Schoenoplectus Clade (Glon et al., cite69†2017 ; Spalink et al., cite78†2018 ) as sister to Cypereae or sister to each other (Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ).
L345: Early molecular analyses have also suggested that Schoenoplectiella (Muasya et al., cite30†1998 ; Simpson et al., cite80†2007 ) or Fuirena (Muasya et al., cite66†2009a ) could be sister to tribe Eleocharideae Goetgh. or that Bolboschoenus could be sister to tribe Abildgaardieae Lye (Muasya et al., cite66†2009a ).
L346: These relationships were not entirely implausible, given the early cladistic analyses of sedge morphology by Goetghebeur (cite35†1986 ) and Bruhl (cite36†1995 ), but support for major clade relationships in molecular analyses was poor and taxonomic sampling was limited.
L347: No previous molecular phylogenetic study that has generated novel data has included more than 25 species of Fuireneae s.l., and no study has included more than five plastid markers (matK, ndhF, rbcL, rps16, trnL-F) and two nuclear markers (ETS-1f, ITS; Semmouri et al., cite44†2019 ), with the exception of the supermatrix analysis of Hinchliff and Roalson (23 loci; Hinchliff & Roalson, cite68†2013 ), where the relationships of the major Fuireneae s.l. clades were completely unresolved.
L348: Moreover, the limits of Schoenoplectus and Schoenoplectiella remain unresolved, because molecular studies have often been regional in scope (e.g., Japan, Yano & Hoshino, cite81†2005a ; Korea, Jung & Choi, cite76†2010 ), and even when geographic sampling was wider (e.g. Shiels et al., cite79†2014 ), a lack of critical species of African Schoenoplectus (e.g., Schoenoplectus rhodesicus (Podlech) Lye, S.
L349: paludicola (Kunth) Palla ex J.Raynal; Smith & Hayasaka, cite72†2001 ; Browning, cite82†2012 ) or low branch support (Muasya et al., cite66†2009a ) has meant that a satisfactory separation for these two genera has yet to be achieved.
