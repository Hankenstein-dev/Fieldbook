L262@P10: Introduction
L263@P10: Species from the genus Sphagnum have the reputation of being difficult to identify by naturalists
L264@P10: or botanists and even by experienced bryologists. However, their importance in the northern hemisphere, as well as their abundance in peatlands and swamps, makes their identification essential
L265@P10: for botanical, ecological or environmental studies. This visual identification guide is not a flora. This
L266@P10: means that you will not find a complete botanical description of each species. However, you can refer
L267@P10: to the chapter on the Sphagnaceae of the “Flore des bryophytes du Québec-Labrador” for complete
L268@P10: botanical descriptions, and to view the magnificent plates that illustrate each species (Faubert, Ayotte,
L269@P10: Gauthier and Rochefort 2013), or refer to the description of gametophyte traits by Gauthier (2001a).
L270@P10: We hope that this visual key supplemented with tips for recognizing the species in the field and field
L271@P10: notes will make identification easier. The beginner is invited to read Appendices 1, page 227, 2,
L272@P10: page 237, and 3, page 245, before starting to consult the identification keys.
L273@P10: The guide is structured as follows:
L274@P10: An alphabetical list of the Sphagnum mosses
L275@P10: The first page gives an alphabetical list of the 60 species present in Quebec, Labrador and the Maritime
L276@P10: provinces (excluding the island of Newfoundland) to help rapidly find a given taxon within the guide.
L277@P10: Illustrated field key and visual identification key for the subgenera
L278@P10: A dichotomous key of visual traits easily observed in the field is presented to facilitate the often arduous
L279@P10: task of assigning a Sphagnum moss to a subgenus. This key is followed by another complementary
L280@P10: visual key which combines macroscopic and microscopic criteria. Correctly placing a specimen in a
L281@P10: subgenus is probably the most difficult part in successful Sphagnum identification. However, with a
L282@P10: bit of practice, and as the species become more familiar, it becomes possible to assign a species to
L283@P10: a subgenus without having to refer to the subgenera key. Identifying the species is easier once the
L284@P10: subgenus has been determined.
L285@P10: This guide innovates by presenting illustrated couplets in the dichotomous keys. Sphagnum identification uses a botanical jargon that is difficult to remember when used sporadically. This illustrated guide
L286@P10: makes it easier to recall Sphagnum terminology. It is the result of more than ten years of development
L287@P10: and improvement. During this period, the dichotomous keys were tested by students in the “Ecology
L288@P10: of Boreal Peatlands” and “Taxonomy and Peatland Sampling Methods” classes of Université Laval and
L289@P10: by other professional users. These tests have confirmed the logical basis of the couplets.
L290@P10: Many species have variants. For this reason, some species appear more than once in the keys. The
L291@P10: term “in part” following the species name indicates that it appears elsewhere in the key. This method
L292@P10: is used throughout and permits, for example, to present a taxon with eroded stem leaf apices, and
L293@P10-11: elsewhere, the same taxon with entire leaf apices.Sphagnum Mosses of Eastern Canada

L295@P11: Subgenera: main traits, dichotomous key and species description
L296@P11: This guide is then divided by taxonomic subgenus for which the microscopic main traits are presented
L297@P11: and sometimes macroscopic traits also. These traits are followed by a visual key to the species of
L298@P11: the subgenus and the description of each species in alphabetical order.
L299@P11: In the species description, the reader will find the macroscopic and microscopic traits used to
L300@P11: identify the species. Only the most distinctive traits, intrinsic to the species, as perceived by the
L301@P11: authors are presented. For a complete species description, refer to the Flora of North America North
L302@P11: of Mexico, Volume 27 (Flora of North America Editorial Committee 2007).
L303@P11: Distribution map, description of biotopes and habitats
L304@P11: A map of the geographical distribution of the species is found next. The distribution maps cover
L305@P11: Quebec, Labrador and the Maritime provinces, but exclude the main island of Newfoundland. They
L306@P11: were created from the geographical coordinates of more than 20,000 herbarium specimens. Each map
L307@P11: shows the location (using a dot), where the presence of a given taxon was confirmed by a duly identified and validated herbarium specimen. The distribution maps are underlain by the main vegetation
L308@P11: zones as described by Payette (1983) and Payette and Filion (1993). The taxa are thus presented in
L309@P11: their ecological and bioclimatic context.
