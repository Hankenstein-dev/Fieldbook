L142: Veronica strictissima, commonly known as the Banks Peninsula hebe, is a cite49†flowering plant in the family cite53†Plantaginaceae . It is endemic to cite60†Banks Peninsula in cite61†Canterbury , New Zealand.
L143: ## Description
L144: 
L145: [cite62†edit ]
L146: 
L147: Veronica strictissima grows to a rounded shrub to 2 m (6 ft 7 in) tall.[ 3 ][ 4 ] Its Latin name refers to its erect stems.[ 5 ]
L148: 
L149: Leaves are opposite and narrowly oblong, 9–49 mm (0.35–1.93 in) long, 3–8 mm (0.12–0.31 in) wide.[ 4 ] They are light green in colour with slightly paler undersides.[ 4 ]
L150: 
L151: Inflorescences are racemes up to 17–107 mm (0.67–4.21 in) long.[ 4 ] Flowers are cite63†hermaphrodite or female on separate plants.[ 4 ]
L152: Capsules contain multiple tiny seeds.[ 6 ] Seeds are flat and brown, 1.0–1.6 mm (0.04–0.06 in) long.[ 7 ]
L153: ### Natural global range
L154: 
L155: [cite64†edit ]
L156: 
L157: Veronica strictissima is endemic to Banks Peninsula, cite65†Canterbury, New Zealand .[ 6 ][ 8 ] Multiple separate populations have been identified in the region.[ 7 ][ 9 ][ 10 ][ 11 ]
L158: ### Habitat preferences
L159: 
L160: [cite66†edit ]
L161: 
L162: Veronica strictissima is an cite67†early coloniser of disturbed habitats, occurring in sunny, open areas, such as rocky outcrops, cliffs,[ 6 ] scrubland,[ 7 ] and roadside banks from sea level up to 853 m (2,800 ft).[ 6 ]
L163: Banks Peninsula differs in soil composition compared to other areas of Canterbury, it is made up of cite68†loess and cite69†basalt rock, while lower Canterbury is predominantly cite70†alluvial .[ 12 ] V. strictissima has been found to prefer moderate levels of moisture with low salinity and good soil drainage.[ 13 ]
L164: ## Phenology
L165: 
L166: [cite71†edit ]
L167: 
L168: Veronica strictissima flowers in summer, sometimes till early autumn.[ 4 ][ 7 ] Individual plants will flower for 2–5 weeks.[ 9 ] The flowers on the spike of one plant will normally flower simultaneously.[ 9 ] Flowers are pollinated by a single species of bee native to New Zealand, cite72†Lasioglossum sordidum .[ 9 ] Seed capsules form during autumn[ 7 ] before dehiscing in late autumn.[ 9 ] Seeds are wind dispersed,[ 14 ][ 15 ] but can also be dispersed by birds and lizards.[ 13 ]
L169: ## Ecology
L170: 
L171: [cite73†edit ]
L172: 
L173: Veronica strictissima is grazed upon by sheep, goats and cows.[ 6 ][ 9 ] The nectar and fruit are eaten by lizards and birds.[ 13 ] V. strictissima and other species from the genus Veronica are fed on by cite74†Platyptilia , cite75†Xyridacma veronicae .[ 16 ]
L174: ## Cultural uses
L175: 
L176: [cite76†edit ]
L177: 
L178: Veronica strictissima was used as a cure for cite77†dysentery during cite78†World War II .[ 16 ] cite79†Māori used the plant as a traditional medicine for the treatment of ulcers, headaches, kidney and bladder troubles.[ 16 ]
L179: ## Other information
L180: 
L181: [cite80†edit ]
L182: 
L183: Veronica strictissima can be confused with other Veronica species, and was once included with cite81†Veronica leiophylla [ 5 ] and cite82†Veronica parviflora .[ 10 ] It is very similar to cite83†Veronica traversii , which is far more widespread throughout New Zealand, being found in the cite84†Marlborough and cite85†Nelson districts as well as Canterbury.[ 6 ]
L184: 
L185: Veronica strictissima may hybridise with cite86†Veronica salicifolia .[ 4 ]
