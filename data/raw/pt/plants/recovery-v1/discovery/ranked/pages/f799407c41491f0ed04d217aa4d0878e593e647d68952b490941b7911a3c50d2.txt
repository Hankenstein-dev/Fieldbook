L150: The Echinocereeae are a tribe of cite57†cacti in the subfamily cite58†Cactoideae . Since 2006, the tribe has included the former tribe Pachycereeae in many treatments of cactus classification. The exact cite61†circumscription of the tribe has been subject to considerable change, particularly since cite62†molecular phylogenetic approaches have been used in determining classifications, and remains uncertain.
L151: The tribe includes large treelike species, such as the cite48†saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea), as well as shorter shrubby species. Most members of the tribe are found in desert regions, particularly in cite63†Mexico and the southwestern United States.
L152: ## Description
L153: 
L154: [cite64†edit ]
L155: 
L156: The tribe includes large treelike species, as well as shorter shrubby species.[ 1 ] Some species can grow to be over 15 m (50 ft) tall, like the cite48†saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea)[ 2 ] and cite65†Cephalocereus macrocephalus (syn. Neobuxbaumia macrocephala).[ 3 ] Their stems are ribbed and columnar, not divided into segments. Most have flowers that open at night.[ 1 ]
L157: ## Taxonomy
L158: 
L159: [cite66†edit ]
L160: In 1958, cite67†Franz Buxbaum published a division into tribes of what is now the subfamily Cactoideae, then Cereoideae. Two of these tribes were Pachycereeae (which he spelt "Pachycereae") and Echinocereeae (which he spelt "Echinocereae").
L161: Pachycereeae was a newly published taxon; Echinocereeae was an elevation of the subtribe Echinocereinae ("Echinocereanae") established by Britton and Rose in 1922.[ 4 ][ 5 ] Studies based on cite68†chloroplast cite69†DNA showed that Echinocereus, the type genus of the Echinocereeae, was nested within the Pachycereeae, as then cite61†circumscribed , and Pachycereeae was expanded to include Echinocereeae.[ 6 ][ 1 ] In 2006, cite70†David Hunt and the cite71†International Cactaceae Systematics Group used the name Echinocereeae rather than Pachycereeae for the tribe.[ 7 ] This has been followed in much subsequent work, including a major 2011 cite62†molecular phylogenetic analysis of the family Cactaceae.[ 8 ] However, some authors continue to use the name Pachycereeae for the tribe.[ 9 ]

L179: "Core Echinocereeae" recovered in molecular phylogenetic analyses in 2005 and 2011 contains the following genera, often only in part compared to earlier generic circumscriptions.[ 8 ][ 13 ] Some genera were further placed in two subtribes, Pachycereinae and Echinocereinae.[ 14 ] The position of some genera remained unclear, as of October 2023cite38†[update] . Brachycereus was not included in many studies, but has been placed with Armatocereus and Jasminocereus, both included in Echinocereeae.[ 15 ][ 16 ]

L224: ## Distribution and habitat
L225: 
L226: [cite110†edit ]
L227: 
L228: Members of the Echinocereeae are native from the southwestern United States, through cite63†Mexico , cite111†Central America and the cite112†Caribbean to northern South America.[ 1 ] Many are classic columnar desert cacti of Mexico and the southwestern US, like the saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea).[ 2 ] A few have a wider distribution: cite113†Stenocereus griseus is found from northeastern Mexico to cite114†Colombia .[ 19 ]
