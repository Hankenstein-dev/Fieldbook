L261: Dendrobium is a cite112†genus of mostly cite113†epiphytic and cite114†lithophytic cite99†orchids in the cite115†family Orchidaceae. It is a very large genus, containing more than 1,800 species that are found in diverse habitats throughout much of cite116†south , cite117†east and cite118†southeast Asia , including cite119†China , cite120†Japan , cite121†India , the cite122†Philippines , cite123†Indonesia , cite124†Australia , cite125†New Guinea , cite126†Vietnam and many of the islands of the cite127†Pacific .
L262: Orchids in this genus have roots that creep over the surface of trees or rocks, rarely having their roots in soil. Up to six leaves develop in a tuft at the tip of a shoot and from one to a large number of flowers are arranged along an unbranched flowering stem. Several attempts have been made to separate Dendrobium into smaller genera, but most have not been accepted by the cite128†World Checklist of Selected Plant Families .
L263: ## Description
L264: 
L265: [cite129†edit ]
L266: Dendrobium species are mostly cite113†epiphytic , or cite114†lithophytic although a few species are cite130†terrestrial . They are cite131†sympodial cite132†herbs with cylindrical roots usually arising from the base of a cite133†pseudobulb . The pseudobulbs, when present, are hard, sometimes cane-like, cylindrical or cone-shaped and more or less covered with the bases of the leaves.
L267: There are from one to many leaves arranged in two ranks, the leaves varying in shape from linear to oblong, sometimes cylindrical but never channelled or grooved. They are usually much longer than wide and last for only a single season.^{cite134†[3] }^{cite135†[4] }^{cite136†[5] }
L268: Between one and a large number of cite137†resupinate or non-resupinate flowers are arranged along an unbranched flowering stem and may be short or long-lived. The flowers may be white, green, yellow, or pink to purple, often with contrasting colours in the cite138†labellum . The cite139†sepals and cite140†petals are usually free from and more or less similar to each other but markedly different from the labellum.
L269: The labellum is more or less egg-shaped, with the narrower end towards the base and flanks the cite141†column . There is often a cite138†callus consisting of narrow, parallel ridges, in the centre of the labellum.^{cite134†[3] }^{cite135†[4] }^{cite136†[5] }
L270: ## Taxonomy and naming
L271: 
L272: [cite142†edit ]
L273: 
L274: The genus Dendrobium was first formally described in 1799 by cite143†Olof Swartz and the description was published in Nova Acta Regiae Societatis Scientiarum Upsaliensis.^{cite104†[1] }^{cite144†[6] } The name Dendrobium is derived from the cite145†ancient Greek words dendron meaning "tree" and bios meaning "life", referring to the epiphytic habit of most species.^{cite144†[6] }^{cite146†[7] }
L275: In 1981, cite147†Friedrich Brieger reclassified all cite148†terete -leaved dendrobiums from cite124†Australia and cite125†New Guinea into a new genus Dockrillia, and in 2002 cite149†David Jones and cite150†Mark Clements separated the genus into smaller genera, including Thelychiton, Tropilis, Vappodes and Winika, but all of these genera are regarded as cite110†synonyms by the World Checklist of Selected Plant Families.^{cite104†[1] }
