L134: cite62†Rose L135: 
L136: Sedum pachyphyllum is a species of cite56†flowering plant in the stonecrop family, cite59†Crassulaceae .^{cite63†[1] }
L137: ## Description
L138: 
L139: [cite64†edit ]
L140: 
L141: Sedum pachyphyllum is a cite65†succulent cite66†perennial growing 10–30 cm (4–12 in) tall from a woody base. Its stems are branched and can either stand erect or spread across the ground. The cite67†glaucous leaves are 3–4 cm (1.2–1.6 in) long with circular cross sections 8–10 mm (0.3–0.4 in) wide and grow perpendicularly from the stems.^{cite68†[2] } In dry conditions, the tips of the leaves become reddish.^{cite69†[3] }
L142: The flowering stem is 2 cm (0.8 in) long, spreading laterally, with flowers attached by short cite70†pedicels . The flowers have 5 green cite71†sepals , about 6 mm (0.24 in) long and widely spreading; cite72†petals are yellow and 7 mm (0.28 in) long. The cite73†type specimen was observed blooming in January.^{cite68†[2] }
L143: ## Distribution
L144: 
L145: [cite74†edit ]
L146: Sedum pachyphyllum is cite75†endemic to central cite76†Mexico , where it grows in the cite77†Sierra Mixteca .^{cite63†[1] }^{cite78†[4] } Because of its use as an ornamental plant, S. pachyphyllum has become established outside its native range, especially in Spain, where cite79†naturalized populations have been documented in the provinces of cite80†Valencia and cite81†Castellón .^{cite82†[5] } In Italy, a naturalized population of plants growing near cite83†Riva del Garda were identified as hybrids between S.
L147: pachyphyllum and Echeveria agavoides.^{cite84†[6] }
L148: ## Taxonomy
L149: 
L150: [cite85†edit ]
L151: The cite73†type specimen of Sedum pachyphyllum was collected by Carl Albert Purpus in 1907 near cite86†San Luis Amatlán in cite87†Oaxaca .^{cite68†[2] } cite62†Joseph Nelson Rose formally described the species in 1911. Its species epithet, pachyphyllum, comes from the Greek words pachys (meaning thick) and phyton (meaning plant), referring to the plant's thick, fleshy leaves.^{cite88†[7] } S.
L152: pachyphyllum belongs to a cite89†clade of about 90 closely-related species within the Sedum genus called section Pachysedum, one of 22 sections named by cite90†Alwin Berger in 1930.^{cite78†[4] }^{cite91†[8] }
L153: Two cite92†hybrids with S. pachyphyllum parentage have been formally described. The artificial hybrid cite93†Sedum × rubrotinctum (S. pachyphyllum × S. stahlii), sometimes called the jelly bean plant, is a popular cite94†ornamental plant .^{cite95†[9] } The intergeneric hybrid × Sedeveria mauroi (Echeveria agavoides × S. pachyphyllum) is an cite96†introduced species in Italy.^{cite97†[10] }
L154: ## Uses
L155: 
L156: [cite98†edit ]
L157: 
L158: Sedum pachyphyllum is cultivated, including for use in gardens and on cite99†green roofs , in part because of its cite100†drought tolerance .^{cite69†[3] }^{cite101†[11] }
