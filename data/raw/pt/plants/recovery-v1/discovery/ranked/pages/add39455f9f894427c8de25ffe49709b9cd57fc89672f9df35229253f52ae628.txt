L137: cite73†Pinus , the pines, is a genus of approximately 110–120 cite74†extant tree and shrub species. The genus is currently split into two cite75†subgenera , subgenus Pinus (hard pines), and subgenus Strobus (soft pines).
L138: Each of the subgenera have been further divided into cite76†sections and subsections based in the past on morphology, ecology and biogeography, and more recently increasingly from cite77†chloroplast DNA sequencing^{cite78†[1] } and whole cite79†plastid genomic analysis.^{cite80†[2] } While the genetic analysis has given robust results at the higher levels, they often give conflicting results lower in the phylogenetic trees, with species allocated to different subsections (and sometimes different sections) by different studies or even within a study.^{cite81†[3] } Within subsections, the genetic relationships between species can be even more complex and conflicting; in one study, three samples of the very distinctive and morphologically constant cite82†Pinus lambertiana were placed in three different cite83†clades of the subsection Strobus, and similar problems with many other species with widespread nonmonophyly.^{cite84†[4] }

L157: Several features are used to distinguish the subgenera, sections, and subsections of pines; the number of leaves (needles) per cite85†fascicle , whether the cite86†fascicle sheaths are cite87†deciduous or cite88†persistent , the number of cite89†fibrovascular bundles per needle (two in Pinus, one in Strobus), the position of the cite90†resin ducts in the needles (internal or external), the presence or shape of the seed wings (rudimentary or effective, articulate or adnate), and the position of the cite91†umbo (dorsal or terminal) and presence of a prickle on the scales of the cite92†seed cones .^{cite93†[5] }
L158: The two subgenera are thought to have ancient divergence from each other, having diverged at some point between the late cite94†Jurassic (around 160 million years ago),^{cite95†[6] } the mid cite96†Cretaceous (around 125 million years ago),^{cite97†[7] } to the late Cretaceous (around 100 million years ago).^{cite98†[8] }
L159: ## Subgenus Pinus
L160: 
L161: [cite99†edit ]
L162: Subgenus cite100†Pinus includes the yellow and hard pines. Pines in this subgenus have two to five needles per fascicle (rarely as many as eight, in cite101†P. durangensis ).^{cite102†[9] } They have two cite89†fibrovascular bundles per needle, and the fascicle sheaths are cite88†persistent , except in cite103†P. leiophylla and cite104†P. lumholtzii .
L163: The cone scales are thicker and more rigid than those of subgenus Strobus, and have a resin sealing band before opening (resulting in the cones opening with an audible crack); the cite105†cones either open soon after they mature or are cite106†serotinous .^{cite107†[10] }
L164: ### Section Pinus
L165: 
L166: [cite108†edit ]
L167: 
L168: Section cite109†Pinus has two or three needles per fascicle. The cones have moderately thin to thick scales; in most they open at maturity, but are weakly serotinous in some species in subsection Pinaster. Species in this section are native to cite110†Europe , cite111†Asia , and the cite112†Mediterranean , except for P. resinosa in northeastern cite113†North America and P. tropicalis in western cite114†Cuba .^{cite107†[10] }

L228: ### Section Trifoliae
L229: 
L230: [cite161†edit ]
L231: 
L232: Section Trifoliae (American hard pines), despite its name (which means "three-leaved"), has two to five needles per fascicle, or rarely eight. The cones of most species open at maturity, but a few are cite106†serotinous . All but two American hard pines belong to this section.^{cite107†[10] }
L233: The timing of divergences within this section is disputed, with subsections Australes and Ponderosae having diverged during the mid cite96†Cretaceous (about 110 million years ago) according to one study,^{cite95†[6] } but not until the mid cite162†Oligocene (about 30–35 million years ago) in others.^{cite97†[7] }^{cite98†[8] }

L333: 
L334: [cite246†edit ]

L336: Subgenus Strobus includes the white and soft pines. L338: 
L339: [cite249†edit ]
L340: 
L341: Pinus nelsonii
L342: This unique pine is remarkable in differing from all other pines in multiple cone and foliage characters.
L343: Genetically, its position has varied between and within studies; three nuclear genes and chloroplast DNA placed it as cite250†sister taxon to the rest of Section Parrya, while a late embryogenesis abundant (LEA)-like gene (IFG8612) resolved it as sister to Section Quinquefoliae;^{cite84†[4] } another study resolved it as sister to Subsection Balfourianae.^{cite98†[8] } It has probably evolved separately from the rest of the genus since the late cite117†Eocene , with its cite251†most recent common ancestor around 37 million years ago.^{cite98†[8] } These unique characters warrant recognition as a monotypic section.^{cite144†[15] }

L351: ### Section Parrya
L352: 
L353: [cite253†edit ]
L354: Section Parrya has one to five needles per fascicle. The seeds have cite254†articulate (jointed) wings; in most, the wing is vestigial, and remains attached to the cone scale when the seed is released apparently wingless. This section is native to the southwestern United States and Mexico.^{cite107†[10] }
