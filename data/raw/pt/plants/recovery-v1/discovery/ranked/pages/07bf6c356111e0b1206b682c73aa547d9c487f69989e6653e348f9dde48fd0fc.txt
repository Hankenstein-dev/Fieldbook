L37@P5: ABSTRACT
L38@P5: Cymbidium is the epiphytic, terrestrial, lithophytic orchid with a distinguishing
L39@P5: character of linear to elliptic-lanceolate leaves, 2 or 4 pollinia in 2 unequal pairs, and
L40@P5: the genus mostly occur on the tree trunks covered by mosses. This study is based on all
L41@P5: the species of the taxa that are naturally distributed in Nepal.
L42@P5: This taxonomic revisionary study is based on the herbarium specimen and fresh plant
L43@P5: materials. The herbarium specimens are thoroughly studied from KATH and TUCH
L44@P5: and the examination of available images of herbarium specimens collected from Nepal
L45@P5: are also done in international herbaria like BM, CAL, E, K and TI. This study confirms
L46@P5: the presence of 10 species of Cymbidium in Nepal namely; C. aloifolium, C.
L47@P5: crassifolium, C. cyperifolium, C. devonianum, C. elegans, C. erythraeum, C. ×
L48@P5: gammieanum, C. hookerianum, C. iridioides and C. lancifolium.
L49@P5: The synonyms, type specimens, description, ecology, altitudinal range, flowering
L50@P5: season, specimen examined and distribution map for all the species present in Nepal is
L51@P5: prepared. Also, the illustration and bracketed taxonomic key is made to identify the
L52@P5: species. The character of lip is more important in taxonomic view point for delimiting
L53@P5: the species of this genus.
L54@P5: Micromorphological character like the stomata complex in 10 species of Cymbidium
L55@P5: were studied for understanding the taxonomic significance of stomatal complex within
L56@P5: the genus. Stomatal index and stomatal frequency were calculated using formula given
L57@P5: by Salisbury. Paracytic type of stomata were found in the species that are either
L58@P5: arranged in linear form or scattered. The elliptical shape of stomata was found in more
L59@P5: species than the circular shape.
L60@P5: Furthermore, the cladistic analysis is also interpreted to find out the phylogenetic
L61@P5: relationship among the species. The cladogram showed that C. aloifolium and C.
L62@P5: crassifolium are closely related.
L63@P5: The genus Cymbidium is found to be distributed in all 3 regions of Nepal with the
L64@P5: altitudinal range from 800 – 2800 m in 23 districts of Nepal. The flowering period of
L65@P5: the genus Cymbidium is usually throughout the year as several species bloom in
L66@P5-6: different season.vi

L228@P13: 1. INTRODUCTION
L229@P13: 1.1. Background
L230@P13: Orchidaceae is one of the widespread and diverse families of flowering plants
L231@P13: distributed in tropical and subtropical regions of the world. This family includes 736
L232@P13: genera and 27000 species worldwide (Chase et al., 2015) whereas according to De
L233@P13: (2021), 29,199 species of this family have been identified and accepted. It consists of
L234@P13: beautiful long-lived flowers having ornamental, medicinal, and nutritional values and
L235@P13: are found in a wide variety of ecological conditions, except in marine conditions
L236@P13: (Acharya and Rokaya, 2010; Hinsley et al., 2017). Orchids are unique flowers with a
L237@P13: pleasant aroma and beautiful comical mimics in nature (Raskoti, 2009).
L238@P13: Orchidaceae is the second largest family of flowering plants with 28,237 species after
L239@P13: Asteraceae with 32,581 species in the world. However, it is the largest family in Nepal
L240@P13: with 440 species belonging to 95 genera (Shrestha, 2022) comprising 7.1% of total
L241@P13: flowering plants of Nepal (Rokaya et al., 2013), Recently, Rajbhandari (2023)
L242@P13: mentioned that Orchidaceae is third largest families having 96 genera and 381 species
L243@P13: in Nepal including 18 species known to be endemic (Rajbhandari, 2014) and distributed
L244@P13: between 60 and 5200m above sea level in the range of habitats from low tropical land
L245@P13: to high Himalayan of Nepal (Rokaya et al., 2013; Rajbhandari and Rai, 2017; Shrestha
L246@P13: et al., 2018; Bhandari et al., 2020).
L247@P13: According to Royal Botanic Garden, Kew (2009), it is estimated that more than 850
L248@P13: species of orchids are found in the Himalayan region. 239 species of orchids are
L249@P13: reported from Western Himalaya (Singh Jalal and Jayanthi, 2015). 372 species are
L250@P13: reported from Central Himalaya and 490 species of orchids are reported from Eastern
L251@P13: Himalaya (Acharya et al., 2011).
L252@P13: Orchids are mostly epiphytic, terrestrial and rarely saprophytic. Epiphytic orchids are
L253@P13: usually beautiful and attractive than the terrestrial orchids and they are also excellent
L254@P13: biological indicators of ecological changes in the ecosystem. The diagnostic characters
L255@P13: of family Orchidaceae have been enlisted in different points by many scientists till date.
L256@P13: According to Jezek (2006), there are four basic features of orchids that admittedly can
L257@P13: be found individually with other plants, but which apply all at once in the case of
L258@P13-14: orchids; flowers are bilaterally symmetrical, pollen grains are grouped into sticky 2
L259@P14: masses termed ‘pollinia’, the seeds are very minute and contain only undeveloped
L260@P14: embryos with no nutritive material and in natural condition, seed can germinate only
L261@P14: when symbiotic fungi are present.
L262@P14: They are further characterized by having modified petals called lip or labellum
L263@P14: intensely colored with scent attracting the pollinating agent and has a column, the fusion
L264@P14: of stamen filaments with the style, presence of rhizomes or tuberous root, stem forming
L265@P14: pseudobulbs bearing aerial assimilating roots, pollination mechanism, association with
L266@P14: fungal partners and miniscule seeds (Kumar et al., 2007).
L267@P14: 1.2 Genus Cymbidium Sw.
L268@P14: The genus Cymbidium was first described in 1799 by Olof Swartz derived from the
L269@P14: Latin word “cymba” which means cup, bowl, or boat with the diminutive suffix –idium,
L270@P14: i.e. boat, apparently based on the shape of the labellum in some species. Cymbidium
L271@P14: Sw. belongs to the sub-family Epidendroideae, Cymbidieae, and Cymbidinae (Chase et
L272@P14: al., 2015). It is among the most popular winter and spring flowering orchids, widely
L273@P14: distributed in tropical and subtropical Asia (De, 2022).
L274@P14: Cymbidium, commonly known as boat orchids, is a genus of evergreen flowering plants
L275@P14: characterized by epiphytic, lithophytic, terrestrial, or rarely leafless saprophytic herbs.
L276@P14: It is characterized by short and stout pseudobulbs, long, narrow, coriaceous leaves,
L277@P14: attractive flowers often large in erect, arching or drooping raceme, equal sepals and
L278@P14: petals, lip 3-lobed, embracing the column disc with pubescent ridges, column long, and
L279@P14: pollinia 2, deeply grooved or 4 (Bose and Bhattacharjee, 1999).
L280@P14: Total of 71 species of Cymbidium are present worldwide (Chase et al., 2015). The
L281@P14: online database; The World Flora Online (WFO) includes 513 species of Cymbidium
L282@P14: with 229 accepted name of Cymbidium (www.worldfloraonline.org; retrieved on 22
L283@P14: May, 2023). Nepal encompasses about 16 species of Cymbidium (Rokaya et al., 2013;
L284@P14: Shrestha et al., 2018). Rajbhandari and Rai (2017) enlisted 10 species of Cymbidium in
L285@P14: Nepal.
L286@P14: 1.3 Systematic position of the genus Cymbidium Sw.
L287@P14: Lindley (1833), the first taxonomist to produce a working classification of the
L288@P14: Orchidaceae placed Cymbidium in the tribe Vandeae, followed by Bentham (1881),
L289@P14-15: Hooker (1890) and King & Pantling (1898). Schlechter (1924) incorporated many of 3
L290@P15: Lindley’s and later botanist ideas, thus providing a base for the modern classification.
L291@P15: He placed Cymbidium in tribe Kerosphaerae of sub family Monandreae. Duthie (1970)
L292@P15: again placed back the Cymbidium in tribe Vandeae.
L293@P15: Dressler (1981) placed Cymbidium into the sub-family Vandioideae, the tribe
L294@P15: Cymbidieae containing all of the sympodial vandioid orchids. Deva & Naithani (1986)
L295@P15: placed Cymbidium in subtribe Cymbidiinae of tribe Vandeae within the sub-family
L296@P15: Vandioideae. Balogh and Funk (1986) placed Cymbidium in subfamily Epidendroideae
L297@P15: of tribe Vandeae. Dressler (1993) revised his work and gave the phylogenetic
L298@P15: classification of orchids and placed Cymbidium in a broadly defined subtribe
L299@P15: Cyrtopodiinae of tribe Cymbidieae in the subfamily Epidendroideae. Szlachetko (1995)
L300@P15: placed Cymbidium in subfamily Vandoideae of tribe Cymbidieae and subtribe
L301@P15: Cymbidiinae, Pearce and Cribb (2002) placed Cymbidium to sub family
L302@P15: Epidendroideae, tribe Cymbidieae and subtribe Cyrtopodiinae followed by and Chen et
L303@P15: al. (2009) and Yonzone (2015).
L304@P15: Chase et al. (2015) updated the classification of Orchidaceae and placed Cymbidium in
L305@P15: subtribe Cymbidiinae, tribe Cymbidieae within the sub-family Epidendroideae.
L306@P15: However, Orchidaceae family is kept in the order Asparagales by classification of
L307@P15: ‘Angiosperm Phylogeny Group’ (APG) and remained same in APG I, II, III and IV
L308@P15: based on molecular study. The present study follows the classification by Chase et al.
L309@P15: (2015).
L310@P15: 1.4 Generic and sectional delimitation of Cymbidium Sw.
L311@P15: The generic name Cymbidium was established by Olof Swartz (1799) including 44
L312@P15: species where majority of them are no longer considered to be members of this genus.
L313@P15: Only three of them C. aloifolium, C. ensifolium and C. pendulum (= C. aloifolium),
L314@P15: remain recognized within Cymbidium with the first being selected as the genus type.
L315@P15: Lindley (1833) also included many species that are now placed in other genera.
L316@P15: Blume (1848, 1849, 1858) removed C. elegans to form genus Cyperorchis on the basis
L317@P15: of the sessile lip parallel to the column, elongated column with a beaked rostellum and
L318@P15: a pair of pyriform pollinia on the middle of a flat, transversely ovoid viscidium. Blume
L319@P15-16: (1858) transferred C. giganteum (= C. iridioides) to the genus Iridorchis.4
L320@P16: Reichenbach (1852) initially accepted the separation of Cyperorchis from Cymbidium.
L321@P16: However, he returned C. elegans to Cymbidium in 1864, noting the difference in the
L322@P16: pollinarium and pollinia shape were not sufficient for their separation. Hooker (1891)
