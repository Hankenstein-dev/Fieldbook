L187: Cymbidium cite113†/sɪmˈbɪdiəm/ ,^{cite114†[2] } commonly known as boat orchids, is a cite115†genus of cite116†evergreen cite93†flowering plants in the orchid cite117†family cite118†Orchidaceae . Orchids in this genus are cite119†epiphytic , cite120†lithophytic , cite121†terrestrial or rarely leafless cite122†saprophytic cite123†herbs usually with cite124†pseudobulbs . There are usually between three and twelve leaves arranged in two ranks on each pseudobulb or shoot and lasting for several years.
L188: From one to a large number of flowers are arranged on an unbranched flowering stem arising from the base of the pseudobulb. The cite125†sepals and cite126†petals are all free from and similar to each other. The cite127†labellum is significantly different from the other petals and the sepals and has three lobes. There are about fifty-five species and sixteen further natural cite128†hybrids occurring in the wild from cite129†tropical and cite130†subtropical Asia to Australia.
L189: Cymbidiums are well known in horticulture and many cite131†cultivars have been developed. The genome of Cymbidium mannii has been sequenced to study epiphytism and cite132†crassulacean acid metabolism .^{cite133†[3] }
L190: ## Description
L191: 
L192: [cite134†edit ]
L193: Plants in the genus Cymbidium are epiphytic, lithophytic or terrestrial plants, or rarely leafless saprophytes. All are cite135†sympodial cite116†evergreen herbs. Some species have thin stems but in most species the stems are modified as pseudobulbs. When present, there are from three to twelve leaves arrange in two ranks and last for several years. The leaf bases remain after the leaf has withered, forming a sheath around the pseudobulb.
L194: The flowers are arranged on an unbranched flowering stem which arises from the base of the pseudobulb or rarely from a leaf cite136†axil . The sepals and petals are usually thin and fleshy, free from, and more or less similar to each other. The labellum (as in other orchids, a highly modified third petal) is significantly different from the other petals and sepals. It is sometimes hinged to the cite137†column , or otherwise fused to it.
L195: The labellum has three lobes, the side lobes erect, sometimes surrounding the column and the middle lobe often curving downwards. After cite138†pollination a cite139†glabrous†en.wiktionary.org cite140†capsule containing many light coloured seeds is produced.^{cite141†[4] }^{cite142†[5] }^{cite143†[6] }
L196: Labelled image of cite103†Cymbidium aloifolium L197: ## Taxonomy and naming
L198: 
L199: [cite144†edit ]
L200: 
L201: The genus Cymbidium was first formally described in 1799 by cite100†Olof Swartz who published the description in Nova acta Regiae Societatis Scientiarum Upsaliensis.^{cite145†[7] }^{cite101†[1] }^{cite146†[8] } The genus name Cymbidium means 'little boat-shape', referring to the labellum.^{cite147†[9] }
L202: In 1848 cite108†Blume raised the genus Cyperorchis in cite148†Fedde's Repertorium Specierum Novarum Regni Vegetabilis and moved 19 species and 26 hybrids previously included in Cymbidium to the new genus.^{cite149†[10] } In 1984 cite150†Christopher Seth and cite151†Phillip Cribb divided the genus into three subgenera Cyperorchis, Jensoa, and Cymbidium and each subgenus into cite152†sections .
L203: In 1988, cite153†Dupuy and Cribb modified the sections.^{cite145†[7] } The three subgenera were confirmed by molecular phylogeny, however the sections did not show monophyly.^{cite154†[11] }^{cite155†[12] }
