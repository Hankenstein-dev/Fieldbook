L7@P0: Begonia sect. Petermannia (Begoniaceae) is revised for the Philippine island of Palawan.
L8@P0: Three species are recognised, namely Begonia palawanensis Merr. and the newly described
L9@P0: B. androturba C.Coyle and B. georgei C.Coyle.
L10@P0: Keywords. Begonia, IUCN Red List, new species, Palawan.
L11@P0: Introduction
L12@P0: Our understanding of the flora on the Philippine island of Palawan has been held
L13@P0: back by a lack of basic alpha taxonomic research, in turn hampered by the paucity of
L14@P0: herbarium collections from the island. Recent collections from Palawan as part of
L15@P0: the Philippine Plant Inventory project have thus provided a welcome addition to our
L16@P0: knowledge.
L17@P0: Begonia sect. Petermannia is the most species-rich section of the genus in Asia,
L18@P0: containing 247 species (Hughes, 2008). Previously, only one species of Begonia from
L19@P0: this section was known from Palawan, the endemic B. palawanensis Merr. (Merrill,
L20@P0: 1912; Hughes, 2008). Here we describe two new species in Begonia sect. Petermannia,
L21@P0: B. androturba C.Coyle and B. georgei C.Coyle, both also endemic to the island (Figs
L22@P0: 1, 2). Their novelty and endemism has been confirmed following consultation with
L23@P0: types and specimens for the other Philippine and Bornean species in Begonia sect.
L24@P0: Petermannia in A, B, BISH, BM, BO, BRIT, E, K, L, MICH, P, PNH, SING and U.
L25@P0: Protologues were consulted when no specimens were available.
L26@P0: Given the small number of specimens for all three species covered it is likely
L27@P0: that more species will be found with further collecting. Therefore, this revision must
L28@P0: be regarded simply as a starting point for further research rather than ‘the final
L29@P0: word’. All specimens cited are available as digital images from Hughes & Pullan
L30@P0: (2007).

L79@P4: This species is distinct from the other known species in Begonia sect. Petermannia
L80@P4: from Palawan in having oblong-ovate leaves with a well-developed basal lobe on one
L81@P4: side giving a cordate appearance, rather than lanceolate leaves which are cuneate
L82@P4: (wedge-shaped) at the base; it is also a larger, coarser plant. The unbranched
L83@P4: inflorescence with its clusters of male flowers spaced along a central axis is also
L84@P4: distinctive. Its large size and congested inflorescence are somewhat reminiscent of
L85@P4: Begonia ramosii Merr. (Luzon), but the leaves of B. androturba are more oblong in
L86@P4: shape and the inflorescence has a more distinct central axis. The epithet ‘androturba’
L87@P4: (‘male crowd’) is derived from the inflorescence morphology.

L118@P5: This species is vegetatively most similar to Begonia palawanensis but can be readily
L119@P5: distinguished vegetatively by its glabrous petioles, larger stipules and slightly more
L120@P5: asymmetric leaves with venation which is usually more palmate-pinnate than
L121@P5: pinnate. The male flower tepals have four (not two) tepals and are also noticeably
L122@P5: larger with much larger anthers; the female flowers remain unknown. Begonia
L123@P5: palawanensis and B. georgei are most similar to B. longistipula Merr. (Samar,
L124@P5: Mindanao) with which they share membranous leaves of a broadly similar shape and
L125@P5: an erect branching habit; inter alia B. palawanensis differs in having much smaller
L126@P5: fruit and stipules; B. georgei differs in having male flowers with four tepals. Argent &
L127@P5: Romero 9669 was chosen as the type because the label indicates there are 10
L128@P5: duplicates, although the whereabouts of seven of these are unknown; unfortunately
