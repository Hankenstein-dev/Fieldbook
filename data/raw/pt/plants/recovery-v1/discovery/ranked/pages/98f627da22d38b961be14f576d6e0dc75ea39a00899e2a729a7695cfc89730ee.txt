L148: Veronica sect. Hebe is a group of plants within the genus cite71†Veronica , native to New Zealand, cite73†Rapa in French Polynesia, the cite74†Falkland Islands and South America. It was formerly treated as the separate genus Hebe (cite75†/ˈhiːbiː/ ^{cite76†[1] }). It includes about 90 species. Almost all species occur in New Zealand, apart from cite77†Veronica rapensis (cite78†endemic to Rapa) and cite79†Veronica salicifolia , found in South America. It is named after the cite80†Greek goddess of youth, cite81†Hebe .
L149: Informally, species in the section may be called shrubby veronicas or hebes.
L150: Hebes are widely grown as ornamental plants (see cite21†Cultivation below).
L151: ## Description
L152: 
L153: [cite82†edit ]
L154: Species in Veronica sect. Hebe have four perpendicular rows of cite83†leaves in opposite cite84†decussate pairs. The cite85†flowers are cite86†perfect , the cite87†corolla usually has four slightly unequal lobes, the flower has two cite88†stamens and a long cite89†style . Flowers are arranged in a cite90†spiked cite91†inflorescence . Identification of species is difficult, especially if they are not in flower.
L155: The plants range in size from dwarf shrubs to small trees up to 7 metres (23 feet), and are distributed from coastal to cite92†alpine ecosystems. Large-leaved species are normally found on the coast, in lowland scrub and along forest margins. At higher altitudes smaller-leaved species grow, and in alpine areas there are whipcord species with leaves reduced to thick scales.
L156: ## Taxonomy
L157: 
L158: [cite93†edit ]
L159: 
L160: There are differing classifications for the genus. The former genus Hebe, together with the related Australasian genera Chionohebe, cite94†Derwentia , Detzneria, Parahebe, Heliohebe and Leonohebe are now included in the larger genus cite71†Veronica (hence the common name shrubby veronicas).^{cite95†[2] }^{cite96†[3] }^{cite97†[4] }

L229: 
L230: [cite157†edit ]
L231: 
L232: Hebes are valued in gardens in cite158†temperate climates as cite159†evergreen shrubs with decorative (sometimes cite160†variegated ) leaves. The flowers, in shades of blue, purple, pink or white, appear throughout summer and autumn. Their ability to withstand salt-laden winds makes them especially suited to coastal areas, for instance the South West of England, where they are often grown as hedges. cite161†Prostrate varieties can be used as cite162†groundcover .^{cite163†[6] }^{cite164†[7] }
L233: Hebes cope with most soil types, and can be cite165†propagated easily from both seed and cite166†cuttings . Wild cite167†hybrids in section Hebe are uncommon; however, there are many cultivated hybrids, such as Veronica × franciscana.
L234: 
L235: The cite168†Hebe Society , formed in 1985 under the auspices of the British cite169†Royal Horticultural Society , promotes the cultivation and conservation of hebes and other cite170†New Zealand native plants .
