L161@P5: decision to format a digital edition of the Handbook was easy. The entire digital edition

L164@P5: are also available for download and easy to print. Because it is in a digital format,

L167@P5: keeping the Handbook up to date. Revised and approved chapters will be accessible
L168@P5: almost immediately for download or purchase.

L173@P5: numbering system was employed: each chapter begins with page 1. This system of

L175@P5: page one. To avoid any craziness, page numbers are not included in the Table of
L176@P5: Contents. To help with navigation, each page has a header with an abbreviation of its
L177@P5: chapter title. A suggestion is to add a set of 31-tab dividers to your Handbook, one tab
L178@P5: for each chapter. These 31-tab dividers are called “daily dividers” and are available for

L223@P8: ACCREDITATION OF JUDGES
L224@P8: Approved by the AIS Board of Directors, January 5, 2021
L225@P8: Revision approved by the AIS Board of Directors, May 25, 2022
L226@P8: The judges training program is one of the most vital parts of the American Iris Society.
L227@P8: Members who serve as judges have duties and responsibilities to fulfill. Accomplishing
L228@P8: those duties and responsibilities, requires a judge to study, visit gardens and grow a
L229@P8: representative collection of irises.
L230@P8: The accredited judge is the backbone of the American Iris Society (AIS) judging
L231@P8: program and is responsible for the selection of award winners. It is the judges’ votes
L232@P8: that determine which irises receive awards and thus are recommended to the public.
L233@P8: Serving as a judge is a privilege and the duties should be fulfilled with dedication.
L234@P8: Because they represent the American Iris Society, judges should conduct themselves in
L235@P8: a manner which will bring credit to the organization.
L236@P8: Because the judge is, in many ways, the most important official of the AIS, guidelines
L237@P8: have been established for the training and selection of judges. The regulations are a
L238@P8: minimum standard and the conscientious judge will usually exceed the requirements.
L239@P8: APPOINTMENT OF JUDGES
L240@P8: Annually, regional vice presidents (RVPs) or designee (e.g., regional judges training
L241@P8: chair) recommend members from their region to be AIS judges for the following year.
L242@P8: These recommendations are sent to the AIS chair of judges for presentation to the AIS
L243@P8: Board of Directors. The board has the sole authority to approve the appointment of
L244@P8: judges and when opposing facts are presented, the board may choose to nullify an
L245@P8: appointment.
L246@P8: Those persons recommended as judges must have held continuous membership in the
L247@P8: AIS for the past three years and must have completed all other requirements.
L248@P8: Appointments are made for one year, beginning on January 1 after the AIS fall board
L249@P8: meeting. Reappointment for each succeeding year is based on satisfactory
L250@P8-9: performance and continuous membership in AIS. Ch 1 Judges – p 2

L252@P9: REGIONAL RESPONSIBILITIES
L253@P9: The regional responsibility ultimately resides with the regional vice president (RVP) but
L254@P9: may be delegated to the designee by the RVP if the region has a judges training chair.
L255@P9: Each region is responsible for carrying out its judges training program. It is necessary
L256@P9: that each region maintain records of the work of each judge and prospective judge.
L257@P9: Some regions do not have a large membership, availability of instructors, financial
L258@P9: resources or a geographical area that affords development of a truly comprehensive
L259@P9: training program. Therefore, the AIS requirements for judges training are the minimum
L260@P9: requirements that each region can meet. It is expected, however, that some regions will
L261@P9: require more than the minimum and some regions do have far more extensive
L262@P9: requirements. All training sessions must be approved in advance and copies of all
L263@P9: training rosters should be sent to and retained by the RVP or designee.
L264@P9: Each region should establish training guidelines which are in agreement with those of
L265@P9: the AIS. In regions where approved requirements for the judges training program are
L266@P9: more demanding than those of the AIS, the AIS will recognize the additional
L267@P9: requirements and will support them. Each region is required to send an activity report to
L268@P9: each judge annually. Emeritus judges are not required to return these reports, but all
L269@P9: other judges must complete the report and return it to the proper regional official. These
L270@P9: reports enable the regional vice presidents or designee to keep accurate records so
L271@P9: they will know when a candidate or apprentice has completed the requirements for
L272@P9: advancement to the next level and will know the training status and performance of their
L273@P9: accredited judges.
L274@P9: Regions should seek the most qualified members to become judges. Recruitment
L275@P9: should be based on finding those with knowledge of irises and a willingness and ability
L276@P9: to serve the AIS. No person should be appointed as a judge unless qualified. The RVP
L277@P9: or designee has the responsibility to see that guidelines are followed in the training of
L278@P9: these judges.
L279@P9: The AIS chair of judges will send each RVP or designee a packet of information each
L280@P9: year. The packet is to be used in making the recommendations for judges for the
L281@P9: coming year and is to be returned to the AIS chair of judges by a date set by the AIS
L282@P9: chair of judges, usually in the fall. It is imperative that the RVP or designee return this
L283@P9: information in a timely manner in order to avoid any delay in approval of judges for that
L284@P9-10: region. Ch 1 Judges – p 3

L286@P10: GUIDELINES FOR
L287@P10: APPROVAL AND REPORTING OF JUDGES TRAINING
L288@P10: 1. Judges training sessions are to be approved in advance by the RVP or by the
L289@P10: designee when so authorized by the RVP.
L290@P10: 2. An approved judges training class is defined as a structured in-classroom or ingarden instruction for two or more participants and presented by a fully accredited AIS
L291@P10: judge. A written test of at least ten questions for each hour of training credit shall be
L292@P10: administered, discussed and filed with the RVP or designee.
L293@P10: 3. Fully accredited judges may judge an accredited show. They also may conduct forcredit judges training courses. A retired judge may not conduct a for-credit judges
L294@P10: training course. The retired judge may serve as a show judge on a panel with at least
L295@P10: one fully accredited judge at an accredited show.
L296@P10: 4. A report of the training session is to be forwarded to the approving RVP or designee
L297@P10: within two weeks following the session. The report should state the region, sponsoring
L298@P10: group, date, subject, instructor, hours of credit and include a list of attendees showing
L299@P10: their name as given on AIS membership records, type of judge and the judge’s region.
L300@P10: 5. A written test containing a minimum of twenty questions on areas to be covered in a
L301@P10: two-hour or longer session must be prepared by or under the supervision of the
L302@P10: instructor and completed by the attendees at the conclusion of the session. Sessions of
L303@P10: less than two hours may have fewer questions with a minimum of ten questions for a
L304@P10: one-hour session. A blank copy of the test is to accompany the report sent to the RVP
L305@P10: or designee. Completed judges training tests should be kept by the RVP or designee for
L306@P10: a minimum of three years.
L307@P10: THE CANDIDATE FOR JUDGESHIP
L308@P10: Any AIS member may study to become a judge. After three years of continuous AIS
L309@P10: membership and ten hours of judges training, the candidate may apply to become an
L310@P10: apprentice. Continuous membership is maintained by paying dues on time. If dues are
L311@P10: not received within the grace period, the three-year membership requirement must
L312@P10: begin anew. The membership of the candidate must be in the person’s name. Credit
L313@P10: cannot be given for membership carried in the name of a garden, commercial enterprise
L314@P10-11: or another person’s name. Ch 1 Judges – p 4

L354@P12: THE APPRENTICE JUDGE
L355@P12: After successfully completing all the required training, a candidate will be recommended
L356@P12: for appointment as apprentice judge. By this time the candidate should have learned
L357@P12: the duties and responsibilities of serving as a judge; should have grown and studied
L358@P12: enough irises to be familiar with different classifications, varieties/cultivars and their
L359@P12: characteristics; and should know the qualities of a good iris well enough to make a
L360@P12: conscientious evaluation. The apprentice judge has three years in which to complete
L361@P12: the requirements for becoming a garden/exhibition judge.
L362@P12: Each apprentice judge is required to judge seedlings or named varieties in bloom in the
L363@P12: garden on two separate occasions under the tutorship of two different fully accredited
L364@P12: AIS judges. Each tutoring session must be for a minimum of two hours. This tutoring
L365@P12: must be more than just a stroll through the garden. The time should be spent in truly
L366@P12: evaluating iris plants: the plant vigor, the stalk, branching, substance, durability, bud
L367@P12: count, form, etc.
L368@P12: The AIS chair of awards will send each apprentice judge a copy of the official AIS Ballot
L369@P12: which will be marked “Void.” The apprentice should use it as a study guide during visits
L370@P12: to gardens for garden training. This ballot is not to be returned to AIS.
L371@P12: Each apprentice judge is required to receive exhibition training at two different
L372@P12: accredited shows under the tutorship of different fully accredited AIS judges on each
L373@P12: occasion. The apprentice is only a student and cannot participate in the selection of
L374@P12: “Best Specimen of Show” or help to break ties when the other judges disagree on the
L375@P12: ribbon to be awarded. The apprentice judge is to listen to the evaluations being made
L376@P12: by the judges of the show and learn from their deliberations. Apprentices should be
L377@P12: given the opportunity to evaluate one or more specimens. Apprentices have no vote,
L378@P12: and the ribbons are awarded by the accredited judges. Only one apprentice is to be
L379@P12: assigned to each panel of judges. Under no circumstances is the apprentice to serve
L380@P12: as a clerk while receiving this training. If more than one apprentice needs training, the
L381@P12: panel of judges may be divided so there is still only one apprentice per panel.
