L5@P0: The plant family Musaceae, composed of bananas, plantains, and ornamental bananas,
L6@P0: originally evolved in Southeast Asia and surrounding tropical and subtropical regions (including New Guinea). Africa is a secondary
L7@P0: center of diversity.
L8@P0: The two genera Ensete and Musa in the family
L9@P0: Musaceae are covered here.
L10@P0: Please note that this manuscript is not all-inclusive and that much of the complex species
L11@P0: and cultivar taxonomy is in the process of revision and expansion. Readers are encouraged
L12@P0: to consult with the many excellent online resources listed in the “Bibliography” for current
L13@P0: information.

L21@P1: The genus Ensete ranges throughout Africa and southern
L22@P1: Asia. Depending on the authority, the genus Ensete contains
L23@P1: as many as nine species. They are monocarpic, unbranched
L24@P1: herbs that sucker rarely and are used for food, fiber, and as
L25@P1: ornamentals. They resemble banana plants, but their widespreading and immensely long, paddle-shaped leaves with
L26@P1: usually crimson midribs, are unmistakable. Their fruits are
L27@P1: similar in appearance to those of banana, but they are dry,
L28@P1: seedy, and inedible. The entire plant dies after fruiting.

L74@P2: Musa
L75@P2: The genus Musa’s center of origin is Asia (primarily southern and southeastern). A great number of important plants
L76@P2: are found in the genus; those that bear edible fruit are the
L77@P2: most significant. In addition to fruit, bananas and plantains
L78@P2: provide many cultures with medicines, beverages, fibers,
L79@P2: edible floral parts, dyes, fuel, steam for cooking, cordage,
L80@P2: wrapping materials, etc. With few exceptions, the familiar
L81@P2: eating bananas are naturally occuring hybrids among the
L82@P2: various subspecies of M. acuminata and interspecific hybrids between M. acuminata and M. balbisiana.
L83@P2: Musa taxonomy is confused by several factors including
L84@P2: the sterility, ancient domestication, and hybrid origins of
L85@P2: the cultivated varieties (cultivars), and the unwillingness
L86@P2: of many to adopt newer, correct names. For example, Linnaean binomials such as M. paradisiaca (‘French’ plantain)
L87@P2: and M. sapientum (‘Silk’) are still used decades after the
L88@P2: cultivars to which these names refer were recognized as M.
L89@P2: acuminata × M. balbisiana hybrids (see Part 2). Constantine summarized the confusion (Constantine, 2004).
L90@P2: Historically, four sections have been recognized in Musa,
L91@P2: Australimusa, Callimusa, musa (formerly
L92@P2: known as EUMUSA), and Rhodochlamys. Recent molecular analyses indicate a reduction to two sections, but much further study is required before the above
L93@P2: system is abandoned.
L94@P2: Section Australimusa (chromosome number: x = 10)
L95@P2: Seeds subglobose or compressed, smooth, striate, tuberculate or irregularly angled. Contains the Fe‘i bananas, which
L96@P2: are important in the Pacific. Their origins are complex and
L97@P2: may involve as many as three species, M. lolodensis, M. maclayi and M. peekelii. Also included in the section is an important source of fiber, abacá (M. textilis).
L98@P2: Plants in the Australimusa section are generally tall,
L99@P2: with seeded fruit, and distinctive green or greenish-yellow
L100@P2: buds (if present). Their seed structure is important for classification purposes, viz. either subglobose or compressed,
L101@P2: smooth, striate, tuberculate, or irregularly angled.
L102@P2: Section Australimusa ranges naturally from New
L103@P2: Guinea and northern Queensland into the western Pacific.
L104@P2: However, a principal component, the Fe‘i bananas have
L105@P2: become famous because of their association with French
L106@P2: Polynesia, where they were transported long distances
L107@P2: in canoes by Polynesian seafarers as far back as 250 BC
L108@P2: (Marquesas Islands) and later in ~800 AD (Tahiti). In
L109@P2: 2004, Micronesian Fe‘i bananas enjoyed spotlighting in
L110@P2: Pacific agricultural circles. ‘Karat’, ‘Utin lap’ and other bananas have been shown to contain the high levels of beta
L111@P2: carotene (vitamin A precursor).

L113@P2: baked or boiled, especially if the slices are swathed in fresh

L115@P2: of reddish-amber to red urine by those who consume the
L116@P2: fruit.
