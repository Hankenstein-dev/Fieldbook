L51: A phylogenomic analysis and revised infrageneric classification of Thesium (Santalaceae)
L52: Lombard, N., Forest, F., Maurin, O., le Roux, M.M., Poma Alarcon, M.A., Carruthers, A. and van Wyk, B.-E. (2026), TAXON, 75: e70171. cite23†https://doi.org/10.1002/tax.70171†doi.org L53: A phylogenomic analysis and revised infrageneric classification of Thesium (Santalaceae) is presented based on targeted enrichment using the Angiosperms353 probe set. This first targeted sequencing study includes 137 samples representing 106 species (approximately 32% of the genus). Our results provide substantially improved resolution compared to previous studies that utilised only a few gene regions, with an almost fully supported backbone topology.
L54: Thesium (including Austroamericium, Chrysothesium, Kunkeliella, and Thesidium) is confirmed as monophyletic and sister to a clade containing Lacomucinaea and Osyridicarpos. We recognise six monophyletic subgenera with full or strong support: T. subg. Hagnothesium, T. subg. Thesium, T. subg. Spinosa (newly described), T. subg. Discothesium (with revised circumscription), T. subg. Frisea, and T. subg. Psilothesium. We found T. subg.
L55: Discothesium as previously delineated to be paraphyletic and describe a new subgenus, T. subg. Spinosa, to ensure monophyly of all subgenera. We formally recognise 18 sections in Thesium, of which 8 are newly described and 2 are raised from lower ranks. Our phylogenetic clades show a strong biogeographic signal and despite sporadic morphological synapomorphies, most subgenera and sections can be distinguished by a combination of their distribution and morphology.
L56: We provide the first genetic evidence of potential hybridisation in Thesium paving the way for further exploration of this long-hypothesised compounding factor. This study provides a robust framework for future taxonomic, biogeographic, and evolutionary studies of this diverse and problematic genus.

L82: ### Thesium subg. Thesium
L83: 
L84: `This does not affect us at all. `
L85: predominantly Eurasian of ~74 species
L86:   * lack the apical beard present on the flower lobes of many African species (but some have lateral auricles - see also subg. Spinosa)
L87:   * herbaceous plants that lack woody growth above the ground and have well-developed leaves (except for 5 spp from Canary Islands (sect. Kunkeliella)
L88:   * mostly with flowers placed in lax panicles or branched or unbranched raceme-like inflorescences
L89:   * placental columns twisted
L90:   * fruit morphology variable
L91: 
L92: five sections proposed
L93:   * T. sect. Kunkeliella (Canary Islands endemics)
L94:   * T. sect. Chrysothesium (Eurasia) - two clades: but morphological defining features lacking
L95:   * T. sect. Humilia (Thesium humile)
L96:   * T. sect. Thesium (Eurasia) lacking features but: unbranched or sparsely branched stems and long bracts reaching to the top of the flower or beyond
L97:   * T. sect. Orientale (Eurasia, mainly Asia) lacks clear diagnostic characters but: bracts fused to the entire pedicel/peduncle and reaching to the top of the flowers or far beyond
L98: cite29†https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/1397998-Thesium L99: 
L100: Plants with hermaphroditic or gynodioecious flowers; stems and leaves not spinescent; flowers mostly 5-merous, rarely 4-merous (Thesium alpinum, T. cathaicum, T. unicaule) with or without a flat disc in the hypanthium; apical beard on the flower lobes absent; lobe margins glabrous or erose, rarely slightly hairy, with or without lateral auricles; placental columns usually, potentially always, twisted.

L148: ### Thesium subg. Frisea
L149: 
L150:   * sulcate stems with decurrent leaves
L151:   * twisted placental columns
L152:   * primary phloem fibers are deep within the cortex near the vascular cambium and are encircled by chlorenchyma from the fused portions of the leaves - forming distinct grooves that are visible in cross-section.
L153:   * often compact, terminal, more or less capitate inflorescences
L154: Second most speciose subgenus in Thesium with about 87 species largely restricted to the Cape Floristic Region and Succulent Karoo and a few species extending east
L155: 
L156: seven sections:
L157: #### T. sect. Scirpioidea
L158: 
L159:   * combination of inconspicuous unarmed herbaceous or suffrutescent habits, scale-like leaves, true spike inflorescences and twisted placental columns
L160: 
L161: extends from Fynbos Biome northeast into KwaZulu-Natal, the Highveld and the Lowveld
L162: Some 8 species
L163: #### T. sect. Nigromontanum
L164: 
L165:   * shrubs and subshrubs
L166:   * flowers arranged in few-flowered cymose clusters
L167:   * flower lobes lack an apical beard (exc. T. microcephalum)
L168:   * Some are leaf-less in appearance with most or all leaves highly reduced, whereas others are covered in short but well-developed leaves
L169: 
L170: Some 5 Fynbos species
L171: #### T. sect. Subglabra
L172: 
L173:   * fruit with five longitudinal veins prominently swollen at the base
L174:   * most appear leaf-less (but T. erectiramosum is leafy)
L175:   * lack apical beards on the flower lobes
L176: 
L177: Some 7 spp
L178: 
L179: #### T. sect. Barbata
L180: 
L181:   * apical beards on the flower lobes
L182:   * cymose inflorescences usually with less than 10 flowers, including some consisting of only a single flower and involucral bracts
L183: 
L184: 16 Fynbos species
L185: #### T. sect. Foliacea
L186: 
L187:   * leafy plants with cymose inflorescences, including capitate heads, corymbs and elongated spicate cymes
L188:   * flowers with an apical beard on the lobes
L189:   * inflorescences usually more than 10 flowers,
L190:   * a few long styles more than 1.5 mm long - even up to 4 mm
L191: 
L192: Some 18 Fynbos, Succulent Karoo and Nama-Karoo biomes, but some extend into the Grassland biome
L193: #### T. sect. Stricta
L194: 
L195:   * Plants usually tall and erect either leafy or not (T. albomontanum is prostrate)
L196:   * Flowers in dense terminal globose or subglobose clusters or lax terminal corymbs
L197:   * Apical beard on the flower lobes always absent
L198: 
L199: Some 4 spp
L200: #### T. sect. Frisea
L201: 
L202:   * tufts of downward facing hairs in the flowers, arranged in a ring at the level of filament attachment
L203:   * flowers lack the post-staminal hairs
L204: 
L205: Some 18 species from the GCFR and Succulent Karoo
L206: 
L207: cite34†https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/1397996-Frisea L208: Plants hermaphrodite; stems not spinescent; leaves rarely spinescent; flowers mostly 5-merous with or without a flat disc in the hypanthium; apical beard on the flower lobes present or absent; lobe margins glabrous, erose, or variously fringed without lateral auricles; placental columns usually, or potentially always, twisted.

L213: ### Thesium subg. Psilothesium
L214: 
L215:   * morphologically diverse. Includes annual herbs and woody perennials, with diverse inflorescence types ranging from simple spikes to complex branched systems (but lacks compact capitate terminal clusters). Many species have short straight placental columns, but exceptions with twisted placental columns do occur)
L216: 
L217: largest subgenus in Thesium with around 146 species worldwide
L218: 
L219: Internal groupings are especially problematic. Many species show high morphological variation.
L220: #### Thesium sect. Confinia
L221: 
L222:   * leaves reduced to scales,
L223:   * determinate spike-like inflorescences,
L224:   * bearded flower lobes
L225:   * twisted placental columns,
L226: 
L227: Some 2 spp in Grassland biome
L228: 
L229: #### Thesium sect. Gnidiacea
L230: 
L231:   * dense imbricate leaves
L232:   * indeterminate spicate inflorescences that sometimes continue as vegetative growth at the apex after fruiting
L233: 
L234: Some 8 spp across South Africa
L235: #### Thesium sect. Psilothesium
L236: 
L237:   * lack apical beards on the inner flower lobes
L238:   * short straight placental columns
L239: 
L240: Some 65 spp in s Afr, with 3 in S America and 2 in Madagascar
L241: 
L242: #### Kilimandscharicum clade
L243: 
L244: Some 3 spp
L245: #### Amicorum clade
L246: 
L247:   * annuals (unusual in Thesium)
L248:   * often with 2 well-developed subopposite basal leaves that may be persistent cotyledons
L249:   * slender erect herbs, that are leaf-less in appearance, with a single stem at ground level, a few virgate branches distally
L250:   * elongate spike-like inflorescences without involucral bracts.
L251:   * solitary flowers are sometimes accompanied by one or two additional flowers.
L252: 
L253: Some spp in
L254: #### Viride clade
L255: 
L256:   * much-branched perennial herbs usually with multiple stems at ground level
L257:   * irregularly branched (variable in structure even on one plant) axillary cymes in raceme-like arrangement.
L258: 
L259: Some spp in Tropical Africa
L260: 
L261: #### Ussanguense clade
L262: 
L263:   * robust rootstocks, numerous branches with well-developed leaves,
L264:   * flowers in elongated true spikes, long corolla lobes with hairy margins but lacking an apical beard, and well-developed hypanthia
L265: 
L266: Some 3 spp in Tropical Africa
L267: #### Brachyblast clade
L268: 
L269:   * stems arising from slender rootstocks
L270:   * often favour water-logged areas Some 6 species in the Thesium wilczekianum group Some 6 species In the Thesium stuhlmannii group Some 3 species in the Austroamericium group
L271: 
L272: Some 19 species - also in S. Am & Mad.
L273: #### Thesium sect. Psilotoidia
L274: 
L275: morphologically diverse,
L276: 
L277:   * both annual herbs and woody perennials
L278:   * well-developed or reduced scale-like leaves,
L279:   * inflorescences ranging from true spikes to complex cymes
L280:   * flowers with or without apical beards
L281: 
L282: Some 70 species widespread in grassland and savanna of Africa (& Madagascar and China)
L283: #### Celatum group
L284: 
L285:   * leafy shrubs up to 150 cm tall with much-branched woody stems,
L286:   * apical beards on the flower lobes,
L287:   * well-developed styles (about 0.6–1.0 mm long),
L288:   * fruits with prominent stipes 0.5–2.0 mm long
L289:   * 3 species in grasslands.
L290: 
L291: #### Gracilarioides group
L292: 
L293:   * erect shrubs and subshrubs (up to 2 m tall),
L294: 
L295: #### Resedoides group
L296: 
L297:   * leafy herbs
L298:   * flowers placed in simple racemes,
L299:   * apically bearded flower lobes
L300:   * well-developed styles (0.2–1.1 mm long).
L301: #### Gracile group
L302: 
L303:   * leafy herbs
L304:   * flowers in lax cymes on long peduncles
L305: 
L306: #### Goetzeanum group
L307: 
L308:   * grassland species in South Africa, Lesotho & Eswatini
L309:   * glabrous,
L310:   * well-developed leaves,
L311:   * determinate inflorescences
L312:   * flower lobes with apical beards
L313: #### Costatum group
L314: 
L315:   * small statured erect herbs that grow up to about 400mm tall
L316:   * prominently ribbed stems,
L317:   * long hypanthia - usually equal to the lobes in length
L318:   * styles 0.5–3.0 mm long
L319:   * filaments attached at the base of the lobes
L320:   * anthers exserted.
L321:   * flower lobe margins often inflexed and papillose or rarely glabrous, but without an apical beard.
L322: #### Cupressoides group
L323: 
L324:   * flowers with hypanthium a flat disc, and glabrous lobes
L325:   * much-branched stems with short, rather dense leaves
L326:   * twisted placental columns
