L154:   * Sphaerostephanaceae Ching 1940 nom. nud.
L155: Thelypteridaceae is a family of about 900^{cite80†[2] } species of cite73†ferns in the order cite74†Polypodiales .^{cite81†[3] }^{cite82†[4] }^{cite83†[5] } In the cite84†Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I), it is placed in the suborder cite75†Aspleniineae .^{cite81†[3] } Alternatively, the family may be submerged in a very broadly defined family Aspleniaceae as the subfamily Thelypteridoideae.^{cite85†[6] }
L156: The ferns are cite86†terrestrial , with the exception of a few which are cite87†lithophytes (grow on rocks).^{cite83†[5] } The bulk of the species are tropical, although there are a number of temperate species.^{cite83†[5] }
L157: These ferns typically have creeping cite88†rhizomes . The cite89†fronds are simply cite90†pinnate to pinnate-cite91†pinnatifid . There is either no frond cite92†dimorphism or only mild dimorphism, either open cite93†venation or very simple cite94†anastomosing . The cite95†sori are mostly reniform in shape and have indusia, except for the Phegopteris group.
L158: ## Classification
L159: 
L160: [cite96†edit ]
L161: 
L162: During the early and mid 1900's^{cite97†[7] } all thelypterioid ferns were included in the genus cite98†Dryopteris because of the sorus shape. However, there are a great many differences between the groups, and these plants are now segregated in their own family.^{cite80†[2] }^{cite81†[3] } cite99†Genetic evidence shows that the family is clearly cite100†monophyletic .^{cite81†[3] }

L180: ### Genera
L181: 
L182: [cite114†edit ]
L183: The family can be divided into two major clades, which the cite84†Pteridophyte Phylogeny Group classification of 2016 (PPG I) recognizes as two subfamilies, Phegopteridoideae and Thelypteroideae.^{cite81†[3] } Their division into genera has been described as "highly controversial and fluctuating".^{cite115†[10] } The family includes several complexes of species that are difficult to distinguish, and seem to represent a remarkable evolutionary radiation.
L184: Some researchers include the entire family Thelypteridaceae in the genus cite116†Thelypteris ; others divide the family into as many as 30 genera.^{cite115†[10] } An intermediate position is to place the bulk in Thelypteris (which can then be divided into subgenera and sections corresponding to the genera of other authors) but to separate out cite117†Phegopteris and cite118†Macrothelypteris .^{cite80†[2] } Another choice is to divide the family into a half a dozen or so genera.^{cite83†[5] }
