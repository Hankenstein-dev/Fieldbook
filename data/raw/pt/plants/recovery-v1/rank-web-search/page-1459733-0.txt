L220: cite151†New York Carex cladogram ⇗ L221: ## Carex
L222: 
L223: [cite152†edit | cite153†edit source ]
L224: The genus cite154†Carex†en.wikipedia.org is represented by more than 200 species in New York. Of these, all but about a dozen species are native to New York State.^{cite155†[1] } Of the non-native taxa, the two species of Asiatic sand sedge, included here in cite40†Carex sect. Macrocephalae , are considered to be potentially invasive.^{cite156†[2] } Only one of these, Carex kobomugi, has been reported in New York (a single population in 2013), but both have been reported in nearby states.
L225: Here, the genus was previously organized by sections, using the modified version of cite157†Kenneth Kent Mackenzie†en.wikipedia.org ’s system used in the Flora of North America (FNA).^{cite158†[3] }^{cite159†[4] } This page was later re-organized somewhat based on the intermediate framework recommended by the Global Carex Group (GCG)(2021).^{cite160†[5] } Where practicable, the traditional (FNA) sections were left intact within any larger clades into which their members were placed by GCG.

L246: 
L247: [cite182†edit | cite183†edit source ]
L248: Carex leptalea III.B.1. Rara Clade contains traditional sections Rarae (23/23), Rhizopodae (3/3), Leptocephalae (1/1). Of the 27 or so species in the resulting clade, only Carex leptalea is native to North America.
L249: The rest are Asian or Australasian species.^{cite184†[1] } The traditional Carex section Leptocephalae contains the single species Carex leptalea which is found in North America and the Caribbean Basin.^{cite185†[2] } iNaturalist currently treats the Rara Clade as Complex Carex leptalea (Carex Rara Clade).^{cite186†[3] }
