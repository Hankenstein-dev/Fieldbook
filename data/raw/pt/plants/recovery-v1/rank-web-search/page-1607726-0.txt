L5: # Erigeron
L6: 
L7: RHS Genus Guide
L8: 
L9: Erigeron is a large genus of daisy-like perennials and annuals in the aster family and ranges from ground-hugging alpines to taller plants suitable for mixed borders. Appreciated for their long flowering season and informal looks, many are ideal for cottage gardens and gravel planting or spilling from walls or containers.
L10: 
L11: Botanical name: Erigeron
L12: 
L13: Common name: fleabane
L14: 
L15: cite2†Image†assets.rhs.org.uk RHS / Anthony Masi
L16: ## Appearance
L17: 
L18: Though varying in size, they often form mounds of narrow, green leaves, topped with masses of small flowers with yellow centres. Petals range across the spectrum from white and pink to lavender, purple and blue, with a few in yellow or orange.
L19: ## Preferences
L20: 
L21: Erigeron thrive in well-drained soil and full sun but tolerate partial shade. Once established, they are drought-tolerant and well-suited to dry, poor soils and coastal gardens. Some, like the Mexican fleabane (Erigeron karvinskianus), spread naturally into cracks in paving or walls.
L22: 
L23: ## Dislikes
L24: 
L25: They dislike heavy, poorly drained soils and overly rich conditions, which can lead to root rot or leggy growth. Wet winters without good drainage can damage the roots.
L26: ## Did you know?
L27: 
L28: The name fleabane comes from the old belief that dried Erigeron leaves could repel fleas. However, with their flat, open flowers many species are definitely attractive to pollinators.
