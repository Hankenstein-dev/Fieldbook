L9@P0: Introduction
L10@P0: Asplenium sect. Thamnopteris C.Presl, commonly called birdnest fern, is a morphologically distinct group with distribution in
L11@P0: the tropics and subtropics of the Old World. The distinguishing
L12@P0: features of this section are in fronds simple, entire, and set in
L13@P0: a close spiral, and veins parallel, close, with their tips united
L14@P0: and forming an intramarginal vein (Holttum 1974). Stressing
L15@P0: the distinct morphology, some authors have preferred to accept
L16@P0: this group as a separate genus Neottopteris J.Sm. (Smith 1841,
L17@P0: Ching 1978, Wu 1999). In 1974 Holttum recognized 15 species
L18@P0: and five infraspecific taxa within sect. Thamnopteris based
L19@P0: on herbarium specimens, which is the most comprehensive
L20@P0: summary so far available on the taxonomy of bird-nest ferns.
L21@P0: Since Holttum’s revision (1974) an additional eight taxa of Asplenium sect. Thamnopteris have been described from various
L22@P0: regions, i.e., N. longistipitata R.H.Miao (1980) from southern
L23@P0: China, A. harmanii D.L.Jones (1988) from northern Australia
L24@P0: (Queensland), N. antrophyoides var. cristata Ching & S.H.Wu,
L25@P0: N. latipes Ching ex S.H.Wu, N. longistipes Ching ex S.H.Wu and
L26@P0: N. subantiqua Ching ex S.H.Wu from southwestern China (Wu
L27@P0: 1989), A. goudeyi D.L.Jones (1996) from Australia (Lord Howe
L28@P0: Island), and A. setoi N.Murak. & Seriz. (Murakami et al. 1999b)
L29@P0: from Japan (Ryukyu). Recently, molecular data suggest that
L30@P0: Thamnopteris is probably not monophyletic (Murakami 1995,
L31@P0: Murakami et al. 1999a, Perrie & Brownsey 2005). Yatabe and
L32@P0: his colleagues treated the whole section of Thamnopteris as
L33@P0: a complex containing multiple cryptic species (Yatabe et al.
L34@P0: 2001, 2002, 2009, Yatabe & Murakami 2003), but this places
L35@P0: too much emphasis on molecular evidence and shows little
L36@P0: attention to morphological variation, because most species
L37@P0: within this section can be clearly recognized by morphological
L38@P0: characters such as frond shape, midrib, stipe scale, sorus, and
L39@P0: spore ornamentation.
L40@P0: When attempting to update the taxonomy of Asplenium sect.
L41@P0: Thamnopteris, we found a cultivated plant with peculiar morphology in Bogor Botanical Garden, West Java, which was
L42@P0: readily recognized as belonging to Thamnopteris but could
L43@P0: not be identified as one of the known species. An earlier collection from Nusakambangan, Central Java was subsequently
L44@P0: found with similar morphological features to the living plant in
L45@P0: Bogor. Further observations on the morphology of spores and
L46@P0: leaf epidermis confirmed that the two plants were conspecific
L47@P0: and they represented a new species. The new species is now
L48@P0: described as A. riswanii S.Y.Dong.
L49@P0: Material and Methods
L50@P0: Only two specimens of the new species are hitherto known.
L51@P0: The leaf epidermis of the two specimens, as well as some
L52@P0: related species, was anatomically examined using the method
L53@P0: described in Wang & Lu (2010). The spore samples of both
L54@P0: specimens and related species were observed in a scanning
L55@P0: electron microscope (SEM) following the method in Dong &
L56@P0: Zhang (2005). The measurements of spore size or stoma size
L57@P0: are the average of 20 spores or 20 stomata, respectively. The
L58@P0: recognition of seven species in Malesia and the diagnostic
L59@P0: characteristics used in the key are based on the morphological
L60@P0: examination of c. 2 500 specimens in the following herbaria:
L61@P0: BM, BO, GAUA, IBK, IBSC, K, KUN, KYO, L, P, PE, PNH,
L62@P0: PYU, SING.
L63@P0: For the extraction and amplification of DNAand the subsequent
L64@P0: analysis, we refer to Wei et al. (in prep.).

L92@P0: Abstract A new species, Asplenium riswanii (sect. Thamnopteris), is described from Central Java and West Papua,
L93@P0: Indonesia. It is distinct from any known species by having thick and rigid fronds, abaxially keeled midribs, broadly
L94@P0: lanceolate scales, and distantly spaced sori. Morphologically, A. riswanii is not similar to any species from Malesia
L95@P0: but somewhat close to A. antrophyoides from mainland Southeast Asia. Molecular data reveal it has an isolated
L96@P0: position in the phylogeny. The micromorphology of spores and leaf epidermis of A. riswanii is also documented and
L97@P0: a key to the species of Asplenium sect. Thamnopteris from Malesia is given.

L133@P3: West Papua in 2007. The two specimens are in agreement
L134@P3: with each other in many key characters of macromorphology:
L135@P3: broadly lanceolate scales; the size, shape, and particular thick
L136@P3: texture of fronds; abaxially keeled midribs; and the distribution,
L137@P3: length, and the distantly spaced sori. Likewise they are similar
L138@P3: in the micromorphology of leaf epidermis and spores: the shape
L139@P3: and size of epidermal cells on either surface, the pattern and
L140@P3: size of stomata, the lophate perispores, and the alate folds and
L141@P3: echinulate ornamentation on perispores (Fig. 2). Clearly the two
L142@P3: specimens represent a single species.
L143@P3: Being characterized by broad scales, keeled midribs, distantly
L144@P3: spaced sori, and extraordinary thick texture of fronds, A. riswanii
L145@P3: is not morphologically similar to any known sympatric species
L146@P3: but somewhat similar to A. antrophyoides H.Christ from mainland Southeast Asia (southwestern China, northern Vietnam,
L147@P3: and northern Thailand). The two species are similar in frond
L148@P3: size and features of stipe scales and midribs, but A. riswanii
L149@P3: differs from A. antrophyoides in fronds being much thicker
L150@P3: and more rigid in texture, gradually tapering toward the base
L151@P3: from the middle (vs more or less abruptly narrowed at upper
L152@P3: part of fronds in A. antrophyoides), and much more distantly
L153@P3: spaced sori (4–5 vs 7–12 sori every 2 cm along midribs). In
L154@P3: micromorphology of both leaf epidermis and spore ornamentation, there are also some differences between A. riswanii and
L155@P3: A. antrophyoides. The ornamentation of the perispore, which
L156@P3: was recently demonstrated to be taxonomically significant
L157@P3: between species in Asplenium sect. Thamnopteris (Wei &
L158@P3: Dong 2012), shows lower folds in A. riswanii (c. 2 μm) than
L159@P3: in A. antrophyoides (c. 5 μm). The stomata of A. riswanii are
L160@P3: of the anisocytic, diacytic, or tetracytic type, and the outline of
L161@P3: the stomata is nearly circular (with the length/width ratio being
L162@P3: 1.2–1.3); while the stomata in A. antrophyoides are usually of
L163@P3: the diacytic or polocytic type and are elliptic in shape (length/
L164@P3: width ratio 1.5–1.7) (Fig. 2).
L165@P3: Besides the morphological comparison, we also conducted
L166@P3: phylogenetic analyses of A. riswanii and other species of
L167@P3: the section Thamnopteris based on two datasets of plastid
L168@P3: sequences (separate rbcL and a combination of psbA-trnL,
L169@P3: rbcL, and trnL-F). The plastid sequences, either the rbcL or the
L170@P3: combination of the three, indicate that A. riswanii is not clearly
L171@P3: close to any known species. In a phylogenetic tree based on
L172@P3: rbcL sequences of 37 accessions (including one each of two
L173@P3: outgroups) (Fig. 3), A. riswanii is resolved as clearly separate
L174@P3: from a number of well-supported clades. A comprehensive
L175@P3: discussion of the phylogeny of this group will be presented in
L176@P3: a separate paper (Wei et al, in prep.).
