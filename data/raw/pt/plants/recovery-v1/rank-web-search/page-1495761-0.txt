L144: Cerastium fontanum
L145: cite74†Baumg. L146: Cerastium fontanum, also called mouse-ear chickweed, common mouse-ear, or starweed, is a species of mat-forming cite75†perennial or, rarely, cite76†annual plant . It is native to Europe but cite77†introduced elsewhere. Its identifying characteristics are tear-shaped leaves growing opposite one another in a star pattern, cite78†hairy leaves, and small white flowers.
L147: Mouse-ear chickweed typically grows to 4"-8" tall and spreads horizontally along the ground via the formation of roots wherever the stem falls over and contacts the ground.^{cite79†[1] }^{cite80†[2] }
L148: ## Description
L149: 
L150: [cite81†edit ]
L151: 
L152: Cerastium fontanum is a low growing plant covered with small hairs which are not sticky, that is, without glandular tips. The erect flowering stems up to 45 cm (18 in) long and leaves, opposite, up to 20 mm (0.79 in) long without stalks. It has prostrate branches which do not bear flowers. The 5 petals are shorter than the sepals or a little longer and are deeply divided. The flowers have 10 cite82†stamens with 5 separate cite83†styles .^{cite84†[3] }^{cite85†[4] }
L153: ## Habitat
L154: 
L155: [cite86†edit ]
L156: 
L157: It is common in grassland and along roadsides.^{cite84†[3] }
L158: ## Distribution
L159: 
L160: [cite87†edit ]
L161: 
L162: It is native to Europe, Greenland, the Himalayas and Japan.^{cite88†[5] } It is common throughout cite89†Great Britain ^{cite84†[3] } and cite90†Ireland .^{cite91†[6] } It has been introduced to the Falkland Islands and MacQuarie Island,^{cite88†[5] } and to many other places around the world, including all 50 of the cite92†United States and all provinces and territories of cite93†Canada except cite94†Nunavut . ^{cite95†[7] }
L163: ## Etymology
L164: 
L165: [cite96†edit ]
L166: 
L167: Cerastium is derived from the cite97†Greek word for 'horned', in reference to the shape of its fruit capsule.^{cite98†[8] }
L168: 
L169: Fontanum means 'of fountains', 'of springs', or 'of fast-running streams'. It is a cognate with 'fountain' and 'font'.^{cite98†[8] }
