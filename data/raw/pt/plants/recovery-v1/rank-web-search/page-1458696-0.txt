L135: Schistanthe cite57†Schltr. L136: Vireya rhododendron
L137: Rhododendron section Vireya (vireyas) is a tropical group of cite52†Rhododendron species, numbering about 300 in all.^{cite58†[1] } The group may also be treated as Rhododendron subgenus Vireya.^{cite59†[2] } Vireyas are native to cite60†southeastern Asia and range from cite61†Thailand to cite62†Australia .^{cite63†[3] }
L138: ## Description
L139: 
L140: [cite64†edit ]
L141: 
L142: Rhododendron 'Kamrau Bay', a hybrid whose background includes R. zoelleri and cite65†R. laetum ^{cite66†[4] }
L143: 
L144: Vireya are morphologically diverse, and characterised by cite67†seeds with tailed appendages, the presence of cite68†leaf idioblasts and capsule valves which twist upon opening.^{cite69†[5] }
L145: The formal description (Craven 2008) is: Scales sessile or sometimes stalked, lobed to deeply incised or sometimes entire; corolla campanulate, trumpet-like, salver-shaped, tubular or funnel-shaped; stamens (5–)10(–16), exserted to included, staminal filaments glabrous or hairy from the base; capsule valves twisting after dehiscence; seeds with a distinct tail at each end.
L146: ## Taxonomy
L147: 
L148: [cite70†edit ]
L149: 
L150: Main article: cite71†List of Sections in Subgenus Rhododendron L151: Vireya is the largest of the three sections constituting cite72†subgenus Rhododendron, and includes about a third of all Rhododendron species.^{cite73†[6] } The exact classification has varied among various authors, some authors considering Vireya to be a separate cite72†subgenus ^{cite74†[7] } rather than as here, a cite75†section of the subgenus cite53†Rhododendron .^{cite58†[1] }^{cite59†[2] } It has been suggested that taxonomic nomenclatural correctness requires changing the name of the Vireya rhododendrons to Schistanthe.
L152: Thus the term 'Vireya' has been used to refer to Section Vireya (Sleumer), Subgenus Vireya, Section Schistanthe, or the majority of the cite76†Malesian tropical rhododendrons. (Fayaz). Goetsch (2011) gives the number of species as 320, and the total taxa including subspecies, forms and varieties as 380.
L153: ### Subdivision
L154: 
L155: [cite77†edit ]
L156: 
L157: The section has traditionally been considered to consist of seven cite78†subsections based on morphology,^{cite79†[8] } although Brown et al., using cite80†phylogenetic analysis , found a lack of support for cite81†monophyly of these subsections, and rather a series of cite82†clades based on geographical distribution:^{cite69†[5] }
L158:   * R. subsect. Albovireya Sleumer, type cite83†Rhododendron album Blume
L159:   * R. subsect. Euvireya Copel.f., type cite43†Rhododendron javanicum (Blume) Benn
L160:   * R. subsect. Malayovireya Sleumer, type cite84†Rhododendron malayanum Jack
L161:   * R. subsect. Phaeovireya Sleumer, type cite85†Rhododendron beyerinckianum Koord.
L162:   * R. subsect. Pseudovireya Clarke, type cite86†Rhododendron vaccinioides Hook.f.
L163:   * R. subsect. Siphonovireya Sleumer, type cite87†Rhododendron habbemae Koord
L164:   * R. subsect. Solenovireya Copel.f., type cite88†Rhododendron jasminiflorum Hook.
L165: In Argent's (2006) treatment of Vireya as a subgenus, he included seven sections, with Euvireya consisting of five subsections,^{cite74†[7] } but this is not supported by phylogenetic analysis.^{cite58†[1] } These studies, for instance that of Hall (2006),^{cite89†[9] } suggested a very different approach.
L166: The Asian mainland species represented by Pseudovireya appear as a cite90†sister group to the other six subsections, while the Malesian species within Pseudovireya also formed an cite91†outgroup , resulting in three major cite82†clades . Thus Craven et al.
L167: proposed there be only two subsections of Vireya, corresponding to the core species, Euvireya and the much smaller Malayovireya, while the other two groups formed out of Pseudovireya be raised to section rank, keeping the original name Pseudovireya for the mainland species, and using Argent's name, Discovireya for the Malesian species.^{cite58†[1] } For a comparison of the Sleumer, Argent and Craven schemata, see Craven (2008), Table 1.
L168: Thus the new subsection structure is:
L169: 
L170:   * R. subsect. Euvireya Copel.f., type Rhododendron javanicum (Blume) Benn
L171:   * R. subsect. Malayovireya Sleumer, type cite84†Rhododendron malayanum Jack
L172: 
L173: Characteristics:(Craven 2008)
L174:   * Euvireya – Scales sessile or stalked, scattered or dense, not of two obviously different size classes, lobed to deeply incised (or sometimes entire) and the centre not dark-coloured.
L175:   * Malayovireya – Scales sessile, dense, of two obviously different size classes, lobed and the centre dark-coloured.
L176: 
L177: As of December 2023, cite92†World Flora Online treated the group as R. subg.Vireya, divided into seven sections:^{cite59†[2] }
L178:   * cite93†section Albovireya (Sleumer) Argent
L179:   * cite94†section Discovireya (Sleumer) Argent
L180:   * cite95†section Hadranthe Schltr.
L181:   * cite96†section Malayovireya (Sleumer) Argent
L182:   * cite97†section Pseudovireya (Clarke) Sleumer
L183:   * cite98†section Schistanthe Schltr.
L184:   * cite99†section Siphonovireya Argent
L185: ## Distribution
L186: 
L187: [cite100†edit ]
L188: Vireya are found throughout the cite101†Malesian Archipelago – Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra, Borneo, Java, the Philippines, Sulawesi, the Lesser Sunda Islands, and Maluku – and in cite102†Papuasia , which includes New Guinea, the Bismarck Archipelago, and the Solomon Islands. Some Vireya species also occur in Australia, China, India, Nepal, Taiwan, and Vietnam.
L189: Only a few non-Vireya species occur in Malesia, in the Malay Peninsula, Sumatra, and the Philippines.^{cite69†[5] } Malesian species are found in all subsections, while species from other areas appear only in Euvvireya, Malayovireya and Pseudovireya.^{cite58†[1] }
L190: cite103†New Guinea has the most Vireya species of any island, with 167 species. Borneo has 46 species, the second most of any island.^{cite104†[10] }
