### According to Flora of Tropical East Africa under the synonym Heteromorpha trifoliata

Umbelliferae, C.C. Townsend. Flora of Tropical East Africa. 1989

Morphology General Habit
    Perennial herb, scrambling or erect shrub or small tree with loose, greyish, flaking bark, 0.9–8 m. tall, much branched or spindly, rather sofdy woody; twigs purplish to dark grey or brown, glabrous or pilose.
Morphology Leaves
    Main stem leaves simple to trifoliolate or pinnate with 1–2 pairs of leaflets (to 3 pairs chiefly on sucker shoots), or the terminal leaflet divided almost to the base, sessile or shortly petiolulate; leaflets entire or crenulate, cartilaginous-margined, lanceolate to oblong or narrowly elliptic (the laterals sessile or very shortly petiolulate and sometimes again bifid to the base or almost so), 1.5–11 × 0.5–3(–4.5) cm., acuminate to obtuse, mucronate, glabrous except usually at least a few hairs on the upper surface of the midrib and often along the margins, rarely entirely glabrous or with a few hairs on the dorsal surface of the midrib; principal veins widely divergent; petiole slender, 1–7 cm.; sheaths short and broad, ± 1–3 mm., glabrous or ciliate, forming a shallow rim right round the stem or almost so, the rims remaining as concentric scars on the twigs.
