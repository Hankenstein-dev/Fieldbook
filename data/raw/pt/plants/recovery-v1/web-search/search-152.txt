### According to Kew Bulletin under the synonym Plectranthus glabratus

Suddee, S., A. J. Paton, & Parnell, J. (2004). A Taxonomic Revision of Tribe Ocimeae Dumort. (Lamiaceae) in Continental South East Asia II. Plectranthinae. Kew Bulletin, 59(3), 379-414. doi:10.2307/4110950

Type
    India, Madras, Wight s.n. [(holotype K! (Herb. Benthamianum) ].
Morphology General Habit
    Erect or ascending annual herbs up to 50 cm tall
Morphology Stem
    Stems sometimes branched, sometimes rooting at nodes below, succulent, round-quadrangular, glabrous or puberulous
Morphology Leaves
    Leaves fleshy, orbicular, ovate or oblong, (15-)50 - 120 x (10-)40 - 75 mm, apex acute, base truncate, cordate, or shortly cuneate, margin crenate, glabrous or puberulous on both sides, with sessile glands beneath; petioles slender, 1 - 10 cm long, puberulous
Morphology Reproductive morphology Inflorescences
    Inflorescence terminal, up to 30 cm long; axis puberulous or pubescent with or without sessile glands; verticils clearly interrupted; cymes 1.5 - 15 cm long, pedunculate, producing lax lateral cincinni, few- to many-flowered; bracts sessile, early-caducous, lanceolate, up to 7 mm long, acute or acuminate, pubescent; pedicels 2 - 4 mm long at anthesis, 3 - 7 mm long in fruit, puberulous with gland-tipped hairs
    Stamens attached close together but not united, included in anterior corolla lip
Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Gynoecium Style
    Style obscurely cleft at apex, subequal to anterior stamens
Morphology Reproductive morphology Flowers Disc
    Disc with anterior side very conspicuous, obtuse or truncate at apex, twice as long as ovary
Morphology Reproductive morphology Fruits
    Nutlets black or brown, oblong or ellipsoid, 1 - 1.5 mm diameter, smooth, shining, not or slightly producing mucilage when wet.
Distribution
    India, Sri Lanka, Thailand (new record) & Vietnam.
Ecology
    On limestone rocks; 50 - 1200 m.
Phenology
    Flowering & fruiting October -January.
Note
    Coleus paniculatus Benth. appears to be the earliest name for this taxon but when transfered to Plectranthus, the epithet 'paniculatus' becomes unavailable due to the earlier Plectranthus paniculatus Jacq. (1800). Coleus glabratus Benth. and Coleus wightii Benth. have equal priority and were both available. The epithet 'glabratus' was taken up first by Alston who recognised this species under Plectranthus. The Vietnamese Plectranthus bernardii Doan (nom inval.) differs from Plectranthus glabratus only in its smaller leaves and shorter cymes; comparison with Thai and Indian material shows that there is no clear delimitation.
[KBu]
